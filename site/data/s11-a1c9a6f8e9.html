Core Principles

This page measures today's political parties against my framework. Each party is described from its own official platform, then compared section by section with the positions I set out. No party matches my framework. Each one aligns with some principles and conflicts with others, and the pattern of those overlaps says more than any party label. The United States gets the most detail. Selected foreign systems follow for contrast.

Part A: United States

The Party Landscape

tldrIn short

Most Americans are independents, but almost all votes go to the two major parties. I judge each party position by position.

Position

American politics is a two-party system with a large unaffiliated middle. Third parties shape the debate but win almost no federal offices. I treat party labels as bundles of positions, not identities, and judge each bundle item by item.

exampleData: party lean by sex, marriage, education and race

Survey and exit-poll figures as reported by the cited sources.

  • Pew, registered voters (2023 data): women 51% Democratic, 44% Republican. Men 52% Republican, 46% Democratic. Married men 59% Republican. Married women 50% Republican; never-married women 72% Democratic. Pew Research Center – Partisanship by Gender, Sexual Orientation, Marital and Parental Status
  • Pew, by race and education: white adults without a college degree show no sex gap (men 64% Republican, women 62% Republican). Among white college graduates, men are 53% Republican while women are 57% Democratic and 42% Republican. Black women are 84% Democratic and Black men 81%. Hispanic men are 61% Democratic and Hispanic women 60%. Pew Research Center – Partisanship by Race, Ethnicity and Education
  • The 2024 gender gap in the presidential vote was about 20 points, roughly the same as in 1980, 2012, 2016 and 2020. AEI – The Exit Polls from A to Z
question"If no party fits, why compare them at all?"

Voters choose among the options that exist. Mapping where each party agrees and disagrees with my framework shows which trade-offs a vote for that party involves.

Rationale

Party identification is weak while results are concentrated. Most voters do not identify with either major party, yet nearly all votes still go to one of them. That gap is why I set out my views as positions rather than as an affiliation (Relation to Other Political Traditions).

Democratic Party

tldrIn short

I fully align with Democrats on same-sex marriage and directionally on marijuana and drug reform. I conflict with them on NATO, Israel, policing and crime, wages, taxes, entitlements, abortion, capital punishment, immigration, group preferences, and gender policy.

Position

The 2024 Democratic platform pairs an expanded federal role in wages, health care, and climate with a path to citizenship for people who entered unlawfully. I share its acceptance of same-sex marriage. I conflict with it on immigration and borders, alliances, Israel, policing and crime, economics, abortion, and capital punishment, and agree with it only in part on guns.

question"Isn't the Democratic Party the party of civil liberties?"

No. Denying negative rights is not protecting civil liberties. A party that takes people's earnings through compulsory redistribution, mandates what they must pay and buy, and restricts their right to defend themselves is not defending liberty. Civil liberties are best defended by a party that is strong on crime and protects civilians' security and rights, because a person who is not safe from violence, theft, and coercion is not free in any meaningful sense. The platform's minimum wage, progressive taxation, and mandatory entitlement system all rest on the compulsory redistribution I reject (Welfare and Entitlements), and its record on crime leaves civilians less secure (Police Power and Accountability).

summaryDemocratic Party checklist
  • Platform: a federal minimum wage of "at least $15-an-hour". No tax increase for anyone earning under $400,000, with higher taxes above that. Support for Medicaid expansion; a ban on assault weapons and universal background checks; restoring abortion access after Roe; a "pathway to citizenship for Dreamers"; a net-zero emissions goal; strengthening NATO; support for Israel; "fund the police, not defund the police."
  • Full alignment: marriage freedom (Marriage Law).
  • Directional alignment: drug reform, where the party moves toward legal marijuana and the law ought to make drugs legal for private, personal use away from minors (Drugs and Vice).
  • Mixed: guns, where its background checks and red flag laws match mine and its assault-weapons ban does not (Gun Rights).
  • Conflicts: unconditional support for NATO, where the United States ought to join alliances only when they serve U.S. interests (Alliances and NATO). A two-state solution and a base that leans Palestinian, where I reject a two-state solution (Israel and the Middle East). Policing and crime, where the party is not pro-police or anti-crime in practice and the state ought to fund police heavily and punish harshly (Police Power and Accountability); the minimum wage (Labor and Unions); progressive rates instead of a flat tax (Taxation); a mandatory entitlement system (Social Security and Medicare Transition); elective abortion after conception, which I believe should be banned by constitutional amendment (Abortion and Bioethics); no support for capital punishment (Mandatory Death Penalty); a path to citizenship for people who entered unlawfully (Immigration and Borders).
  • Culture: the platform champions "an ambitious equity and racial justice agenda," including expanded federal contracting for small disadvantaged businesses, which conflicts with my ban on group preferences (Equal Treatment). It supports "medically necessary gender-affirming care" and pledges to pass the Equality Act, which would add gender identity as a protected category. The law ought to protect race, biological sex, and sexual orientation but not recognize gender identity as a legal category (Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions).

Rationale

I agree with the Democratic Party on same-sex marriage and on several social freedoms. It disagrees on whether government should set wages, redistribute income, and run compulsory insurance. Because the platform rests on those tools, it scores low on the economic principles despite the agreement on marriage.

Directional Alignment

Not directionally aligned. In order of weight, the divergences are crime, group-based equity, gender policy, and then the size of the state. On crime the platform funds police ("fund the police, not defund the police") but moves away from punishment. It would "restrict state and local practices like solitary confinement," limit federal reincarceration "to only serious violations," and expand second-chance programs. It does not support capital punishment. I move the other way, toward mandatory death for murder and violent rape and forfeiture at three marks (Mandatory Death Penalty; Repeat Offenders). It then champions "an ambitious equity and racial justice agenda," against equal treatment without preferences (Equal Treatment), and "medically necessary gender-affirming care" and the Equality Act, against my biological-sex positions (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues). Last come a mandated wage floor, higher taxes on top earners, and expanded public coverage, against free labor markets, a flat tax, and opt-in programs (Taxation; Welfare and Entitlements). I fully agree on same-sex marriage, but that is one point of agreement inside an opposite trajectory; its NATO, Israel, and police-funding language does not match my positions in practice.

Republican Party and the MAGA Faction

tldrIn short

I fully align with Republicans on borders, DEI, sex-based policy, missile defense, and alliances only in the U.S. interest. I'm directionally aligned on punishment, policing, guns, abortion, Israel, energy, and limited government (the party goes partway). I conflict with them on animal agriculture, broad baseline tariffs, entitlements, and private-school vouchers.

Position

The 2024 Republican platform, written under Trump's leadership, combines tax cuts and deregulation with large-scale deportation, broad tariffs, and a promise not to cut Social Security or Medicare. I share its emphasis on border enforcement, gun rights, policing, energy production, and Israel. It conflicts with the populist economics: broad baseline tariffs that go beyond my cautious, targeted use, and an untouchable entitlement system.

question"Isn't the MAGA movement national conservatism, which you partly share?"

Partly. I share its emphasis on borders, patriotism, and a strong military (Civilizational Nationalism). It parts ways on broad baseline tariffs, which go beyond my view that tariffs are good in theory and questionable in practice, used as leverage, to protect strategic industries, and against national-security threats such as China (Trade and Economics), and on protecting a mandatory Social Security system "with no cuts." That pledge rules out the opt-in transition I call for.

question"How does the platform's abortion position compare?"

The platform leaves abortion to "a vote of the People, from within the States" and opposes late-term abortion. The law ought to ban elective abortion from conception by constitutional amendment. Sentience is the rights theory, and the rights claim starts at 12 weeks. The earlier ban rests on the moral weight of a fetus on its way to sentience and on caution where no one can pin down exactly when sentience starts, and it is a safeguard, because countries that set a week limit keep raising it (Abortion and Bioethics). In my framework, a right does not change from state to state (Federalism).

summaryRepublican Party checklist
  • Platform: "carry out the largest deportation operation in American history". Make the Trump tax cuts permanent and add "no tax on tips". Baseline tariffs; protect Social Security and Medicare "with no cuts"; "DRILL, BABY, DRILL"; cancel the electric-vehicle mandate; close the Department of Education; the right to keep and bear arms; replenish police forces; an Iron Dome missile shield; stand with Israel.
  • Full alignment: border enforcement and deportation (Border Security and Immigration Enforcement). Ending DEI preferences. Sex-based policy; missile defense (Military Spending and Projection); alliances judged by the U.S. interest (Alliances and NATO).
  • Directional alignment: punishment, where federal executions are restored by executive order but death is not mandatory (Mandatory Death Penalty). Policing, funded but with qualified immunity kept (Police Power and Accountability). Guns, where the party protects ownership but most of its House members voted against red-flag funding and its states spread permitless carry, while the law ought to keep background checks and red flag laws (Gun Rights); abortion, through state bans rather than my single line (Abortion and Bioethics); Israel, stopping short on Iran (Israel and the Middle East); energy, where EV mandates are cut but the ethanol mandate is set at record volumes (Subsidies and Mandates); limited government, through deregulation alongside deficits.
  • Culture: the platform pledges to "keep men out of women's sports," "ban Taxpayer funding for sex transition," teach "Knowledge and Skills, Not CRT and Gender Indoctrination," and enforce civil rights laws "to stop schools from discriminating on the basis of Race." These match my sex-based and equal-treatment positions (Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions; Equal Treatment). It does not state protection for sexual orientation in employment, which the law ought to require.
  • Conflicts: broad baseline tariffs, beyond my cautious, targeted use (Trade and Economics). A mandatory entitlement system protected "with no cuts". A tax code that adds carve-outs instead of a flat 20% (scored 0, since rate cuts still move toward lower, simpler taxes); leaving abortion to the states; federal tax-credit scholarships for private and religious schools, where my money follows the child only to public and charter schools (Education); the push to override California's Prop 12, where the United States ought to ban animal agriculture (Animal Rights and Sentientism).

Rationale

On security and borders the Republican Party is the closest major party to my framework. On economics it is closer than the Democrats on regulation and energy but not on trade or entitlements. The populist turn has moved the party toward broad baseline tariffs, where the United States ought to treat tariffs cautiously and use them in a targeted way, and toward a protected status for current entitlements, which runs against my opt-in principles.

Directional Alignment

Directionally aligned. On borders, policing, guns, energy, education, and sex-based policy, the platform moves the same way I do (Border Security; Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions). The main exceptions run against that direction: broad baseline tariffs, beyond my cautious, targeted use (Trade and Economics), and a pledge to protect Social Security and Medicare "with no cuts", which rules out the opt-in transition (Social Security and Medicare Transition).

Libertarian Party

tldrIn short

I fully align with the Libertarians on opt-in welfare, free labor markets, self-defense, and equal treatment in law. I'm directionally aligned on drugs and vice (the party goes further). I conflict with them on capital punishment, borders, alliances, and private-employer discrimination.

Position

The Libertarian Party is the closest match to my framework on economics and personal freedom. It is the furthest on punishment and borders. Its platform repeals the income tax, phases Social Security out into a voluntary private system, keeps government out of labor contracts and marriage, and repeals laws against victimless crimes. It also opposes the death penalty and calls for open movement across borders.

question"You agree with libertarianism on rights. Why not adopt the Libertarian platform?"

Because I accept far more force against people who violate rights. The platform states, "We oppose the administration of the death penalty by the state," and it supports "unrestricted movement of human as well as financial capital across national borders." I hold the opposite on both (Mandatory Death Penalty; Immigration and Borders).

summaryLibertarian Party checklist
  • Platform: repeal the income tax and abolish the IRS. Phase out Social Security toward a private voluntary system. Labor contracts outside government's scope; repeal laws on drugs, gambling, and sex work; restitution to victims; the Second Amendment and self-defense; no government role in marriage; a sufficient military, no entangling alliances, no compulsory service; opposition to the death penalty; open borders.
  • Full alignment: opt-in retirement (Social Security and Medicare Transition). No minimum wage (Labor and Unions). Self-defense with no duty to retreat (Self-Defense and Defense of Others); equal legal treatment.
  • Directional alignment: negative rights and limited government (Political Philosophy). Vice, where the party legalizes the commercial sale and public use of all drugs and the law ought to legalize private, personal use only (Drugs and Vice).
  • Mixed: guns, where the party protects ownership but opposes every law that restricts or monitors firearms, including the background checks I support (Gun Rights). Taxes, where abolishing the income tax overshoots my flat 20% (Taxation).
  • Culture: the platform says government should not treat anyone differently by "sex ... or sexual orientation," and that gender identity "should have no impact on the government's treatment of individuals." It also holds that private organizations "retain their rights to set whatever standards of association they deem appropriate." I agree on equal legal treatment by sex, but the law ought to bar private employers and businesses from treating people by race, sex, or sexual orientation, and record birth sex in law (Equal Treatment).
  • Conflicts: the death penalty. Open borders. Non-interventionism and avoiding alliances (Alliances and NATO); no position on animal rights.

Rationale

I sit closest to libertarianism on what government may do to peaceful people. It differs sharply on what government should do to people who violate rights and to threats from outside the country. The party's 2024 nominee won 0.42% of the vote.

Directional Alignment

Directionally aligned on economics and personal liberty; not on security. The platform shrinks the state toward voluntary exchange, as I do (Political Philosophy). It moves the opposite way on the use of force against people who violate rights and on borders, where I accept far more state power (Order and Security).

Green Party

tldrIn short

I fully align with the Greens on ranked-choice voting, and directionally on animal welfare, drugs, sex work, and same-sex marriage. I conflict with them on economics, energy subsidies, nuclear power, defense, Israel, bail, voting rules, guns, and capital punishment.

Position

The Green Party combines an ecosocialist economic program with the strongest animal-welfare commitments of any US party. I share the animal-welfare direction and the interest in voting reform. I conflict with the Greens on economics, energy, defense, and punishment.

question"The Greens want animal protections. Doesn't that make them the sentientist party?"

Yes, they are the closest on animal rights. They would extend the Animal Welfare Act to rats, mice, and birds, replace factory farms (CAFOs), and use non-animal chemical testing where available (Animal Rights and Sentience Technology). However, nearly all other social and cultural issues are at odds: they are the opposite on crime and immigration, and the opposite on redistribution. Though they are the best on animal rights, that is nearly all they are aligned on. I would rather put my lot into the vastly more aligned party and rely on private innovation to produce lab-grown meat.

summaryGreen Party checklist
  • Platform: "abolish the death penalty". Single-payer health care. An Ecosocialist Green New Deal; a national minimum wage indexed to inflation; phasing out nuclear power and fossil, nuclear, and biomass subsidies; replacing CAFOs and banning corporate ownership of farms; extending the Animal Welfare Act; cutting the defense budget; nuclear disarmament; proportional representation and ranked-choice voting.
  • Directional alignment: animal welfare, where the party replaces factory farms and the United States ought to ban animal agriculture (Animal Rights and Sentientism). Drug decriminalization, where the law ought to go further and make private use legal, and decriminalized sex work, which ought to be legal under licensing (Drugs and Vice); same-sex marriage (Marriage Law).
  • Full alignment: ranked-choice voting in every election (Ranked-Choice Voting).
  • Culture: the platform supports "affirmative action," names feminism among its key values, and would add "sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression" to civil rights law. I reject group preferences and agree on protecting sexual orientation, and the law ought not to recognize gender identity as a legal category (Equal Treatment; Feminism and Sex Equality).
  • Conflicts: single-payer health care (Healthcare). The minimum wage. The phase-out of nuclear power; defense cuts (Military Spending and Projection); abolishing the death penalty; a planned economy (Economic System); publicly funded renewables, where the government ought to subsidize nothing without opt-in (Subsidies and Mandates); national proportional representation, alongside opposition to the voter ID and voter license test I call for (Voting and Elections); bail as a right (Pretrial Detention and Bail).

Rationale

The Greens show that animal welfare is not tied to free markets. My sentientism is shared on the left, while my economic and security positions are shared on the right. The party's 2024 nominee won 0.56% of the vote.

Directional Alignment

Not directionally aligned. In order of weight: on crime, the platform says "Abolish the death penalty," calls for "freedom on bail as a right of all defendants charged with non-violent crimes," and seeks to reverse mandatory sentencing, the opposite of my capital and compelled-labor system (Mandatory Death Penalty; Compelled Labor). On race, it supports "affirmative action" and would "Teach all students about white supremacy and intersectionality" (Equal Treatment; Curriculum Content). On gender, it would add "gender identity, and gender expression" to civil rights law (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues). On economics, it favors a planned economy, single-payer care, a national minimum wage, and defense cuts (Economic System). Two overlaps are genuine: animal welfare (Animal Rights and Sentientism) and decriminalizing consensual adult sex for pay, which I already call for allowing under regulation (Drugs and Vice).

Democratic Socialists of America and the Progressive Wing

tldrIn short

I conflict with DSA at the foundation: on property, taxation, policing, prisons, bail, borders, the Senate, Israel, and gender policy. I align only directionally on drug decriminalization and marriage.

Position

The Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) is not a ballot party. It is an organization whose members, including Representatives Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Rashida Tlaib and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, mostly run as Democrats. Its program calls for collective ownership of major industries, wealth taxes, abolition of police and prisons, open migration, and abolition of the Senate. It conflicts with my framework at the foundation.

In the 2026 midterms, DSA-endorsed candidates won 7 of the 10 House races they contested (Midterm elections).

question"Is democratic socialism just Nordic-style social democracy?"

No. DSA states, "We want to collectively own the key economic drivers ... such as energy production and transportation," and its program calls for "public ownership of the largest corporations and essential industries." Nordic social democracies keep private ownership and market economies (see Part B). DSA's goal is a different economic system (Economic System).

question"Does the progressive wing share the full DSA program?"

Not necessarily. Many progressive Democrats back Medicare for All and a Green New Deal without endorsing police abolition or public ownership of industry. This page scores DSA's written program, not every progressive politician.

question"Why do progressive politicians as a group run against your positions?"

Because their shared commitments start from the opposite premises, and the gaps can be ranked. First is crime, where the two are opposite. The state ought to impose death for murder and violent rape (Mandatory Death Penalty), forfeiture at three marks (Repeat Offenders), compelled labor for every custodial sentence (Compelled Labor), no duty to retreat (Self-Defense), and larger, better-funded police (Police Power and Accountability). Progressive platforms would abolish the death penalty, widen bail and shorten incarceration, and, in DSA's case, move "towards fully abolishing the police and prison system." Second is group identity: DEI preferences and critical race theory's account of racism as embedded in law and institutions, against one rule for every individual (Equal Treatment). Third is how prevalent gender identity has become in progressive politics, against my biological-sex positions (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues). After those come property, taxes, and the size of the state. I treat property as a negative right (Property Rights). Progressives favor public or collective ownership of key industries. I would set a flat tax and a limited state (Taxation). They favor progressive and wealth taxes to fund a larger state. Some overlaps exist, such as decriminalizing drug use (Drug Policy), detention not set by ability to pay (Pretrial Detention and Bail), and, among Greens, animal welfare. Ending qualified immunity is a surface agreement with the opposite purpose: I pair it with stronger policing, progressives with shrinking it.

summaryDSA program checklist
  • Program: "Demilitarize police departments, disempower police unions, and redirect funding to public services as steps towards fully abolishing the police and prison system". End qualified immunity. "Abolish ICE ... Legalize migration, grant amnesty for all immigrants ... end visa caps and quotas"; "abolish the Senate"; proportional representation; public ownership of the largest corporations; "aggressive wealth taxes"; Medicare for All; a Green New Deal; "End all military and economic aid to Israel." DSA reports more than 95,000 members.
  • Directional alignment: drug decriminalization (Drugs and Vice); same-sex marriage (Marriage Law).
  • Surface only: ending qualified immunity, which I pair with heavily funded police while DSA pairs it with cutting police budgets "annually towards zero" (Police Power and Accountability).
  • Culture: the program calls for ending restrictions on "gender expression and transition" and describes current law as "patriarchal restrictions" on family life, while asking to "ban sex discrimination." I agree on banning sex discrimination but hold that sex is biological and that minors receive no transgender treatment (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues).
  • Conflicts: collective ownership (Property Rights). Wealth taxes (Taxation). Abolishing police and prisons (Order and Security); open migration and amnesty; abolishing the Senate (Federalism); ending aid to Israel; single-payer health care; "Freedom for all incarcerated people" and the end of cash bail, where the state ought to detain anyone found dangerous (Pretrial Detention and Bail).

Rationale

I begin with negative rights and private property. DSA begins with collective ownership. The two disagree at the foundation, and almost every later position follows from that.

The disagreement can be ranked. Crime comes first, because the two are opposite. The law ought to make murder and violent rape capital (Mandatory Death Penalty), should forfeit the right to life at three marks (Repeat Offenders), serves every custodial sentence as compelled labor (Compelled Labor), recognizes no duty to retreat (Self-Defense), and funds larger, better-paid police (Police Power and Accountability). DSA's program would "Demilitarize police departments, disempower police unions, and redirect funding to public services as steps towards fully abolishing the police and prison system," and the Green platform says "Abolish the death penalty."

Second is group identity. DEI preferences and the premise of critical race theory, that racism is "embedded in legal systems and policies" rather than only in individual bias, organize politics around groups. The law ought to apply one rule to every individual and bar treatment by race, biological sex, or sexual orientation in either direction in employment, admissions, and business (Equal Treatment). Third is the prevalence of gender identity in progressive politics: DSA would end restrictions on "gender expression and transition," against my position that sex is biological and that minors receive no transgender treatment (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues).

The economic divergences follow. On property, DSA's goal of public ownership of "the largest corporations and essential industries" overrides the owner's rights that I treat as basic (Property Rights). On the size of the state, "aggressive wealth taxes" and Medicare for All require a larger and more redistributive government, against my flat tax, opt-in programs, and limited state (Taxation; Welfare and Entitlements).

Some agreements are surface only. Both would end qualified immunity, but for opposite purposes: the state ought to hold officers to a higher standard while expanding and funding police, and DSA treats it as a step toward shrinking and abolishing them (Police Power and Accountability). Both would stop setting detention by ability to pay, but the state ought to detain anyone found dangerous (Pretrial Detention and Bail). These agreements are narrow and do not change the overall direction.

Directional Alignment

Not directionally aligned. In order of weight, DSA runs opposite to my framework on crime, group identity and race, gender policy, and then property, taxes, and the size of the state. Its points of agreement are narrow and do not change that direction.

Constitution Party

tldrIn short

I fully align with the Constitution Party on phasing out Social Security, borders, and sex-based policy. I'm directionally aligned on capital punishment, abortion, and limited government (the party goes partway or further). I conflict with them on religion in law, same-sex marriage, tariffs in place of a flat tax, the Federal Reserve, and private-employer discrimination.

Position

The Constitution Party seeks to "restore the American system of law and justice to its Biblical foundations and to limit the federal government to its constitutional boundaries." It matches my framework on limited government, borders, and phasing out Social Security. It conflicts on religion in law and, more mildly, on replacing the income tax with tariff revenue rather than a flat tax.

question"Isn't this the closest party to you on borders and crime?"

On borders, yes: it calls for the return of all illegal aliens, an immigration moratorium, and troops at the border. It supports "capital punishment where appropriate," which is close to my position. But its stated goal of grounding law in the Bible conflicts with my separation of church and state (Role of Religion in Government).

summaryConstitution Party checklist
  • Platform: replace income, payroll, and estate taxes with tariff-based revenue. End the Federal Reserve. Phase out Social Security while meeting obligations already incurred; leave the United Nations; eliminate foreign aid; oppose abortion; return all illegal aliens, impose an immigration moratorium, and station troops at the border.
  • Full alignment: phasing out Social Security while honoring past obligations. Border enforcement (Immigration and Borders); sex-based policy.
  • Directional alignment: capital punishment "where appropriate", where the law ought to make it mandatory (Mandatory Death Penalty). Protecting fetal life, though the platform allows no rape exception, offering "compassionate care" "instead of the further violence of abortion", while I free the woman of obligation after rape (Abortion and Bioethics). Limited government, grounded in Scripture; skepticism of global institutions (Global Institutions).
  • Mixed: guns, where the party's absolutism overshoots my background checks and red flag laws (Gun Rights).
  • Culture: the platform rejects "legal favor or special protection" for homosexual and transgender people. I agree on not recognizing gender identity in law, but the law ought to protect sexual orientation in employment and public business, and I avoid that wording (Equal Treatment).
  • Conflicts: biblical foundations for law. Opposition to same-sex marriage. No employment protection for sexual orientation; tariff-based revenue in place of a flat income tax (Taxation; Trade and Economics); ending the Federal Reserve (Monetary Policy); eliminating all foreign aid (Foreign Aid); no position on animal rights.

Rationale

The Constitution Party agrees with me on many policies but disagrees on their basis. It grounds rights in the Creator and law in Scripture. I ground rights in sentience, and religion ought to stay out of government. The party's 2024 nominee received 12,786 votes.

Directional Alignment

Directionally aligned on policy; not on foundations. On borders, capital punishment, phasing out Social Security, and sex-based policy, the platform moves the same way. It differs on why: it grounds law in Scripture, while I ground rights in sentience, and religion ought to stay out of government (Role of Religion in Government). It also rejects protection for sexual orientation, which I call for (Equal Treatment).

Public Opinion Trends: Socialism and Islamism

tldrIn short

Positive views of socialism among all Americans were flat at 35% to 39% from 2010 to 2025, then rose to 43% in 2026. The change is concentrated among Democrats, younger adults, and, most recently, independents; Republicans are at 14%. DSA's self-reported membership rose, fell, and rose again, and a DSA member won New York City's mayoralty in 2025. Islam is growing in the U.S., now about 1% of adults, mainly through immigration and higher birth rates. I believe general Islamic population growth, not just conversion, is extremely dangerous to the cohesion of our nation. There is no reliable national U.S. trend data on acceptance of Islamist ideas.

Position

These are the measured trends. My views of socialism and Islam are set out in Socialism and Islam and Extremism.

question"Is acceptance of socialism rising?"

Overall, only recently. Gallup's rating of socialism stayed between 35% and 39% from 2010 to 2025 and reached 43% in August 2026, the first reading above 39%. Through 2025 the stability hid a split: Democrats rose from 50% to 66% while Republicans fell from 19% to 14%. The 2026 rise came entirely from independents, from 37% to 45%. Capitalism's rating fell from 61% in 2010 to 54% in 2025 and was 55% in 2026. Among Democrats it fell from 51% to 35%. Pew's surveys moved the other way over a shorter span, with positive views of socialism falling from 42% in 2019 to 36% in 2022.

question"Are young Americans more socialist?"

Yes. In Gallup's 2026 poll, adults 18 to 34 viewed socialism more positively than capitalism, 57% to 43%, while those 55 and older favored capitalism 65% to 34%. In Pew's 2022 survey, 44% of adults 18 to 29 viewed socialism positively and 40% capitalism, the lowest capitalism rating of any age group.

question"How large is DSA?"

By its own counts, DSA had about 5,000 members for decades and grew to a peak of just under 79,000 members in good standing in 2021, then fell to about 51,000 by October 2024. Its dues-paying membership rose to 92,912 by December 2025 during Zohran Mamdani's campaign, according to DSA data reported by City & State. The New York City chapter more than doubled, from 5,910 to 13,145. These are self-reported figures.

question"How did Mamdani win New York?"

question"Is Islam growing in the U.S.?"

Yes. Pew estimated 2.35 million Muslims of all ages in 2007, 2.75 million in 2011, and 3.45 million in 2017, about 1.1% of the population, growing by roughly 100,000 a year. The 2020 U.S. Religion Census counted 2,771 mosques with about 4.45 million adherents, an increase of nearly 700 mosques and 1.9 million adherents since 2010. Its adherent count uses a different method and runs higher than Pew's estimates. General Islamic population growth, not just conversion, is extremely dangerous to the cohesion of our nation.

Survey shares of adults remain about 1%: Pew's Religious Landscape Study found 0.9% in 2014 and 1% in 2023–24, and Gallup's combined 2020–2024 data found 0.9% (0.8% in 2016 and 2017). Pew notes that the 2023–24 study was conducted mainly online and on paper, while earlier waves were by telephone, so comparisons with earlier years should be made cautiously. Gallup finds Muslim identification higher among younger adults, 1.9% of Gen Z and 1.4% of millennials.

The growth comes mainly from immigration and births. Pew found 58% of U.S. Muslim adults were born abroad and another 18% are children of immigrants, and Muslims ages 40 to 59 average 2.4 children against 2.1 for all Americans. Pew also reports that conversions into and out of Islam roughly balance.

Part B: International Comparison

tldrIn short

Here I compare my framework with parties and governments abroad. The comparisons cover the European Union, Nordic social democracy, the United Kingdom, Germany, Switzerland, and Singapore and Japan.

European Union

Position

The European Parliament elected in 2024 is led by the center-right European People's Party (188 seats), followed by the Socialists and Democrats (136). The national-conservative groups, Patriots for Europe (84), European Conservatives and Reformists (78), and Europe of Sovereign Nations (25), together outnumber the Socialists. The Union pools trade and regulation across member states, which I view cautiously as a supranational layer above national self-government (Global Institutions).

exampleData: European Parliament seats, 2024–2029

2024 seats: EPP 188, S&D 136, Patriots 84, ECR 78, Renew 77, Greens/EFA 53, Left 46, ESN 25, non-attached 33. The rise of the national-conservative groups mirrors the populist turn among American Republicans.

Directional Alignment

Using the same order as the US profiles (crime, then DEI and critical race theory, then gender policy, then property, taxes, and the size of the state), no EU group is fully aligned, because of crime. The EU Charter of Fundamental Rights states, "No one shall be condemned to the death penalty, or executed," so every party that accepts the Union's legal order rejects my capital punishment (Mandatory Death Penalty). On gender, the Commission's LGBTIQ equality strategies, now renewed for 2026 to 2030, move toward legal recognition of gender identity, against my biological-sex positions (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues). The national-conservative groups (Patriots, ECR, ESN) are the closest on borders and national sovereignty, the area where I'm most skeptical of the Union (Global Institutions).

Closest alignment: the national-conservative groups (Patriots for Europe, ECR, ESN), for their emphasis on borders and national sovereignty. Conflict: like every group, they operate within a Charter that bars the death penalty.

Nordic Social Democracy

Position

The Nordic countries combine market economies and private ownership with large, tax-funded welfare states. Denmark has the highest tax-to-GDP ratio in the EU. Wages are set mainly through collective agreements rather than a statutory minimum wage. This model is the strongest argument that a generous welfare state can coexist with free markets. I still reject it because the welfare state is compulsory (Welfare and Entitlements). It also shows how DSA's program differs from social democracy: the Nordic countries kept private ownership.

Directional Alignment

Not directionally aligned on the size of the state; partly aligned on crime in Sweden. The large compulsory welfare state runs against my framework (Welfare and Entitlements). On crime, Sweden's 2022 Tidö Agreement between the Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals, and Sweden Democrats called for "dubbla straff för gängkriminella" (double penalties for gang criminals), an end to sentencing discounts for offenders over 18, and tougher penalties for violent and sexual crimes. Doubled gang sentences took effect in August 2026. That moves in my direction on punishment, but no Nordic party proposes capital punishment (Mandatory Death Penalty).

Closest alignment: the Sweden Democrats and their Tidö partners (Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals), whose agreement produced the crime package. Conflict: the welfare state they govern within stays compulsory and tax-funded.

United Kingdom

Position

Labour won 411 seats in 2024 with 33.7% of the vote. The Conservatives won 121 seats with 23.7%, and the Liberal Democrats 72 with 12.2%. A large majority on a third of the vote shows how first-past-the-post voting turns pluralities into majorities, the same mechanism behind the American two-party system (Voting and Elections). Like the US, Britain has a right-of-center and a left-of-center party, with a third party far underrepresented relative to its vote share.

Directional Alignment

On gender, the most important British development came from the courts rather than the parties. In For Women Scotland v The Scottish Ministers (2025), the Supreme Court held unanimously that "sex," "man," and "woman" in the Equality Act 2010 "refer to biological sex," which matches my definition (LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues).

Conflicts: it makes no death-penalty pledge, and replacing the Equality Act outright would leave open whether sexual orientation stays protected in employment, which I call for.

Switzerland

Position

Switzerland limits the power of parties through direct democracy. A popular initiative needs 100,000 valid signatures within 18 months to force a national vote on a constitutional amendment. An optional referendum needs 50,000 signatures within 100 days to put a new federal law to a vote. Citizens can overrule what parties agree on in parliament. I protect rights from majority votes, but of the systems on this page, it is the closest to my preference for decentralized power under citizen control (Federalism).

Directional Alignment

The Swiss People's Party (SVP) is the closest on crime and borders. Its 2010 initiative for the automatic deportation of foreigners convicted of serious crimes passed with 52.91% of the vote and a majority of cantons. The Social Democrats, Greens, FDP, and CVP opposed it (Border Security).

Closest alignment: the SVP, for its record on deporting foreign offenders and on borders.

Singapore and Japan

Position

Both countries have long been governed by a dominant party and both keep capital punishment. Singapore's People's Action Party won 87 of 97 seats with 65.57% of the vote in 2025. Singapore carried out at least 9 executions in 2024, mostly for drug offences, against 25 in the US. Japan's Liberal Democratic Party has governed almost continuously since 1955, though it lost its lower-house majority in 2024. Japan retains the death penalty but carried out no executions in 2024. I share their emphasis on order. Directional Alignment

Gender and marriage: in 2022 Singapore's Parliament repealed Section 377A, which criminalized sex between men, 93 to 3, a move toward my framework. It also added Article 156 to the Constitution so that no fundamental-liberties challenge can invalidate a law defining "marriage as a union between a man and a woman," which conflicts with my marriage freedom (Marriage Law).

Closest alignment: Singapore's People's Action Party and Japan's Liberal Democratic Party, the long-governing parties under which capital punishment is kept. Conflicts: Singapore executes for cannabis trafficking and protects a man-woman definition of marriage from constitutional challenge.

Summary Scorecard

tldrIn short

No American party matches my positions. Republicans score highest (+9): they lead on borders, DEI, sex-based policy, and defense, and move my way on punishment, policing, guns, and abortion, though they keep qualified immunity, defend animal agriculture, and fund private schools through tax credits. The Libertarian and Constitution parties tie (+3). The Libertarians match on economics and personal freedom but oppose the death penalty and leave private employment unprotected. The Constitution Party matches on borders, punishment, and sex-based policy but grounds law in religion and rejects protection for sexual orientation. Democrats follow (−10), agreeing on marriage and drug reform but opposing me on crime, alliances, Israel, economics, group preferences, and gender policy. The Greens and DSA conflict most (−13). DSA and the Greens run further than the Democrats on punishment and feminism, the Greens also on guns, and the Greens are the only party that moves my way on animals.

Method

Each US party is scored on sixteen core principles of my framework, using its official platform or program together with its officeholders' record and its base's stance. Each party is graded against the realistic options, not against a perfect match: a party that moves my way earns alignment, and conflict is reserved for parties that move the opposite way. On the eleven general rows, +1 means the party moves my way, 0 means it is mixed or silent, and −1 means it moves the opposite way. The crime row (mandatory death penalty, firm punishment) and the four culture rows (DEI and group preferences, gender and transgender policy, feminism, and protected traits) are double-weighted from −2 to +2: ±2 for full alignment or full conflict, ±1 for directional. An unweighted total, counting only the sign of each row, is shown beneath the weighted total. The culture rows are measured against my equal-treatment rule: race, biological sex, and sexual orientation are protected in either direction, gender identity is not a legal category, and there are no DEI preferences. Scores are summed. Against progressive politics, I rank my divergences in this order: crime first, then DEI and critical race theory, then gender policy, then property, taxes, and the size of the state. Apart from the double weighting, the total shows how many principles a party shares, not how important each one is, so it should be read alongside the profiles above. Under the earlier equal-weight scorecard of twelve rows the totals were Democrats −7, Republicans +2, Libertarians +3, Greens −5, DSA −6, Constitution +3.

question"Isn't the scorecard self-serving? Of course you rank parties by how close they are to you."

Yes, by design. The scorecard measures alignment with my framework, not which party is better in general, and a reader who holds different principles would score the parties differently. What keeps it honest is the method. Each score rests on the party's own platform and its record in practice, the rows and weights are published, mixed or silent positions score zero, each party is graded against the realistic options rather than against a perfect match, and conflicts are listed even for the parties that score highest, such as the Republican platform's protection of officers from lawsuits. The double-weighted rows score −2 to +2, ±2 for full alignment or full conflict and ±1 for directional. The unweighted totals count only the sign of each row, so readers can see what the weights change. Without them Republicans still lead (+7), the Libertarian and Constitution parties tie (+3), Democrats follow (−8), and the Greens and DSA tie at the bottom (−9).
