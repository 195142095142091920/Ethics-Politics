Student internship limits blocked

November 17, 2026 · Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)

Status as of Nov 18, 2026: Open. Preliminary injunction and stay; the case continues in the district court.

Since 1991 the federal government has counted any internship or practicum a certified school authorizes for academic credit as curricular practical training (CPT), work that foreign students on F-1 visas may do as "an integral part of an established curriculum." In broadcast messages of August 12 and 24, 2026, the Student and Exchange Visitor Program told schools that training counts only if it is required of every student in the degree program, not elective training taken for credit, and warned school officials of civil and criminal liability. Schools responded by denying or rescinding CPT and changing curricula. Immigration and Customs Enforcement counted 140,775 student visa holders authorized for CPT in 2024, according to Reuters. On November 17, 2026, in Association of American Universities v. Department of Homeland Security (D. Mass., No. 1:26-cv-14520), Judge Julia E. Kobick stayed both broadcasts and barred the government from applying their reading to the plaintiffs' members. She found the change likely arbitrary and capricious because the agency departed from its long-standing position "without acknowledging, let alone explaining, the change or considering serious reliance interests." The government had argued the messages "merely implement the correct reading of the existing regulation."

A student visa is for study, and work for foreign nationals belongs to the employment path, which opens only for labor needs the domestic workforce cannot meet first (Immigration and Borders; Skilled-Worker Visas (H-1B)). Confining student work to training the degree actually requires keeps the visa to its purpose, and protects American graduates competing for the same internships. The objection is that elective internships are part of a real education and that schools relied on the old reading for 35 years. Reliance is a reason to change the rule in the open, not a reason to keep it: an elective placement for credit is still a job, and a credit-bearing elective is the easiest place to pass off work as study. The method lost, and it should have. A policy that reverses three decades of practice belongs in a published rule or a statute, with notice and a date set in advance, not in a broadcast email threatening school officials with prosecution for approvals they gave under the old reading. This builds on Fixed terms for foreign students struck down.

Fixed terms for foreign students struck down

November 16, 2026 · Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)

Status as of Nov 17, 2026: Open. Vacated by the district court; the administration's appeal of the September stay is before the First Circuit.

A Homeland Security rule published July 17, 2026 would have ended "duration of status," which lets foreign students (F), exchange visitors (J) and foreign journalists (I) stay as long as they keep their status and progress in their programs. In its place it set fixed admission periods capped at four years for students and exchange visitors, with extensions granted at the agency's discretion. It also limited changes of program and joint degrees. The department said fixed terms would let it check periodically whether these visitors are complying and reduce overstays. On November 16, 2026, in Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. Department of Homeland Security (D. Mass., No. 1:26-cv-13799), Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV vacated the rule. He had already postponed it in September. He found the rule exceeded the department's authority and wrote, "Put bluntly, DHS is seeking to prevent or discourage the very activity—providing educational opportunities for foreign students in the United States—that Congress sought to permit and encourage."

Student visas are one of the named entry paths I keep, and every path runs under values vetting (Immigration and Borders). A fixed term with a check before any extension fits that: a visitor is re-examined rather than admitted once and left alone for as long as a program runs. The objection is that four years is too short for many degrees and that discretionary extensions disrupt honest students. That objection lands on the details, not the direction. An extension tied to documented progress and a fresh values check answers it. Rules on changing majors or joint degrees do nothing for vetting, and the court was right that they reach past the purpose of the visa. The cleaner route is for Congress to write fixed terms and periodic re-vetting into the statute, so the policy does not depend on a rule a court can set aside.

Labor test for employer-sponsored green cards

November 16, 2026 · Grade: Aligned

Status as of Nov 17, 2026: Open. Proposed rule published November 17, 2026 (91 FR 73916). Comments close January 19, 2027.

The Labor Department proposes to rebuild the labor market test that employers must pass before sponsoring a foreign worker for a green card (permanent labor certification, or PERM). It would eliminate Schedule A, the list of occupations exempt from the test, which has named only nurses and physical therapists since 1991. The department says the Immigration and Nationality Act requires a finding that no able, willing, qualified and available U.S. workers exist for each position, so it cannot exempt a whole occupation, and that more than 2,000 comments on an earlier request for information offered no reliable method to keep the list. The rule would also strengthen recruitment requirements, add safeguards for U.S. workers laid off by the sponsoring employer or by third-party staffing firms, widen notice of filing to U.S. workers, and write electronic filing into the regulations. It cites Executive Orders 14159 and 14431.

American labor needs should be met first through the domestic workforce, and only then by immigration, which I rank behind values fit and assimilation (Skilled-Worker Visas (H-1B); Immigration and Borders). A test applied to each job, rather than a standing exemption for whole occupations, does that. The strongest objection is the nursing shortage: the Health Resources and Services Administration projects a 10% shortfall of registered nurses in 2027, 24% outside metropolitan areas. The rule does not bar foreign nurses. Where no qualified American applies, the test certifies the job, so a real shortage shows up in the recruitment results. What ends is certification without looking, on a list the department says it could not verify.

Highway funds withheld from New York over immigrant truck licenses

November 13, 2026 · Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)

Status as of Nov 16, 2026: Open. The Second Circuit set aside the withholding on November 13, 2026. The government can seek rehearing or certiorari.

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration withheld 4% of New York's fiscal 2027 federal-aid highway funds, about $73.5 million, because the state issued commercial driver's licenses to non-domiciled immigrants that stayed valid for five or eight years instead of expiring with the holder's lawful-presence documents. In an audit sample of 200 such licenses, 101 outlasted the documents; about 32,600 New Yorkers held one. The agency conceded that the regulations in force when it audited New York contained no expiration-matching requirement. That requirement came later, in a final rule published February 13, 2026, and effective March 16, 2026, after an earlier interim rule was stayed by the D.C. Circuit. In State of New York v. U.S. Department of Transportation, the Second Circuit (Judge Nathan, joined by Judge Merriam) granted New York's petition and set the determination aside. Judge Schwartz concurred in the judgment only, writing that the case turns on the text alone. The Transportation Department said, "We've put a stop to the madness," and says about 30,000 such licenses have been canceled nationwide.

A license that lets a non-citizen drive a truck for years after his lawful presence ends is a work permit the law does not give him, and the law ought to be enforced as written (Immigration and Borders). The current rule, which requires these licenses to expire with lawful presence, is the direction I want. But agencies cannot go beyond the rules that authorize them, and a penalty for breaking a requirement that was not yet written fails that test (Government Structure and Checks). The court was right, and the rule now in force fixes the gap the proper way.

Citizenship checks for HUD housing aid

November 12, 2026 · Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)

Status as of Nov 12, 2026: Open. Final rule. Takes effect December 14, 2026.

HUD's final rule under Section 214 of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1980 requires every applicant and recipient in covered housing programs to prove U.S. citizenship or eligible immigration status, at any age, ending the exemption for people 62 and older. Prorated aid to "mixed" families, where some members are ineligible, becomes temporary pending verification instead of running indefinitely, and a deferral of termination of up to 18 months stays available. HUD estimates that about 20,000 mixed-status families received assistance in December 2024, about 78 percent of them with children. It rejected grandfathering current families because low turnover would delay moving the aid to fully eligible families on waiting lists by more than a decade.

Non-citizens should not draw housing aid. A green-card holder who takes it should be removed after a court finding (Removal of Non-Citizen Residents), and the safety floor ought to offer emergency shelter, not housing (Housing, Zoning, and Homelessness). Verifying status before paying is the direction I want. Commenters warned that mixed-status families, most with children, would be evicted. HUD's answer holds: there is more demand than aid, so every subsidy kept by an ineligible household is one denied to an eligible family on the waiting list. The details differ: I hold that a U.S.-citizen child keeps any benefit the child qualifies for, while this rule ends a mixed family's whole subsidy after the deferral.
