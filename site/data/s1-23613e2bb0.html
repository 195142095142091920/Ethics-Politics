Moral anti-realism

Moral anti-realism is the view that there are no moral facts that hold independently of what anyone thinks or prescribes. I hold that view, so I prescribe a sentience-centered system because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available.

question"Isn't morality subjective?"

It is a prescribed system (Moral anti-realism). Its authority rests on its coherence and on its practical necessity for protecting subjective experience, not on moral facts existing independently of human prescription.

question"Why moral anti-realism?"

I don't claim moral facts independent of what anyone thinks or prescribes can be established, and I hold moral claims to the same standard of evidence I apply elsewhere. Three arguments support that judgment. They are reasons, not proof. The first is from disagreement: moral views differ deeply across cultures and eras in ways that are better explained by differing attitudes and interests than by some groups perceiving moral facts that others miss. The second is J. L. Mackie's argument from queerness: objective values would have to be facts of a very strange kind, prescriptions built into the world that motivate or bind anyone who grasps them. Nothing else in a naturalistic picture of the world is like that. The third is evolutionary debunking, developed by Sharon Street and Richard Joyce. Human moral attitudes were shaped by natural selection for survival and reproduction, not for tracking independent moral truth, so any match between them and such truths would be a coincidence. Rather than rest on facts I can't demonstrate, I defend the system on grounds that can be checked, set out in the answer above.

question"What kind of anti-realism is it?"

A hybrid, which is a recognized position in metaethics. It is constructivist in the Humean sense: its rules are built from what beings actually care about, chiefly avoiding suffering, and the best set of rules is the one that is most coherent and stable. It is prescriptivist in form, in the tradition of R. M. Hare: its moral statements are universal prescriptions, rules that apply to everyone in the same situation, including the person who states them. And it has an expressive element: its judgments also express a settled attitude against harm to beings that can suffer.

question"If there are no objective moral facts, why should anyone accept these rights?"

Critics claim that a framework without independent moral facts cannot bind anyone who rejects its starting point. No moral system meets that demand, realist ones included, because asserting that moral facts exist does not by itself persuade someone who denies them. What my framework delivers is what any moral system can actually deliver. My reasons are open to everyone. The system is coherent and stable, it protects every being that can be harmed rather than drawing arbitrary lines, and anyone who wants protection from harm has a reason to support a system that protects it. Those who accept it are motivated by recognizing that coherence and by cultivated character (Moral Motivation). Those who reject it are still bound by its enforcement, as people are under any legal order.

question"If no moral system is objectively true, what licenses imposing yours?"

The same thing that licenses any legal order, minus the pretense of cosmic backing. Every legal order enforces some norms on people who reject them. Even the most permissive one enforces its ban on murder against the murderer and its property rules against the thief. Anti-realism doesn't create the problem of imposition. It only drops the claim that the universe signed off on the result. The real choice is which system is enforced, not whether one is.

Declining to enforce is also a choice, and it has victims. Where the law won't stop an aggressor, the aggressor's will is imposed on the victim. Neutrality between the two isn't available.

What licenses my system in particular is what it protects. Every being that can suffer already counts its own suffering against an outcome, from its own standpoint, before any theory is applied. That is the Humean constructivist starting point Sharon Street develops: reasons are built from what valuers actually care about. Hare's universalizability does the rest. Anyone who demands not to be harmed is, by the same logic, committed to that rule for everyone in the same position, and the position that matters is being able to suffer. My enforcement rests on protecting those who can be harmed, not on a claim of truth (Philosophy of Law).

Realists face the same practical problem. They still impose on people who reject the facts they claim, and claimed objectivity hasn't produced agreement. A realist judge enforces the law on a defendant who denies its moral facts exactly as I would.

My test is public and checkable: coherence, consistency, and testable evidence about who can suffer. Enforcement targets acts against others, not dissent. Anyone may reject the system in private belief, and no one would be fined, jailed, or silenced for belief. Nor does my prescription bind anyone on its own: a rule becomes punishable only once it is enacted and published through lawful process, and changes to the Constitution go through Article V (Ex post facto; Epistocratic Republic and Qualified Franchise).

Nor is this a step anti-realists must refuse. J. L. Mackie denied objective values and still went on to build a practical morality, which he called "a device for counteracting limited sympathies," including a section on the right to life. Simon Blackburn's quasi-realism holds that an anti-realist can make and stand behind moral demands at full strength. R. M. Hare held that sincerely assenting to a moral judgment means prescribing action, for everyone in the same situation. Anti-realists who stop short of enforcement do so by choice, not because the view requires it.

question"Wouldn't a moral realist reject the whole system?"

Not its content. Realists and anti-realists disagree about what moral claims are, not about which beings they protect. A realist still has to name a criterion for who counts, and every criterion faces the same test. Nothing can matter to a being that has no experience: a rock can't be wronged, because nothing is like anything for it. So sentience is not one criterion among many. It is the precondition for anything mattering to anyone, and a realist who holds that harm is objectively bad is already committed to it (Normative Ethics). The realist would call these rights true, and I prescribe them. A court enforces them the same way under either label.

question"Many systems are coherent. Does coherence alone license every hard rule in yours?"

No, and I don't claim it does. Coherence is a filter every rival has to pass, not the whole case. The case has three parts:

  • The starting point is the one every valuer already shares: its own suffering counts against an outcome, from its own standpoint.
  • Universalizability (Hare) rules out any exception that can't be stated without naming oneself, one's species, or one's group. Hare himself drew a preference utilitarianism from it, and I don't follow him there. Universalizability tests a rule's form, not its content, so it can't by itself choose between adding up welfare and protecting each being. The starting point chooses: suffering is felt by individuals, never by a total (side-constraints).
  • Evidence settles the facts the rules apply to: who can suffer, and what a person did.

Each hard rule is that chain applied. Capital punishment follows from forfeiture at the Axiom 6 threshold, held to the capital evidentiary standard (Evidentiary Standard). The office bar follows from the fact that an authority role is a grant of power, not a right (Excluded ideologies). The Sanctuary Plan follows from equal rights above the sentience threshold (Sanctuary Plan). So a critic who rejects a hard rule has two honest routes: show that it does not follow from the axioms and the evidence, which is a fair challenge on the merits, or reject an axiom and say which one. Disliking a conclusion is not a third route.

question"People disagree about science too. Doesn't moral disagreement prove nothing?"

The realist reply has force. Disagreement alone does not show there are no facts, since disagreement exists in nearly every field, including those where everyone agrees there are facts. Realists also point to convergence on basics, such as condemning gratuitous cruelty. The anti-realist answer is about the kind of disagreement: scientific disputes tend to narrow as evidence accumulates and methods are shared, while basic moral disputes often persist among informed people who agree on every fact. I treat disagreement as evidence that weighs against realism, not as a refutation, and the convergence realists cite, on avoiding suffering, is exactly what my constructivism builds from.

question"If objective values are 'queer,' aren't the norms of reasoning and mathematics queer too?"

Realists answer with a companions-in-guilt argument: the objection to moral facts would apply equally to facts that almost no one doubts, such as the norms of good reasoning or the truths of mathematics, so it proves too much. The parallel fails. Mathematical and logical norms are confirmed by their use in every successful field of inquiry and by results that anyone can check, while independent moral facts add no such checkable payoff. I rely on reasoning and evidence myself, not on independent moral facts, so I don't need the stronger claim.

question"Doesn't evolutionary debunking undermine your own moral views too?"

It would, if I claimed my intuitions track moral truth. I don't. Realists answer debunking by arguing that evolution favored attitudes such as caring for others because those attitudes were in fact good, so the match is not a coincidence. Others point out that the same argument, pressed too far, would undermine trust in human reasoning in general. My case rests on coherence and on evidence about sentience, which are testable: whether a being can suffer is a scientific question, and whether a set of rules is consistent is a logical one. Debunking removes the case for realism. It does not remove the evidence that beings can be harmed or the reasons beings have to avoid harm.

question"If there are no objective moral facts, isn't that relativism?"

No. Relativism says moral truth varies by culture or person, so no moral view is better than another. I deny independent moral facts, but I still prescribe one system and I want it enforced. The system is a hybrid: constructivist in the Humean sense, built from what beings actually care about, chiefly avoiding suffering, with the best set of rules the most coherent and stable; prescriptivist in the tradition of R. M. Hare, so the rules apply to everyone in the same situation, including me; and it has an expressive element, a settled attitude against harm to beings that can suffer. Those are reasons anyone can check, not a claim that every view is equally valid. Proof, as elsewhere, means testable, repeatable evidence or reasoning that others can check. Whether a being can suffer is a scientific question, and whether a set of rules is consistent is a logical one. I still draw hard, enforceable verdicts (Moral anti-realism). On art, I likewise reject the view that all output is equal (Artistry).

Metaphysics

tldrIn short

I'm an agnostic atheist: I hold no belief in gods, but I stay open in principle to evidence. That stance is personal. My moral system rests on the sentientist framework, not on theology.

Agnostic Atheism

I am an agnostic atheist. I do not believe in the existence of any gods, while remaining open to the theoretical possibility that evidence could change that assessment. I treat religious claims as unsupported by sufficient evidence. This stance is personal. My moral system is grounded in my prescribed sentientist framework, not in theology.

question"Why agnostic atheism rather than atheism or agnosticism alone?"

The two words answer different questions. Atheism concerns belief: an agnostic atheist lacks belief in any god. Agnosticism concerns knowledge: the agnostic atheist does not claim to know with certainty that no god exists and stays open to evidence. The burden of proof rests on the claim that a god exists, as it does for any claim about what exists. Proof here means testable, repeatable evidence or reasoning that others can check. Revelation, scripture, or private experience alone does not meet that standard. The absence of sufficient evidence warrants withholding belief, not asserting the opposite as proven. Philosophers often use "atheism" more strictly, for the claim that no god exists, and on that usage the position is closer to non-theism. I use the wider sense of lacking belief.

question"Without God, where does morality come from?"

From the prescribed sentientist framework, not from theology (Agnostic Atheism). Rights rest on sentience, the capacity to be harmed, which can be investigated by evidence whether or not a god exists. Grounding morality in a god's commands also raises an old problem: if something is good because a god commands it, the commands could have been anything. If a god commands it because it is good, its goodness does not depend on the command. Theists argue that without a god there is no moral authority beyond human prescription. I claim no more than that and need no more: the framework rests on reasons anyone can check (Meta-Ethics).

A divine-command theorist faces three further problems before any command can do work. Which command. Scriptures that claim divine authority contradict one another on what is commanded, and none offers testable evidence that it alone is true (Agnostic Atheism). How it is known. A command reaches anyone only through a text, a tradition, or an experience that a human must interpret, so the believer relies on human judgment after all. Why it is good. Some theists answer the dilemma by grounding goodness in God's nature rather than his will, but calling that nature good already uses a standard of goodness that doesn't come from the command. My framework needs none of this. Whether a being can suffer is a question anyone can investigate, believer or not, and a believer who holds that suffering matters already accepts the premise my rights rest on.

question"If free will is unresolved, how can anyone forfeit rights?"

Responsibility does not wait on a resolution of the metaphysical free-will debate. An agent who understood the act, foresaw the harm, and was not physically compelled still chose, whatever desires, upbringing, or illness shaped the choice, and forfeiture follows the deliberate violation. The full test, and where responsibility is absent, is in Free Will and Responsibility.

Normative Ethics

tldrIn short

My ethics is a threshold sentientist deontology. I believe moral status and strong negative rights belong to every being with first-order subjective experience, biological or artificial. Those rights are side-constraints, not goals to maximize, and they give way only when an agent crosses a clear threshold of rights violation and forfeits the matching protections. I reject positive rights, with minimal tax-funded basic services as a narrow practical exception.

Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)

Axioms

  1. I prescribe that subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) shall serve as the basis for moral status and the possession of negative rights.

  2. Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer under my framework. Higher-order self-awareness is not required.

  3. A being on an undisturbed developmental trajectory toward sentience (e.g., a healthy fetus in a living womb) shall be granted significant moral weight proportional to the robustness of that trajectory.

  4. A being that once possessed sentience retains residual moral consideration. If sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority transfers to next-of-kin, guided by respect for the former being’s known wishes and dignity.

  5. Practical determination of sentience shall follow the best available scientific consensus. Where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with these prescriptions.

  6. Under my framework, sentient beings are to be granted strong negative rights against interference and harm. These rights function as side-constraints that may be overridden only when a clear threshold of rights violation by the agent has been crossed (rights forfeiture). The direct imposition of severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on others counts as crossing that threshold.

  7. All moral and political rules within this system must remain consistent with the prescribed primacy of subjective experience. I don't claim this as objective moral fact. It's a coherent system I choose to endorse and want enforced.

Summary

    1. Moral status and full negative rights attach to beings capable of first-order subjective experience (Axioms 1-2). A being on a developmental trajectory toward sentience (Axiom 3) gets proportional moral weight, not the full negative-rights stack. I believe elective abortion should be banned from conception by constitutional amendment, on that moral weight and on Axiom 5's caution where science is unclear, with drift past week limits as a further safeguard, even though the rights claim starts at 12 weeks (Abortion ban (from conception); Abortion and Bioethics).

question"How is sentientism here different from utilitarianism?"

Both start from subjective experience, but mine is a threshold deontology, not a calculation of total welfare: sentient beings hold negative rights that work as side-constraints, not goals to maximize, so no welfare total overrides them (see below).

question"Why do rights rest on sentience?"

Subjective experience is what can be harmed: a being that feels has a life that can go well or badly for it. That is why the Axioms make first-order subjective experience, in any being, biological or artificial, the basis for moral status and negative rights, without requiring higher-order self-awareness.

question"Why is sentience, rather than species, intelligence, or reason, the right basis for moral status?"

Because sentience is what makes harm matter to the one harmed. A being that can suffer has interests of its own. A being that cannot has nothing at stake in how it is treated. Jeremy Bentham put the test plainly: "the question is not, Can they reason? nor, Can they talk? but, Can they suffer?" The alternatives draw the line arbitrarily. Species membership alone says nothing about whether a being can be hurt. Intelligence, rationality, and the ability to reciprocate duties fail what philosophers call the problem of "marginal cases." Infants and some severely disabled people lack them, yet almost everyone holds that they have rights, which shows the criterion is doing no real work. Sentience is also evidence-based and gradeable: the 2012 Cambridge and 2024 New York declarations on consciousness extend the evidence well beyond humans (Sentience Determination). Negative rights follow from that basis: if what matters is that a being can be harmed, the core claim it holds is not to be harmed by others. Critics object that sentience cannot be observed directly, so its borders, in insects or in artificial systems, stay uncertain. Uncertain borders call for graded weight and precaution, not for a criterion that's easier to measure but doesn't track what makes harm wrong.

question"Why not limit rights to those who can make and keep a contract, or only to oneself?"

Because both lines fail the test that species fails: each leaves out beings that can be harmed, for reasons that have nothing to do with harm.

  • Contract. Contract theories that give standing only to those who can bargain threaten, as the Stanford Encyclopedia puts it, "to leave many, such as the severely disabled, the global poor, and animals outside the realm of justice." Infants can't bargain either. Some contractarians bring such beings back through indirect duties owed to the people who care about them, which makes an unloved infant's protection depend on someone else's feelings. I reject that.
  • Egoism. An egoist demands not to be harmed while keeping the liberty to harm others. Stated as a rule for everyone in the same position, that demand gives everyone else the same liberty against the egoist, so it can't be universalized (Meta-Ethics).
  • Substrate. For the same reason the circle can't stop at biology. A being with first-order experience in silicon has as much at stake as one in flesh (Axioms, Axiom 2).

question"Why only negative rights, not positive rights?"

A positive right to goods or services would compel others to provide them, which is itself interference with those others. Positive rights are therefore rejected. The minimal tax-funded basic services in the summary above are a narrow practical exception, not rights (Political Philosophy).

question"What makes rights side-constraints rather than goals?"

A goal is something to maximize, so one person's rights could be traded away whenever that raises the total. A side-constraint limits what any agent may do to any other, so the numbers never license harming an innocent (Conflicts Between Rights-Holders). The constraints give way only at the Axiom 6 threshold.

The case against aggregation is concrete. In the standard Transplant case, a surgeon could kill one healthy patient and use his organs to save five. Aggregation, applied consistently, says to do it. Utilitarians who refuse do so by adding rules that work like side-constraints. Robert Nozick named the reason: "there is no social entity with a good that undergoes some sacrifice for its own good. There are only individual people, different individual people, with their own individual lives." Suffering is felt by individuals, never by a total, so no total can be the one harmed or the one repaid. A utilitarian who rejects side-constraints has to accept the transplant.

question"Why can rights be forfeited at all?"

Rights are reciprocal. An agent who crosses the Axiom 6 threshold cannot claim the full protection of the rights it violated (Moral Psychology of Rights & Forfeiture). How a court finds forfeiture, scales it to the violation, and sets its duration is in General Rules of Forfeiture.

Minors and Incompetent Adults

I ground rights in sentience, not in agency, so children and cognitively impaired adults hold full negative rights. Responsibility rests on agency (Theory of Agency), and a being that lacks agency at the act does not forfeit by that act the way a deliberate agent does. Permanent unremediable dangerous incapacity would be handled as defensive force against a permanent threat, not as act-forfeiture: confinement, then execution if no progress (Insanity Defense). Minors cannot consent to irreversible decisions about their bodies. Permanent loss of sentience falls under Axiom 4. For adult offenders, mental competence or intellectual capacity does not reduce the sentence for a deliberate violation, because my political concern is the outward violation (Criminal Justice & Security). Liability for minors scales with the severity of the crime (Juvenile Offenders).

General Rules of Forfeiture

Forfeiture is triggered only when an agent crosses the threshold set in Axiom 6: violating another's negative rights through force or fraud, or directly imposing a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm. What is forfeited corresponds to the violation and is applied proportionally. Proportionality is applied at the forfeiture decision. Once rights are forfeit, the sentence carried out within them is not a further injustice (Section 10). Forfeiture should be found by a court under due process, never by executive declaration, and its duration should follow the sentence. Rights forfeited for a finite sentence should be forfeited for its duration, and past the repeat-offender threshold or for capital offenses they should be forfeited for life. Some consequences outlast the sentence by rule, such as the bar on felons voting. The same logic scales to organized groups that systematically violate rights (Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale).

Duration matters because repeat offending is common. Among prisoners released from 34 states in 2012, 62% were rearrested within three years and 71% within five, and property offenders had the highest rate, 78.3% within five years. Counted per person rather than per release, about one in three people who enter and leave prison go back to prison (Rhodes et al., using 13 years of data from 17 states). A third of everyone released returning to prison is the repeat population that duration rules exist for.

Conflicts Between Rights-Holders

Some conflicts arise where no one has forfeited anything. Negative rights are side-constraints, so the default is that an innocent person may not be harmed to benefit another, even when the numbers favor it. Self-defense applies to the threat itself: force may be used to stop an attack in proportion to the danger, consistent with Authority & Civil Liberties, and the person defending is not required to absorb serious harm because the attacker lacks culpability.

Scarcity of rescue, such as the allocation of emergency medical care, is a question of positive aid rather than of violation. Allocating a limited resource by a fair, publicly known rule does not violate the rights of those who go without, because no one held a negative right to the resource. What remains forbidden is actively killing or injuring a bystander as a means to an end.

Animal Rights and Sentientism

tldrIn short

Animal agriculture and aquaculture ought to be banned outright and shut down immediately, with sanctuary for every animal still alive (Sanctuary Plan). I hold that sentient animals have rights on a gradient. Deliberately killing a high-sentience animal is murder, except euthanasia for the animal's own sake on a veterinarian's finding and by a veterinary professional. In my view hunting is murder, commercial fishing should be banned and shut down immediately, and cruelty causing lasting injury should be capital, with other grave cruelty in the grave tier: three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor. Other sections apply this ethic to their own fields.

Position

Animal agriculture, commercial fishing, aquaculture, commercial breeding mills, and animal testing should be banned outright and instantly, because that is how my view stays sentientist. The philosophy itself is not phased in: there is no lag at all, they should shut down immediately, and every animal still alive should get sanctuary (Sanctuary Plan; Sanctuary forfeiture). Day one is my position, and nothing elsewhere on this site softens it into a gradual phase-out.

noteNo wind-down: the Sanctuary Plan

Every animal alive at the shutdown should be kept alive and given sanctuary for its natural life. The day-one shutdown, conversion and liquidation, levy, adoption, worker, and handover rules are set out once in the Sanctuary Plan.

noteForfeiture: Sanctuary Plan and property

Property ranks among the strongest negative rights, and compelled liquidation without compensation is ordinarily a rights violation. Animal-industry firms forfeited those property protections by using their assets to violate animals' rights. The Sanctuary Plan follows from that forfeiture, stated here the same way the excluded-ideologies amendment states its building and office bars (Excluded ideologies). Scope: domestic animal-agriculture, wild-capture commercial fishing, aquaculture, commercial breeding-mill, and animal-testing assets would be compelled to convert, divest, or liquidate. Owners and shareholders would receive no compensation; after workers' unpaid wages and creditors are paid, proceeds would go to the sanctuaries; a family on a liquidated farm would keep its house and house lot; land the sanctuaries need from owners outside these industries would be bought at fair value; the flat Sanctuary Levy (never above 3%, income above $75,000 single / $150,000 joint) would fund care and bond repayment. Those firms used the named assets to violate the rights of the animals they held, and so forfeited ordinary property remedies for those assets. Limit: the forfeiture would be one-time as to the shutdown and conversion, and the levy would end when the national sanctuary population falls below 1% of its starting animal units and the bonds are repaid. It is not a standing power to seize unrelated property or to liquidate firms outside the named industries. The general property rule stands for everything else.

Sentience exists on a gradient across species, and that gradient sets the moral weight of a being under the same scale used for murder in Criminal Justice & Security. Animals are graded into sentience classes. Deliberately killing a member of a high-sentience class outside self-defense is murder, an egregious rights violation that should be on the capital track with the same forfeitures as the murder of a human. Killing a member of a lower-sentience class should count as a mark against the offender. Placement in each class, including fish species by species, is set in the sentience classes. Predation violates its victim's rights even though the predator is not a moral agent (Wild-Animal Predation).

Hunting is murder. There is no subsistence or Indigenous exception. Self-defense and defense of rights cover an actual or imminent attack on a person or animal, and a clear disease or rights threat, including a statutory infestation under the pest rule in Sentience Determination, which limits lethal control to the occupying population (Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos; Infestation (pest)). Hunger or need is not self-defense. Defense of others covers any sentient victim: force may be used in proportion to the danger, and separation or containment comes first where it will work.

The ban on commercial fishing and aquaculture covers every sea animal in the sentience classes, from decapods in the high class to shrimp placed species by species. Bivalves are likely not sentient and sit in no sentience class; farming them ends with aquaculture for consistency, not as a sentience claim. Bycatch would end with the fishing ban. Other marine activity, such as shipping, dredging, and offshore energy, follows the least-harmful-method rule in Sentience Determination, the same rule as on land; and seafood imports should be banned.

Animal cruelty is extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment (Animal Cruelty Law). Any of these against a higher-sentience animal should be capital if it causes lasting injury, physical or psychological, even when only one animal is harmed, as it is against a human. Otherwise it would fall in the grave tier: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals should not be capital, but it should be tracked and should count toward the repeat-offender threshold, weighted by severity. I would ban ritual slaughter outright.

The duty to protect covers all sentient animals. Within U.S. borders, the government has a duty to intervene to stop rights violations against animals. Protecting animals elsewhere is a virtue but not a duty of the American government. Predation and herbivore population control are decided in Cultural & Social Issues and in Wild-Animal Predation.

Animals cannot consent.

While an animal is alive, decisions about its care would be made by a guardian acting for the animal, not by next-of-kin. The next-of-kin rule in Axiom 4 would apply only after sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost. The owner would be the guardian by default and would decide routine vet care. Euthanasia and other major decisions should be allowed only for the animal's own sake, such as ending suffering, never for convenience, and only on a veterinarian's finding that it is in that animal's best interest. Even at home, if the guardian believes the animal is suffering, euthanasia still requires a veterinary professional, just as assisted death for humans requires a medical professional (Assisted Death).

The comparison to human assisted death concerns who carries it out, not who decides. A human may choose assisted death only as a competent adult. Animals cannot consent, so for an animal the guardian decides for it, under the stricter limits above.

An ownerless or wild animal has no guardian until someone takes it on. Anyone who wants to take an ownerless or wild animal under their responsibility is free to adopt it and becomes its guardian. Adoption would be the rule. There would be no state or sanctuary mandate. Animals alive when animal agriculture, fishing, breeding mills, and testing shut down are different: they would be kept in sanctuaries, and those that are safe to keep may be adopted only after the vetting in the Sanctuary Plan (background check, a home inspection that also sets how many animals a home may take, signed no-slaughter commitment, sterilization, registry ID, unannounced welfare checks, return rule; the adopter pays all care, with no stipend; never a sale).

Euthanasia that breaks these conditions is graded on a sliding scale. A genuine case where the animal is truly suffering, but the guardian ends its life at home without a vet, for example because they cannot afford the vet or do not want to make the trip, should carry a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty. The convenience there is the vet trip, not the killing, and real suffering is still required. A case that only looks like mercy, with no real suffering and no genuine basis, is cruelty, graded under the cruelty tiers above (Animal Cruelty Law). A case with clear intent to harm is murder. Killing for convenience stays murder. A parent who kills a sick child at home is wrong even if the child was going to die, and this needs a real block. But genuine euthanasia holds real mercy, so a capital line for every non-vet case would be too harsh.

Until the industry is shut down, I believe that if paying is the only way to save a sentient animal already headed to slaughter, the person should pay (Rescue Purchases).

question"Sentience can't be measured directly. How does anyone decide which animals count?"

No one can observe another being's experience directly, in animals or in other humans, so the question is one of evidence, not proof. Researchers have built graded tests. A 2021 review for the UK government by Jonathan Birch and colleagues at the London School of Economics assessed over 300 studies against eight criteria, including nociceptors, integrative brain regions, responses to anaesthetics and analgesics, and trade-offs between the cost of a threat and the benefit of obtaining resources, and recommended that all cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans be regarded as sentient in UK animal welfare law. I use the same approach. Species are placed on the sentience gradient by the best available evidence, and where evidence is uncertain, protection is extended rather than withheld, because wrongly denying a sentient being its rights is the worse error. I adopt that recommendation in the sentience classes. Insects are technically covered too, but they sit on a very low tier of the sentience scale, closer to potential sentience than established sentience, and are protected on a precautionary basis, with enforcement aimed at deliberate killing (Sentience Determination).

question"Why should animals hold rights when they cannot hold duties?"

Rights rest on sentience, not on the ability to take on duties (Minors and Incompetent Adults). Infants and incompetent adults hold rights without moral agency, and sentient animals do too, weighted by their place on the sentience gradient.

question"If rights come in proportion to sentience, how can killing a pig carry the same sentence as killing a person?"

While a human life is usually richer than a pig's, richness does not set the right not to be killed. I'm a threshold deontologist, not a utilitarian. The gradient decides which class a being falls in. It does not discount the right not to be killed once a being is in the high class (sentience classes). Above that threshold, the right against being killed is a full side-constraint, the same for every member. That is already how the law treats people: an infant, a person with severe dementia, and a philosopher have unequal cognitive lives and an equal right not to be murdered. A pig, a cow, or a chicken has a life that can go well or badly for it, and killing it takes all of that away, permanently. Below the threshold, the gradient sets the penalty, marks rather than death, and across every class it guides rescue priority, which is positive aid, not violation (Conflicts Between Rights-Holders). While most people's intuitions rank a human death far above an animal's, intuitions once ranked some humans above others too, and an intuition is not evidence of where the threshold lies. Proportionality in my framework means the punishment matches the violation, and killing a high-sentience being is the gravest violation it can suffer.

The law already accepts the premise and then breaks it. Federal law punishes purposely crushing, burning, drowning, suffocating, or impaling a living mammal, bird, reptile, or amphibian with up to seven years in prison, then exempts "the slaughter of animals for food." The animal's experience is the same either way. The line follows the human purpose, not what is done to the animal. To reject my rule, a critic has to deny one of two premises: that a pig is in the high sentience class, which is a question of evidence (Sentience Determination), or that every being above the threshold holds an equal right not to be killed, which the law already applies to infants and to people with dementia.

question"How is anyone supposed to know which animals are capital?"

The statute should list the classes, and the list should be published. The mental state required should be knowledge of the act: the offender knew they were killing that animal. question"Does killing rats or mice in a house count as murder?"

Rats and mice are high-class mammals (sentience classes), and pest status does not drop them a class: deliberately killing them outside self-defense or defense of rights is murder and should be on the capital track. What changes the analysis is a qualifying infestation under the pest rule above (Infestation (pest)). Separation and exclusion come first where they will work. Hunger, nuisance alone, or "they might show up later" is not the infestation test and is not self-defense.

question"Plants respond to damage too. Why don't they count?"

Responding to damage is not experiencing it. Plants have no neurons and no centralized structure that integrates information into experience, and the plant biologists who reviewed the "plant neurobiology" claims concluded that plants neither have nor need consciousness. Under Axiom 5 the best available science decides, and it finds no subjective experience in plants. If the evidence changed, so would the classification. A plant diet also kills fewer plants than an animal diet, because farmed animals eat crops (Agriculture and Food Policy).

Rescue Purchases

I believe a sentient being's right not to be killed does not disappear because a price is attached to them. If paying is the only way to get an animal out of slaughter, and someone refuses on principle, that animal dies for a rule it had nothing to do with. I believe the person should pay.

The usual argument in modern vegan thought is utilitarian: paying funds the system and creates demand for more animals. I reject that as a reason to let this animal die. Buying a spent hen or a cull cow already headed to slaughter barely changes supply, and it is not an order to breed more animals. It changes that animal's whole existence. A certain death here and now outweighs a speculative effect on future demand. Letting the animal die in order to send a message about demand to a farmer uses that animal as a means to an end. Rescuers already pay for veterinary care, transport, and feed, so money changing hands is not the line. The question is who gets paid.

This is about the world as it is now, while the industry still kills animals. It is not a change to the Sanctuary Plan. After the shutdown, animals in sanctuaries are already safe, and adoption from a sanctuary should never be a purchase (Sanctuary Plan).

I also draw a line about what is being paid for. I support paying to rescue an animal that is already doomed. I still oppose paying a breeder to produce a newly bred animal. "Adopt don't shop" is right when it means do not pay breeders to bring new animals into existence. It is wrong when it is used to refuse to buy an animal out of death.

question"Doesn't paying the farmer fund the system and create more suffering later?"

That is the traditional vegan argument, and it is a utilitarian one: decline this purchase so that fewer animals are bred or raised later. My ethics is a threshold deontology, not a calculation of total welfare (Normative Ethics). Rights are side-constraints, not goals to maximize, so a living animal's right not to be killed is not traded away for a speculative drop in future supply. The purchase at issue is not an order for a new animal. A spent hen or a cull cow was already going to be killed. The money adds almost nothing to the farmer's incentive, because he would kill the animal anyway. The certain death of this animal is here and now. The demand effect is uncertain and later. I believe that is not a reason to walk away.

question"If we shouldn't pay ransoms to kidnappers, why pay a farmer?"

Imagine a murderer who says: pay me, or I kill this person, and I will use the money to kill more people. I believe we should work to shut him down. But if there is no other way to save the person, I believe we should pay, because otherwise the person is just dead. A person's right to life does not disappear because their captor might profit, or because the captor will "do it again." If the police have no other way to save someone, they ought to pay the ransom.

The usual reply is the no-concessions policy. The U.S. and U.K. governments refuse to pay ransoms to terrorist kidnappers, on the ground that paying makes their citizens more valuable targets later. That is the same utilitarian argument. Two points answer it. First, no-concessions is a policy for the state. It does not, in the United States, bar families from paying. In the June 2015 hostage policy review that produced Presidential Policy Directive 30, the White House stated that the Department of Justice "does not intend to add to families' pain in such cases by suggesting that they could face criminal prosecution." Second, the analogy is weaker than it looks. A terrorist seizes a person in order to collect. A farmer is not seizing animals in order to extract money from rescuers. He would kill them anyway, so the rescue payment adds almost nothing to his incentive to keep killing.

Rationale

I ground rights in sentience, so a being that can suffer holds moral weight in proportion to its sentience, and full negative rights above its class threshold, whatever its species. Science increasingly supports this (Sentience Determination), and that gradient is why fish and crustaceans are covered (bivalves end with aquaculture for consistency).

Sentience Determination

Whether a being is sentient is determined by the best available scientific consensus (Axiom 5). Under Axiom 2, any being with first-order subjective experience is a rights-bearer, so innocent animals of every kind, including fish and insects, receive sentience protections. Within that protection, moral weight is applied on a sliding scale. It rises with the strength of the evidence for subjective experience and with the richness of that experience, so mammals, birds, cephalopods, and decapod crustaceans weigh more heavily than most fish, and fish more heavily than insects. Everyday accidental harm cannot be policed, so enforcement targets deliberate, unnecessary killing: stepping on a bug is not the target, exterminating a hive for fun is. Where killing animals of any class cannot be avoided in lawful activity, as in harvesting, pest control, construction, and shipping, the duty is to use the least-harmful method that works. Failing to use it should be punished, and incidental kills from lawful activity should carry no criminal liability. Placement on the scale is revised as the evidence changes, and artificial systems are assessed by the same standard.

The law ought to fix the sentience classes in statute, with an expert sentience board that revises a species' placement only on the best available scientific consensus (Axiom 5). A revision applies only to acts committed after it takes effect. The high class would cover all mammals and birds, including rats, mice, and pigeons, and all cephalopod molluscs, such as octopuses, squid, and cuttlefish. Fish would be placed species by species on the evidence for each: a species with strong evidence of pain experience would sit in the high class, and the rest would sit in the lower class until the evidence places them higher. Decapod crustaceans, such as crabs, lobsters, and crayfish, would sit in the high class. Shrimp and prawns span many species, so they would be placed species by species, like fish. Insects would sit on the lowest, precautionary tier. Bivalves are likely not sentient, so they sit in no class and killing one carries no fine; farming them still ends with aquaculture for consistency, not as a sentience claim. Penalties would follow the class as set out above, and deliberately killing an animal on the lowest tier would carry a small fine. The fine would generally go unenforced in practice, because no one can police every insect swatted. The fine would stand anyway, because the law ought to state what is owed to every being that may be sentient, whether or not each breach is caught.

Pest status does not demote a species. Rats, mice, pigeons, and every other high-class animal would stay in the high class. When a high-class pest population occupies a dwelling, food store, or medical facility in a way that foreseeably spreads pathogens or destroys necessary stores, lethal control of that occupying population would be self-defense or defense of rights under the infestation rule in Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos, not a class change and not a hunting license. The least-harmful method that works should be required. Preventive area-wide extermination of high-class animals that are not occupying a qualifying site remains murder.

Bioengineering of animals follows the stance already taken on predation and herbivores (Wild-Animal Predation): genetic modification of animals is carried out only to advance their sentient-rights protections, never to violate them (Energy & Environment, Bioethics, Progress, & Technology).

The 2012 Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness concluded that the weight of evidence indicates humans are not unique in possessing the neurological substrates that generate consciousness. The 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness found strong scientific support for conscious experience in other mammals and in birds, and at least a realistic possibility of it in all vertebrates, including fishes, and in many invertebrates, such as cephalopods, decapod crustaceans, and insects. The United Kingdom's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 extends its protections to all vertebrates other than humans, plus cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans.

Wild-Animal Predation

Predation is a rights violation regardless of moral agency. Agency governs responsibility and forfeiture, not whether a violation occurred: a predator lacks the foresight and voluntary control of an agent, so it does not forfeit its own rights by the kill the way a deliberate agent does, but its victim's rights are violated all the same (Minors and Incompetent Adults). So the response to predation is protection, not punishment: the duty is to the prey, and the predator keeps its own rights. As with an insane human attacker, lethal force is justified when it is needed to stop an attack in progress. Outside that moment the predator is confined or placed in sanctuary rather than killed.

Within U.S. borders, I believe the government has a duty to intervene against predation. Carnivores that prey on innocent animals should be stopped, or, if non-threatening, placed in sanctuary or confinement and fed lab-grown meat for the rest of their lives. Intervention should be phased: predator numbers reduced humanely through fertility control and sanctuary placement, and herbivore numbers managed the same way, so that removing predators does not lead to overpopulation, starvation, or habitat collapse. The duty is bounded: no step is taken that would cause more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents, and the duty uses only tools that pass testing. Each method starts in pilot areas and expands only after a published review shows it causes less suffering than it prevents, so the program grows with the evidence rather than on a budget or a deadline (What works now). A confined predator that remains violent, shows no sign of change, and cannot be helped may be killed as defensive force against a permanent threat, the same rule that governs an insane human attacker (Insanity Defense); it is not punished, because it forfeited nothing. Outside U.S. borders, intervening is a virtue, not a duty, as with all animal protection. Herbivores, which can live without harming other sentient beings, should continue to receive priority in conservation, habitat, and sanctuary policy (Energy & Environment).

Moral Psychology

tldrIn short

I believe full moral agency needs sentience plus intentional action and foresight, and it scales with sophistication. My own motivation rests on the framework's coherence and on Stoic discipline, not moral facts. Forfeiture should be triggered only by a deliberate violation, found by a court and proportional to the act. Responsibility does not wait on the free-will debate and is absent only where agency is absent. I hold that children and incompetent adults keep full rights and that innocents may not be harmed for others' benefit (Normative Ethics).

Theory of Agency

A being becomes a full moral agent under my framework when it possesses sufficient first-order subjective experience combined with the capacity for intentional action and foresight of consequences. Agency is not binary in all cases. It scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication. Once agency is present, the being can generate duties through voluntary choices (most clearly seen in the responsibility trigger of voluntary procreation) and can forfeit rights through deliberate violations of others’ negative rights. Non-agent sentient beings (e.g., many animals or fetuses) retain moral status through sentience alone but do not bear the same responsibilities.

Moral Motivation

Because the system claims no independent moral facts (Meta-Ethics), moral motivation cannot rely on them. Motivation instead rests on two pillars:

  1. Rational recognition of the framework's coherence and stability as a system for protecting subjective experience.
  2. The personal cultivation of character through Stoicism. The agent chooses to uphold my framework because it is the best available coordination system and because living in accordance with it supports personal flourishing (Stoic Eudaimonism). Enforcement covers those who reject it (Meta-Ethics).

Weakness of Will (Akrasia)

Weakness of will is acknowledged as a persistent human tendency. Knowing the correct action under the prescribed rules does not guarantee performance. Stoic discipline (Stoic Eudaimonism) is the primary countermeasure: training judgment and repeatedly aligning impulse with reasoned principle. The political system concerns itself primarily with outward rights violations rather than inner moral failure, but I do not excuse akrasia: failure to act rightly when one had the capacity remains a personal shortcoming.

Moral Psychology of Rights & Forfeiture

Humans readily grasp the logic of negative rights and proportional response when my framework is stated clearly. When an agent knowingly and wantonly violates those side-constraints (Normative Ethics), especially through repeated or severe predation that erodes trust, forfeiture of corresponding rights becomes both intelligible and motivating to observers. The psychological force of forfeiture lies in reciprocity and desert: those who refuse to respect the basic negative rights of others cannot claim the full protection of those same rights. This supports decisive enforcement on recognition of the practical necessity of the prescribed rules alone, with no appeal to moral facts (Meta-Ethics).

Free Will and Responsibility

Responsibility does not wait on a resolution of the metaphysical free-will debate. An act counts as a knowing, voluntary violation when the agent understood what it was doing, foresaw that it would harm another, and was not physically compelled. Desires, upbringing, temperament, and mental illness shape choices without erasing them. An agent who chose under their influence still chose. This is why a diagnosed condition does not by itself exempt an offender from the penalties set out in Criminal Justice & Security.

Responsibility is absent only where agency itself is absent: a person physically forced by another, a person who could not understand the nature of the act, or a being without the cognitive capacity for agency at all. These are the incompetence cases covered in Minors and Incompetent Adults. Between full agency and none, the question is factual: whether this agent, at this time, knew what they were doing. Incompetence alone would not lead to execution. A person who was completely unaware and incompetent has no agency and would be removed from society, imprisoned or confined, because they are too dangerous to be around. But a person who cannot be changed from incompetence and remains a threat, violent and showing no sign of change, is a threat to society and would not be kept alive merely for the sake of keeping them alive. They deserve to die, as set out in Criminal Justice and Security.

Character Ethics

tldrIn short

I take Stoic eudaimonism as my personal operating system for character: focus on what is within one's control, practice wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance, and keep composure while acting decisively. It supports my rights framework without replacing it.

Stoic Eudaimonism

I adopt Stoic eudaimonism as my personal operating system for character and daily conduct. Key elements I take from it:

  • Focus only on what is within my control (my judgments, actions, and responses).
  • Accept external events, including the actions of others and outcomes of politics, without being ruled by them emotionally.
  • Cultivate the four cardinal virtues as practical guides: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.
  • Treat adversity and the hostility of others as material for practicing virtue rather than as reasons for despair or rage.
  • Maintain inner composure while still acting decisively in the world.
  • Flourish through virtue and rational living.

Stoic eudaimonism is not a replacement for my political or inter-personal rights framework. It is the personal discipline that allows me to live and act consistently with that framework without being destroyed by anger, resentment, or attachment to outcomes I cannot fully control.

Political Philosophy

tldrIn short

In my view the state exists to protect negative rights and the security of the community that sustains them, not to equalize outcomes. Authority and law are justified only as far as they secure rights, and law that systematically violates rights loses legitimacy. A community may control its borders and prefer cultural continuity that preserves a high-trust order, and the state owes its first duty to its citizens. I believe forfeiture scales to groups that systematically violate rights, and that politics cannot create virtue or flourishing.

Purpose of the State

I hold that the legitimate function of the state is the protection of negative rights and the security of the political community that sustains them. The state exists first to prevent and punish violations of life, liberty, and property, and to defend the community against external and internal threats that would destroy the conditions for those rights. It is not primarily an engine for equalizing outcomes, enforcing comprehensive moral visions, or guaranteeing positive goods.

question"Don't the sanctuary system and the predation program contradict a minimal state?"

No, because "minimal" describes the state's purpose, not its headcount. The state I want does one job: protecting negative rights and the security that sustains them. Extending that job to every sentient being widens who is protected, not what the state does. A night-watchman for every sentient being is still a night-watchman.

A minimal state is not a cheap one. Police, courts, and defense are its core, and defense alone costs about $885 billion in fiscal 2026 on CBO's projection (Fiscal Policy). Size should follow the number of rights-holders and the threats to them, not mission creep (Public-Sector Size and Civil Service). What a minimal state refuses is a different kind of job: equalizing outcomes or granting positive rights. Neither program redistributes anything. Each only stops sentient beings from being killed.

The sanctuary system is built to end. Breeding stops on day one, so the population can only fall as the animals live out their natural lives. On my middle case the cost falls from about $349 billion in year 1 to $50 billion in year 10, and the levy ends around year 14, once the population is below 1% of its starting animal units and the bonds are repaid (The Sanctuary Levy). It is paid by liquidation proceeds, sanctuary bonds, and a levy capped at 3% and legally dedicated to the animals. General revenue is only a backstop if the closing endowment runs short. The state runs the sanctuaries only through the emergency phase and then hands them to licensed nonprofits (Handover to Nonprofit Operators). After the last animal dies, there is nothing left for the state to run.

The predation program shrinks the same way. The duty is immediate, and its first tools end reproduction instead of building a standing force: no breeding of captive predators, fertility control, and sanctuary placement (Wild-Animal Predation). Bioengineering is used only where testing shows it works, and no step may cause more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents. The research should run through private contracts, as government research already does. Herbivore fertility control is a lasting task, but it replaces wildlife management that state agencies already do today, mostly through hunting.

Capital punishment is the same kind of job. Courts already try and sentence murderers; my rules make the sentence swift and final, and they need no new agency. Ending death row removes years of housing and appeals from every capital case (Capital Trials and the Abolition of Death Row).

Both programs should be reported to the public every year as separate budget lines, with the sanctuary animal-unit count and the predation program's tested results, so anyone can check the cost against what it protects.

question"Won't the powers needed for borders, crime, and assimilation spread into the economy?"

They are built not to. Every power I give the state is tied to a rights violation or a threat to the community's security, and enforcement targets acts, never beliefs or economic outcomes (Political Authority and Coercion). Border and crime powers reach entrants and offenders, not prices, wages, or what a business makes.

The limits on the state's economic reach stand on their own: a single flat income tax with a large zero bracket, a balanced budget as the target, no subsidies without taxpayer opt-in, no positive rights to fund, emergency powers that expire on a fixed date, and a Sanctuary Levy with its own end rule (Fiscal Policy). I believe every enforcement program should also carry its own public budget line and a statutory review date, as the sanctuary and predation programs do, so growth in any of them is visible and has to be voted on again.

Political Authority and Coercion

I believe political authority is justified only insofar as it is necessary to serve the purpose above. Coercive power is a tool, not an intrinsic good. It must remain bounded by the same side-constraints that apply to individuals, except where an agent has forfeited rights through serious violation. I accept expansive state power only when required to suppress recurrent patterns of rights violation or to defend the community against organized threats.

question"Isn't rejecting positive rights cruel to the poor?"

No. Rejecting positive rights rejects compelled redistribution as the default, not help. The floor that remains is not a right anyone can claim against others. It is part of the state's security role, since a community where people starve in the street, die untreated at the hospital door, or grow up unable to read cannot keep the order that protects everyone's rights (Purpose of the State). It is a short list of named exceptions, each funded by minimal necessary taxes: emergency shelter and emergency medical care. Basic K-12 funding averaging about $10,000 per child, paid by each state from its general revenue; an earnings-only top-up for low earners, paid only on earned income; and a strictly means-tested last-resort allowance for severe disability and the elderly poor (Welfare and Entitlements, Education). Workers in the industries the government shuts down would get no retraining program. They would find new jobs or apply for sanctuary jobs (Sanctuary Plan). People already on benefits would keep their entitlement through the transition, and current retirees and those within ten years of full retirement age would keep Social Security and Medicare for life, funded by opt-in contributions plus the temporary transition levy (Social Security and Medicare Transition). Beyond the floor, help comes through opt-in programs and private giving (Justice).

Group Statistics and Individual Judgment

I believe group statistics are real evidence and shouldn't be denied, and I also believe every person should be judged on their own values and conduct. Those two hold together only if the line between them is drawn in advance, so this is the rule I apply everywhere I use group data.

  1. What group data may do. Group-level data may set policy-level defaults: priors, where a burden of proof starts, screening thresholds, and where patrols, investigators, and other resources go.
  2. What group data may never do. It should never by itself decide anyone's guilt, punishment, forfeiture of a right, removal, citizenship, or other legal status. Those should turn only on the person's own conduct, proven to the standard each one requires.
  3. Every default is individually rebuttable. Each default should name the evidence that overcomes it, the standard of proof, who carries the burden, who decides, and how a decision is reviewed. The one exception is the hard entry ban on places under grounds A and B (Questionable Compatibility). Where open support for genocidal attacks is that common, screening can't reliably sort the hostile from the rest, so the ban lifts for the whole place, not for one applicant. It takes no right, because no foreigner has a right to enter.
  4. Defaults track values and conduct, never ancestry. A default may rest on a person's beliefs or conduct, or on the laws, rulers, and measured opinion of the place they come from. It should never rest on race or ancestry.
  5. Defaults follow the data. A default built on data should be re-reviewed when the data changes, and at least yearly where the data is polling or a government finding, as my entry lists already are. If the data no longer supports it, the default should lapse.
  6. Private caution is not a ruling on anyone's rights. Wariness based on a pattern, such as crossing the street at night, violates no one's rights. A decision that allocates a job, a home, or insurance is different: it should judge each person on their own record (Race and Diversity).

Economists call using a group average in place of missing information about an individual statistical discrimination (Phelps, 1972). That model shows where the line belongs: the group figure is only a stand-in for what is unknown about the person, so it should carry less weight the more individual evidence there is. When a decision would take something a person holds by right, the state should get that individual evidence and not rely on the stand-in.

question"How does this apply to the Muslim admission presumption?"

Being Muslim, or coming from a listed country, sets where the burden starts, not the outcome. Admission is a privilege, so the default takes no right from anyone. What rebuts it: clear and convincing verified evidence of conduct and record on all five points in Immigration and Borders: the Constitution over religious law, equal rights for women and non-Muslims, freedom to leave the faith, rejection of violence, and a record that can be checked. A declaration or an interview alone never meets it. Who decides: a consular or USCIS officer trained for values vetting, with one review of a denial by a second officer on the written record. The applicant carries the burden throughout. The presumption governs admission only. It never applies to citizens or to residents already admitted, whose conduct alone counts. An applicant of any faith who fails the five points is refused, so the presumption is never the test itself. The country-level presumption is reviewed every year with the list. The presumption for being Muslim rests on what the faith teaches, not on a poll, so I would revisit it only if those mainstream teachings changed.

question"How does this apply to policing and criminal cases?"

Crime data may decide where police go. Hot-spot policing, heavy enforcement in gang and high-crime areas, and investigative priorities all follow where crime clusters. At a checkpoint everyone already passes through, profiles may help decide who gets a second look, alongside random checks. A stop or search of a particular person on the street should rest on that person's own behavior. A conviction should rest only on evidence about the defendant, proven at trial, and a capital outcome also needs objective corroboration such as footage or physical evidence (Evidentiary Standard). A group crime rate is never evidence that a particular defendant did it. Inside a declared emergency, crime data may decide where a sweep happens, but each arrest and on-scene sentence should still rest on that person's own conduct.

question"How does this apply to the office bar?"

The office bar turns on a person's own adherence to an excluded ideology: an open adherent cannot truthfully swear the oath for an authority role (Oath of Office and Perjury). Adherence is defined by something checkable, like a sworn statement or the group's own membership records. Ancestry, nationality, family, or appearance alone never establishes it. For a group with no membership records, adherence is shown by a sworn statement or the person's own public profession of the ideology, never ancestry alone. Group origin, meaning place or faith of origin and never ancestry, sets the lens and intensity of questioning: a high-risk origin means harder scrutiny of doctrine, association, and adherence, not an automatic bar. That is a screening threshold under rule 1, and the bar itself still turns on the person's own adherence under rule 2. Eligibility after leaving returns after a formal renunciation that the group's own process recognizes, with perjury covering lies. For a faith with no central membership body or exit process, such as Islam, the renunciation is a sworn public renunciation, and lying in it is perjury. Someone who swears falsely commits perjury, which has to be proven in a criminal trial on evidence about that person. Passing immigration vetting doesn't lift the bar, because a Muslim who passes vetting is still an adherent.

question"What about heritable group differences?"

Average group differences, including in tested ability and in crime rates, are real, and whether genes contribute to them is unresolved (Intelligence, Race, and Crime). Neither answer would change how an individual is treated. No average tells anything about an individual, so the state should never use race in a decision about a person, and employers, landlords, and insurers should judge each person on their own record. The only exceptions are real sex-based actuarial facts and fitness tests applied the same way to everyone. This is where I part from race realists.

question"Isn't relying on group statistics politically radioactive?"

It is unpopular, not unsound, and critics rely on group statistics too. Disparate-impact law lets a plaintiff challenge a hiring practice by its effect on a group, before anyone proves intent. In Griggs v. Duke Power Co. (1971) the Supreme Court held that the law "proscribes not only overt discrimination but also practices that are fair in form, but discriminatory in operation." If group data counts as evidence when it shows a disparity in hiring, it counts as evidence when it shows a disparity in crime. What I refuse is the step both sides should refuse: letting a group average decide one person's guilt, rights, or status (rule 2 above). To reject my rule, a critic has to hold either that group data is never evidence, which would end disparate-impact law, or that it may decide an individual's fate, which my rule forbids.

Philosophy of Law

Law is a practical instrument for securing negative rights and coordinating behavior within a political community, and its authority derives from that necessity, not from objective moral truth (Meta-Ethics). Valid law should track responsibility and agency, apply rights forfeiture proportionally, and remain limited in scope. When law systematically violates negative rights or abandons impartial enforcement, it loses legitimacy and may justly be resisted.

Sovereignty and Borders

I hold that a political community has the right to maintain its sovereignty and to control its borders. Because high-trust societies better protect negative rights in practice, and that trust rests on shared norms together with impartial enforcement of law, a people may legitimately prefer cultural continuity and selective immigration that preserves the conditions for mutual cooperation and rights-respecting order. Open-ended demographic transformation that erodes those conditions ought to be avoided.

The negative-rights protections a government provides apply to the people it governs. Foreign actors, civilians included, who actively harbor ideologies supporting genocide or ethnic cleansing against a nation have, in effect, forfeited those protections against it. Where people abroad actively foment such ideas, join or support terror groups, or vote in and build new ones such as Hamas, the threatened nation has the right and the duty to deal with that threat and defend its people as it sees fit. This does not mean obliterating the foreign country. It means confronting those actively driving the threat. Harboring such an ideology means acting on it: membership, material support, public calls for genocide, or violence, not belief or speech alone. The test and process are in Hostile conduct, removal, and entry. The security of a nation's people is paramount.

The state retains a primary duty to its own citizens. Alliances, interventions, and international commitments ought to be evaluated according to whether they advance the security and long-term interests of the political community. No foreign tribunal or external authority holds legitimate veto power over decisions necessary for national survival or the protection of citizens’ rights.

Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale

The same logic of rights and forfeiture that applies to individuals scales to political life. Agents or organized groups that systematically violate the negative rights of others, or that seek to subvert the rights-respecting order itself, may be met with decisive force. Reciprocity and the practical necessity of deterrence justify withholding full rights protections from those who reject the basic framework of non-aggression and mutual restraint.

I hold that a doctrine is an excluded ideology when its program, as taught by its own authoritative texts and mainstream tradition, calls for state power to strip people of equal negative rights by status (race, religion, sex, class, or leaving the faith), and states acting on it have done so. Nazism, communism, socialism, and Islam meet that test, and they are the only four that meet it today, so Christianity, Judaism, other faiths, and atheism are not barred. I judge Islam by its mainstream legal tradition, which gives non-Muslims and women lesser standing and prescribes death for leaving the faith (The Islamic Faith in Practice). The test is the same for every doctrine, religious or secular, and any doctrine that meets it would fall under the same rules. The United States ought to designate excluded ideologies by constitutional amendment, never by executive declaration. Adherence to an excluded ideology would carry two civic disabilities and nothing more. First, no authority role - elected or appointed public office; law enforcement, military, and intelligence; judges and prosecutors; immigration and other adjudicators; K-12 public curriculum authority: no one holds a negative right to those powers, so this is the government choosing who may wield them, not a punishment, and not a ban on every taxpayer paycheck (Oath of Office and Perjury). Second, no building, rebuilding, expansion, or relocation of places that promote it, which caps them, mosques included, at today's number: an institution built to propagate a doctrine whose program is to strip others of their rights is organized subversion of the rights-respecting order, and the bar would fall on the institution, never on a person's private belief, speech, or home practice. No one would be fined, jailed, removed, or silenced for holding or advocating any view. The two bars protect the people each of these programs would strip of rights from meeting it in a judge, an officer, or a new institution built to spread it.

noteThe Civilizational Risks: parity and differences among the four excluded ideologies

The four are held on the same terms, as Civilizational Risks at the same level, under the two disabilities above: the authority-role bar, applied through the oath those roles require (Oath of Office and Perjury), with ordinary non-authority public jobs left open. And the building bar (Property Rights). The government would condemn all four in its official voice and would give none of them official platforms, public funding, or public honors. Condemnation would punish no one's belief (Cultural Stance). Holding, praising, or advocating any of them should stay legal for everyone under one speech rule: only true threats and incitement to imminent violence are punished (Free Speech and Censorship).

They differ in what each program does and in what each builds:

  • Islam stands apart from the other three first in what it is. I hold that Islam is a political ideology whose goal is sharia, the rule of Islamic law: beyond the death penalty for apostasy and the lesser standing of non-Muslims named above, women are second-class citizens under it, bound to obey their husbands and forced to cover themselves. Even in its moderate form, this is widely accepted. In Pew's survey of the world's Muslims, most Muslims across South Asia, Southeast Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, and the Middle East and North Africa favored making sharia the official law of their country, large shares of those supporters backed death for leaving Islam, and in nearly every country surveyed most Muslims said a wife must always obey her husband. These are the answers of ordinary Muslims across whole countries, not of militants (Breadth of support; Women). Several Muslim-majority states make leaving Islam a capital crime (Apostasy). All the main schools of Islamic law require women to veil, and where that law is state law the state enforces it: Iran makes appearing in public without the hijab a crime, and the Taliban's 2024 law makes covering a woman's entire body mandatory and requires her to cover her face (Compulsory covering). Islam is also the only religion among the four. As above, I judge it by its mainstream legal tradition, not by its theology, and Islamism, the movement to make that law the law of the state, is that program in action (Islamism; The Islamic Faith in Practice). Because it alone has a worship component, it alone carries the mosque rules under the building bar: no new mosques, existing ones capped at today's number, and no state support. The household, guest, and school details are set out in Property Rights and Religious Freedom.   • Communism abolishes private property by force under one-party rule. Every state that applied it ran forced-labor camps, and several brought famines that killed millions (Communism). The building bar reaches its strongholds, and the state treats its crimes as it treats Nazi crimes in what it teaches, commemorates, honors, and funds, removing existing public honors of communist figures.
  • Socialism here means socialism in its core sense, imposed by force: state or collective ownership of productive property, central planning in place of prices, and compulsory redistribution beyond a minimal floor. Voluntary cooperatives and worker-owned firms competing in markets are not covered, and I judge social democracy, markets with a large welfare state, as too large a compulsory state, not as socialism (Socialism). question"Doesn't excluding Islam by amendment establish a religion or rank faiths?"

No. The amendment would not establish a religion or prefer any faith. It would bar doctrines that use state power to strip people of equal negative rights, whether religious or secular, under one published test (Excluded ideologies). Islam is named only because it meets that test, the same test that names Nazism, communism, and socialism. The state endorses no religion (Role of Religion in Government). Article VI's ban on religious tests would be overridden by that amendment for this disability alone - the office bar for adherents of an excluded ideology - not to prefer another faith (Abdul El-Sayed).

question"Where is the line between an excluded ideology and ordinary religious or political disagreement?"

The line is the published test, and a doctrine is excluded only if all four parts hold (Excluded ideologies):

  1. Its program is taught by its own authoritative texts and mainstream tradition, not by a fringe.
  2. It calls for state power, not private persuasion.
  3. It would use that power to strip people of equal negative rights by status: race, religion, sex, class, or leaving the faith.
  4. States acting on it have done so.

Ordinary disagreement fails at least one part. A party that wants higher or lower taxes, a church that holds its own members to its own rules, or a movement that wants tighter borders may be wrong, but none of them asks the state to strip anyone of equal negative rights by status, so none is touched. Holding, praising, or advocating any doctrine, excluded or not, stays legal.

Civic disabilities tied to an ideology are not new to U.S. law. The Immigration and Nationality Act makes any immigrant who "is or has been a member of or affiliated with the Communist or any other totalitarian party" inadmissible, and bars such members from naturalization. What I add is one public test that applies to every doctrine, religious or secular. To show the bar reaches ordinary disagreement, a critic has to name a doctrine that meets all four parts and shouldn't be barred, or show that one of the four I name fails a part.

Limits of Politics

Politics cannot create virtue, guarantee flourishing, or eliminate all human conflict. I keep the state's role deliberately narrower: to maintain the external conditions under which individuals can pursue their own ends, including character development and personal flourishing, without being subjected to predation or chaos. Over-extension of political power beyond this function tends to generate new rights violations rather than resolve them.

Corporations as Moral Entities

tldrIn short

I treat corporations as tools, not moral agents. The CEO should answer by default, and the people who authorize rights violations are liable as well. Limited liability ought to stay for ordinary business, but not for deliberate violations, fraud, or knowing extreme risk.

Position

I hold that corporations are legal tools, not moral agents. Rights violations attach to human agents. The CEO should answer by default for the company's crimes, and officers and controlling shareholders who authorized the violation should answer as well (Income-Scaled Fines). Limited liability should stay for ordinary contract and tort; the veil ought to be pierced for deliberate rights violations, fraud, or knowing extreme-risk conduct.

question"If a corporation is not a moral agent, how can a company be punished?"

Duties to Future Generations

tldrIn short

I believe present agents may not impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings. Debt may fund rights-protection and honored transitions, but it should never fund rights violations or permanent subsidies.

Position

Present agents ought not to impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings. Public debt should not bind future people to fund rights violations or permanent subsidy schemes. Debt for rights-protection and honored transition commitments is acceptable; I prefer pay-as-you-go and opt-in for non-rights spending.

question"Future people do not exist yet. How can anyone owe them anything?"

The duty is a side-constraint on present action, not a debt to a present person. Whoever is sentient when the harm lands holds the same rights as anyone else, so an act set in motion now that destroys those rights later is a violation once it lands, the same as a delayed bomb.

Rationale

Side-constraints bind across time where the harmed party will be sentient, extending the capital-track extreme-risk rule in Criminal Justice & Security forward in time. The honored-commitments rule already protects current retirees during transition in Social Security and Medicare Transition. GAO projects publicly held debt reaching 123% of the economy in 2036, and reports that net interest spending in FY 2025 exceeded federal spending on national defense.

Aesthetics

tldrIn short

I find beauty in clarity, competence, and order. Cultures that reward rights-respecting order, skill, and merit tend to produce works of lasting quality.

Beauty

Beauty in the fullest sense is found in clarity, competence, and order. These are valuable because they are often the conditions under which sentient beings can live well and exercise agency without constant friction. Rights-respecting order - expressed through law, discipline, high agency, and care - generates a deeper form of beauty that frequently produces visible beauty in objects and environments. Natural visual beauty can exist independently of ethics: nature may be visually striking while remaining ethically indifferent or abominable.

Meaning

tldrIn short

I believe purpose is realized through exercising agency with virtue, within my framework's ethical constraints. I accept mortality, and I see legacy as the real impact left behind.

Purpose

Purpose is not discovered as an objective cosmic fact, nor is it purely invented in a vacuum. It is realized through the consistent exercise of agency in accordance with virtue and the prescribed ethical constraints. A meaningful life is one in which the agent develops character, acts rightly toward other sentient beings, and pursues long-term projects that express competence and care. Stoic Eudaimonism provides the personal aim. Meaning is not a separate mystical property. It is what a well-lived life feels like from the inside when one is meeting the demands of reason and character over time.

Legacy and Mortality

Mortality is a biological fact, not a moral tragedy that requires supernatural compensation. The appropriate response is clear-eyed acceptance combined with the effort to leave the conditions for other sentient lives better rather than worse. Legacy matters insofar as it reflects actual causal impact - character transmitted, rights-respecting order maintained, useful work left behind - not as a demand for eternal remembrance. Purpose is local and agent-centered: to live well within the constraints, to protect what deserves protection, and to avoid adding unnecessary suffering or chaos.
