This page is the top-level look at my worldview with descriptions describing my values. Rationale, definitions, rebuttals, and argumentation exists in the sites sections.

In one sentence: I am a sentientist threshold deontologist whose rights theory runs on forfeiture. I want a qualified-franchise epistocratic republic, which is a modern Aristotelian polity. That republic would have:

  • a minimal, opt-in fiscal state;
  • a maximal, forfeiture-based penal code;
  • values-based civilizational nationalism with secular pro-American/Western assimilationism;
  • hawkish America-First realism;
  • a transhumanist and bioengineering program to abolish animal agriculture and reorder nature against predation.

I vote Republican as the best directional fit.

In a few words: Sentientist epistocratic national-individualist.

In full: Sentientist, agnostic-atheist, negative-rightist, forfeiture-retributivist, anti-collectivist individualist, epistocratic-republican, civilizational-nationalist, classical-liberal free-market capitalist, transhumanist, futurist, predation-abolitionist anti-naturalist.

1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework

Moral anti-realist: constructivist-prescriptivist (Hume plus Hare), not relativist, and I still draw hard, enforceable verdicts

Threshold sentientist deontologist: rights are side-constraints, "not goals to maximize" (S1)

Forfeiture, not catastrophe: the threshold is the agent's own violation, not bad consequences (Moore)

Sentientist: status is substrate-neutral and graded; rights-based, unlike Singer, Regan, and Francione, with a duty to stop predation

Evidentialist scientific naturalist; a secular Stoic in personal ethics

  • Meta-Ethics: I claim no objective moral truth.
  • Metaphysics: I'm an agnostic atheist: I hold no belief in gods, but I stay open in principle to evidence.
  • Normative Ethics: My ethics is a threshold sentientist deontology.
  • Moral Psychology: I hold that full moral agency needs sentience plus intentional action and foresight, and that it scales with sophistication.
  • Animal Rights and Sentientism: Animal agriculture and aquaculture should be banned outright and shut down immediately, with sanctuary for every animal still alive (Sanctuary Plan).
  • Rescue Purchases: I believe a sentient being's right not to be killed does not disappear because a price is attached to them, so if paying is the only way to save an animal already headed to slaughter, one should pay.
  • Character Ethics: I take Stoic eudaimonism as my personal operating system for character: focus on what is within one's control, practice wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance, and keep composure while acting decisively.
  • Limits of Politics: I believe the state exists to protect negative rights and the security of the community that sustains them, not to equalize outcomes.
  • Corporations as Moral Entities: I see corporations as tools, not moral agents.
  • Duties to Future Generations: Present agents ought not to impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings.
  • Aesthetics: I find beauty in clarity, competence, and order.
  • Meaning: I believe purpose is realized through exercising agency with virtue, within my framework's ethical constraints.

3. Authority & Civil Liberties

Epistocratic constitutional republic: a modern Aristotelian polity or timocracy, close to Brennan, with a fiscal stake test and a voter license

Brennan pairs epistocracy with cosmopolitan liberalism; I pair it with civilizational nationalism and keep Swiss-style initiatives

Militant democracy (Loewenstein) without party or speech bans: bars on sworn authority roles and buildings only

Negative-rights side-constraints with forfeiture: Nozickian, plus a minimal floor that is "not a right anyone can claim"

Procedural civil libertarian, sunset-clause constitutionalist, conventional federalist

  • Individual Liberty vs State Power: The government should leave people alone unless they are actively harming others or about to do so.
  • Emergency Powers: Emergency powers should be necessary, narrow, and temporary.
  • Government Structure and Checks: Government exists to protect rights and ought to justify every expansion.
  • Term Limits: I oppose term limits on any elected office.
  • Federalism: Defense, borders, and immigration should be federal, and schools should be local, with each state funding about $10,000 per child that follows the child to any public school in the state.
  • Property Rights: Once ownership is properly established, it should last.
  • Self-Defense and Defense of Others: People should be free to use force to protect themselves or others anywhere, with no duty to retreat.
  • Freedom of Association: People, clubs, and religious groups should be free to choose whom they associate with, but only so long as they violate no one's rights: joining or organizing a group to commit or plan rights violations, such as a conspiracy, gang, or organized violent group, ought not to be protected, and no one should be held in a group against their will.
  • Privacy Rights: To me, privacy means control over access to one’s body, home, private spaces, and personal information.
  • Surveillance State: Facial recognition and social credit should be opt-in only.
  • Surveillance and Digital Technology: Data privacy should be respected and all data trading should be opt-in.
  • Due Process and Rule of Law: Everyone should answer for what they do, and people with more power should answer to a higher standard.
  • Pretrial Detention and Bail: Defendants should be released before trial unless a judge finds they are dangerous or likely to flee.
  • Civil Asset Forfeiture: The state should not take property from people who have not been convicted, except assets of foreign cartels formally designated as war enemies or Foreign Terrorist Organizations, which would be seized as enemy property on the combatant track, and true contraband, which may be seized and destroyed or returned to owners.
  • Role of Juries: Conviction should require a unanimous jury of at least twelve, in civil and criminal cases alike.
  • Epistocratic Republic and Qualified Franchise: I support a constitutional republic with a qualified franchise, an epistocratic republic rather than a full democracy: only net taxpayers who hold a voter license, earned by passing a test on issues, civics, and laws, should vote, and ranked-choice voting and the Constitution would stay.
  • Voting and Elections: Voters should show ID, mail-in ballots ought to be fully authenticated and encrypted, felons should not vote, and only net taxpayers should vote — a conflict of interest, because the franchise decides other people's money.
  • Election Administration: Elections should require ID, authenticated mail ballots, and clean voter rolls, with money limited to individual donations.
  • Federal Enforcement Near Elections: In my view federal enforcement around an election must follow rules fixed before it.
  • Redistricting: I believe districts should be redrawn once after each census, by fixed neutral criteria, without partisan data, and never mid-decade unless a court orders it.
  • Ranked-Choice Voting: Ranked-choice voting should be required for every federal, state, and local election, by constitutional amendment.
  • Primary Elections: I see parties as private associations that choose their nominees by their own rules.
  • Ballot Access and Deceptive Candidacies: I believe a candidacy filed to deceive voters, such as a stand-in with the same name, can be kept off the ballot under a rule written before the election and decided by a court before ballots are printed.
  • Qualified Citizen Initiatives: States and localities should be free to offer citizen initiatives and referendums.

Flat-tax, free-market classical liberalism with an opt-in welfare state and a Friedmanite, NIT-style floor

Sowellian on prices and disparities: markets over price controls, and outcomes explained by culture and choices, not by discrimination alone

Market pronatalist; bounded executive discretion over federal property

Unlike conservatives: no vouchers, broad tariffs, entitlement protection, or farm subsidies

Unlike libertarians: a public K-12 guarantee, a voluntary public health plan, natalist credits, and an AI-displacement trigger on capital taxes

Tariffs treated cautiously; tax and regulatory relief over mandates

  • Socialism: I see socialism in its core sense, social or state ownership of the means of production and planning in place of markets, as antithetical to my framework and a terrible idea, because it seizes property, forces redistribution, compels labor and speech, and suppresses dissent.

  • Antitrust: I don't believe big should be illegal.
  • Taxation: The main federal tax should be simple: most people below a clear income line would pay nothing, and those with low earnings would receive a top-up on what they earn.
  • Fiscal Policy: The budget should balance, deficits should be kept to a minimum, and the national debt should be paid down.
  • Monetary Policy: Balanced budgets keep money stable.
  • Public-Sector Size and Civil Service: Government should be sized to its essential functions, with defense first.
  • Public Funding of NGOs: The government ought to defund NGOs that run on public money: no grants, payment only under contracts with measurable deliverables, and no public money for advocacy.
  • Civil Service Protections: Hiring should be by merit.
  • Welfare and Entitlements: People already on benefits who followed the rules should keep their entitlement through a phase-out period.
  • Child Support: I hold that both genetic parents who consented to the sex owe support.
  • Births and Family Formation: America should get back above 2.1 children per woman through support that grows with family size: a child credit that doubles from the third child, a birth credit for third and later children, and income-tax exemptions for mothers of three (half) and four (full) (family credits).

  • Housing, Zoning, and Homelessness: Housing should be left mainly to property owners and the market.
  • Government Home Lending: The government should not use taxpayer money to make home loans cheaper.
  • Healthcare: People should choose between a publicly supported plan and private insurance.
  • Healthcare Funding, Emergency Care, and Drug Approval: The public health option should be voluntary, with taxpayers funding it only by choice.
  • Private Education Oversight: Private and religious schools have the full right to exist and should be privately funded.
  • K-12 Funding: Each state should fund about $10,000 per child per year for basic K-12 education, with a disability add-on set from actual need, following the child to any public school, district or charter, statewide.
  • Governance and Curriculum: I would abolish the federal Department of Education and put control local first.
  • Homeschooling: Homeschooling should be fully allowed, with no school-performance testing.
  • Higher Education and Student Loans: I would make Pell Grants opt-in, end state subsidies, and stop new federal student lending.
  • Trade and Alternative Pathways: I see trade schools, apprenticeships, and certificate programs as equal pathways to a career, and they should operate under the same rules as college.
  • Infrastructure and Regulation: Taxes should pay to keep core infrastructure safe and working.

6. Criminal Justice & Security

Hard retributivist through rights forfeiture, or penal maximalist, after Wellman

Punitive civil libertarian: maximal penalties under strict procedure, close to Singapore and Bukele (S11, S13)

Unlike tough-on-crime conservatives: no qualified immunity, pretrial release by default, unanimous 12-person juries

An "objective evidence" capital standard, and a capital scope wider than any U.S. tradition

  • Evidentiary Standard: Nobody should be executed unless the proof is overwhelming and backed by objective evidence such as video and physical evidence, not just testimony.
  • Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty: Deliberate murder should be punished by death, and so should violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, human trafficking, large-scale distribution of hard drugs, and giving or selling any hard drug to a minor proven to the same strict standard.
  • Insanity Defense: Mental illness does not undo a rights violation: a mentally handicapped person who kills still commits murder.
  • Lesser Homicide: A self-defense killing that went too far but was close to justified should be a lesser homicide with a lighter sentence set by the judge.
  • Human Trafficking: I see human trafficking of adults or children, and kidnapping, as egregious rights violations that ought to be capital.
  • Age of Consent: The age of consent should be 18, except under the close-in-age exception below, and a minor cannot consent to sex with an adult.
  • Incest: All sexual contact between blood relatives should be banned, even between consenting adults, as a misdemeanor punished by an income-scaled fine plus labor (one mark on a third offense) and a precaution against reproduction, since any child conceived would have to be carried to term.
  • Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life: Repeat offenders who keep committing crimes after being punished should lose their right to life once they reach three marks, provided they are still active.
  • Income-Scaled Fines: All fines, criminal, civil, and regulatory, should be scaled to income and wealth, always paired with labor for individuals, with corporations paying up to 4% of global revenue, or their gain from the offense if higher, and the CEO personally responsible as the one ultimately responsible for the company's actions, so no designated fault-officer can take the fall.
  • Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor: No one should be imprisoned for life with nothing to do.
  • Capital Trials and the Abolition of Death Row: Capital trials should take 30 days at most, and the sentence should be carried out the day it is handed down.
  • Public Executions: Public executions should return, carried out in public or openly recorded so citizens can see what the state does in their name, with hanging and gunshot both acceptable methods.
  • Deterrence and the Justification for Capital Punishment: Studies of today's slow, uncertain system cannot show whether a swift, certain death penalty deters, and the case for it rests on desert, incapacitation and forfeiture.
  • Wrongful Convictions and Compensation: Convicting an innocent person should be treated as the state violating their rights.
  • Juvenile Offenders: There should be no fixed age of criminal responsibility.
  • Civil Commitment: No one should be confined for being homeless or mentally ill.
  • Statutes of Limitations: Murder, violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, human trafficking, large-scale distribution of hard drugs, and giving or selling any hard drug to a minor should be prosecutable no matter how much time has passed, as long as the strict evidence standard is met.
  • Restorative Justice Secondary to Justice: The justice system ought to be first about protecting victims and the public and giving offenders what they deserve.
  • Victims' Rights: I hold that offenders lose their rights when they violate others'.
  • Police Power and Accountability: Police should get more money, more officers, and more respect, but officers who break the law should be punished more harshly than ordinary people.
  • Police and Law Enforcement: Body cameras should be required everywhere, the worst crime areas should get militarized enforcement under zero tolerance, and theft and burglary should be treated severely.
  • Drug Policy: Using drugs privately should be legal, but not while a minor is present or while impaired around one.
  • Border Security and Immigration Enforcement: Illegal immigrants should be removed by force, the border ought to be heavily fortified, and lethal force should be allowed when necessary.
  • Terrorism and Domestic Extremism: Domestic terrorism should be treated as a serious threat.
  • Political Violence: The 2020 riots were left-wing political violence that terrorism counts leave out, and I condemn both sides' violence equally.
  • Race and Crime: Black Americans commit violent crime at much higher rates than white Americans (arrest and victim surveys).
  • Gun Crime and Violence: Gang and high-crime areas should get heavy, hot-spot policing and fast, harsh crackdowns, and high-rate violent offenders should be locked up, with full due process.
  • Domestic Violence: Extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, and withholding food, water, or shelter as punishment against a spouse or family member should be capital if they cause lasting injury.

  • Gun Rights: Qualified citizens should be able to buy guns, with background checks and red flag laws aimed at stopping bad actors.
  • Drugs and Vice: Marijuana should be legal and hard drugs legal for private, personal use, with no drug, marijuana included, used in public or around minors, large-scale distribution of hard drugs capital, prostitution legal and regulated, and adults free to gamble.
  • Patriotism and National Identity: I believe a cohesive, patriotic culture is best, and multiculturalism works only with shared values.
  • Language and Assimilation: Assimilation is paramount and English should be the shared civic language.
  • Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos: Deliberately killing a sentient animal outside of self-defense or defense of rights is murder, graded by the animal's sentience: high-sentience animals should be on the capital track, and killing lower-sentience animals should still be punishable and count as marks against the offender.
  • Animal Cruelty Law: I define animal cruelty as extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment.

  • Russia, Ukraine, and China/Taiwan: I see Russia as an aggressor, and the U.S. should help those who fight it.

  • World Health Organization: The United States should not be bound by the WHO.
  • Foreign Aid: Foreign aid ought to serve America's own security and interests.
  • Military Spending and Projection: Current high military spending is fine with me and should rise if needed.
  • Cyber Warfare and Autonomous Weapons: I see cyber and intelligence capabilities as a core defense priority.
  • Military Space: The Outer Space Treaty should stay as the civil baseline but be read to allow defense, with clauses that block missile defense renegotiated or exited.
  • Laws of War: Civilians ought to be absolutely protected, except foreign actors who forfeit protection by hostile conduct (conduct test).
  • Veterans and Defense Budget: Defense should be the top budget priority, and the care owed to those injured in service is part of the cost of defense.
  • Nuclear Weapons and WMD: Nuclear weapons cannot be uninvented, so I believe deterrence is the main safeguard.
  • North Korea and Nuclear Rogue States: North Korea's arsenal should be deterred and contained.

Market eco-modernist: "reliability first, no mandates, nuclear yes"

Sentientist anti-naturalist: "Nature herbivorization."

Unlike conservatives, I treat climate change as a real harm; unlike environmentalists, no mandates or subsidies, and nature preserved only where rights are not violated

  • Climate Change: Climate change is real and nearly all recent warming is human-caused (IPCC), but it is not apocalyptic.
  • Adaptation and Disaster Policy: I see climate adaptation as practical.
  • Energy Mix: Fossil fuels should stay where they work best while renewables grow through private innovation.
  • Energy Policy: Any shift toward cleaner energy should be slow and market-driven, with no mandates and no source forced to shut down early.
  • Grid Reliability and Permitting: Reliability comes first, no source should be forced off early, and permitting should be streamlined while environmental protections stay.
  • Regulation and Conservation: Environmental rules should protect sanctuary lands and herbivore populations, and pollution should be regulated to keep people healthy.
  • Conservation vs Development: Nature should be preserved where rights are not violated.
  • Pollution and Liability: I hold that pollution that harms people violates their rights.
  • Subsidies and Mandates: I support no green-tech subsidies unless taxpayers opt in, and no mandates.
  • International Climate Agreements: I welcome awareness efforts, and I see ending animal agriculture as the ideal contribution.
  • Nuclear Power: Nuclear power should expand because it is proven safe, with strict safety standards.
  • Nuclear Waste and Fuel Cycle: Nuclear power should expand, spent fuel should be stored safely, recycling can develop, and storage sites ought to go in low-density areas.
  • Water Resources and Rights: Water rights are property, pollution of water is a violation, and markets should allocate water within health standards.
  • Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat: Animal agriculture should be banned outright and shut down immediately, with no wind-down.
  • Sanctuary Plan: Every farmed, fished, breeding-mill, and test animal alive at the shutdown should be kept alive for its natural life, in sanctuaries run by the state only in the emergency phase and then by licensed nonprofits, or adopted after strict vetting where safe.
  • Agriculture and Food Policy: I support no farm subsidies without opt-in funding, food safety to health standards, and lab-grown meat, the goal and the replacement for animal agriculture, allowed to compete and scale up over time.
  • Commercial Fishing and Aquaculture: Commercial fishing and fish farming of sentient animals should be banned outright and shut down immediately, with no wind-down.

Sentientist transhumanist techno-optimist, with Pearce-style abolition of predation

Procreative liberty on cloning (John Robertson), against the bioconservatives (Kass)

Deontological and forfeiture-based, where most transhumanists are utilitarian

What a critic must reject

Every position here is meant to follow from the premises below plus the evidence. To reject a conclusion, a critic has to show that it doesn't follow, or reject one of these premises and say which. Each premise links to its fullest defense.

  • Axiom 7, status: "I don't claim this as objective moral fact. It's a coherent system I choose to endorse and want enforced." Every legal order enforces norms on people who reject them, and a realist or a theist enforces the same rules under another label (Imposing it; Realists; Divine command).
  • Axiom 1: "subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) shall serve as the basis for moral status and the possession of negative rights." Nothing can matter to a being that experiences nothing, so experience is the precondition for anything mattering to anyone (Why sentience).
  • Axiom 2: "Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer." Every narrower line, by species, contract, intelligence, or substrate, leaves out beings that can be harmed, infants included, for reasons that have nothing to do with harm (Contract, egoism, substrate).
  • Axiom 3: "A being on an undisturbed developmental trajectory toward sentience ... shall be granted significant moral weight proportional to the robustness of that trajectory." Left undisturbed, it becomes a being that can be harmed, and a voluntary act of procreation creates responsibility for it (Normative Ethics; Abortion).
  • Axiom 4: "A being that once possessed sentience retains residual moral consideration." Known wishes don't lapse when a person can no longer voice them, as advance directives already recognize (End-of-life care).
  • Axiom 5: "Practical determination of sentience shall follow the best available scientific consensus." Who can suffer is a question for evidence, not opinion, and where the evidence is unclear the rule leans toward consistency with the axioms, not toward convenience (Sentience Determination).
  • Axiom 6: "strong negative rights against interference and harm," working as "side-constraints" that give way only at "a clear threshold of rights violation by the agent." No total feels the suffering of the one sacrificed, and aggregation applied consistently would kill one patient to save five (Side-constraints). Rights are reciprocal, so a deliberate violator can't claim the protection he violated (Forfeiture).
  • Negative rights only: a positive right to goods compels someone else to provide them, which is itself interference (Negative rights).
  • Axiom 7, consistency: "All moral and political rules within this system must remain consistent with the prescribed primacy of subjective experience." A rule anyone demands for themselves binds them for everyone in the same position (Hare), so every hard rule has to follow from the axioms and the evidence (Coherence and hard rules).
  • Standard of proof: proof means testable, repeatable evidence or reasoning that others can check, for moral and factual claims alike (Meta-Ethics).

Apparent tensions and my resolutions

Each of these can look like a contradiction. Here is how each one follows from my framework.

  1. Liberty versus the office bars and the mosque cap. The bars are prophylaxis, not punishment. They protect the rights order from ideologies that are hostile to it. No one would be fined, jailed, or silenced for belief. Only the four named ideologies meet the test, and adherence is defined by something checkable, never by ancestry alone (Oath of Office and Perjury). The office bar withholds a power no one has a right to, and the building cap falls on institutions that propagate the program, while existing buildings and private worship stay. Ordinary religious and political disagreement fails the four-part test (Where the line is).
  2. Property versus the two exceptions. The exceptions follow from forfeiture (animal-industry firms violated rights) and from the excluded-ideology rules. They are not ad hoc breaks in the property right. Banning a harmful use without compensation is settled law, and the Fourteenth Amendment has already voided claims to property in sentient beings (Compensation).
  3. A minimal state versus the sanctuary system and predator removal. Protecting rights is the state's core duty. The sanctuary system and predator removal are part of that job, not an expansion of it. "Minimal" means minimal in purpose, not in size, and both are built to shrink: no animal is bred into the sanctuaries, and predator numbers fall through fertility control. The full answer is in Purpose of the State.
  4. Legal drug use versus capital punishment for large-scale distribution. Personal use affects only the person using. Large-scale distribution puts doses of unknown strength, often cut with fentanyl, into the hands of many people who cannot know what they are taking, which is why I believe it should be a capital crime. Sharing a known, tested dose with a consenting adult is not capital, because the recipient knows the risk he accepts. Where each line sits, and what would move it, is in Drug Policy.
  5. A 12-week rights claim versus a ban from conception. Before 12 weeks a fetus on its way to sentience already carries significant moral weight (Axiom 3), and where science is unclear I err on the side of consistency with my axioms (Axiom 5). The ban from conception rests on both, and it is also a safeguard against documented drift past 12 weeks: Denmark, Norway, France, and Massachusetts Chapter 188. Before 12 weeks the ban protects no right of the embryo. It rests on that moral weight and on the responsibility created by voluntary procreation, which is why a pregnancy from rape is treated differently (Normative Ethics; Abortion and Bioethics).
  6. Values, not ethnicity, versus group statistics. Group statistics never become verdicts on individuals; each person is judged on their own values and conduct. Group data may set defaults, such as priors, screening thresholds, and where police go, but it never decides anyone's guilt, punishment, or legal status, and every default can be rebutted by the person's own record. The operating rule, with worked cases, is in Group Statistics and Individual Judgment. How the immigration presumption fits that rule is in Group presumption and individual vetting. Stereotypes and prejudice aren't necessarily bad as long as they don't lead to rights violations. Pattern recognition is reasonable even when the person turns out to be harmless, such as wariness of a hooded Black man at night or a suspicious-looking Muslim on a plane. Statistics showing much higher criminality among Black Americans shouldn't be denied either. Pretending nothing is going on isn't virtuous, it's stupid. Wariness of that kind is private caution, which takes no one's rights; a state default may never rest on race (rules 4 and 6). Critics who use group statistics to prove discrimination already accept them as evidence (Group statistics).
  7. Anti-realism versus strong moral rankings. Every ranking runs on my own prescribed scale, which is the stated basis of everything I write here. Why a prescribed system can still be enforced is answered in Meta-Ethics. A realist would enforce the same rules under a different label, and each hard rule has to follow from the axioms and the evidence (Realists; Coherence).
  8. Conservation versus "no wilderness." Wilderness is obsolete. It is full of animal-rights violations and should be engineered by humankind. Preserved habitat stays, but it is managed against predation rather than left wild. Conservation in my sense protects the animals living in a habitat, not the predation in it. The methods are stated: captive predator lines end, non-threatening predators go to sanctuary, herbivorization is pursued where the science works, and every step is held to the stop rule (Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos; Burden of proof).
  9. Backing Trump versus opposing his oversteps. Supporting the administration's direction while opposing its oversteps is ordinary support with limits. Each action is graded on its own, from Aligned to Opposed, and the running totals show both the direction and the dissents (Summary of Grades).
  10. Shared ground with the Greens. The Greens are right only on animal rights. They're wrong on economics and on the other social issues. Even the Greens offer reforms, such as extending the Animal Welfare Act and replacing factory farms, not the day-one end of animal agriculture I want, so a Green vote would buy reform on animals at the cost of opposition on nearly everything else (Green Party).

  11. Ranked choice and initiatives inside a restricted vote. How people vote is separate from how many vote. I believe the electorate should be drastically reduced, and those who qualify should vote by the best method. Where the franchise criteria come from, and what would change them, is in Epistocratic Republic and Qualified Franchise.

  12. Feasibility versus accepted costs. Some of my positions can't be carried out today, and some carry real costs I accept rather than deny. Not possible today: cultivated meat at the scale and price of meat, fertility control for wild predators, and any gene drive in a wild vertebrate (What works now); and, without constitutional amendment or new Supreme Court rulings, the qualified franchise, mandatory death sentences, and public execution (Qualified Franchise, Mandatory Death Penalty). Costs I accept: scarce and costlier foods during the switch, lost jobs concentrated in farming counties, and the Sanctuary Levy (Sanctuary Plan); ecological risk from removing predators, held to the rule that no step causes more suffering than it prevents (Wild-Animal Predation); and extradition refusals and diplomatic friction (Immigration and Borders); and, if AI displaced nearly all work quickly, taxing capital beyond what "minimal" means today to fund the floor (AI and Automation). One gap I haven't closed: what enforcing the food ban will cost in inspectors, customs checks, and prosecutions has no estimate yet, and I flag it as Needs research (Enforcement). Where a tool doesn't exist yet, the duty uses the tools that do. Where a cost is real, I name it and say why the rights at stake outweigh it. Why I accept these risks at all: the status quo kills billions of land animals and over a trillion fish every year, so the burden of proof falls on keeping it, not on ending it (Burden of proof).

    Each hard position names its own cost where I argue it: - Death where the victim lived: a proven offender who would never have offended again is executed too (Why death where the victim lived). - Finality: an error built on evidence that looked solid at trial, and that no new objective evidence exposes, will not be caught (New evidence after the verdict). - Compelled labor: the work is hard and unpaid (Compelled labor). - Qualified franchise: a capable citizen who skips the test goes unheard by choice (Why a license to vote). - Office bar: open adherents lose authority roles they could otherwise hold (The office bar and free speech). - Mosque cap: peaceful congregations cannot build new places of worship (Why cap mosques). - Group presumption: some applicants who would have been fine are turned away (Group presumption). - No compensation: shareholders, including fund savers, lose that value (No compensation). - Opt-in welfare: some people get less from the state than today's programs give (Opt-in welfare). - Research on offenders: some research will be slower or will not happen (Research capacity). - Predator removal: less carrion for scavengers, disease spread without predators, and herbivore overshoot, each watched under the stop rule (Risks I accept and watch).

  13. Treaty law versus my rules. Exiting the ICCPR, possibly denouncing the Convention Against Torture, compelled labor for prisoners of war beyond Geneva's limits, and West Bank removals that conflict with Geneva IV Art. 49 and the Rome Statute all depart from treaties. Treaty law is not my moral standard: a rule is not right or wrong because a convention forbids it, any more than a punishment is wrong because other democracies dropped it (the same test as the death penalty). The standard is forfeiture by the person's own conduct, and every departure is individual, decided by a court or military tribunal, never en masse or by identity (Hostile conduct, removal, and entry). The United States should leave through its own constitutional procedure, because no nation is bound forever to a foreign committee's reading of a treaty it chose to join (Treaty exits). I acknowledge that Article 49's text bars individual transfers too, so this answers the charge of ethnic cleansing, not the treaty.

  14. Due process versus emergency on-scene sentencing. Ordinary times get the 30-day capital trial and a unanimous jury. On-scene sentencing exists only inside a declared emergency over riots or violent crime that local policing can't contain, and the emergency expires on a fixed date unless the legislature renews it. It speeds up the process, not the standard of proof: the officer must be certain to the full capital standard, every case is recorded on body camera, a civilian court reviews it afterward, and an officer whose sentence the record doesn't support is charged as for murder. That is stricter than today's law, where Tennessee v. Garner already allows deadly force on probable cause (Emergency sentencing).
  15. Double jeopardy versus a capital retrial after nullification. I allow one retrial, only after a finding of nullification, only in mandatory-death-penalty cases, and before a different jury held to the same unanimity rule and capital standard. Successive trials for the same act already exist: under the dual-sovereignty doctrine, which the Supreme Court declined to overturn in Gamble v. United States (2019), a state and the federal government may each prosecute the same conduct. I acknowledge this departs from the Fifth Amendment rule that an acquittal is final (Role of Juries).
  16. Causal liability versus the secret ballot. Reparations are owed only by those who caused the harm. Lawmakers' votes and officials' enforcement acts are on the public record, so the people who passed or carried out a policy can be held liable now. Voters can't be, because the secret ballot records no one's vote, and I keep the secret ballot (Race and Diversity).
  17. Free association versus birth-sex bathroom rules for private businesses. It is a mandate, and it rests on protection and privacy in multi-person rooms where people undress. It is not a new kind of rule: federal workplace law already requires toilet rooms "separate for each sex" in all places of employment (29 CFR 1910.141). Single-occupancy lockable rooms are open to anyone, so no one is left without access (Gender Identity).
