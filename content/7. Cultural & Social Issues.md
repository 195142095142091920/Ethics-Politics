## Core Principles
I stand for a traditional nuclear family preference, free speech absolutism (with narrow exceptions), meritocracy, cultural compatibility for cohesion, and personal responsibility. I strongly oppose identity politics and progressive social engineering, and I am skeptical of mandates.

## Family and Gender

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe the healthiest family is close to the traditional nuclear family, and I see traditional roles as best, but nothing should be mandated. Divorce should be allowed, and custody should have no default. A neutral court decides, considering the child's wishes.

#### Position
Marriage and family have multiple definitions depending on one's culture and religion. I believe the healthiest family is something very close to the nuclear family: a mother and father, married and loyal to one another, with two to three children. Divorce should be allowed. Child custody should not default to either parent; if the parents cannot reach a resolution, a neutral court ought to arbitrate the dispute, informed by the child's privately expressed preference. Traditional roles, with a stronger male working while a female raises children, cares for the home, and supports the working man, I judge superior. But they should not be mandated in any way, and people ultimately live as they decide.

> [!question]- "Does favoring traditional roles conflict with individual freedom?"
> No. The preference is a judgment about what constitutes the best-functioning society, not a rule imposed by law. Individuals remain free to structure their families and roles as they choose.


#### Rationale
I treat the nuclear family as the structure best suited to raising children, and cohesive families support a high-trust society. Married parents are far more stable: about a quarter separate before a child turns 12, against about two-thirds of cohabiting parents, though part of the difference in child outcomes reflects who marries. The preference for traditional roles is a value judgment, not an empirical finding, which is why it is never mandated. A neutral custody standard avoids presuming either parent is more fit. Keeping traditional roles voluntary respects my commitment to individual liberty.
The fertility drop since 2007 is mostly fewer marriages at each age, not a collapse of birth rates inside marriage ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Births and Family Formation|Births and Family Formation]]); that is one more reason I treat the nuclear married family as the healthiest default without mandating it.

## Feminism and Sex Equality

>[!tldr] In short
> Both sexes hold equal rights and should be judged by the same rules. I reject outcome-based feminism, sex quotas, and treating traditional roles as oppression, while traditional roles stay a preference, not a law.

#### Position
Men and women hold the same rights, because rights rest on sentience, not sex ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). I support equal treatment under law and reject the strands of modern feminism that seek equality of outcome between the sexes, rely on sex-based quotas or preferences, or treat traditional family roles as oppression. Traditional roles, with a working husband and a wife who raises children and keeps the home, I judge superior but would never mandate ([[#Family and Gender]]). My abortion position also departs from mainstream feminism ([[#Abortion and Bioethics]]).

Young men's held at 25–27%. Gallup – [Exploring Young Women's Leftward Expansion](https://news.gallup.com/poll/649826/exploring-young-women-leftward-expansion.aspx)

> [!question]- "Doesn't the pay gap prove women face discrimination?"
> Mostly not. Most of the raw gap tracks differences in occupation, industry, hours, and the effect of maternity and children on mothers' careers. In 2025 women working full time earned 82.1% of men's median weekly earnings, and women working full time year-round earned 83.9% of men's median annual earnings. In Blau and Kahn's decomposition, occupation and industry were the largest measured factors in 2010, accounting for about half of the gap (51%), while work interruptions and shorter hours still mattered in high-skill jobs. Goldin finds that most of today's gap arises between men and women in the same occupation and largely begins with the birth of the first child, because many jobs pay disproportionately for long and particular hours. In Danish data, the arrival of children creates a long-run gender gap in earnings of around 20 percent, driven by time out of work, fewer hours, and lower wage rates after childbirth. A smaller residual remains: about 38% of the 2010 gap was not explained by measured factors. An unexplained residual is not proof of discrimination, but experimental evidence means discrimination "cannot be discounted." In one audit study, women applying to high-price Philadelphia restaurants were much less likely to receive job offers, while a nationwide experiment at 108 large U.S. employers found no significant average gap in callbacks between men and women, with some firms favoring men and others favoring women. The remedy I accept is equal rules, enforced against discrimination in either direction ([[#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business]]), not outcome targets.

> [!question]- "Isn't preferring traditional roles itself sexist?"
> No law follows from the preference. Women and men have identical rights and may arrange their lives as they choose. I'm only stating my judgment of which arrangement best serves families.


#### Rationale
Equal rights and equal opportunity follow directly from my framework's foundations, so the earlier, legal-equality aims of feminism are compatible with it. The conflict is with outcome-based and identity-based approaches, which I reject for every group ([[#Race and Diversity]]). The pay data show a persistent raw gap that is mostly explained by occupation, industry, hours, and the effect of maternity and children on mothers' earnings, with a smaller unexplained share, some of which experimental studies attribute to discrimination.

## Marriage Law

>[!tldr] In short
> I see marriage as a legal contract between consenting adults, same-sex couples included. Minors should not be able to marry, and churches can decline.

#### Position
I see civil marriage as a legal contract between consenting adults, and it should be recognized equally for opposite-sex and same-sex couples. The state ought to record and enforce the contract but not define its religious meaning, and religious bodies should remain free to perform or decline ceremonies. Minors cannot consent, so they should not be able to marry. Blood relatives should not marry; step relatives other than parental figures may ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Incest|Incest]]). Divorce should be allowed, and custody follows the existing family rules.

>- Party identification (Pew, registered voters, 2023 data): 59% of married men and 50% of married women identify with or lean Republican. Never-married women are 72% Democratic and 24% Republican; never-married men 61% Democratic and 37% Republican. Pew Research Center – [Partisanship by Gender, Sexual Orientation, Marital and Parental Status](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/04/09/partisanship-by-gender-sexual-orientation-marital-and-parental-status/)

> [!question]- "Should the state be in marriage at all?"
> The state's role should be limited to recording and enforcing a contract, including property and custody consequences. It does not regulate the relationship itself.


#### Rationale
In *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015) the Supreme Court held that the Constitution guarantees same-sex couples the right to marry. Family and custody rules are in [[#Family and Gender]].

## Abortion and Bioethics

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe elective abortion should be banned from conception by constitutional amendment. Sentience can begin as early as 12 weeks, and no one can pin down exactly when. Before then a fetus on its way to sentience already carries significant moral weight, and where the science is unclear I err on the side of consistency with my axioms. Countries that set a week limit keep raising it, so a line drawn at conception is the only one that holds. Rape and a threat to the mother's life are the exceptions.

#### Position
My position is [[Glossary Index#^g-sentientism|sentientist]] and affirms fetal rights. Sentience is the moral and rights theory: full negative rights attach when the fetus is sentient, and a fetus on an undisturbed trajectory toward sentience already carries significant moral weight ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Axioms|Axioms]]). The rights claim starts at 12 weeks, the earliest point in the published onset range (~12 weeks to after 24-28 weeks). I believe elective abortion should be banned from conception by constitutional amendment ([[Glossary Index#^g-abortion-ban-from-conception|Abortion ban (from conception)]]). Before 12 weeks the ban rests on two axioms. Under Axiom 3, a fetus on an undisturbed trajectory toward sentience carries significant moral weight. Under Axiom 5, where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with my axioms, and no one can pin down exactly when sentience starts. That is not a claim of full negative rights from conception. The ban is also a safeguard against drift, because countries that set a week limit keep raising it - Denmark and Norway from 12 to 18 weeks in 2025, France from 12 to 14 in 2022, Massachusetts Chapter 188 in 2026. I hold that a woman may not terminate a pregnancy that exists because of her own decisions. If the fetus is the product of rape, she ought to be freed of obligation, since she did not enter any agreement to raise it; voluntary sexual intercourse resulting in a fetus is what creates the responsibility. The exception does not cover sex an adult willingly entered with their parent or parental figure: the law ought to treat that as rape because consent is not valid ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Incest|Incest]]), but the act was willing. Forced parental-figure rape keeps the exception, and so does a minor's pregnancy from sex with a relative, since a minor cannot consent.

>

On November 6, 2026, Healey filed an emergency preamble with Secretary of the Commonwealth William Galvin. That made the law take effect immediately, before the ordinary 90-day clock, and blocked any attempt to suspend it while opponents pursue repeal. Sam Miele, chair of the Compassionate Care Coalition (Massachusetts Citizens for Life and Massachusetts Family Action), said the group dropped off 113,477 clerk-certified signatures that day - more than the 37,289 needed to put repeal on the 2028 ballot, and more than the 49,719 that would have been needed to suspend the law in the meantime. > This is the outcome I believe follows when abortion is allowed without a locked limit. A state that already allowed abortion after 24 weeks for set reasons now leaves those abortions to the physician's judgment, at a stage inside the published range for possible fetal sentience (~12 weeks to after 24-28 weeks), and used emergency procedure so voters cannot pause the change before 2028. That is why I believe elective abortion should be banned from conception by constitutional amendment: no one can pin down exactly when sentience starts, and countries that set a week limit keep raising it ([[Glossary Index#^g-abortion-ban-from-conception|Abortion ban (from conception)]]). Chapter 188 is evidence of that drift. The rights claim still starts at 12 weeks ([[13. News Commentary#Case: Massachusetts abortion law, Chapter 188, 2026|Massachusetts Chapter 188]]).

> [!question]- "Why ban before sentience?"
> The rights claim starts at 12 weeks, the earliest point in the published onset range (~12 weeks to after 24-28 weeks). A fetus before that point does not hold full negative rights on my theory, but it is not without moral standing. I believe elective abortion should still be banned from conception, by constitutional amendment, on two grounds. Axiom 3 gives a fetus on an undisturbed trajectory toward sentience significant moral weight. Axiom 5 says that where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with my axioms, and no one can pin down exactly when sentience starts ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Axioms|Axioms]]). The ban is also a safeguard against drift: countries that set a week limit keep raising it. Denmark and Norway raised their limits for abortion on request from 12 to 18 weeks from 1 June 2025, and France raised its limit from 12 to 14 weeks in 2022. Massachusetts moved the same way in 2026. The House passed it 119–33 and the Senate 15–4. On 6 November 2026 Governor Healey filed an emergency preamble, so the law took effect that day and cannot be suspended pending a 2028 referendum ([[13. News Commentary#Case: Massachusetts abortion law, Chapter 188, 2026|Massachusetts Chapter 188]]).

> [!question]- "Does a ban from conception mean rights start at conception?"
> No. Sentience is the moral and rights theory, and the rights claim starts at 12 weeks. The earlier ban rests on the significant moral weight Axiom 3 gives a fetus on its way to sentience and on Axiom 5's rule to err on the side of consistency where science is unclear, since no one can pin down exactly when sentience starts. It is also a safeguard, because countries that set a week limit keep raising it. It is not a claim that a pre-12-week fetus holds full negative rights, and it is not revised by the animal sentience-class board ([[Glossary Index#^g-abortion-ban-from-conception|Abortion ban (from conception)]]).

> [!question]- "What if the pregnancy threatens the mother's life?"
> She may end it. A person is not required to absorb serious harm because the threat lacks culpability ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Conflicts Between Rights-Holders|Conflicts Between Rights-Holders]]). Where the fetus is viable, I prefer early delivery to termination.

> [!question]- "How is the rape exception established?"
> By a report to law enforcement and a medical record. A criminal conviction should not be required first, because a trial cannot finish in time. A knowingly false claim is fraud and should be punished. The exception is responsibility-based: obligation arises from voluntary action, and rape involves none.


#### Rationale
Sentient beings hold rights, and a sentient fetus is no exception. Expert estimates of when sentience begins range from about 12 weeks to after 24-28 weeks, depending on whether a mature cortex is required. No one can pin down exactly when sentience starts, and countries that set a week limit keep raising it, which is why I believe elective abortion should be banned from conception by constitutional amendment: the rights claim starts at 12 weeks, the bottom of the range, and the earlier ban rests on the moral weight of a fetus on its way to sentience (Axiom 3) and on erring toward consistency where science is unclear (Axiom 5), with drift as a further safeguard, not on a claim of full negative rights from conception. Voluntary sexual intercourse carries responsibility for the life it creates, which grounds the duty not to terminate. Where the pregnancy results from rape, no voluntary act created the duty to sustain the fetus, so the obligation does not arise; this follows the distinction I draw between withdrawing support and killing. After Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization returned abortion regulation to the states, laws vary widely, which is the setting in which the domino concern applies. In the U.S., most abortions occur early (92.8% at or before 13 weeks in 2022) and 1.1% at 21 weeks or later. The full framework is set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]].

> [!info]- What is the responsibility framework?
> The responsibility framework holds that voluntarily engaging in sexual intercourse creates a duty toward a resulting fetus. Where intercourse was not voluntary, as in rape, no such duty arises.

### Abortion and Fetal Rights: Technology

>[!tldr] In short
> In my view new technology should change abortion policy only if it reveals new rights violations. Sentience and responsibility remain the standard.

#### Position
Policy should respond to advancing technology only where sentient rights are violated. The core sentience and responsibility framework ought to remain the standard as artificial wombs, earlier viability, and better sentience detection develop. New detection may refine how the standard is applied; it does not move the constitutional ban on elective abortion from conception, and it does not turn the pre-12-week protection into a claim of full rights from conception ([[#Abortion and Bioethics]]).

> [!question]- "Would artificial wombs change the abortion debate?"
> Only if they create or clarify sentient-rights violations. The standard remains sentience and responsibility. New technology informs how it is applied.


#### Rationale
Technologies that extend viability or detect sentience earlier may refine where the line falls, but the principle does not change. Extra-uterine support research shows the direction of the technology: in 2017, an extra-uterine system kept extremely premature lambs developing normally for up to 4 weeks.

## Assisted Death

>[!tldr] In short
> I support letting competent adults choose assisted death with a willing doctor, after an independent capacity check. Non-terminal cases wait 90 days, need two assessors, and get a documented offer of care. No one should be forced to help, taxpayers should not pay for it, minors should be excluded, and pushing someone toward death should be punished.

#### Position
I believe a competent adult may choose medically assisted death with a willing provider, as a decision about their own body and life. No provider should be compelled to take part, and no taxpayer funding should be used. Minors ought to be excluded, as they are from all irreversible bodily decisions. Every request should require an independent capacity check by a second clinician. Where death is reasonably foreseeable (Track 1), a 48-hour waiting period applies, waivable when death is imminent. Where it is not (Track 2), the waiting period is 90 days, two assessors should have to agree, one a specialist in the person's condition, and the person should receive a documented offer of care and support. Whenever mental illness is involved, a psychiatric assessment should be mandatory; mental illness as the sole condition qualifies only if it is long-standing, assessed by a psychiatrist, and documented treatment attempts have failed. Cases should be reported publicly each year. Anyone who pressures or deceives a person into choosing death violates that person's rights and ought to be punished accordingly. Where sentience has been permanently and irreversibly lost, decisions belong to next-of-kin under Axiom 4, as set out in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#End-of-Life Care and Lost Sentience|End-of-Life Care and Lost Sentience]].

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics. They illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Canada, 2023:** Health Canada reported 15,343 people received medical assistance in dying, 4.7% of deaths in Canada, an increase of 15.8% over 2022; 622 were cases where natural death was not reasonably foreseeable. Health Canada (2024) – [Fifth Annual Report on Medical Assistance in Dying in Canada, 2023](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/publications/health-system-services/annual-report-medical-assistance-dying-2023.html)

> [!question]- "Won't legal assisted death pressure the sick, disabled, and elderly into dying?"
> Voluntary choice by a competent adult is the condition. Pressure, deception, or coercion toward death is a rights violation in its own right and should be punished. No provider should be compelled, and no public money should pay for it, so there is no institutional incentive to steer people toward it. The law ought not to restrict assisted death to terminal illness. Instead it guards against drift with process: an independent capacity check, a 90-day wait, two assessors, and a documented offer of care for non-terminal cases, and psychiatric assessment whenever mental illness is involved. Oregon's data show why the concern is real. In 2024, 42% of those who died under its law listed being a burden on family, friends, or caregivers among their end-of-life concerns, though loss of autonomy, at 89%, was the most common.

> [!question]- "What does the data show where non-terminal assisted death is legal?"
> Critics point to growth and to non-terminal cases, and the review data show the safeguards holding. In Canada, which allows both tracks, 16,499 people received assisted death in 2024, 5.1% of deaths, and yearly growth slowed from 36.8% in 2019–20 to 6.9%. Non-terminal (Track 2) cases were 732, or 4.4%. Those cases raise the concern my extra safeguards address: 44.7% cited isolation or loneliness, compared with 21.9% in Track 1, and only 23.2% received palliative care. Isolation was never the sole reason given, and 45 requests were refused for external pressure. Canada has twice delayed eligibility for mental illness alone, now set for March 17, 2027. In the Netherlands, 9,958 cases made up 5.8% of deaths in 2024, including 219 psychiatric and 427 dementia cases, and the review committees found six cases (0.06%) that failed the due-care standard. While critics argue that vulnerability is not captured by demographics alone, a 2007 review of Oregon and Dutch data found no heightened risk for the elderly, the poor, the disabled, or people with psychiatric illness, and my extra safeguards address the individual case.

> [!question]- "Why allow assisted death but not abortion?"
> Assisted death is a competent adult's decision about their own body and life. Abortion of a sentient fetus ends another sentient being's life, one that exists because of the mother's own decisions. The line in both cases is the same: rights rest on sentience, and no one may end another sentient being's life without [[Glossary Index#^g-forfeiture-rights-forfeiture|forfeiture]].


#### Rationale
Adults are free to make choices about their own bodies with willing providers, even choices others judge mistaken. That principle already governs adult transition in [[#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]. [[Glossary Index#^g-negative-rights|Negative rights]] protect a person against interference by others, and a competent person's own considered choice is not an interference. Oregon's experience shows the practice operating at small scale. In 2025, 637 people received prescriptions under the Death with Dignity Act and 400 died from ingesting the medication, about 1.0% of deaths in the state. Since 1997, 5,520 people have received prescriptions and 3,691 have died from taking them. Most had cancer (61%) or a neurological disease (14%), and the most common concerns were loss of autonomy (89%) and less ability to enjoy life (89%); 39.8% cited being a burden on family or caregivers. Jurisdictions that allow non-terminal cases, such as Canada and the Netherlands, show steady growth and some cases driven by isolation. So the law ought to allow any qualifying condition while adding time, a second specialist view, and a documented offer of care where death is not near.

### End-of-Life Care and Lost Sentience

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe patients decide their own care while they are conscious, and their written wishes must be followed. Once consciousness is permanently gone, the family should decide, guided by what the person wanted. Medical science decides when that point has been reached.

#### Position
While a person remains sentient, their own decisions should govern their care, including refusing treatment. Advance directives and other known wishes ought to be honored. When sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, the being keeps residual moral consideration, and decision-making authority, including whether to continue life support, passes to next-of-kin, guided by the person's known wishes and dignity (Axiom 4). Whether sentience is irreversibly lost is decided by the best available scientific consensus and accepted medical standards (Axiom 5).

> [!question]- "Who decides when a patient can no longer speak and has left no instructions?"
> Next-of-kin decide, guided by what is known of the person's wishes and by respect for their dignity. Where the person left an advance directive, it governs. The decision ought to rest with the family rather than with the state, which intervenes only to protect a still-sentient person's rights.


#### Rationale
Axiom 4 already sets the rule; this topic applies it to medical care. Current law offers the working standard. The Uniform Determination of Death Act defines death as the irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory functions, or of all functions of the entire brain including the brain stem, determined by accepted medical standards. In *Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health* (1990), the Supreme Court allowed a state to require clear and convincing evidence of an incompetent patient's wishes before life support is withdrawn. That shows why recorded wishes matter in practice. Assisted death for competent adults is covered in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Assisted Death|Cultural & Social Issues]].

## LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues

>[!tldr] In short
> I see male-female pairing as best, but same-sex marriage should be legal. I view transgenderism as a mental illness. Adults may transition privately, but not compete in opposite-sex sports or use opposite-sex multi-person bathrooms and changing rooms open to the public. Minors should get no transition treatment, and pronouns are never mandated. I would ban coercive or aversive "conversion" methods. Voluntary exploratory therapy for adults and non-affirming therapy for minors under parental authority should be allowed.

#### Position
I believe the best outcome for society is male and female, naturally and biologically, but same-sex marriage should be legal. I would not mandate this belief, but male-male adoption raises concern for me because same-sex parents do not fully fulfill the best-case male/female dynamic of traditional parenting. I treat transgenderism as a mental illness and view the DSM's definitions of gender as ideologically driven; the illness should be treated rather than validated. Adults may transition, and private practices may train for and perform the surgery, as a freedom of both parties. Transgender individuals should not compete in their transitioned-sex sports in any case, minors should undergo no transgender treatment in any case, and every multi-person bathroom or changing room open to the public, in government or private facilities, ought to be separated by birth sex, while single-occupancy lockable rooms are open to anyone. Pronouns may be respected or not as each individual chooses and should never be mandated. Because I treat dysphoria as a mental illness rather than validating it, talk therapy aimed at reducing incongruence is ordinary treatment when it is voluntary. Coercive or aversive methods - force, electroshock, or any therapy a minor is compelled into against parental authority or an adult against their will - should be banned. Voluntary exploratory therapy for adults, and non-affirming / exploratory therapy for minors under parental authority, should be allowed; affirming medical transition for minors should remain banned.

> [!question]- "If transgenderism is a mental illness, why allow adults to transition?"
> Adults are free to make choices about their own bodies with willing providers, even choices judged mistaken. The state should not validate or promote transition, and it should protect others where the choice affects them: in sports, bathrooms, and speech. Minors, who cannot consent to such decisions, should be excluded entirely.

> [!question]- "Isn't the DSM the scientific consensus? Why call its classification ideologically driven?"
> The DSM is a consensus document, and the American Psychiatric Association states its reasons openly. In 2013 DSM-5 replaced "gender identity disorder" with "gender dysphoria" to "avoid stigma and ensure clinical care," stating that gender nonconformity "is not in itself a mental disorder" while keeping a diagnosis that preserves treatment and insurance access. The APA itself calls the diagnosis "a double-edged sword." The 2022 text revision changed "natal male" to "individual assigned male at birth" to use "less stigmatizing language." Reducing stigma and preserving coverage are social and practical aims, not new findings about causes or outcomes, so I treat the classification as a value judgment I may reject.

> [!question]- "What makes the DSM an ideologically captured text?"
> I agree with the APA that homosexuality is not a disorder. But look at how psychiatry actually decides what counts as one: in 1974 its members put it to a vote, 5,854 to 3,810. In 2013 the DSM renamed "gender identity disorder" to "gender dysphoria" not because of any new discovery, but, in the APA's own words, "to avoid stigma and ensure clinical care," and it kept a diagnosis on the books so insurance would keep paying. In 2022 it swapped "natal male" for "individual assigned male at birth" for "less stigmatizing language." A manual that rewrites its categories to manage feelings and keep billing codes is not describing reality. It is doing activism with a medical letterhead. A male who believes he is a woman holds a false belief about his own body, and any honest manual would say so.

> [!question]- "Don't the major medical associations support gender-affirming care?"
> Yes. The American Medical Association calls such treatment medically necessary and opposes laws that restrict it, and the American Psychiatric Association supports puberty suppression for youth when indicated. The question is what their guidance rests on. The Cass Review's systematic review of 23 guidelines found that few systematically reviewed the evidence and that most drew on two, WPATH's and the Endocrine Society's. Court documents reported by The BMJ show WPATH required Johns Hopkins reviewers it commissioned to use the data "for the benefit of advancing transgender health in a positive manner" and to seek board approval before publishing. The lead reviewer reported finding "little to no evidence about children and adolescents," and only two reviews were published. WPATH says it disputed only the timing of publication. The 2025 HHS umbrella review rated the evidence on psychological outcomes, quality of life, regret, and long-term health as very low quality. Endorsement by an association does not raise the quality of the studies underneath it.

> [!question]- "Doesn't childhood dysphoria usually persist?"
> Follow-up studies suggest most cases do not. In a Toronto sample of 139 clinic-referred boys, 17 (12.2%) persisted and 122 (87.8%) desisted (Singh, Bradley, and Zucker, 2021). In a Dutch sample of 127, persistence was the minority outcome and was more likely with more intense dysphoria and among girls (Steensma et al., 2013). These studies have limits: they used older DSM criteria, 36.7% of the Toronto boys were below the diagnostic threshold, and they predate today's adolescent-onset caseload. Comorbidity is high: in Finland's adolescent service, severe psychiatric problems often preceded the dysphoria and autism-spectrum problems were very common (Kaltiala-Heino et al., 2015). That pattern supports treating the underlying distress first. Advocates cite a 2022 U.S. cohort of 317 children who had socially transitioned, 94% of whom identified as transgender about five years later (Olson et al.). The study followed only children who had already transitioned, so it cannot separate persistence from the effect of the transition itself. Transgenderism is a cultural mental illness propagated and supported by society, when it should instead be supported out of, not into.

> [!question]- "Hasn't the change in referrals just reflected greater acceptance?"
> Advocates credit greater acceptance, but the researchers who documented the rise say the reasons "are not known," naming awareness, destigmatization, and social and media influences as possibilities (de Graaf et al., 2020). Referrals rose on a similar pattern across four Nordic countries and the UK, with natal girls predominating among adolescents. Whatever the cause, a fast change in who is referred argues for more diagnostic caution, not less. Several of the countries with the longest experience have pulled back. Sweden judged in 2022 that the risks of blockers and hormones for adolescents "are likely to outweigh the expected benefits," Finland made psychosocial support first-line, Norway's investigation board classed the treatments as investigational, and NHS England stopped routine prescribing of puberty blockers in March 2024, and in December 2024 the government made the under-18 ban indefinite, to be reviewed in 2027 (GOV.UK announcement).

> [!question]- "Why have trans identifications skyrocketed if this is just people finally coming out?"
> Some of the rise is greater willingness to speak, and the main study of sudden teen onset relies on parent reports. The numbers still don't look like a hidden population stepping out. UK gender clinic referrals went from 51 in 2009, mostly boys, to 3,115 confirmed new cases in 2021/22, with about 73% female in a later audit. In 2023, 3.3% of U.S. high schoolers identified as transgender, triple the 1.1% of all adults, and about two-thirds of them were girls. In the parent-report study, 86.7% of the teens had more social-media use, trans-identifying friends, or both. The kids drawn in are disproportionately vulnerable: in Finland, 75% of teen referrals already had other psychiatric treatment and 26% were autistic, trans-identified people are three to six times as likely to be autistic, and clinic studies found sexual-abuse histories in 5-19%. I'm not talking about sexual grooming, but adults steering vulnerable children from troubled homes toward an irreversible identity follow the same pattern of isolating and shaping a child. This is not just acceptance. It is recruitment.

> [!question]- "Isn't sex a spectrum?"
> No. Biological sex is defined by the type of gamete an organism is organized to produce, and there are two, small and large. No third gamete exists (Goymann, Brumm, and Kappeler, 2023). Some biologists argue sex is better described as a spectrum because of differences of sex development, and those conditions are real. They are variations in how the two-sex system develops, not additional sexes, so they do not change the binary. Law and public facilities that rely on sex therefore track biological sex.

> [!question]- "Isn't being trans about gender, not sex?"
> Critics separate the two: sex is the body, gender is an inner identity or a social role. I think the distinction is hollow. Strip "gender" down and what's left is surface stereotypes - clothes, hair, makeup, mannerisms, interests. None of that is manhood or womanhood. A woman who hates dresses and fixes engines is still a woman. A man who loves fashion is still a man. Manhood and womanhood are lived through a sexed body: puberty, fertility, motherhood, fatherhood, and the vulnerabilities and strengths that come with each. Activists ask to be placed by sex while defining themselves by stereotype. That gives no claim to the word "woman," or to the spaces set aside for women. Either gender is stereotype, and changes nothing, or it is sex, and sex cannot be changed.

> [!question]- "Does medical transition change a person's sex?"
> Hormones and surgery can change how a body looks. They cannot change what it is. Sex is set by which of two gametes a body is built to produce, and no drug or operation has ever turned a male body into one that makes eggs. A man is an adult human male and a woman is an adult human female. A "trans woman" is a male who has altered his appearance, nothing more. Law built on that fiction forces everyone else to pretend, in locker rooms, on playing fields, on birth certificates, and in courtrooms. That is why I reject the claim that anyone can change sex, or be treated as the other sex by law. Adults can do what they like to their own bodies. They cannot draft the rest of us into affirming it.

> [!question]- "If gender can be changed by self-identification, why can't race?"
> Race is blurrier than sex, which only makes this harder for the other side. If saying "I am a woman" makes a male a woman, then saying "I am black" should make a white woman black. Ancestry is no more fixed than sex. The philosopher Rebecca Tuvel made exactly this argument in 2017: whatever justifies transgender identity justifies transracial identity just as well. Activists demanded her paper be retracted, and they never answered her logic. When Rachel Dolezal said "I identify as black," the country laughed, because everyone could see that wanting to be something doesn't make you that thing. The same reasoning applies to a male who says he is a woman. Either identity overrides biology in both cases or in neither. I say neither.

> [!question]- "Why is it offensive for a male to claim to be a woman?"
> Adults may dress and speak as they like, and not every woman will take offense. The claim is still an insult to the people who live the sex being borrowed. A male who has never gone through a female puberty, never menstruated, never faced pregnancy or motherhood, and never lived with the ordinary female fear of sexual violence is not a woman, and a female who has never lived fatherhood or a male adolescence is not a man. That is not a feeling. It is a life. More than one in five U.S. women have experienced completed or attempted rape (CDC, 2023/2024). After puberty, males typically carry 15 to 20 times the testosterone of females, the basis of the male strength advantage (Handelsman et al., 2018). Sex is lived in the body. Claiming it without living it is an offense to everyone who does.

> [!question]- "Don't adults who transition report low regret and better well-being?"
> Advocates cite low reported regret: a meta-analysis of 27 studies with 7,928 patients found about 1%, though its own authors note subjective measurement and a lack of standardized questionnaires (Bustos et al., 2021). Long-term outcomes are less clear. A Swedish cohort found post-surgery mortality 2.8 times and suicide 19.1 times that of controls, though it could not show what would have happened without surgery (Dhejne et al., 2011). A national Swedish study first reported reduced mental-health treatment after surgery, then issued a correction after reanalysis finding no advantage of surgery for mood or anxiety treatment or suicide-attempt hospitalization (Bränström and Pachankis, 2020). So the state ought to leave adult transition to adult choice without treating it as the established cure, and without public funding.

> [!question]- "You call dysphoria a mental illness and also oppose conversion therapy — which is it?"
> Both, under a defined rule. Oppose means ban coercive and aversive methods: force, electroshock, or compelling a minor into any therapy against parental authority, or an adult against their will. Allow means voluntary exploratory talk therapy for adults who want to reduce incongruence, and non-affirming or exploratory therapy for minors under parental authority, consistent with treating the illness rather than affirming it. Affirming hormones or surgery for minors stay banned. Adult medical transition stays a private choice with willing providers, not a state-validated cure.

> [!question]- "Why not affirm a trans identity, as the person asks?"
> The distress is real, some patients say affirmation helped, and the main follow-up study tracked only children who had already transitioned. We still don't affirm an anorexic's belief that she is fat, or remove a healthy limb for someone with body integrity dysphoria. We treat the disorder. Affirmation is not neutral kindness; it is an intervention. The Cass Review found no clear evidence that social transition in childhood improves mental health, found that children who socially transitioned were more likely to go on to medical treatment, and warned it may change the course of gender identity development. Of 317 American children socially transitioned early, 94% still identified as transgender about five years later (Olson et al., 2022). Affirm a child into an identity and expect it to stick. You treat a disorder. You don't build a child's life around it.


#### Rationale
The state ought to protect adult liberty while refusing to compel others to affirm beliefs they do not hold. Sex-based categories in sports and bathrooms exist for fairness and safety, so they follow biological sex. Minors cannot consent to irreversible medical treatment, and the Supreme Court upheld a state ban on such treatment for minors in United States v. Skrmetti. England's Cass Review (2024) found the evidence base for youth gender medicine remarkably weak and recommended puberty blockers only within research protocols. My classification departs from the DSM-5-TR, which diagnoses gender dysphoria only where incongruence brings clinically significant distress. The departure rests on three points. First, the 2013 reclassification was justified by the APA in terms of stigma and access to care rather than new evidence about causes or outcomes. Second, the guidelines built on it have been judged weak by systematic review, and the evidence for treating minors has been rated low or very low quality by England's Cass Review, Sweden, and the 2025 HHS review. Third, sex is binary, defined by gametes, so an identity that contradicts it should be treated as a condition to be understood and treated, not as a fact the law must adopt. Counter-evidence is acknowledged: the AMA and the American Psychiatric Association support affirming care, and reported regret after adult surgery is low. Same-sex marriage remains legal under Obergefell v. Hodges, consistent with my limited role for the state in private relationships.

##### Outcomes
Columbia Law School's What We Know project reviewed 79 peer-reviewed studies. Of these, 75 found children of gay or lesbian parents fare no worse than other children. The American Academy of Pediatrics and the American Psychological Association agree. Supporting work includes Rosenfeld's 2010 census analysis, the National Longitudinal Lesbian Family Study (Gartrell and Bos), and studies of adoptive gay fathers by Golombok and by Farr and Patterson.

Three lines of research dissent. Regnerus (2012) reported worse outcomes for adults raised by a parent with a same-sex relationship. Allen, Pakaluk, and Price (2013) reanalyzed Rosenfeld's data and found children of same-sex couples 35% less likely to make normal progress through school. Allen also studied high school graduation in Canadian census data. Sullins (2015–2016) found emotional problems more than twice as common and attributed this to not living with both biological parents. Cheng and Powell (2015) reanalyzed Regnerus's data and found that at least a third of the respondents he counted as raised by a same-sex parent were misclassified, many having never lived with that parent or having lived with them for a year or less. Once corrected, the differences were small or absent.

Planned same-sex household studies do not settle the male-male adoption concern: the no-difference literature mostly uses small, self-selected samples of planned lesbian families. Breakup-contaminated designs cut the other way — dissenting studies often count a household as same-sex if a parent ever had a same-sex relationship, which mixes in children who went through family breakup — and the journal that published Regnerus released an audit concluding its peer review failed. Residual uncertainty stays. The preference for mother-and-father parenting stands on developmental priors and the family section, not on Regnerus alone, and male-male adoption remains the sharper concern.

##### Abuse Data
No published study measures abuse rates in gay-father adoptive or foster homes. In the National Longitudinal Lesbian Family Study, 0 of 78 adolescents reported physical or sexual abuse by a parent or caregiver. In Regnerus's survey, 23% of respondents whose mother had a same-sex relationship reported ever being touched sexually by a parent or adult, against 2% from intact biological families. The question did not ask who did it, and Cheng and Powell's reanalysis found many of those respondents misclassified. The federal NIS-4 has no data specific to gay-father homes, so no national figure measures abuse in them.

##### Boys
Goldberg (2009) studied couples waiting to adopt. Among those with a sex preference, 46% of same-sex couples preferred boys, against 29% of heterosexual couples. Several studies find gay fathers do end up adopting boys more often. Whether this comes from parent preference or agency matching is unclear, and no data ties it to abuse. The pattern is noted as a point to watch, not as evidence of harm.

## Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions

>[!tldr] In short
> Public schools should stay out of gender-identity advocacy, never socially transition students, and never keep it from parents. No one should be compelled to use pronouns. I'd have ID, prisons, and shelters follow birth sex, adult transition gets no public money, military service follows ordinary medical standards, and government buildings fly official flags only.

#### Position
This section applies [[#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]] to public institutions. Public schools should teach core skills and objective facts without ideological instruction in either direction ([[5. Schools should not socially transition students and should never withhold a student's gender-identity concerns from parents. No student, teacher, or public employee should be required to use another person's chosen pronouns. Government identity documents ought to record birth sex, with no change of marker. Prisons and shelters should house people by birth sex. No public money should fund adult transition. Transgender people should be able to serve in the military under the same medical-fitness standards as any other condition, set by the military. Government buildings ought to fly official flags only.

## Curriculum Content

>[!tldr] In short
> History and civics ought to be taught unmodified, and I want more curriculum on taxes, government, how society works, and jobs.

#### Position
History and civics should be taught unmodified. The curriculum ought to add more instruction on taxes, government, how society works, and jobs. Charter schools set their own curricula and remain publicly accountable; private schools set their own curricula.

> [!question]- "Who decides what counts as unmodified?"
> Public standards are set locally and published so parents can see them. Families who disagree may choose a private school or homeschooling.

> [!question]- "Is critical race theory taught in public schools?"
> Not as doctrine. Critical race theory is an academic framework from legal scholarship whose core claim is that racism is "embedded in legal systems and policies," not only in individual bias. Public schools teach history unmodified, including slavery, Jim Crow, and the civil rights movement, as facts. They do not teach critical race theory, intersectionality, or any other ideological framework as settled truth, consistent with a curriculum limited to core skills and objective facts ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Education]]). Some platforms call for closely related content: the Green Party would "Teach all students about white supremacy and intersectionality." Several states have passed laws on the subject, though Brookings found most of them never name critical race theory.

> [!question]- "Doesn't 'unmodified' history still leave out African and Arab roles in the slave trade?"
> Unmodified means the full record, not a single villain. Public schools ought to teach the Atlantic trade as a system: European demand and shipping, and African kingdoms and middlemen—including Dahomey, Asante, and Fante networks—as essential suppliers on the coastal side, plus the separate Arab/East African trades, U.S. emancipation through the Civil War, Jim Crow, the civil rights movement, and Black achievement. "Primary brokers" is fair for the African supply side and incomplete without European demand. That is history as fact, not critical race theory as doctrine. A curriculum that casts only one group as eternal oppressor fails the unmodified test the same way a curriculum that erases slavery would.


#### Rationale
In 2022, 13% of eighth graders performed at or above proficient in U.S. history and 22% in civics on the National Assessment of Educational Progress. Education structure is set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Domestic Policy & Governance]].

The institutions that tell the story pick the villain. Schools, universities and publicly funded culture that teach a single oppressor-and-oppressed lens train students to cast the capitalist, the employer, the landlord and "the rich" as villains, and a generation taught one lens applies it everywhere, to housing, to climate and to conflicts abroad. That is why no ideological framework is taught as doctrine and civics is taught unmodified.

Teaching only a domestic oppressor lens while skipping African supply-side kingdoms or the Arab trade is the same selective storytelling I reject.

## Free Speech and Censorship

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe speech must be fully protected except for true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Private platforms set their own rules. Misinformation should be answered by platforms and individuals, not the government.

#### Position
I hold free speech absolutely: no one should be fined, jailed, or removed for any opinion, and the only speech the law should punish is true threats and incitement to imminent violence that infringe on others' sentient rights. The two civic disabilities for excluded ideologies govern who holds authority roles and which institutions may be built, not what any person may say or believe ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^excluded-ideologies|Excluded ideologies]]). Separate from speech restrictions, minors' access is limited by law in two areas: pornography, as set out in [[#Pornography and Minors' Access]], and social media accounts, as set out in [[#Minors and Social Media]]. Adults' speech and access are unaffected. Private platforms are private and should be free to restrict speech under their own codes of conduct and terms of service, just as a homeowner may remove someone from a private home for speech the owner does not approve. Misinformation is the responsibility of the private platform or, in public, of individuals to debunk as factually false. Peaceful protest is protected speech and assembly. Once a protest turns to looting, arson or attacks, it is a riot, and it should be met with immediate enforcement and serious prison time for those who took part. Organizers and endorsers ought to be criminally liable only if they planned or incited the violence. Otherwise their endorsement is speech, which others are free to condemn.

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics. They illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
>- United Kingdom, 17 Oct 2024: Lucy Connolly was jailed for 31 months after pleading guilty to inciting racial hatred over an X post on the day of the Southport killings calling for "mass deportation now" and for hotels housing asylum seekers to be set on fire. The post was viewed about 310,000 times. Crown Prosecution Service – [UPDATED WITH SENTENCE: Childminder admits inciting racial hatred over social media post](https://www.cps.gov.uk/cps/news/updated-sentence-childminder-admits-inciting-racial-hatred-over-social-media-post); BBC News – [Politician's wife Lucy Connolly jailed for race hate post](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cp3wkzgpjxvo)

> [!question]- "Does letting private platforms moderate undermine free speech absolutism?"
> No. Free speech is a protection against government censorship. A private platform setting its own rules is exercising property rights, the same way a homeowner controls who speaks in their home. The state's only role in speech is to restrict true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Limits on minors' access are set out separately.

> [!question]- "Is hate speech protected?"
> Yes, legally. The only speech restricted is true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Private platforms may still remove it under their own terms, and misinformation is answered by platforms and individuals, not the government.

> [!question]- "Don't limits on minors' access contradict free-speech absolutism?"
> They are separate from speech restrictions. They limit minors' access to pornography and to social media accounts. Adults' speech and access are unaffected.

> [!question]- "Is self-censorship among professors overblown?"
> No. In a 2021 anonymous survey, 470 of 4,603 invited U.S. psychology professors answered, 92.6% of them at or left of the political midpoint. Those more confident that controversial conclusions were true were more reluctant to say so in a professional setting, which can make a field look more settled than the private beliefs are. Professors reported substantial fear of social-media attacks, ostracism, and pejorative labels if they stated their own empirical beliefs, and tenured faculty reported as much self-censorship and as much fear, including of being fired, as the untenured. A larger 2024 faculty survey found the same pattern across departments: 35% had recently toned down their writing for fear of controversy, against 9% of social scientists asked the same question at the end of the McCarthy era. Public universities are the government. The law should not punish a professor for a research conclusion any more than it punishes any other opinion.


#### Rationale
Speech does not violate sentient rights unless it threatens or incites violence. The Supreme Court's standard in Brandenburg v. Ohio protects advocacy unless it is directed to and likely to produce imminent lawless action, which closely matches the line drawn here. Federal law already protects platforms' ability to moderate content on their own terms.

## Minors and Social Media

>[!tldr] In short
> Following Australia's model, I would bar minors under 16 from holding social media accounts. Going one step further than Australia, 16- to 18-year-olds should have access only with parental opt-in. I rest the rule on parental authority, not on proven harm.

#### Position
Social media should follow Australia's model for under-16s: minors under 16 should not hold social media accounts. Australia sets no rule for 16- and 17-year-olds; I would allow access from 16 to 18 only by parental-consent opt-in. Opt-in consent for data collection should apply everywhere, given by parents for their children.

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics. They illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Australia, 2024:** parliament approved a ban on social media accounts for children under 16, with fines of up to A\$50m for non-compliant companies. BBC News (2024) – [Australian social media ban on under-16s approved by parliament](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c89vjj0lxx9o)

> [!question]- "Doesn't this take the decision away from parents?"
> Under 16, the rule is the same for every family. From 16 to 18, parents decide through opt-in consent.

> [!question]- "Is there proof that social media harms teenagers?"
> Critics cite the National Academies, which found in 2023 that the research does not support the conclusion that social media causes changes in adolescent health across the population, while noting possible harms and benefits for individual teens. The ban never rested on population-level harm. It rests on parental authority: children cannot consent to contracts or data collection on their own, and the rule backs parents against platforms built to hold a child's attention.


#### Rationale
Since December 10, 2025, Australian law has required age-restricted social media platforms to take reasonable steps to prevent Australians under 16 from creating or keeping accounts, according to Australia's eSafety Commissioner. In 2024, 16% of U.S. teens said they use TikTok almost constantly and 15% said the same of YouTube, according to the Pew Research Center. The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine concluded in 2023 that the evidence does not show social media causing population-level changes in adolescent health. The age limit is grounded in parental authority over minors, not in a proven harm.

## Race and Diversity

>[!tldr] In short
> I want no quotas, no DEI preferences, and only merit in hiring and admissions. Reparations should be paid only by the responsible government, charged to those who voted for it, and expire after some generations. More unbiased research on group differences is needed. The current evidence on IQ and crime is set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Intelligence, Race, and Crime|Criminal Justice & Security]]. In my view race carries real patterns that shouldn't be ignored or politically corrected away, without that meaning race-based policy. Profiling by police, aimed at those who fit the statistics and data, should not be stopped, and random checks are also allowed.

#### Position
There should be absolutely zero hiring quotas based on innate characteristics, which are fundamentally racist and sexist. I would abolish government DEI programs. Equality of outcome is discriminatory by definition, and equality of opportunity should be the goal. Meritocracy ought to be the only relevant factor in hiring and admissions, public and private, with narrow carve-outs ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-carveouts|Merit-rule carve-outs]]) ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment in Employment and Business]]). Reparations should be paid only by the offending government, and only where a causal link runs from the electorate's voting decisions to the candidate or administration responsible. Those whose vote is on record as electing the offending government are liable for the reparations tax, and those who did not vote for it are not. Reparations ought to apply only for a limited number of generations, expiring once enough time has passed. I believe more unbiased scientific research on racial IQ is needed. Race doesn't matter in itself; culture and assimilation do. But race carries real patterns that shouldn't be willfully ignored or politically corrected away, without that meaning race-based policy. Profiling by police and law enforcement is acceptable and should not be stopped. It is justified because it targets the people who fit the statistics and data: a hooded young Black man fits them in a way an elderly white woman does not. Random checks should also be allowed.

I would end every federal DEI program by closing it outright, through Congress or a rewrite of the grant rules that applies to everyone. The government may decide what it funds. It may not cancel a researcher's grant inside a program because of the views the researcher holds, since that turns funding into a penalty on speech, which my absolute free-speech rule forbids ([[12. Current Administration#DEI grant cancellations left blocked|DEI grant cancellations]]).

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics. They illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Supreme Court, 2023:** the Court held that Harvard's and UNC's race-conscious admissions programs violate the Equal Protection Clause. Supreme Court of the United States (2023) – [Students for Fair Admissions, Inc. v. President and Fellows of Harvard College, No. 20-1199](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/20-1199_hgdj.pdf)

> [!question]- "How could voter records determine reparations liability when ballots are secret?"
> The position ties liability to a voter's recorded support for the responsible government. Where no such record exists, liability cannot be assigned. The principle is that only those causally responsible pay. Implementing it would require a record of votes that current secret-ballot systems do not keep, so the mechanism is a statement of principle and does not change the secret ballot. Part of it can be applied today: legislators' roll-call votes and officials' enforcement acts are on the record, so those who enacted or carried out the policy can be held liable now. Voter liability waits on a record the secret ballot doesn't keep.

> [!question]- "Without DEI, what stops discrimination in public institutions?"
> Merit. Meritocracy is the only relevant factor in public hiring and admissions, so neither preference nor exclusion by innate characteristics is permitted. Equality of opportunity should be the goal. I reject equality of outcome as discriminatory by definition.

> [!question]- "What makes research on group differences unbiased, and how is misuse prevented?"
> Unbiased means open data, preregistered methods, and peer review, with any public funding opt-in. No finding about a group changes anyone's rights, which rest on sentience, and in hiring and admissions individuals should be judged by merit, not by group averages.

> [!question]- "If more unbiased research on group differences is needed, why is it scarce?"
> Because many scholars will not say what they think. Interviews with 41 psychology PhDs pointed to genetic, evolutionary, or other natural explanations of group differences in outcomes such as intelligence and crime as the most taboo topics. In the follow-up survey of 470 professors, self-censorship was common, and those who leaned toward the claims were the most silent. That is the climate I want the open, preregistered work I already described to be free of. The evidence I already use is in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Intelligence, Race, and Crime|Intelligence, Race, and Crime]].


#### Rationale
Judging people by innate characteristics in hiring, admissions, and business violates the principle of individual responsibility at the core of my framework. The Supreme Court held in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard that race-conscious admissions violate the Equal Protection Clause. Defenders of racial preferences cite California, which ended race-based admissions at its public universities under Proposition 209 in 1998: Bleemer (2022) found that underrepresented minority applicants then enrolled at less selective colleges, earned fewer degrees, and earned about 5% less a year at ages 24 to 34, driven by Hispanic applicants. That finding measures what a preference gave one group; it does not make the preference just. Every seat awarded by race is a seat denied to another applicant for the same innate trait, which is the discrimination the Court struck down. The law ought to ban race preferences in all hiring and admissions, public and private. Merit admission does not depend on group outcomes; equality of opportunity is the goal. Liability for reparations follows the same principle: those who caused the harm pay, and those who did not are not charged. Unbiased research aims at an honest account of group differences, without race-based policy built on them.

> [!info]- What is equality of opportunity versus equality of outcome?
> Equality of opportunity means everyone is judged by the same rules and standards. Equality of outcome means adjusting rules or results so that groups end up with similar results.

## Equal Treatment in Employment and Business

>[!tldr] In short
> Employers and businesses, public or private, must not treat anyone better or worse for race, sex, or sexual orientation: no firing for being gay, no firing a transgender worker for traits or conduct that would be accepted in the other sex, and no quotas or DEI preferences, with narrow carve-outs ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-carveouts|Merit-rule carve-outs]]). Wide advertising is fine, I would abolish government DEI, and freedom of association covers private life, not jobs and public business.

#### Position
One symmetric rule should govern employment and public business. Hiring, firing, pay, promotion, and treatment of workers or customers by race, sex, sexual orientation, or another immutable or protected trait is a rights violation. That holds for private employers and businesses as well as government, with narrow carve-outs ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-carveouts|Merit-rule carve-outs]]). For government as an employer, the oath-standard exception also applies. Anti-American and anti-constitutional beliefs, namely Nazism, communism, socialism, and Islam, all on the same terms, bar authority roles ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Freedom of Association|Freedom of Association]], [[4. Religion & Secularism#Oath of Office and Perjury|Oath of Office and Perjury]]). Private employers are unchanged by that oath exception. Religion stays a protected trait. Adherence to an excluded ideology ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^excluded-ideologies|Excluded ideologies]]) is a chosen commitment judged by one published test, not an immutable trait. The rule cuts both ways. No one may be fired for being gay. A transgender worker may not be fired for traits or conduct that would be accepted in a worker of the other biological sex, because that is discrimination by sex. No one may be hired or promoted through a quota or DEI preference. Government DEI offices, diversity statements, and mandatory diversity training should be abolished. Voluntary outreach that does not affect selection, such as advertising widely, should stay legal. Sex means biological sex: male, female, and rare intersex conditions. Gender identity is not a legal category and is not a protected trait. Freedom of association covers personal, religious, and club life, not employment and public business ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Freedom of Association|Freedom of Association]]).

**The merit rule.** I hold that merit is the one legitimate basis for hiring and admissions, public and private. Race, sex, ethnicity, and other immutable traits should never be a requirement or an effective quota in any law or policy. Any rule that makes them one is unjust. Judging a person by status rather than by merit or conduct is a status harm. It violates that person's equal negative rights. It is the same assignment of standing by group that the [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^excluded-ideologies|excluded-ideologies test]] condemns. The law ought to bar the use of these traits in selection, and nothing more. The rule would impose no outcome requirement and no quota. A workforce or a class that does not mirror the population would break no law. A rule that is neutral on its face but designed to reach a racial or sex balance is covered too, as Thomas Jefferson High School's test-free admissions were ([[2. Political and Social Character#^anti-merit|Anti-meritocratic beliefs in practice]]). Disparate-impact liability ought to be repealed in every law, not only in employment. A rule that makes unequal group results the offense requires equality of outcome ([[2. Political and Social Character#^equality-of-outcome|From disparity to equal outcomes]]). Intentional discrimination stays unlawful, including any neutral-looking rule designed to reach a racial or sex balance. Current statutes, cases, and recent executive and agency actions are in the box below. ^merit-rule

> [!example]- Current law: disparate-impact liability, Title VII, and admissions
> Under 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(k), and since Griggs v. Duke Power Co. (1971), a neutral employment practice that causes a disparate impact on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, or national origin is unlawful unless the employer shows it is "job related for the position in question and consistent with business necessity." Griggs held that "good intent or absence of discriminatory intent does not redeem" such a practice. The Supreme Court allowed the same liability under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act in Smith v. City of Jackson (2005). It held the liability "cognizable under the Fair Housing Act" in Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project (2015). Executive Order 14281 (2025) made it "the policy of the United States to eliminate the use of disparate-impact liability in all contexts to the maximum degree possible." The Justice Department removed disparate-impact liability from its Title VI regulations in December 2025. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau amended Regulation B in 2026 to state that the Equal Credit Opportunity Act "does not provide that the 'effects test' applies." An executive order and agency rules can be reversed by the next administration. None of them changes the statutes as the Court has read them, so the repeal ought to be written into law. Current law already reaches much of this. Title VII bars employment discrimination by race, color, religion, sex, and national origin at employers with fifteen or more employees. Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard (2023) held race-conscious admissions unlawful at a public university and at a private college receiving federal funds.

**Carve-outs.** Three narrow carve-outs apply. Employers with fewer than fifteen employees, the threshold Title VII already uses, would be outside the merit rule for hiring only. Choosing who joins a household or a shop of a few people is close to personal association. Firing, pay, promotion, and service to the public would stay covered for every employer. No employer of any size may fire anyone for being gay or for their race. Sex or race may be a requirement where it is a genuine, essential part of the role. Examples include casting an actor to portray a real or historical person, or a character of that race or sex. Hiring a model for a specific look; and staffing a locker room with an attendant of the same sex. It never applies to ordinary jobs, and it is never a quota or a balance target. This goes beyond Title VII, which allows a job-requirement exception for sex, religion, and national origin but none for race. Religious groups may hire by their faith for religious roles, meaning clergy and religious leaders, under the ministerial exception ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Religious Freedom|Religious Freedom]]). Ordinary jobs at those groups would stay under the merit rule. This is stricter than current law. 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a) and § 2000e-2(e)(2) let religious organizations and religious schools prefer members of their own faith for all jobs. The law ought to limit that preference to religious roles. ^merit-carveouts

**Tests in selective admissions.** Every selective public school ought to require a standardized test score for admission. So should every selective college or university that receives federal funds, meaning one that ranks applicants for a limited number of seats. The test should be the SAT or ACT, or an equivalent national exam for applicants schooled abroad, alongside auditions or portfolios where the school's work calls for them. Grades vary from school to school. A common test lets an applicant from any school show what they can do. Dartmouth's and MIT's own data, and the Opportunity Insights study across Ivy-Plus colleges, show that scores predict college performance and help identify disadvantaged students who would otherwise be missed ([[2. Political and Social Character#^anti-merit|Anti-meritocratic beliefs in practice]]). A fully private school that takes no public money stays free to set its own admissions. The bar on immutable traits as a requirement or a quota still covers every school. Test-free admissions designed to change the racial mix fall under the merit rule ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-rule|The merit rule]]). ^test-required

> [!question]- "Doesn't a merit rule for private employers take away an owner's freedom to choose?"
> It takes away one choice only: selecting by race, sex, or another immutable trait outside the narrow carve-outs. The owner stays free to hire for skill, for fit with the work and the team, and at whatever pay the two sides agree, and to set every standard of the job. The state does not pick the hire, require any outcome, or audit the mix of the workforce. Freedom of association protects whom people befriend, worship with, or admit to their clubs ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Freedom of Association|Freedom of Association]]). A job is an exchange of labor for pay, and judging the other party to it by status is a status harm the law ought to stop. The small-employer carve-out keeps hiring at the household and the very small shop, where work and personal life overlap, outside the rule. Once someone is hired, firing, pay, and promotion stay covered ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-carveouts|Merit-rule carve-outs]]).

> [!question]- "Doesn't banning private DEI preferences violate freedom of association?"
> Freedom of association protects whom people befriend, worship with, or admit to their clubs. An employer or a business open to the public is not choosing companions. It is judging workers and customers, and judging them by group rather than merit or conduct is the rights violation the rule stops. The same reasoning that bars firing a worker for being gay bars hiring a worker through a racial or sex quota. A rule that banned one and allowed the other would not be neutral.

> [!question]- "Isn't this just existing civil-rights law?"
> Partly. Title VII already bars employment discrimination by race, color, religion, sex, and national origin. The Supreme Court held in Bostock v. Clayton County that firing someone for being gay or transgender is sex discrimination, reasoning that the employer penalizes in one sex what it accepts in the other. The law ought to apply that reasoning through biological sex alone and not recognize gender identity as a legal category. In Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services (2025) the Court held unanimously that majority-group plaintiffs face the same standard as anyone else. The law ought to add an explicit statement that preferences are barred as firmly as exclusion. My rule departs from current law in three places ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-rule|The merit rule]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^merit-carveouts|Merit-rule carve-outs]]): disparate-impact liability is repealed in every law; religious groups may hire by faith only for religious roles, not for every job as 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-1(a) now allows. And race may be a requirement where it is a genuine, essential part of the role, such as casting an actor, which Title VII does not allow.

> [!question]- "Don't diversity trainings reduce bias?"
> Supporters cite lasting effects on knowledge: a meta-analysis of 260 samples found them, while effects on attitudes faded. Effects were larger when training was sustained and combined with other measures (Bezrukova et al., 2016). A network meta-analysis of 492 studies found changes in implicit bias were weak and generally produced trivial changes in behavior (Forscher et al., 2019). Dobbin and Kalev reported that mandatory training often fails to increase managerial diversity and can provoke backlash. My objection is to judging people by group, not to training as such: private voluntary training that does not affect selection stays legal.

> [!question]- "Can employers, landlords, or insurers use group statistics?"
> No. Race carries real patterns that shouldn't be ignored, without that meaning race-based policy: employers, landlords, and insurers judge each person on their own record, not on race or sex averages. The exceptions are real sex-based facts, such as life expectancy in actuarial tables, and fitness tests applied the same way to everyone. Current federal law is stricter on one point: in Los Angeles Department of Water and Power v. Manhart (1978), the Supreme Court barred employers from charging women larger pension contributions based on longer average life expectancy.

> [!question]- "Does merit-only admission apply to private colleges?"
> Yes. Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard (2023) held race-conscious admissions unlawful at Harvard, a private college receiving federal funds, and at the University of North Carolina. The law ought to extend the merit rule to all private colleges, with no race or sex preference. Single-sex colleges may remain, since they exclude by a defined mission rather than ranking applicants by group.

> [!question]- "Isn't wide advertising a form of preference?"
> No. Outreach widens who applies without changing who is selected. Selection itself should turn on merit.


#### Rationale
Judging individuals by group membership conflicts with the individual responsibility at the core of my framework ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]). Applying one rule in both directions keeps it neutral and avoids treating any group's claim as special. The evidence on DEI training is mixed rather than uniformly negative, but I don't rest my case on that evidence: even an effective group-based preference treats individuals unequally.

## Gun Rights

>[!tldr] In short
> Qualified citizens should be able to buy guns, with background checks and red flag laws aimed at stopping bad actors. I'd set a higher bar for concealed carry. Explosives should be banned or limited to construction, and full automatics heavily restricted.

#### Position
Guns should be available for purchase by those who are qualified. Background checks and red flag laws ought to keep bad actors from acquiring firearms. Concealed carry should require a higher level of qualification than open carry. Assault weapons are vaguely defined, and handguns are more dangerous because they are easy to conceal. High-ordnance explosives should be banned or heavily restricted to construction work. Full automatics should be heavily restricted but potentially available to extremely trained or vetted individuals.

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics. They illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Highland Park, 2022:** police had responded in 2019 to a report that the future July 4 parade shooter threatened to kill everyone in his home and seized 16 knives, a dagger and a sword. He was not arrested, no one sought a red-flag order, and he later obtained a firearm ID card with his father's sponsorship. NPR (2022) – [Illinois' gun laws failed to stop the Highland Park shooter from getting weapons](https://www.npr.org/2022/07/08/1110435835/illinois-gun-laws-failed-to-stop-the-highland-park-shooter-from-getting-weapons)

> [!question]- "Do red flag laws violate due process?"
> No, when a judge issues the order. A red flag order removes firearms only on a court finding that the person directly imposes a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on others, the forfeiture threshold in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#General Rules of Forfeiture|Section 1]]. It is never an executive declaration. A judge may issue a short emergency order before the hearing, and the person then has a prompt hearing where the order should have to be justified. The position supports red flag laws as part of qualification, alongside strong support for the right of qualified citizens to own guns.

> [!question]- "Why are handguns treated as more concerning than assault weapons?"
> Assault weapons are vaguely defined, while handguns are more dangerous in practice because they are easy to conceal. In FBI data for 2019, handguns were used in about 91% of firearm murders where the type of gun was known, and rifles of every kind in about 5%. That is why concealed carry should require a higher level of qualification than open carry.


#### Rationale
Self-defense protects sentient rights, and the Second Amendment protects an individual right to keep and bear arms, as held in District of Columbia v. Heller and New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen. Safeguards target the people and weapons most likely to cause harm. RAND's review of the research finds moderate evidence that background checks reduce firearm and total homicides, limited evidence that extreme risk protection (red flag) orders reduce suicides, and inconclusive evidence on their effect on violent crime. It also finds supportive evidence that shall-issue concealed-carry laws increase homicides, which fits the higher bar for concealed carry. In FBI data for 2019, handguns accounted for 6,368 of the 6,977 firearm murders where the type was recorded. Federal law already restricts machine guns heavily, which the position retains with a path for highly vetted individuals.

## Drugs and Vice

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe marijuana should be legal and hard drugs legal for private, personal use, but no drug, marijuana included, may be used in public or while a minor is present, and no one may be impaired around a minor. Large-scale distribution of hard drugs is capital, and sharing a known, tested dose with a consenting adult is not. Prostitution should be legal and regulated, and adults can gamble. Impaired drivers ought to lose their license for ten years to life. The drug war should target cartels with military force, including war with governments that refuse to help.

#### Position
I don't condone widespread use, but marijuana should be legal. Hard drugs should be legal for private, personal use. No drug, marijuana included, may be used in public areas or while a minor is also present in the same private home or area, and no one may be impaired or altered by any drug while in the presence of a minor. Large-scale distribution of hard drugs should be a capital crime ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^drug-rule|Drug Policy]]). Prostitution should be legal, less clandestine, and regulated to prevent exploitation of prostitutes. Consensual, regulated sex work is distinct from trafficking, which should be capital ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Human Trafficking|Human Trafficking]]). Gambling should be regulated only to keep it from minors; adults may gamble. Driving while intoxicated or under the influence of hard drugs ought to result in license revocation for a minimum of ten years, up to permanently. The drug war should focus less on domestic addicts and more on the offensive obliteration of drug cartels by force, treating them as terroristic entities endangering the country's safety. Joint operations with compliant Central and South American governments are best, and if they do not comply, war with those governments is justified to eradicate the cartel problem. Refusal to act against cartels operating against the United States ought to be treated as hostility, consistent with [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Interventionism vs Non-Intervention|Foreign Policy & International Relations]].

> [!question]- "Is legalizing prostitution compatible with preventing exploitation?"
> Legal prostitution brings the trade into the open, where regulation can address exploitation directly. A clandestine trade leaves workers without protection. Regulation targets the rights violation, exploitation, rather than the voluntary exchange.

> [!question]- "Don't countries that legalize prostitution see more human trafficking?"
> The best cross-country evidence says they do. Cho, Dreher, and Neumayer found that countries with legalized prostitution report larger human trafficking inflows, apparently because a larger market outweighs the shift toward legal workers. Prostitution should stay legal because consenting adults may trade, and I would answer the trafficking risk directly: every business should be strictly licensed, every worker's age, identity, and consent should be verified, and trafficking should be a capital crime ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Human Trafficking|Human Trafficking]]).


#### Rationale
Adult choices that harm only the chooser fall outside the state's coercive role, while driving impaired endangers others and should be punished severely. Harsher DUI sanctions measurably reduce repeat offending (Washington State data show drops of up to 17% at the 0.08 threshold); the ten-years-to-life term is a protective judgment beyond what studies have tested. Cartels murder and corrupt on a scale that threatens national security, and the United States designated several cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations in 2025. The Supreme Court's decision in Murphy v. NCAA allowed states to legalize sports betting. Related positions are set out in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drug Policy|Drug Policy]].

### Drug Policy

>[!tldr] In short
> Using drugs privately should be legal, but not while a minor is present or while impaired around a minor. Using them in public should be banned, and large-scale distribution of hard drugs is a capital crime. Smaller transfers fall on lower tiers. Adults obtain hard drugs only through licensed clinical channels or by producing their own, never from a shop. I would treat cartels as terrorists and destroy them with military force.

#### Position
Marijuana should be legal, and other drugs legal for private, personal use. No drug may be used while a minor is also present in the same private home or area, and no one may be impaired or altered by a drug while in the presence of a minor. Drug use in public areas should be prohibited. The state ought not to pursue prohibition. Cartels should be designated as terroristic national-security threats and destroyed with military force. Enforcement resources ought to move away from private users and toward the organizations that traffic drugs through violence. Large-scale distribution of hard drugs should be a capital crime. Distribution means any transfer of a hard drug to another person: sale, gift, sharing, or carrying it for someone else. Large-scale means a quantity at or above a threshold fixed in statute for each drug, or organizing or leading a distribution operation. I believe the thresholds should follow the top federal tier, 21 U.S.C. § 841(b)(1)(A): for example, 1 kilogram of a heroin mixture, 5 kilograms of a cocaine mixture, or 400 grams of a fentanyl mixture. Organizing or leading should follow 21 U.S.C. § 848: a series of drug felonies by someone who organizes or manages five or more others and draws substantial income from it. Proven to the capital evidentiary standard, the sentence is death, the same as for the other capital crimes ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]). The ground is the same as for every capital act that knowingly imposes an extreme risk of death: a trafficker puts doses of unknown strength, often cut with fentanyl, into the hands of many people who cannot know what they are taking, at a scale where deaths are close to certain. Cartel violence along the supply chain makes the harm larger, but it is not the reason. Below that scale the tiers step down. Selling or giving another person an untested or unknown hard drug is two marks, because it still imposes a severe, non-consensual risk. Selling a known, tested dose to an adult is one mark, because there should be no commercial market. Sharing a known, tested dose with a consenting adult, for no profit, is a misdemeanor punished by an income-scaled fine without a mark. A known, tested dose is one whose identity and strength have been verified, by a clinical label or a drug-checking test, and disclosed to the recipient. Fentanyl and its analogues never count as a known, tested dose outside clinical supply. Knowingly passing off fentanyl as another drug stays capital at any quantity, as knowingly imposing an extreme risk of death ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Quick Reference: Crimes and Outcomes|Crimes and Outcomes]]). Giving or selling any hard drug to a minor is capital at any quantity. There should be no commercial market for hard drugs and no licensed shops or retail sellers. Hard-drug use is a personal endeavor, and an adult may obtain hard drugs lawfully in only two ways. The first is licensed clinical supply, through medical or therapeutic channels under clinical supervision, never a storefront; the most lethal synthetics, such as fentanyl and its analogues, are available only this way. The second is personal production for one's own use. Producing more than a personal quantity counts as distribution. Legal, regulated marijuana and prescribed clinical psychedelic therapy in a licensed setting are not covered, and private, personal use and possession stay legal. ^drug-rule

U.S. Department of State (2025) – [Designation of International Cartels](https://www.state.gov/designation-of-international-cartels)

> [!question]- "Does legalizing private use contradict a hardline stance on crime?"
> No. Private drug use by an adult does not by itself violate the sentient rights of others. Prohibition has failed to stop it, and it has fed the black market that funds cartels. The hardline stance is aimed at rights violators. Cartels that murder, traffic, and corrupt governments should be treated as terrorist enemies. Public use that imposes on others remains banned, as do use and impairment around minors. Legal private use narrows enforcement to where harm to others actually occurs.

> [!question]- "Why use the military against cartels?"
> Cartels are designated as terroristic national-security threats: they murder, traffic, and corrupt governments. They are rights violators at scale, and organized groups that systematically violate rights may be met with decisive force ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]). In Mexico, drug-related violence rose substantially after close elections of mayors from the party leading the crackdown, as rival traffickers fought over territory the crackdowns had weakened (Dell, 2015). That is a finding about Mexican state capacity: cartels are embedded in Mexico, and the Mexican state cannot govern those territories effectively. It has no bearing on U.S. firepower if the United States gets involved.

> [!question]- "Didn't Oregon's decriminalization raise overdose deaths?"
> Critics cite one study of Oregon's Measure 110 that estimated a 23% rise in unintentional overdose deaths, about 182 more deaths in 2021. Two JAMA studies found no significant effect once fentanyl's spread is accounted for. Either way, the rights line stands: private adult use harms no one else. The law ought to keep sale capital and keep the bans on public use and on use or impairment around minors.

> [!question]- "Why legalize private drug use rather than only decriminalize it?"
> Decriminalization would still treat private use as an offense, handled by fines or administrative penalties, and private use by an adult does not by itself violate anyone else's rights. Legal private use puts the line where harm to others begins: in public, around a minor, behind the wheel, and at the point of sale. Marijuana goes one step further, with a legal, regulated market that takes that trade away from cartels. For other drugs there is no commercial market. Supply is clinical or personal, and any other transfer should be capital ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#^drug-transfer|Drug Policy]]).

> [!question]- "If using hard drugs is legal, how can large-scale distribution be capital?"
> Use and distribution are different acts. Use risks only the user's own life, and that is his to risk. Large-scale distribution knowingly imposes an extreme risk of death on many people, the same ground that makes any act knowingly imposing an extreme risk of death capital: a trafficker's buyers cannot know the dose or whether it is cut with fentanyl. Sharing a known, tested dose with a consenting adult is different, because the recipient knows the risk he accepts, so it is not capital. The right to use is real, not a legal fiction, because the United States ought to leave two lawful sources open: licensed clinical supply under medical supervision, and personal production for one's own use. It ought not to license commercial sellers or shops. A retail market would turn a personal choice into a promoted trade and normalize drug culture. Supervised medical heroin in Zurich coincided with new regular heroin users falling from about 850 a year to about 150, while British Columbia's take-home prescribing was followed by more opioid-poisoning hospitalizations, so supply stays supervised. Prohibiting use is not the goal. It fails, and it reaches an act that harms no one else. ^drug-transfer

> [!question]- "Why is the capital line drawn at large-scale distribution, and what would move it?"
> **Where it comes from.** Axiom 6 counts directly imposing a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on another person as crossing the forfeiture threshold, and knowingly imposing an extreme risk of death sits on the capital tier with murder ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#General Rules of Forfeiture|General Rules of Forfeiture]]; [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Quick Reference: Crimes and Outcomes|Crimes and Outcomes]]). A trafficker's supply is that risk at scale: buyers cannot know the dose or the cut, and across thousands of doses deaths are close to certain. Consent covers only a risk a person can know, so a buyer can't consent to a cut no one told him about. Below that scale the risk reaches fewer people, and with a known, tested dose the recipient accepts a known risk, so the tiers step down: two marks for an untested or unknown drug, one mark for selling a known, tested dose, and a fine for sharing one. Use stays legal because a risk to oneself violates no one's rights.
>
> **The rivals.**
> - Prohibiting use punishes an act that harms no one else, and prohibition has fed the cartel market.
> - A licensed commercial market turns a personal choice into a promoted trade. Supervised supply has the better record (Zurich against British Columbia, above).
> - Ordinary prison terms for traffickers would punish this one kind of extreme risk of death more lightly than every other kind, with no principle for the difference.
> - Death for every transfer would treat a friend who shares a known, tested dose with a consenting adult like a trafficker, though the friend imposes no non-consensual risk.
>
> **What would move it.** The line follows the risk of death, so the list of hard drugs should follow the evidence. Cannabis is already off it. A drug should come off if evidence showed that handing it to someone no longer carries an extreme risk of death, and a new drug should go on when evidence shows it does. The list and the large-scale quantity thresholds should be fixed in statute and revised by an expert board on the best available evidence, as the sentience classes are, with a change applying only to acts after it takes effect. Deterrence studies would not move the line, because the sentence rests on desert, incapacitation, and forfeiture, not on deterrence ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Deterrence and the Justification for Capital Punishment|Deterrence and the Justification for Capital Punishment]]).


#### Rationale
I limit state coercion to protecting the rights of others, and private consumption falls outside that line while public use spills onto others. Portugal decriminalized the personal possession of all drugs in 2001, replacing criminal penalties with administrative responses, and the European drug agency's country reports track that model. I go one step further for private use, making it legal rather than an administrative offense, and keep the bans on public use, use around minors, and sale. On cartels, the United States designated several cartels and transnational gangs, including the Sinaloa Cartel and Cartel de Jalisco Nueva Generación, as Foreign Terrorist Organizations in February 2025, following a January 2025 executive order. That designation treats cartels as security threats rather than ordinary criminal enterprises, which matches my position.

> [!info]- What is the difference between decriminalization and legalization?
> Decriminalization removes criminal penalties for using or possessing drugs, though use can still be restricted or handled through civil or administrative measures. Legalization makes production and sale lawful and regulated. My position is that marijuana should be legal and other drugs should be legal for private, personal use, obtained only through licensed clinical supply or personal production, without a commercial market or shops. Use should be barred in public and while a minor is present in the same private home or area, and no one should be impaired or altered by a drug in the presence of a minor.

## Patriotism and National Identity

>[!tldr] In short
> I believe a cohesive, patriotic culture is best, and multiculturalism works only with shared values. I see some immigrant cultures as compatible and others not, and being Muslim creates a rebuttable presumption of failing the values test. History should be taught straight, and America is exceptional because of liberty, rights, and opportunity.

#### Position
I believe a patriotic nation that is culturally cohesive in its values is the best result for cohesion. Multiculturalism can work only if the cultures share important common values; many legally immigrated Mexican and South American families are compatible because of shared family and religious values. I judge Islamic and third-world African cultures incompatible with Western society, clashing with values such as human rights and civic responsibility; individual immigrants should be judged through values vetting. History should be taught unmodified and objective. American exceptionalism rests not on America as such but on what America stands for: liberty, freedom, rights, and great financial opportunity.

> [!question]- "Does cultural compatibility contradict civic nationalism?"
> Civic nationalism unites people around shared values, and cultural compatibility is the condition for sharing them. Cultures that hold the same core values integrate. Those that clash with them do not. The test is values, which is why compatible immigrant communities are welcomed and Muslim applicants start with the burden of proving compatibility.


#### Rationale
A high-trust society depends on shared norms, and cohesion is easier where citizens hold common values. Teaching history without modification gives citizens an accurate basis for national identity. Grounding exceptionalism in principles rather than in nationality alone makes it a standard the country should live up to. Robert Putnam's 2007 study found that in the short run, residents of more ethnically diverse U.S. neighborhoods showed lower trust, even of their own group, and less community cooperation. He also found that successful immigrant societies overcome this by building shared identities, which is the role shared values play here.

## Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos

>[!tldr] In short
> I hold that deliberately killing a sentient animal outside of self-defense is murder, graded by the animal's sentience: high-sentience animals are on the capital track, and killing lower-sentience animals is still punishable and counts as marks against the offender. Hunting is murder, an egregious rights violation on the capital track, and animal agriculture should be abolished. Pets can be kept if their rights are respected, commercial breeding mills should be banned with animal agriculture, and killing a high-sentience pet for convenience is murder. Lethal pest control should be allowed only against an actual or imminent attack or a clear disease or rights threat - including a statutory infestation in a dwelling, food store, or medical facility - with the least-harmful method required. Preventive area-wide extermination of non-infesting high-sentience wildlife is not allowed. Zoos should become sanctuaries where non-threatening predators eat lab-grown meat. Herbivorization methods are under study. Animal cruelty is graded by severity: the most extreme, violent cruelty that causes lasting injury is on the capital track, and lesser cruelty is tracked and counts toward the repeat-offender threshold.

#### Position
I hold that sentient animals within U.S. borders have negative rights, and the government has a duty to protect them. Animals are graded into classes by sentience ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]). Deliberately killing a high-sentience animal, the capital class, is murder, treated the same as in the human context: it goes on the capital track, and the same forfeitures that apply to human murder apply here. Killing a lower-sentience animal is not capital, but it is still punishable and counts as marks against the offender as a lesser offense. Killing an animal in self-defense or defense of rights is not illegal. That covers an actual or imminent attack, or a clear disease or rights threat, including a statutory infestation: a high-class pest population occupying a dwelling, food store, or medical facility in a way that foreseeably spreads pathogens or destroys necessary stores. Hunger or need is not self-defense; only killing outside those bounds is murder. Rats, mice, and pigeons stay in the high class; pest status does not demote them ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]). Lethal infestation control should use the least-harmful method that works; preventive area-wide extermination of high-class animals that are not occupying a qualifying site remains murder. I consider hunting murder: it is an egregious rights violation and belongs on the capital track. Fishing that kills sentient animals is classified the same way.
 Animal agriculture should be abolished immediately, and lab-grown meat is the goal and the replacement, scaling up over time ([[10. Bioethics, Progress, & Technology#^lab-meat|Animal Rights and Sentience Technology]]). Companion animals may be kept where their rights are respected. Zoos ought to operate as sanctuaries: animals are kept for their own protection, not for display at their expense, and non-threatening predators held in sanctuary are fed lab-grown meat for the rest of their lives. Confined predators that remain violent and cannot be helped fall under the execution rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]].

**My view:** Nature needs to be civilized more than it needs to be "protected". In its current state, quadrillions of animals die every year, often painfully, and conservation often gives no consideration to the welfare of the animals living inside it. Individual welfare should come before "nature".

> [!question]- "Isn't hunting necessary to control herbivore populations once predators are removed?"
> Removing predators does raise the question of herbivore populations, and the answer cannot be to kill the innocent animals I protect. Once predators are removed, herbivore populations should be controlled. Predator removal should start with odd-order predators to avoid a trophic cascade that could harm the herbivores below them. Nature should be herbivorized through bioengineering, with outcomes scientifically tested: infertility to stop predator species from breeding, bringing the environment fully under human dominion so wilderness no longer exists, and keeping animals in sanctuaries. Many of these methods are still in their infancy and need to be studied. Herbivorizing nature and reducing predation is central to my framework, because predation and sentient rights are so intertwined.

> [!question]- "What herbivorization methods are being proposed?"
> Many of these methods are still in their infancy and need to be studied. Methods proposed for study include:
> - Selective breeding of animals over generations for the ability to live on plants.
> - CRISPR gene drives to herbivorize predators.
> - Tunable gene drives that regulate fertility across species.
> - Single-sex gene drives, so predator species die out painlessly.
> - Supplementary feeding of herbivores to reduce competition.
> - Plants engineered so herbivores cannot overgraze them.
> - Sterilization.
> - Plant-based diets for predators.
> - A model sanctuary for wild animals.
> - AI monitoring of wild animals.
>
> No outside proposal has laid out an order for phasing predators out, so the odd-order predator approach above stands as the plan.

> [!question]- "What works now, and what waits on research?"
> **Works now:**
> - Ending the breeding of predators held under human control, so captive lines end. This needs a law, not new science.
> - Sanctuary or confinement for non-threatening predators, fed without killing other animals.
> - Fertility control for herbivores, so removing predators does not lead to overpopulation. EPA has registered the GonaCon immunocontraceptive vaccine for white-tailed deer (2009), for wild and feral horses and burros (2013), and for prairie dogs. PZP held the Assateague horses at zero population growth within 2 years ([[#Evidence: Herbivorization|Evidence: Herbivorization]]).
>
> **Waits on research:**
> - Fertility control for wild predators. None of the registered wildlife contraceptives is for a predator.
> - Gene drives, including herbivorizing and single-sex drives. No gene drive has been released into the wild in any vertebrate, and the two most advanced vertebrate systems, both in house mice, have not reached full proof of concept in the laboratory.
> - Selective breeding of predators for plant diets, and lab-grown meat at the scale needed to feed confined predators.
>
> The duty uses only tools that pass testing. A method that never passes is never used, and the duty is carried out with the tools that work, within the bound that no step causes more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]).

> [!question]- "What about ecosystem collapse, omnivores, and cats?"
> - **Ecosystem collapse.** I take the hazard seriously, and the program is built around it. Predators feed scavengers, move nutrients, and can slow disease, and herbivores without predators can overshoot their range. So herbivore numbers are held by fertility control before predators are removed in an area, each loss is monitored, and no step may cause more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents. Nature is still not my standard: where it can be reordered without breaking that rule, it should be. The risks and the stop rule are set out in the next question.
> - **Omnivory.** Predation is defined by the act, not the species label. Herbivores that kill animals, such as deer eating nestling birds, fall under the same duty and the same herbivorization methods, weighted by the sentience of the animals they kill.
> - **Cats.** Obligate carnivores need specific nutrients, such as taurine, that synthetic or plant-based diets can supply. Each companion animal's diet is set by veterinary judgment of its health, and sentience science decides which prey species count.

> [!question]- "Has anything on this scale been done, and what could go wrong?"
> Not on this scale, and I don't pretend otherwise. The largest federal wildlife fertility-control program, the Bureau of Land Management's for wild horses and burros, treated 1,038 mares in fiscal 2024 and 921 in fiscal 2025, with PZP and GonaCon. No gene drive has been released into the wild in any vertebrate. The program starts from tested tools used at small scale and grows only as they prove out.
>
> **Risks I accept and watch:**
> - **Scavengers and nutrient cycling.** Predator kills feed ravens, eagles, coyotes, bears, and others, and return nutrients to the ground. In Yellowstone, wolf kills became a steady food supply for scavengers through the winter. Fewer kills mean less carrion.
> - **Disease.** Predators can take sick animals first. A modeling study of chronic wasting disease in deer found that selective predation by wolves could slow the disease. Without predators, disease has to be found by monitoring and handled by veterinary care.
> - **Herbivore overshoot.** Yellowstone's elk rose from about 3,000 to about 22,000 after culling stopped and before wolves returned ([[#Case Study: Yellowstone Wolves|Yellowstone Wolves]]). That is why herbivore fertility control comes first.
>
> **The stop rule.** No step may cause more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]). The day-one tools stay day-one: no breeding of captive predators, and sanctuary for non-threatening predators. Removal in the wild and every engineered method should start in pilot areas, with herbivore fertility control already working there, and with herbivore condition, disease, scavengers, and vegetation monitored and reported every year. A step that breaks the stop rule should be halted, and reversed where it can be, before the program goes further.
> - Bureau of Land Management – [Wild Horse and Burro Program Data: Fertility Control Treatments](https://www.blm.gov/programs/wild-horse-and-burro/about-the-program/program-data)
> - Wilmers et al., Journal of Animal Ecology – [Trophic facilitation by introduced top predators: grey wolf subsidies to scavengers in Yellowstone National Park (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2656.2003.00766.x)
> - Wild et al., Journal of Wildlife Diseases – [The role of predation in disease control: a comparison of selective and nonselective removal on prion disease dynamics in deer (2011)](https://doi.org/10.7589/0090-3558-47.1.78)

> [!question]- "What is the long-term future of predator species?"
> The duty to stop predation should apply instantly. Within U.S. borders, predation is a rights violation that the government should stop now, not someday ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]], [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]).
>
> **Methods under the day-one duty:** Within U.S. borders the duty to stop predation should be immediate. Preferred tools, not a soft delay of the duty: stop breeding predators held under human control. Let captive predator lines end rather than restock them; and pursue herbivorization where the science works, in line with gene-drive methods under study and Pearce's proposal of "rewriting the vertebrate genome" (see [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Evidence: Herbivorization|Evidence: Herbivorization]]). Those are how the duty is carried out — not a gradual aside that treats the ban as optional.

> [!question]- "What about people who hunt or fish to feed their families?"
> Killing a sentient animal for food is murder, graded by sentience. Self-defense against an immediate attack remains, as for humans ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Section 1]]). But a predator that can be separated or contained may not be killed for being dangerous by nature. Separation and containment come first. I allow no subsistence exception, and no Indigenous exception whatsoever.

> [!question]- "Don't tribes have treaty rights to hunt and fish?"
> Many do, and the Supreme Court enforces them: in *Herrera v. Wyoming* (2019) it held that an 1868 treaty hunting right survived Wyoming's statehood. But Congress can abrogate an Indian treaty right by a clear statement (*Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock*, *Menominee Tribe v. United States*). The law ought to do exactly that, expressly, for every right to hunt or fish sentient animals. There is no Indigenous exception, because the animal killed is the same animal whoever kills it.
> - Supreme Court of the United States – [Herrera v. Wyoming (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-532_q86b.pdf)
> - Cornell LII – [Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock, 187 U.S. 553 (1903)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/187/553)
> - Cornell LII – [Menominee Tribe v. United States, 391 U.S. 404 (1968)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/391/404)

> [!question]- "Tens of millions of Americans hunt or fish. Are you putting them all on the capital track?"
> In 2022, 14.4 million Americans hunted and 39.9 million fished. Every one of them who kept killing high-sentience animals after such a ban took effect would commit murder and should be treated as a murderer. No one should be prosecuted for a hunt or a catch before the ban ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). The law should be published, licenses should end on day one, and the line should be clear, so continuing would be a choice made in full knowledge. The capital evidentiary standard still applies: no one should be executed on a game warden's word, only on objective evidence such as video and physical evidence. Critics warn that a law this far from current practice invites juries to refuse to convict, as English juries did under the Bloody Code. The state ought to track acquittal rates in these cases as it does at three marks ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]]). Widespread killing does not make the killing anything less than murder. It makes enforcement more urgent.
> - U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service – [2022 National Survey of Fishing, Hunting, and Wildlife-Associated Recreation](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Final_2022-National-Survey_101223-accessible-single-page.pdf)

> [!question]- "How is animal cruelty punished?"
> Under [[#Animal Cruelty Law]]: cruelty to animals in the applicable sentience classes should be capital if it causes [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|lasting injury]] and otherwise should be grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor). Below that, cruelty should be non-capital but tracked and should count toward the repeat-offender threshold. Companion animals should be on the same tiers.

> [!question]- "What about the trade in wild and exotic animals?"
> Capturing wild animals to sell them, and the commercial trade in wild-caught animals, fall under the same ban as commercial breeding mills: they treat sentient beings as stock. Exotic-pet owners are in the same category as any other pet owner: as long as the animals are safe, they may foster and keep them. Anyone who takes an ownerless or wild animal under their responsibility may adopt it and becomes its guardian ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). Most infectious diseases in people can spread from animals, so the trade also carries a public-health cost.

> [!question]- "Can people keep pets, and what about commercial breeding?"
> Companion animals may be kept where their rights are respected. Commercial breeding mills fall under the animal-agriculture ban: I would ban them outright. Killing a high-sentience companion animal for convenience is murder, and the cruelty tiers apply to companion animals the same as to any other sentient animal. Euthanasia is not murder only when it is for the animal's own sake, never for convenience, on a veterinarian's best-interest finding, and carried out by a veterinary professional, even at home ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]).
>
> > [!note]- No wind-down
> > There is no lag here: commercial breeding mills should shut down immediately along with animal agriculture, and the animals in them should be adopted out where possible, after the same vetting as farm animals, and the rest go to sanctuary ([[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]).

> [!question]- "Is pest control allowed?"
> Lethal control of sentient pests should be allowed only to stop an actual or imminent attack on persons or animals, or a clear disease or rights threat. A clear disease or rights threat includes a statutory **infestation**: when a high-class pest population occupies a dwelling, food store, or medical facility in a way that foreseeably spreads pathogens or destroys necessary stores, lethal control of that occupying population is lawful self-defense / defense of rights ([[Glossary Index#^g-infestation|Infestation (pest)]]; [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). Separation, exclusion, and other non-lethal methods come first where they will work. When lethal force is used, the least-harmful method that works should be required, and control is graded by sentience class like every other killing of a sentient animal. Rats, mice, pigeons, and every other high-class pest stay in the high class - pest status does not demote them. Qualifying infestation control is not the banned "preventive extermination." What stays banned is preventive area-wide extermination of high-sentience animals that are not occupying a qualifying site: outdoor kill campaigns, park or field sweeps, and island-wide rodent eradication dressed up as pest control are murder when the animals are high-class. Hunger, nuisance alone, or fear that pests might arrive later is not self-defense and is not an infestation.

> [!question]- "What counts as an infestation?"
> Occupation of a **dwelling**, a **food store**, or a **medical facility** by a high-class pest population in a way that foreseeably spreads pathogens or destroys necessary stores. Those three sites only. A food store means a structure used to store food for persons or animals under care (a pantry, warehouse, or similar). Open fields, pastures, parks, and general agricultural barns that are not food stores do not qualify. A passing animal, or animals outdoors that are not occupying a qualifying site, do not qualify. Once the test is met, lethal control of the occupying population at that site is lawful defense of rights under the rules above. It is not a warrant to clear the neighborhood.


#### Rationale

Fishing, companion animals, breeding mills, euthanasia, pest control, zoos, and the cruelty tiers are set out in the position and the questions above, and cruelty in full in [[#Animal Cruelty Law]].

Rights rest on sentience, not species ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]). Killing a sentient animal for sport or food violates its rights in the same way I treat any other killing of an innocent. So the same murder rules, self-defense exception, and forfeitures apply as in the human context. Grading by sentience sets the severity of the offense. I treat predation as a rights violation even when the predator lacks agency, and within U.S. borders the government intervenes by placing non-threatening predators in sanctuary or confinement and feeding them lab-grown meat ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]). Sanctuary works for predators because their violence can be subdued: a predator can be contained, separated from other animals, and fed. A violent human, short of permanent sedation, requires far more maintenance and is much harder to manage, which is why violent humans face the forfeiture tracks rather than sanctuary. Herbivore populations and sanctuary lands receive priority protection ([[9. Energy & Environment#Regulation and Conservation|Energy & Environment]]). About 15.9 million people held paid hunting licenses in 2024, according to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, so ending hunting is a large change. The evidence for herbivorization is set out below.

#### Evidence: Herbivorization

Jeff McMahan argues that "we have reason to desire the extinction of all carnivorous species," provided herbivorous species replace them "without ecological upheaval involving more harm than would be prevented." David Pearce proposes "reprogramming or phasing out" predators. He writes that the abolitionist project "entails ecosystem redesign, cross-species immunocontraception, marine nanorobots, rewriting the vertebrate genome," and names "the extinction - or genetically-driven behavioural modification - of members of the cat family" as its most controversial case. Removing predators has already helped native species on islands. Across 251 eradications of invasive mammals on 181 islands, 236 native species benefited and 7 were harmed, and four threatened species improved their IUCN category while none got worse (Jones et al. 2016). New Zealand's Predator Free 2050 program, which targets possums, rats, and mustelids, had over 2.5 million hectares under sustained suppression and 122,110 hectares free of all three target groups and defended against reinvasion as of June 2025. Two of its seven 2025 goals were achieved. Contraception works on single herds but lowers numbers slowly. PZP immunocontraception of the Assateague horses was 92–100% effective each year and reached zero population growth within 2 years, but the herd fell only 22.8% (175 to 135) by year 11 because treated mares lived longer. No gene drive has been released into the wild in any vertebrate. The two most advanced vertebrate systems, both in house mice, have not yet reached full proof of concept in the laboratory, according to the 2026 Gene Drive Monitor survey. Without population control, removing predators shifts herbivores to food limitation: they increase, overgraze, and vegetation declines (Hairston, Smith & Slobodkin 1960). That is why herbivore control goes together with predator removal.

##### Case Study: Yellowstone Wolves
Wolves were eradicated from Yellowstone by the 1920s. Park managers held elk to about 3,000 by culling until 1969; after culling stopped, elk peaked at about 22,000 in 1994, and between 1995 and 1997, 41 wolves were reintroduced. A 21-year study of 113 aspen stands found that elk density was 8.6 to 9.5 times stronger than wolf predation risk as a driver of browsing, and about 20 to 28 times stronger for young aspen growing out of elk reach. Herbivore numbers, not elk's fear of wolves, drove both the damage and the recovery (Brice et al. 2025). Recovery took more than a decade: browsing did not begin to decline until 2008. Lower elk numbers alone did not restore willows. In a 20-year experiment, willows reached the heights of a restored community only where simulated beaver dams were present and browsing was reduced. A reorganized community of large herbivores, with bison as a relatively invulnerable prey species, suppressed willow growth (Hobbs et al. 2024). Yellowstone supports controlling the whole herbivore system: every herbivore species, not one, along with the conditions plants need to recover.

## Animal Cruelty Law

>[!tldr] In short
> I define animal cruelty as extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment. Any of these against a human or a higher-sentience animal should be capital if it causes lasting injury, even against a single animal, and otherwise is grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor). Against lower-sentience animals it counts as marks. It counts toward the repeat-offender threshold, and ritual slaughter ought to be banned with no religious exemption. I would ban all sexual acts with sentient animals, forced or not, because animals cannot consent and allowing any form would leave a gray area for abuse. Non-forced acts carry one mark, fixed-term labor with mandatory rehabilitation, an [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Income-Scaled Fines|income-and-wealth day fine]] that goes first to the animal's care, and a ban on keeping animals.

#### Position
I hold that cruelty to a sentient animal is a rights violation and should be a crime. Animal cruelty is any of the following: 1. extreme violence; 2. intentional neglect, a chosen pattern of keeping it up; 3. beating without drawing blood; 4. psychological abuse, withholding food, water, or shelter, not out of neglect but as deliberate punishment. Beating means force beyond ordinary discipline: a closed fist, an object, or repeated strikes. A swat on the rear as ordinary discipline is not beating. If any of these acts is done to a human or to an animal in the higher sentience tier, it should be capital if it causes [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|lasting injury]], physical or psychological; otherwise it falls in the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|grave tier]]: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Marks are uniform across victims: the same act carries the same marks whether the victim is a person or a higher-sentience animal. Lesser cruelty, harm to a higher-sentience animal outside the four acts and without lasting injury, such as a single strike causing pain or abandonment, is two marks; cruelty to a lower-sentience animal is one mark. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is not capital; it counts as marks against the offender. The same intent test that governs crimes against humans applies: what matters is whether the offender acted voluntarily and knew what they were doing. Moral agency is irrelevant to whether a violation occurred, but where agency is absent, no forfeiture-based punishment follows. The same sentience-based classes used for killing animals apply. Cruelty to high-sentience animals in the capital class should be capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise should be grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor). Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is a lesser offense that counts as marks against the offender. Animal cruelty counts toward the repeat-offender threshold, with its weight depending on severity. Extremely severe, violent cruelty that causes lasting injury should be capital even when only one animal is harmed, just as violent torture of a single human should be capital; it does not require large numbers. Torturing large numbers of animals should likewise be capital outright, with no lesser classification. Mass neglect, meaning neglect of multiple animals at once or a sustained pattern, should be capital when it causes lasting injury, physical or psychological, and otherwise grave abuse (three marks). Intentional neglect of a single higher-sentience animal without lasting injury is grave abuse (three marks), the same as for a person, and neglect of a lower-sentience animal counts as marks. Ritual slaughter should be banned, with no religious exemption. Euthanasia that meets the living-animal rule in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]] is not cruelty. Euthanasia that breaks those conditions is graded on a sliding scale. A genuine case of real suffering where the guardian skips the vet, the convenience being the vet trip and not the killing, carries a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty. A cover case with no real suffering is cruelty under these tiers, capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); clear intent to harm is murder.

Animals cannot consent to sexual activity. I would ban all sexual acts with sentient animals. The ban itself makes no distinction between forced and non-forced acts. Forced acts are graded under the cruelty tiers. Non-forced acts carry a separate, lesser penalty. The ban follows from the consent principle in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Age of Consent|Age of Consent]].

> [!question]- "Shouldn't cruelty to animals be punished less severely than violence against people?"
> Sentient animals hold the same negative rights as people, so the same cruelty acts should get the same penalties against a higher-sentience animal as against a human: they should be capital if they cause lasting injury, and grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor) otherwise. Severity should be graded by the animal's sentience, not by species membership.

> [!question]- "Is soring a show horse animal cruelty?"
> Yes. Deliberately injuring a horse's legs or hooves so it steps higher in the ring is violence against a sentient animal for a ribbon, and it is graded under the same cruelty tiers as any other abuse. Inspection ought to be done by inspectors independent of the shows and their owners, never by people the industry appoints.

> [!question]- "How is animal cruelty punished?"
> Any of the four cruelty acts (extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment) against a higher-sentience animal should be capital if it causes lasting injury, even when only one animal is harmed, the same as against a human. Without lasting injury it should fall in the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|grave tier]]: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is non-capital but tracked in the system, and cruelty convictions count toward the repeat-offender threshold. This matches the murder rules in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]] and the threshold in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]], and replaces treatment of animal cruelty as an ordinary crime. Companion animals are included on the same tiers ([[#Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos]]).

> [!question]- "Why ban it if the animal isn't forced?"
> Animals cannot consent. With non-consenting people, the main problem is psychological damage, which can be severe, especially for the young. That harm dynamic is not exactly the same with animals. Even though animals can't consent, if they are not being sexualized in an abusive manner, the sexual activity itself does not create psychological danger to the animal, and sexual activity is a strong biological drive for them. So there is a difference between forced bestiality, where someone forces an animal, penetrative or otherwise, and cases where someone lets an animal do something to them. It is not as clear-cut as human sexual consent. I don't see a problem with the second category in itself. But societally, if any form were allowed, it would be too much of a gray area for people to abuse animals. So the ban is flat and covers all sexual acts with sentient animals.

> [!note]- Penalties
> Forced sexual acts with sentient animals should be treated as cruelty and should be graded under the cruelty tiers above, by severity and sentience class. Non-forced acts should still be banned and should carry a separate penalty, lighter than for forced acts, which should go through the cruelty tiers: one mark. A fixed-term labor sentence with mandatory rehabilitation and a known end date; an [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Income-Scaled Fines|income-and-wealth day fine]], which should go first to the animal's care, then to the victim restitution fund, and never to the seizing agency; and a ban on keeping animals. Fines can be substantial.
> Bestiality and neglect are separate harms: the sex act is one violation of the animal's dignity, and neglect is a failure of care on top of it. Marks should stack only when both are charged, so the same suffering is not punished twice. Neglect should be charged separately only when it is a distinct failure of care, not the harm of the act itself. Forced bestiality should follow the same rule: the force should be graded under the cruelty tiers (capital with lasting injury, otherwise grave abuse, three marks), and distinct neglect should stack on top.


#### Rationale

The cruelty tiers, marks, and the forced versus non-forced sexual-act penalties are set out in the position, the questions above, and the penalties note.

Sentient animals hold negative rights within U.S. borders, so deliberately inflicting suffering on one is an outward rights violation, which is what my criminal law would punish ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]). Because those rights are the same rights humans hold, the same cruelty acts that should be capital against a human should be capital against a higher-sentience animal, with severity graded by sentience under [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]. Religious practice does not exempt anyone from rights protections ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Ritual Slaughter and Animal Sacrifice|Religion & Secularism]]). Federal law already criminalizes certain acts of animal crushing and torture under the Preventing Animal Cruelty and Torture Act (18 U.S.C. § 48).
