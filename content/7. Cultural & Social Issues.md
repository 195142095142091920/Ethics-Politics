## Core Principles
Traditional nuclear family preference, free speech absolutism (with narrow exceptions), meritocracy, cultural compatibility for cohesion, and personal responsibility. Strong skepticism of identity politics, mandates, and progressive social engineering.

## Family and Gender

#### Position
Marriage and family have multiple definitions depending on one's culture and religion. The healthiest family is something very close to the nuclear family: a mother and father, married and loyal to one another, with two to three children. Divorce is allowed. Child custody does not default to either parent; if the parents cannot reach a resolution, a neutral court arbitrates the dispute, informed by the child's privately expressed preference. Traditional roles, with a stronger male working while a female raises children, cares for the home, and supports the working man, are judged superior, but they are not mandated in any way, and people ultimately live as they decide.

> [!question]- "Does favoring traditional roles conflict with individual freedom?"
> No. The preference is a judgment about what constitutes the best-functioning society, not a rule imposed by law. Individuals remain free to structure their families and roles as they choose.

#### Key Elements
- Nuclear family (married mother and father, two to three children) as the healthiest structure.
- Divorce allowed.
- No custody default; a neutral court decides when parents disagree, considering the child's private preference.
- Traditional gender roles judged superior but never mandated.
- People free to live as they decide.

#### Rationale
The nuclear family is treated as the structure best suited to raising children, and cohesive families support a high-trust society. A neutral custody standard avoids presuming either parent is more fit. Keeping traditional roles voluntary respects the framework's commitment to individual liberty.

>[!tldr]- Summary
> The healthiest family is close to the traditional nuclear family, and traditional roles are seen as best, but nothing is mandated. Divorce is allowed, and custody has no default; a neutral court decides, considering the child's wishes.

## Abortion and Bioethics

#### Position
The position is sentientist and affirms fetal rights. Sentience is the primary cutoff for abortion bans, but pro-life is supported in its entirety because of the practical domino effect of accepting pro-choice in society. A woman may not terminate sentient life in her womb if it exists because of her own decisions. If the fetus is the product of rape, she is freed of obligation, since she did not enter any agreement to raise it; voluntary sexual intercourse resulting in a fetus is what creates the responsibility.

> [!question]- "Why support full pro-life policy if sentience is the cutoff?"
> Sentience marks where the moral line lies, but in practice permissive abortion regimes tend to extend beyond that line. Supporting pro-life policy in full guards against that drift. The rape exception follows from the responsibility framework: obligation arises from voluntary action, and rape involves none.

#### Key Elements
- Sentientist foundation with fetal rights.
- Sentience as the primary cutoff for abortion bans.
- Full pro-life support because of the domino effect of pro-choice acceptance.
- No termination of a fetus resulting from the mother's voluntary decisions.
- Rape exception: no obligation without consent.

#### Rationale
Sentient beings hold rights, and a sentient fetus is no exception. Voluntary sexual intercourse carries responsibility for the life it creates, which grounds the duty not to terminate. After Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization returned abortion regulation to the states, laws vary widely, which is the setting in which the domino concern applies. The full framework is set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]].

> [!info]- What is the responsibility framework?
> The responsibility framework holds that voluntarily engaging in sexual intercourse creates a duty toward a resulting fetus. Where intercourse was not voluntary, as in rape, no such duty arises.

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization, 597 U.S. 215 (2022)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-1392)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Abortion is judged by sentience, but pro-life policy is supported in full because permissive laws tend to go further. A woman cannot end a sentient pregnancy that came from her own choices. Rape is the exception.

## LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues

#### Position
Same-sex marriage is legal, though the best outcome for society is male and female, naturally and biologically. Male-male adoption, particularly of male children, raises concern given indications of higher-than-average sexual abuse, and same-sex parents do not fully fulfill the best-case male/female dynamic of traditional parenting, though this belief is not mandated. Transgenderism is treated as a mental illness, the DSM's definitions of gender are viewed as ideologically driven, and the illness is treated rather than validated. Adults may transition, and private practices may train for and perform the surgery, as a freedom of both parties. Transgender individuals do not compete in their transitioned-sex sports in any case, minors undergo no transgender treatment in any case, and bathrooms are entered by birth sex only in every case. Pronouns may be respected or not as each individual chooses and are never mandated. Conversion therapy is opposed.

> [!question]- "If transgenderism is a mental illness, why allow adults to transition?"
> Adults are free to make choices about their own bodies with willing providers, even choices judged mistaken. The state does not validate or promote transition, and it protects others where the choice affects them: in sports, bathrooms, and speech. Minors, who cannot consent to such decisions, are excluded entirely.

#### Key Elements
- Same-sex marriage legal; male-female pairing viewed as the best outcome.
- Concern over male-male adoption of male children.
- Transgenderism treated as a mental illness; treat root causes rather than affirm.
- Adults free to transition privately.
- No participation in transitioned-sex sports.
- No medical transition for minors.
- Bathrooms by birth sex only.
- Pronouns voluntary, never mandated.
- Conversion therapy opposed.

#### Rationale
The framework protects adult liberty while refusing to compel others to affirm beliefs they do not hold. Sex-based categories in sports and bathrooms exist for fairness and safety, so they follow biological sex. Minors cannot consent to irreversible medical treatment, and the Supreme Court upheld a state ban on such treatment for minors in United States v. Skrmetti. Same-sex marriage remains legal under Obergefell v. Hodges, consistent with the framework's limited role for the state in private relationships.

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Obergefell v. Hodges, 576 U.S. 644 (2015)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/14-556)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [United States v. Skrmetti (2025)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/23-477)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Same-sex marriage is legal, though male-female pairing is seen as best. Transgenderism is viewed as a mental illness; adults may transition privately, but not compete in opposite-sex sports or use opposite-sex bathrooms. Minors get no transition treatment, pronouns are never mandated, and conversion therapy is opposed.

## Free Speech and Censorship

#### Position
Free speech is held absolutely. The only speech legally restricted is threats or calls to violence that infringe on others' sentient rights. Private platforms are private and may restrict speech under their own codes of conduct and terms of service, just as a homeowner may remove someone from a private home for speech the owner does not approve. Misinformation is the responsibility of the private platform or, in public, of individuals to debunk as factually false.

> [!question]- "Does letting private platforms moderate undermine free speech absolutism?"
> No. Free speech is a protection against government censorship. A private platform setting its own rules is exercising property rights, the same way a homeowner controls who speaks in their home. The state's only role is to restrict threats and calls to violence.

#### Key Elements
- Speech restricted only for threats and calls to violence.
- Private platforms free to set their own terms of service.
- Misinformation countered by platforms and individuals, not government.

#### Rationale
Speech does not violate sentient rights unless it threatens or incites violence. The Supreme Court's standard in Brandenburg v. Ohio protects advocacy unless it is directed to and likely to produce imminent lawless action, which closely matches the line drawn here. Federal law already protects platforms' ability to moderate content on their own terms.

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [First Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/first_amendment)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Brandenburg v. Ohio, 395 U.S. 444 (1969)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/395/444)
- Cornell LII – [47 U.S.C. § 230: Protection for private blocking and screening of offensive material](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Speech is fully protected except for threats and calls to violence. Private platforms set their own rules. Misinformation is answered by platforms and individuals, not the government.

## Race and Diversity

#### Position
There are absolutely zero hiring quotas based on innate characteristics, which are fundamentally racist and sexist. DEI programs are abolished. Equality of outcome is discriminatory by definition, and equality of opportunity is the goal. Meritocracy is the only relevant factor in hiring and admissions. Reparations are paid only by the offending government, and only where a causal link runs from the electorate's voting decisions to the candidate or administration responsible: those whose vote is on record as electing the offending government are liable for the reparations tax, and those who did not vote for it are not. Reparations apply only for a limited number of generations, expiring once enough time has passed. More unbiased scientific research on racial IQ is needed, and society moves past race as a core dividing issue by speaking about it less, not more.

> [!question]- "How could voter records determine reparations liability when ballots are secret?"
> The position ties liability to a voter's recorded support for the responsible government; where no such record exists, liability cannot be assigned. The principle is that only those causally responsible pay. Implementing it would require a record of votes that current secret-ballot systems do not keep.

#### Key Elements
- No quotas based on innate characteristics.
- Abolish DEI programs.
- Equality of opportunity, not outcome.
- Meritocracy as the only factor in hiring and admissions.
- Reparations only from the offending government, charged to voters on record as electing it.
- Generational sunset on reparations.
- More unbiased research on group differences, including IQ.
- Less public focus on race.

#### Rationale
Judging people by innate characteristics violates the principle of individual responsibility at the core of the framework. The Supreme Court held in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard that race-conscious admissions violate the Equal Protection Clause. Liability for reparations follows the same principle: those who caused the harm pay, and those who did not are not charged. Unbiased research and less emphasis on race aim at a color-blind society.

> [!info]- What is equality of opportunity versus equality of outcome?
> Equality of opportunity means everyone is judged by the same rules and standards. Equality of outcome means adjusting rules or results so that groups end up with similar results.

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard, 600 U.S. 181 (2023)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/20-1199)
- Cornell LII – [Fourteenth Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentxiv)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> No quotas, no DEI, and only merit in hiring and admissions. Reparations are paid only by the responsible government, charged to those who voted for it, and they expire after some generations. More unbiased research on group differences is needed, and race should be talked about less.

## Gun Rights

#### Position
Guns are available for purchase by those who are qualified. Background checks and red flag laws prevent bad actors from acquiring firearms. Concealed carry requires a higher level of qualification than open carry. Assault weapons are vaguely defined, and handguns are more dangerous because they are easy to conceal. High-ordnance explosives are banned or heavily restricted to construction work. Full automatics are heavily restricted but potentially available to extremely trained or vetted individuals.

> [!question]- "Do red flag laws violate due process?"
> Red flag laws are aimed at keeping firearms from bad actors, which protects the sentient rights of potential victims. The position supports them as part of qualification, alongside strong support for the right of qualified citizens to own guns.

#### Key Elements
- Firearms available to qualified citizens.
- Background checks and red flag laws.
- Stricter standard for concealed carry than open carry.
- Assault weapons viewed as vaguely defined; handguns more concerning.
- High explosives banned or restricted to construction.
- Full automatics heavily restricted, possibly available to highly trained or vetted individuals.

#### Rationale
Self-defense protects sentient rights, and the Second Amendment protects an individual right to keep and bear arms, as held in District of Columbia v. Heller and New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen. Safeguards target the people and weapons most likely to cause harm. Federal law already restricts machine guns heavily, which the position retains with a path for highly vetted individuals.

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [District of Columbia v. Heller, 554 U.S. 570 (2008)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/07-290)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen, 597 U.S. 1 (2022)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/20-843)
- Cornell LII – [18 U.S.C. § 922: Unlawful acts (firearms)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/922)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Qualified citizens can buy guns, with background checks and red flag laws to stop bad actors. Concealed carry has a higher bar. Explosives are banned or limited to construction, and full automatics are heavily restricted.

## Drugs and Vice

#### Position
Marijuana is legal, though widespread use is not condoned. Hard drugs likely should be legal, though the position stands very close to banning them. Prostitution is legal, less clandestine, and regulated to prevent exploitation of prostitutes. Gambling is regulated only to keep it from minors; adults may gamble. Driving while intoxicated or under the influence of hard drugs results in license revocation for a minimum of ten years, up to permanently. The drug war focuses less on domestic addicts and more on the offensive obliteration of drug cartels by force, treating them as terroristic entities endangering the country's safety. Joint operations with compliant Central and South American governments are best, and if they do not comply, war with those governments is justified to eradicate the cartel problem.

> [!question]- "Is legalizing prostitution compatible with preventing exploitation?"
> Legal prostitution brings the trade into the open, where regulation can address exploitation directly. A clandestine trade leaves workers without protection. Regulation targets the rights violation, exploitation, rather than the voluntary exchange.

#### Key Elements
- Marijuana legal.
- Hard drugs likely legal, with strong reservations.
- Prostitution legal and regulated against exploitation.
- Gambling legal for adults, kept from minors.
- DUI with intoxication or hard drugs: license revoked for ten years to life.
- Drug war aimed at cartels as terrorist entities; joint operations or war with non-compliant governments.

#### Rationale
Adult choices that harm only the chooser fall outside the state's coercive role, while driving impaired endangers others and is punished severely. Cartels murder and corrupt on a scale that threatens national security, and the United States designated several cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations in 2025. The Supreme Court's decision in Murphy v. NCAA allowed states to legalize sports betting. Related positions are set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Drug Policy|Criminal Justice & Security]].

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Gonzales v. Raich, 545 U.S. 1 (2005)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/03-1454)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Murphy v. NCAA, 584 U.S. 453 (2018)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/16-476)
- Federal Register – [Foreign Terrorist Organization Designations of Tren de Aragua, MS-13, Cartel de Sinaloa, CJNG, and Others (2025)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/20/2025-02873/foreign-terrorist-organization-designations-of-tren-de-aragua-mara-salvatrucha-ms-13-cartel-de-sinaloa-cartel-de-jalisco-nueva-generacion-carteles-unidos-cartel-del-noreste-cartel-del-golfo-and-la-nueva-familia-michoacana)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Marijuana is legal, hard drugs probably should be, prostitution is legal and regulated, and adults can gamble. Impaired drivers lose their license for ten years or more. The drug war targets cartels with military force, including war with governments that refuse to help.

## Patriotism and National Identity

#### Position
A patriotic, monocultural nation is the best result for cohesion. Multiculturalism can work only if the cultures share important common values; many legally immigrated Mexican and South American families are compatible because of shared family and religious values. Islamic and third-world African cultures are judged incompatible with Western society, clashing with values such as human rights, civic responsibility, politeness, and cleanliness. History is taught unmodified and objective. American exceptionalism rests not on America as such but on what America stands for: liberty, freedom, rights, and great financial opportunity.

> [!question]- "Does cultural compatibility contradict civic nationalism?"
> Civic nationalism unites people around shared values, and cultural compatibility is the condition for sharing them. Cultures that hold the same core values integrate; those that clash with them do not. The test is values, which is why compatible immigrant communities are welcomed.

#### Key Elements
- Patriotic, cohesive national culture preferred.
- Multiculturalism viable only with shared core values.
- Many Latin American cultures compatible; Islamic and third-world African cultures judged incompatible.
- Objective, unmodified history education.
- American exceptionalism grounded in liberty, rights, and opportunity.

#### Rationale
A high-trust society depends on shared norms, and cohesion is easier where citizens hold common values. Teaching history without modification gives citizens an accurate basis for national identity. Grounding exceptionalism in principles rather than in nationality alone makes it a standard the country must live up to.

>[!tldr]- Summary
> A cohesive, patriotic culture is best, and multiculturalism works only with shared values. Some immigrant cultures are seen as compatible and others not. History is taught straight, and America is exceptional because of liberty, rights, and opportunity.
