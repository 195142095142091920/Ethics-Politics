## Core Principles
Traditional nuclear family preference, free speech absolutism (with narrow exceptions), meritocracy, cultural compatibility for cohesion, and personal responsibility. Strong skepticism of identity politics, mandates, and progressive social engineering.

## Family and Gender

#### Position
Marriage and family have multiple definitions depending on one's culture and religion. The healthiest family is something very close to the nuclear family: a mother and father, married and loyal to one another, with two to three children. Divorce is allowed. Child custody does not default to either parent; if the parents cannot reach a resolution, a neutral court arbitrates the dispute, informed by the child's privately expressed preference. Traditional roles, with a stronger male working while a female raises children, cares for the home, and supports the working man, are judged superior, but they are not mandated in any way, and people ultimately live as they decide.

> [!question]- "Does favoring traditional roles conflict with individual freedom?"
> No. The preference is a judgment about what constitutes the best-functioning society, not a rule imposed by law. Individuals remain free to structure their families and roles as they choose.

#### Key Elements
- Nuclear family (married mother and father, two to three children) as the healthiest structure.
- Divorce allowed.
- No custody default; a neutral court decides when parents disagree, considering the child's private preference.
- Traditional gender roles judged superior but never mandated.
- People free to live as they decide.

#### Rationale
The nuclear family is treated as the structure best suited to raising children, and cohesive families support a high-trust society. Married parents are far more stable: about a quarter separate before a child turns 12, against about two-thirds of cohabiting parents, though part of the difference in child outcomes reflects who marries. The preference for traditional roles is a value judgment, not an empirical finding, which is why it is never mandated. A neutral custody standard avoids presuming either parent is more fit. Keeping traditional roles voluntary respects the framework's commitment to individual liberty.

> The healthiest family is close to the traditional nuclear family, and traditional roles are seen as best, but nothing is mandated. Divorce is allowed, and custody has no default; a neutral court decides, considering the child's wishes.

## Feminism and Sex Equality

#### Position
Men and women hold the same rights, because rights rest on sentience, not sex ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). The framework supports equal treatment under law and rejects the strands of modern feminism that seek equality of outcome between the sexes, rely on sex-based quotas or preferences, or treat traditional family roles as oppression. Traditional roles, with a working husband and a wife who raises children and keeps the home, are judged superior but never mandated ([[#Family and Gender]]). The abortion position also departs from mainstream feminism ([[#Abortion and Bioethics]]).

> [!question]- "Doesn't the pay gap prove women face discrimination?"
> In part, possibly, but not mostly. In 2024 women working full time earned 83% of men's median weekly earnings. Blau and Kahn found that by 2010 occupation and industry were the largest measured explanations, with hours and work interruptions mattering in high-skill jobs, and that experimental evidence means discrimination "cannot be discounted." The remedy the framework accepts is equal rules, enforced against discrimination in either direction ([[#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business]]), not outcome targets.

> [!question]- "Isn't preferring traditional roles itself sexist?"
> No law follows from the preference. Women and men have identical rights and may arrange their lives as they choose; the framework only states its judgment of which arrangement best serves families.

#### Key Elements
- Equal rights for both sexes, grounded in sentience.
- Equal treatment under law; no sex quotas or preferences.
- Equality of opportunity, not outcome.
- Traditional roles preferred, never mandated.

#### Rationale
Equal rights and equal opportunity follow directly from the framework's foundations, so the earlier, legal-equality aims of feminism are compatible with it. The conflict is with outcome-based and identity-based approaches, which the framework rejects for every group ([[#Race and Diversity]]). The pay data show a persistent raw gap that is mostly explained by occupation, industry, and hours, with a smaller unexplained share.

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Both sexes hold equal rights and are judged by the same rules. Outcome-based feminism, sex quotas, and treating traditional roles as oppression are rejected, while traditional roles stay a preference, not a law.

## Marriage Law

#### Position
Civil marriage is a legal contract between consenting adults, recognized equally for opposite-sex and same-sex couples. The state records and enforces the contract but does not define its religious meaning, and religious bodies remain free to perform or decline ceremonies. Minors cannot consent, so they cannot marry. Divorce is allowed, and custody follows the existing family rules.

> [!question]- "Should the state be in marriage at all?"
> The state's role is limited to recording and enforcing a contract, including property and custody consequences. It does not regulate the relationship itself.

#### Key Elements
- Civil marriage as a contract between consenting adults.
- Same-sex marriage legal.
- Religious bodies free to decline.
- No marriage of minors.
- Divorce allowed.

#### Rationale
In *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015) the Supreme Court held that the Constitution guarantees same-sex couples the right to marry. Family and custody rules are in [[#Family and Gender]].

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [Obergefell v. Hodges (2015)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/14-556)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Marriage is a legal contract between consenting adults, same-sex couples included. Minors cannot marry and churches can decline.

## Abortion and Bioethics

#### Position
The position is sentientist and affirms fetal rights. Sentience is the moral line, and legal bans start earlier as a safeguard, consistent with the significant moral weight given to a fetus on a trajectory toward sentience; pro-life is supported in its entirety because of the practical domino effect of accepting pro-choice in society. A woman may not terminate sentient life in her womb if it exists because of her own decisions. If the fetus is the product of rape, she is freed of obligation, since she did not enter any agreement to raise it; voluntary sexual intercourse resulting in a fetus is what creates the responsibility.

> [!question]- "Why support full pro-life policy if sentience is the cutoff?"
> Sentience marks where the moral line lies, but in practice permissive abortion regimes can extend beyond that line, and legal limits in a number of countries have moved later over time. Supporting pro-life policy in full guards against that drift. The rape exception follows from the responsibility framework: obligation arises from voluntary action, and rape involves none.

> [!question]- "What if the pregnancy threatens the mother's life?"
> She may end it. A person is not required to absorb serious harm because the threat lacks culpability ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Conflicts Between Rights-Holders|Conflicts Between Rights-Holders]]). Where the fetus is viable, early delivery is preferred to termination.

> [!question]- "How is the rape exception established?"
> By a report to law enforcement and a medical record before the legal limit; a criminal conviction is not required first, because a trial cannot finish in time. A knowingly false claim is fraud and is punished.

#### Key Elements
- Sentientist foundation with fetal rights.
- Sentience as the moral line; legal bans start earlier as a safeguard.
- Full pro-life support because of the domino effect of pro-choice acceptance.
- No termination of a fetus resulting from the mother's voluntary decisions.
- Rape exception: no obligation without consent.

#### Rationale
Sentient beings hold rights, and a sentient fetus is no exception. Expert estimates of when sentience begins range from about 12 weeks to after 24–28 weeks, depending on whether a mature cortex is required; that uncertainty is the reason legal bans start earlier than the moral line. Voluntary sexual intercourse carries responsibility for the life it creates, which grounds the duty not to terminate. Where the pregnancy results from rape, no voluntary act created the duty to sustain the fetus, so the obligation does not arise; this follows the distinction in the framework between withdrawing support and killing. After Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization returned abortion regulation to the states, laws vary widely, which is the setting in which the domino concern applies. In the U.S., most abortions occur early (92.8% at or before 13 weeks in 2022) and 1.1% at 21 weeks or later. The full framework is set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]].

> [!info]- What is the responsibility framework?
> The responsibility framework holds that voluntarily engaging in sexual intercourse creates a duty toward a resulting fetus. Where intercourse was not voluntary, as in rape, no such duty arises.

## Assisted Death

#### Position
A competent adult may choose medically assisted death with a willing provider, as a decision about their own body and life. No provider is compelled to take part, and no taxpayer funding is used. Minors are excluded, as they are from all irreversible bodily decisions. Every request requires an independent capacity check by a second clinician. Where death is reasonably foreseeable (Track 1), a 48-hour waiting period applies, waivable when death is imminent. Where it is not (Track 2), the waiting period is 90 days, two assessors must agree, one a specialist in the person's condition, and the person must receive a documented offer of care and support. Whenever mental illness is involved, a psychiatric assessment is mandatory; mental illness as the sole condition qualifies only if it is long-standing, assessed by a psychiatrist, and documented treatment attempts have failed. Cases are reported publicly each year. Anyone who pressures or deceives a person into choosing death violates that person's rights and is punished accordingly. Where sentience has been permanently and irreversibly lost, decisions belong to next-of-kin under Axiom 4, as set out in [[10. Bioethics, Progress, & Technology#End-of-Life Care and Lost Sentience|Bioethics, Progress, & Technology]].

> [!question]- "Won't legal assisted death pressure the sick, disabled, and elderly into dying?"
> Voluntary choice by a competent adult is the condition. Pressure, deception, or coercion toward death is a rights violation in its own right and is punished. No provider is compelled, and no public money pays for it, so there is no institutional incentive to steer people toward it. The framework does not restrict assisted death to terminal illness; instead it guards against drift with process: an independent capacity check, a 90-day wait, two assessors, and a documented offer of care for non-terminal cases, and psychiatric assessment whenever mental illness is involved. Oregon's data show why the concern is real: in 2024, 42% of those who died under its law listed being a burden on family, friends, or caregivers among their end-of-life concerns, though loss of autonomy, at 89%, was the most common.

> [!question]- "What does the data show where non-terminal assisted death is legal?"
> Growth is real but the safeguards mostly hold. In Canada, which allows both tracks, 16,499 people received assisted death in 2024, 5.1% of deaths, and yearly growth slowed from 36.8% in 2019–20 to 6.9%. Non-terminal (Track 2) cases were 732, or 4.4%. Those cases raise the concern the framework's extra safeguards address: 44.7% cited isolation or loneliness, compared with 21.9% in Track 1, and only 23.2% received palliative care. Isolation was never the sole reason given, and 45 requests were refused for external pressure. Canada has twice delayed eligibility for mental illness alone, now set for March 17, 2027. In the Netherlands, 9,958 cases made up 5.8% of deaths in 2024, including 219 psychiatric and 427 dementia cases, and the review committees found six cases (0.06%) that failed the due-care standard. A 2007 review of Oregon and Dutch data found no heightened risk for the elderly, the poor, the disabled, or people with psychiatric illness, though critics argue that vulnerability is not captured by demographics alone.

> [!question]- "Why allow assisted death but not abortion?"
> Assisted death is a competent adult's decision about their own body and life. Abortion of a sentient fetus ends another sentient being's life, one that exists because of the mother's own decisions. The line in both cases is the same: rights rest on sentience, and no one may end another sentient being's life without forfeiture.

#### Key Elements
- Competent adults may choose assisted death with a willing provider.
- No provider is compelled to participate.
- No taxpayer funding.
- Minors are excluded.
- Independent capacity check for every request.
- Track 1 (death reasonably foreseeable): 48-hour wait, waivable when death is imminent.
- Track 2 (not foreseeable): 90-day wait, two assessors (one a specialist in the condition), documented offer of care and support.
- Mandatory psychiatric assessment when mental illness is involved; mental illness alone only if long-standing, psychiatrist-assessed, with documented treatment attempts.
- Public annual reporting.
- Pressure or deception toward death is punished as a rights violation.
- Lost-sentience cases follow Axiom 4.

#### Rationale
Adults are free to make choices about their own bodies with willing providers, even choices others judge mistaken; that principle already governs adult transition in [[#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]. Negative rights protect a person against interference by others, and a competent person's own considered choice is not an interference. Oregon's experience shows the practice operating at small scale. In 2025, 637 people received prescriptions under the Death with Dignity Act and 400 died from ingesting the medication, about 1.0% of deaths in the state. Since 1997, 5,520 people have received prescriptions and 3,691 have died from taking them. Most had cancer (61%) or a neurological disease (14%), and the most common concerns were loss of autonomy (89%) and less ability to enjoy life (89%); 39.8% cited being a burden on family or caregivers. Jurisdictions that allow non-terminal cases, such as Canada and the Netherlands, show steady growth and some cases driven by isolation, so the framework allows any qualifying condition while adding time, a second specialist view, and a documented offer of care where death is not near.

## LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues

#### Position
Same-sex marriage is legal, though the best outcome for society is male and female, naturally and biologically. Male-male adoption raises concern because same-sex parents do not fully fulfill the best-case male/female dynamic of traditional parenting, though this belief is not mandated. Transgenderism is treated as a mental illness, the DSM's definitions of gender are viewed as ideologically driven, and the illness is treated rather than validated. Adults may transition, and private practices may train for and perform the surgery, as a freedom of both parties. Transgender individuals do not compete in their transitioned-sex sports in any case, minors undergo no transgender treatment in any case, and every multi-person bathroom or changing room open to the public, in government or private facilities, is separated by birth sex, while single-occupancy lockable rooms are open to anyone. Pronouns may be respected or not as each individual chooses and are never mandated. Conversion therapy is opposed.

> [!question]- "If transgenderism is a mental illness, why allow adults to transition?"
> Adults are free to make choices about their own bodies with willing providers, even choices judged mistaken. The state does not validate or promote transition, and it protects others where the choice affects them: in sports, bathrooms, and speech. Minors, who cannot consent to such decisions, are excluded entirely.

> [!question]- "Isn't the DSM the scientific consensus? Why call its classification ideologically driven?"
> The DSM is a consensus document, and the American Psychiatric Association states its reasons openly. In 2013 DSM-5 replaced "gender identity disorder" with "gender dysphoria" to "avoid stigma and ensure clinical care," stating that gender nonconformity "is not in itself a mental disorder" while keeping a diagnosis that preserves treatment and insurance access. The APA itself calls the diagnosis "a double-edged sword." The 2022 text revision changed "natal male" to "individual assigned male at birth" to use "less stigmatizing language." Reducing stigma and preserving coverage are social and practical aims, not new findings about causes or outcomes, so the framework treats the classification as a value judgment it may reject.

> [!question]- "Don't the major medical associations support gender-affirming care?"
> Yes. The American Medical Association calls such treatment medically necessary and opposes laws that restrict it, and the American Psychiatric Association supports puberty suppression for youth when indicated. The question is what their guidance rests on. The Cass Review's systematic review of 23 guidelines found that few systematically reviewed the evidence and that most drew on two, WPATH's and the Endocrine Society's. Court documents reported by The BMJ show WPATH required Johns Hopkins reviewers it commissioned to use the data "for the benefit of advancing transgender health in a positive manner" and to seek board approval before publishing; the lead reviewer reported finding "little to no evidence about children and adolescents," and only two reviews were published. WPATH says it disputed only the timing of publication. The 2025 HHS umbrella review rated the evidence on psychological outcomes, quality of life, regret, and long-term health as very low quality. Endorsement by an association does not raise the quality of the studies underneath it.

> [!question]- "Doesn't childhood dysphoria usually persist?"
> Follow-up studies suggest most cases do not. In a Toronto sample of 139 clinic-referred boys, 17 (12.2%) persisted and 122 (87.8%) desisted (Singh, Bradley, and Zucker, 2021); in a Dutch sample of 127, persistence was the minority outcome and was more likely with more intense dysphoria and among girls (Steensma et al., 2013). These studies have limits: they used older DSM criteria, 36.7% of the Toronto boys were below the diagnostic threshold, and they predate today's adolescent-onset caseload. Comorbidity is high: in Finland's adolescent service, severe psychiatric problems often preceded the dysphoria and autism-spectrum problems were very common (Kaltiala-Heino et al., 2015). That pattern supports treating the underlying distress first.

> [!question]- "Hasn't the change in referrals just reflected greater acceptance?"
> Possibly in part. Referrals rose on a similar pattern across four Nordic countries and the UK, with natal girls predominating among adolescents, and the authors state the reasons "are not known," naming awareness, destigmatization, and social and media influences as possibilities (de Graaf et al., 2020). Whatever the cause, a fast change in who is referred argues for more diagnostic caution, not less. Several of the countries with the longest experience have pulled back: Sweden judged in 2022 that the risks of blockers and hormones for adolescents "are likely to outweigh the expected benefits," Finland made psychosocial support first-line, Norway's investigation board classed the treatments as investigational, and NHS England limited puberty blockers to research settings.

> [!question]- "Isn't sex a spectrum?"
> No. Biological sex is defined by the type of gamete an organism is organized to produce, and there are two, small and large; no third gamete exists (Goymann, Brumm, and Kappeler, 2023). Some biologists argue sex is better described as a spectrum because of differences of sex development, and those conditions are real. They are variations in how the two-sex system develops, not additional sexes, so they do not change the binary. Law and public facilities that rely on sex therefore track biological sex.

> [!question]- "Don't adults who transition report low regret and better well-being?"
> Reported regret after surgery is low: a meta-analysis of 27 studies with 7,928 patients found about 1%, though its authors note subjective measurement and a lack of standardized questionnaires (Bustos et al., 2021). Long-term outcomes are less clear. A Swedish cohort found post-surgery mortality 2.8 times and suicide 19.1 times that of controls, though it could not show what would have happened without surgery (Dhejne et al., 2011). A national Swedish study first reported reduced mental-health treatment after surgery, then issued a correction after reanalysis finding no advantage of surgery for mood or anxiety treatment or suicide-attempt hospitalization (Bränström and Pachankis, 2020). The framework therefore leaves adult transition to adult choice without treating it as the established cure, and without public funding.

#### Key Elements
- Same-sex marriage legal; male-female pairing viewed as the best outcome.
- Concern over male-male adoption.
- Transgenderism treated as a mental illness; treat root causes rather than affirm.
- Adults free to transition privately.
- No participation in transitioned-sex sports.
- No medical transition for minors.
- Multi-person bathrooms and changing rooms open to the public separated by birth sex, private businesses included; single-occupancy lockable rooms open to anyone.
- Pronouns voluntary, never mandated.
- Conversion therapy opposed.

#### Rationale
The framework protects adult liberty while refusing to compel others to affirm beliefs they do not hold. Sex-based categories in sports and bathrooms exist for fairness and safety, so they follow biological sex. Minors cannot consent to irreversible medical treatment, and the Supreme Court upheld a state ban on such treatment for minors in United States v. Skrmetti. England's Cass Review (2024) found the evidence base for youth gender medicine remarkably weak and recommended puberty blockers only within research protocols. The framework's classification departs from the DSM-5-TR, which diagnoses gender dysphoria only where incongruence brings clinically significant distress. The departure rests on three points. First, the 2013 reclassification was justified by the APA in terms of stigma and access to care rather than new evidence about causes or outcomes. Second, the guidelines built on it have been judged weak by systematic review, and the evidence for treating minors has been rated low or very low quality by England's Cass Review, Sweden, and the 2025 HHS review. Third, sex is binary, defined by gametes, so an identity that contradicts it is treated as a condition to be understood and treated, not as a fact the law must adopt. Counter-evidence is acknowledged: the AMA and the American Psychiatric Association support affirming care, and reported regret after adult surgery is low. Same-sex marriage remains legal under Obergefell v. Hodges, consistent with the framework's limited role for the state in private relationships.

##### Outcomes
Columbia Law School's What We Know project reviewed 79 peer-reviewed studies. Of these, 75 found children of gay or lesbian parents fare no worse than other children. The American Academy of Pediatrics and the American Psychological Association agree. Supporting work includes Rosenfeld's 2010 census analysis, the National Longitudinal Lesbian Family Study (Gartrell and Bos), and studies of adoptive gay fathers by Golombok and by Farr and Patterson.

Three lines of research dissent. Regnerus (2012) reported worse outcomes for adults raised by a parent with a same-sex relationship. Allen, Pakaluk, and Price (2013) reanalyzed Rosenfeld's data and found children of same-sex couples 35% less likely to make normal progress through school; Allen also studied high school graduation in Canadian census data. Sullins (2015–2016) found emotional problems more than twice as common and attributed this to not living with both biological parents. Cheng and Powell (2015) reanalyzed Regnerus's data and found that at least a third of the respondents he counted as raised by a same-sex parent were misclassified, many having never lived with that parent or having lived with them for a year or less; once corrected, the differences were small or absent.

Both sides have weaknesses. The no-difference studies mostly use small, self-selected samples of planned lesbian families. The dissenting studies count a household as same-sex if a parent ever had a same-sex relationship, which mixes in children who went through family breakup. The journal that published Regnerus released an audit concluding its peer review failed.

##### Abuse Data
No published study measures abuse rates in gay-father adoptive or foster homes. In the National Longitudinal Lesbian Family Study, 0 of 78 adolescents reported physical or sexual abuse by a parent or caregiver. In Regnerus's survey, 23% of respondents whose mother had a same-sex relationship reported ever being touched sexually by a parent or adult, against 2% from intact biological families. The question did not ask who did it, and Cheng and Powell's reanalysis found many of those respondents misclassified. The federal NIS-4 has no data specific to gay-father homes, so no national figure measures abuse in them.

##### Boys
Goldberg (2009) studied couples waiting to adopt. Among those with a sex preference, 46% of same-sex couples preferred boys, against 29% of heterosexual couples. Several studies find gay fathers do end up adopting boys more often. Whether this comes from parent preference or agency matching is unclear, and no data ties it to abuse. The pattern is noted as a point to watch, not as evidence of harm.

## Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions

#### Position
This section applies [[#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]] to public institutions. Public schools teach core skills and objective facts without ideological instruction in either direction ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Education]]), so gender-identity advocacy is not part of the public curriculum. Schools do not socially transition students and never withhold a student's gender-identity concerns from parents. No student, teacher, or public employee is required to use another person's chosen pronouns. Government identity documents record birth sex, with no change of marker. Prisons and shelters house people by birth sex. No public money funds adult transition. Transgender people may serve in the military under the same medical-fitness standards as any other condition, set by the military. Government buildings fly official flags only.

> [!question]- "Isn't refusing chosen pronouns harassment?"
> Pronouns may be respected or not as each person chooses, and that freedom is never mandated away. Compelling speech is coercion, and the Supreme Court has limited compelled expression in 303 Creative v. Elenis. Threats and incitement remain unprotected as with any other speech ([[#Free Speech and Censorship]]).

> [!question]- "Doesn't telling parents put some students at risk?"
> Parents hold primary responsibility for their minor children, and a school that hides a significant psychological matter from them takes a decision that is not its own. Where a school has evidence of abuse, the ordinary child-protection process applies, as it does for any other concern. The HHS review found that the evidence does not show clear benefit from social transition in minors.

> [!question]- "Why birth sex on ID instead of gender identity?"
> Documents record facts the state relies on, and sex is a biological fact defined by gametes ([[#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]). The same record then governs sex-separated facilities consistently.

> [!question]- "Why must private businesses separate multi-person bathrooms by birth sex? Isn't that a mandate?"
> It is, and it rests on protection and privacy: multi-person bathrooms and changing rooms are places of undress where people are most exposed. Direct data on assault are thin. A Massachusetts study of public-record incident reports found that gender-identity nondiscrimination ordinances were not related to the number of safety or privacy incidents, which were exceedingly rare either way (Hasenbush, Flores, and Herman, 2019). The rule therefore rests on privacy and protection rather than a measured crime effect. Single-occupancy lockable rooms are open to anyone, so no one is left without access.

> [!question]- "Isn't housing trans women in men's prisons dangerous for them?"
> Prisons owe safety to every inmate. Housing stays by birth sex. Separate protective units are allowed inside the male and female prisons, assigned by individual risk such as small build, a history of being abused, or sexual orientation. They add protection within each sex's prison and are not a third category. Federal prison-rape standards already require screening on similar factors, including physical build, prior sexual victimization, and being perceived as gay, lesbian, or bisexual.

> [!question]- "Why can transgender people serve in the military?"
> Service follows the same medical-fitness standards as any condition, set by the military, so the decision rests on readiness rather than a political rule in either direction.

> [!question]- "Do official-flags-only rules restrict speech?"
> No. The rule governs only what the government itself displays. Private citizens and businesses fly any flag they choose.

#### Key Elements
- No gender-identity advocacy in public curriculum; core skills and objective facts only.
- Schools do not socially transition students and never withhold it from parents.
- Pronouns never compelled for students, teachers, or public employees.
- Government ID records birth sex; no change of marker.
- Prisons and shelters by birth sex; protective units by individual risk inside each.
- No public funding for adult transition.
- Military service under the same medical-fitness standards as any condition, set by the military.
- Official flags only on government buildings.

#### Rationale
The framework keeps public institutions out of ideological promotion, refuses to compel anyone to affirm beliefs they do not hold, and leaves adult transition a private choice without public support, consistent with no compelled redistribution beyond the floor ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]). Sex-separated spaces follow biological sex for privacy and protection. The evidence for medical treatment of minors is low quality on the key outcomes, which supports the exclusion of minors, who cannot consent to irreversible treatment.

## Free Speech and Censorship

#### Position
Free speech is held absolutely. The only speech legally restricted is true threats and incitement to imminent violence that infringe on others' sentient rights. Separate from speech restrictions, minors' access is limited by law in two areas: pornography, as set out in [[#Pornography and Minors' Access]], and social media accounts, as set out in [[#Minors and Social Media]]. Adults' speech and access are unaffected. Private platforms are private and may restrict speech under their own codes of conduct and terms of service, just as a homeowner may remove someone from a private home for speech the owner does not approve. Misinformation is the responsibility of the private platform or, in public, of individuals to debunk as factually false.

> [!question]- "Does letting private platforms moderate undermine free speech absolutism?"
> No. Free speech is a protection against government censorship. A private platform setting its own rules is exercising property rights, the same way a homeowner controls who speaks in their home. The state's only role in speech is to restrict true threats and incitement to imminent violence; limits on minors' access are set out separately.

> [!question]- "Is hate speech protected?"
> Yes, legally. The only speech restricted is true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Private platforms may still remove it under their own terms, and misinformation is answered by platforms and individuals, not the government.

> [!question]- "Don't limits on minors' access contradict free-speech absolutism?"
> They are separate from speech restrictions. They limit minors' access to pornography and to social media accounts; adults' speech and access are unaffected.

#### Key Elements
- Speech restricted only for true threats and incitement to imminent violence.
- Minors' access to pornography and social media accounts limited by law; adults unaffected.
- Private platforms free to set their own terms of service.
- Misinformation countered by platforms and individuals, not government.

#### Rationale
Speech does not violate sentient rights unless it threatens or incites violence. The Supreme Court's standard in Brandenburg v. Ohio protects advocacy unless it is directed to and likely to produce imminent lawless action, which closely matches the line drawn here. Federal law already protects platforms' ability to moderate content on their own terms.

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [First Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/first_amendment)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Brandenburg v. Ohio, 395 U.S. 444 (1969)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/395/444)
- Cornell LII – [47 U.S.C. § 230: Protection for private blocking and screening of offensive material](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Speech is fully protected except for true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Private platforms set their own rules. Misinformation is answered by platforms and individuals, not the government.

## Pornography and Minors' Access

#### Position
Adult pornography produced with the consent of adults is protected expression. Material involving minors or non-consenting people is a rights violation and is prosecuted. A law prohibits minors' access to pornography, and parental and device-level tools are a layer on top of it. The law and the tools are not mutually exclusive.

> [!question]- "Doesn't keeping it from minors burden adult speech?"
> Adults keep full access. The same principle already keeps gambling and other vices from minors without banning them for adults.

#### Key Elements
- Consensual adult pornography protected.
- Material involving minors or non-consent prosecuted.
- A law prohibiting minors' access to pornography.
- Parental and device-level tools layered on top of the law.
- Law and tools not mutually exclusive.

#### Rationale
In *Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton* (2025) the Supreme Court upheld, 6–3, a Texas law requiring age verification for commercial sites with substantial sexual material, applying intermediate scrutiny. Free speech limits are set out in [[#Free Speech and Censorship]], and vice rules in [[#Drugs and Vice]].

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [Free Speech Coalition, Inc. v. Paxton (2025)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/23-1122)

> Adult pornography made with consent is legal. Anything involving minors or non-consent is prosecuted. A law prohibits minors' access, with parental and device tools layered on top.

## Minors and Social Media

#### Position
Social media follows Australia's model: minors under 16 may not hold social media accounts, and from 16 to 18 access is allowed only by parental-consent opt-in. Opt-in consent for data collection applies everywhere, given by parents for their children.

> [!question]- "Doesn't this take the decision away from parents?"
> Under 16, the rule is the same for every family. From 16 to 18, parents decide through opt-in consent.

> [!question]- "Is there proof that social media harms teenagers?"
> Not at the population level. The National Academies found in 2023 that the research does not support the conclusion that social media causes changes in adolescent health across the population, while noting possible harms and benefits for individual teens. The ban does not rest on proven harm. It rests on parental authority: children cannot consent to contracts or data collection on their own, and the rule backs parents against platforms built to hold a child's attention.

#### Key Elements
- Australia's model.
- No social media accounts for minors under 16.
- Ages 16 to 18: access only by parental-consent opt-in.
- Parental opt-in for children's data.

#### Rationale
Since December 10, 2025, Australian law has required age-restricted social media platforms to take reasonable steps to prevent Australians under 16 from creating or keeping accounts, according to Australia's eSafety Commissioner. In 2024, 16% of U.S. teens said they use TikTok almost constantly and 15% said the same of YouTube, according to the Pew Research Center. The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine concluded in 2023 that the evidence does not show social media causing population-level changes in adolescent health; the age limit is grounded in parental authority over minors, not in a proven harm.

> Following Australia's model, minors under 16 cannot hold social media accounts, and 16- to 18-year-olds have access only with parental opt-in. The rule rests on parental authority, not on proven harm.

## Race and Diversity

#### Position
There are absolutely zero hiring quotas based on innate characteristics, which are fundamentally racist and sexist. Government DEI programs are abolished. Equality of outcome is discriminatory by definition, and equality of opportunity is the goal. Meritocracy is the only relevant factor in hiring and admissions, public and private ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment in Employment and Business]]). Reparations are paid only by the offending government, and only where a causal link runs from the electorate's voting decisions to the candidate or administration responsible: those whose vote is on record as electing the offending government are liable for the reparations tax, and those who did not vote for it are not. Reparations apply only for a limited number of generations, expiring once enough time has passed. More unbiased scientific research on racial IQ is needed, and society moves past race as a core dividing issue by speaking about it less, not more.

> [!question]- "How could voter records determine reparations liability when ballots are secret?"
> The position ties liability to a voter's recorded support for the responsible government; where no such record exists, liability cannot be assigned. The principle is that only those causally responsible pay. Implementing it would require a record of votes that current secret-ballot systems do not keep, so the mechanism is a statement of principle and does not change the secret ballot.

> [!question]- "Without DEI, what stops discrimination in public institutions?"
> Merit. Meritocracy is the only relevant factor in public hiring and admissions, so neither preference nor exclusion by innate characteristics is permitted. Equality of opportunity is the goal; equality of outcome is rejected as discriminatory by definition.

> [!question]- "What makes research on group differences unbiased, and how is misuse prevented?"
> Unbiased means open data, preregistered methods, and peer review, with any public funding opt-in. No finding about a group changes anyone's rights, which rest on sentience, and individuals are judged by merit, not by group averages.

#### Key Elements
- No quotas based on innate characteristics.
- Abolish government DEI programs.
- Equality of opportunity, not outcome.
- Meritocracy as the only factor in all hiring and admissions, public and private.
- Reparations only from the offending government, charged to voters on record as electing it; a statement of principle that does not change the secret ballot.
- Generational sunset on reparations.
- More unbiased research on group differences, including IQ.
- Less public focus on race.

#### Rationale
Judging people by innate characteristics violates the principle of individual responsibility at the core of the framework. The Supreme Court held in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard that race-conscious admissions violate the Equal Protection Clause. Liability for reparations follows the same principle: those who caused the harm pay, and those who did not are not charged. Unbiased research and less emphasis on race aim at a color-blind society.

> [!info]- What is equality of opportunity versus equality of outcome?
> Equality of opportunity means everyone is judged by the same rules and standards. Equality of outcome means adjusting rules or results so that groups end up with similar results.

##### Sources
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard, 600 U.S. 181 (2023)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/20-1199)
- Cornell LII – [Fourteenth Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentxiv)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> No quotas, no DEI preferences, and only merit in hiring and admissions. Reparations are paid only by the responsible government, charged to those who voted for it, and they expire after some generations. More unbiased research on group differences is needed, and race should be talked about less.

## Equal Treatment in Employment and Business

#### Position
One symmetric rule governs employment and public business: hiring, firing, pay, promotion, and treatment of workers or customers by race, sex, sexual orientation, or another immutable or protected trait is a rights violation, for private employers and businesses as well as government. The rule cuts both ways. No one may be fired for being gay. A transgender worker may not be fired for traits or conduct that would be accepted in a worker of the other biological sex, because that is discrimination by sex. No one may be hired or promoted through a quota or DEI preference. Government DEI offices, diversity statements, and mandatory diversity training are abolished. Voluntary outreach that does not affect selection, such as advertising widely, stays legal. Sex means biological sex: male, female, and rare intersex conditions; gender identity is not a legal category and is not a protected trait. Freedom of association covers personal, religious, and club life, not employment and public business ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Freedom of Association|Freedom of Association]]).

> [!question]- "Doesn't banning private DEI preferences violate freedom of association?"
> Freedom of association protects whom people befriend, worship with, or admit to their clubs. An employer or a business open to the public is not choosing companions; it is judging workers and customers, and judging them by group rather than merit or conduct is the rights violation the rule stops. The same reasoning that bars firing a worker for being gay bars hiring a worker through a racial or sex quota. A rule that banned one and allowed the other would not be neutral.

> [!question]- "Isn't this just existing civil-rights law?"
> Largely. Title VII already bars employment discrimination by race, color, religion, sex, and national origin; the Supreme Court held in Bostock v. Clayton County that firing someone for being gay or transgender is sex discrimination, reasoning that the employer penalizes in one sex what it accepts in the other; the framework applies that reasoning through biological sex alone and does not recognize gender identity as a legal category. In Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services that majority-group plaintiffs face the same standard as anyone else. The framework adds an explicit statement that preferences are barred as firmly as exclusion.

> [!question]- "Don't diversity trainings reduce bias?"
> The evidence is mixed. A meta-analysis of 260 samples found lasting effects on knowledge, while effects on attitudes faded; effects were larger when training was sustained and combined with other measures (Bezrukova et al., 2016). A network meta-analysis of 492 studies found changes in implicit bias were weak and generally produced trivial changes in behavior (Forscher et al., 2019). Dobbin and Kalev reported that mandatory training often fails to increase managerial diversity and can provoke backlash. The objection is to judging people by group, not to training as such: private voluntary training that does not affect selection stays legal.

> [!question]- "Can employers, landlords, or insurers use group statistics?"
> No. Each person is judged on their own record, never on race or sex averages. The exceptions are real sex-based facts, such as life expectancy in actuarial tables, and fitness tests applied the same way to everyone. Current federal law is stricter on one point: in Los Angeles Department of Water and Power v. Manhart (1978), the Supreme Court barred employers from charging women larger pension contributions based on longer average life expectancy.

> [!question]- "Does merit-only admission apply to private colleges?"
> Yes. Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard (2023) held race-conscious admissions unlawful at Harvard, a private college receiving federal funds, and at the University of North Carolina. The framework extends the merit rule to all private colleges, with no race or sex preference. Single-sex colleges may remain, since they exclude by a defined mission rather than ranking applicants by group.

> [!question]- "Isn't wide advertising a form of preference?"
> No. Outreach widens who applies without changing who is selected. Selection itself must turn on merit.

#### Key Elements
- No hiring, firing, pay, promotion, or service by race, sex, sexual orientation, or another protected trait, public or private.
- Symmetric: no exclusion, no quotas, no DEI preferences.
- Sex means biological sex (male, female, rare intersex conditions); gender identity is not a legal category.
- Government DEI offices, diversity statements, and mandatory diversity training abolished.
- Voluntary outreach that does not affect selection stays legal.
- No group statistics by race or sex for employers, landlords, or insurers; exceptions for real sex-based facts such as actuarial life expectancy and uniform fitness tests.
- Merit-only admissions at public and private colleges; single-sex colleges may remain.
- Freedom of association covers personal, religious, and club life, not employment and public business.

#### Rationale
Judging individuals by group membership conflicts with the individual responsibility at the core of the framework ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]). Applying one rule in both directions keeps it neutral and avoids treating any group's claim as special. The evidence on DEI training is mixed rather than uniformly negative, but the framework does not rest its case on that evidence: even an effective group-based preference treats individuals unequally.

## Gun Rights

#### Position
Guns are available for purchase by those who are qualified. Background checks and red flag laws aim to keep bad actors from acquiring firearms. Concealed carry requires a higher level of qualification than open carry. Assault weapons are vaguely defined, and handguns are more dangerous because they are easy to conceal. High-ordnance explosives are banned or heavily restricted to construction work. Full automatics are heavily restricted but potentially available to extremely trained or vetted individuals.

> [!question]- "Do red flag laws violate due process?"
> No, when a judge issues the order. A red flag order removes firearms only on a court finding that the person directly imposes a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on others, the forfeiture threshold in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#General Rules of Forfeiture|Section 1]]. It is never an executive declaration. A judge may issue a short emergency order before the hearing, and the person then has a prompt hearing where the order must be justified. The position supports red flag laws as part of qualification, alongside strong support for the right of qualified citizens to own guns.

> [!question]- "Why are handguns treated as more concerning than assault weapons?"
> Assault weapons are vaguely defined, while handguns are more dangerous in practice because they are easy to conceal. In FBI data for 2019, handguns were used in about 91% of firearm murders where the type of gun was known, and rifles of every kind in about 5%. That is why concealed carry requires a higher level of qualification than open carry.

#### Key Elements
- Firearms available to qualified citizens.
- Background checks and red flag laws.
- Stricter standard for concealed carry than open carry.
- Assault weapons viewed as vaguely defined; handguns more concerning.
- High explosives banned or restricted to construction.
- Full automatics heavily restricted, possibly available to highly trained or vetted individuals.

#### Rationale
Self-defense protects sentient rights, and the Second Amendment protects an individual right to keep and bear arms, as held in District of Columbia v. Heller and New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen. Safeguards target the people and weapons most likely to cause harm. RAND's review of the research finds moderate evidence that background checks reduce firearm and total homicides, limited evidence that extreme risk protection (red flag) orders reduce suicides, and inconclusive evidence on their effect on violent crime. It also finds supportive evidence that shall-issue concealed-carry laws increase homicides, which fits the higher bar for concealed carry. In FBI data for 2019, handguns accounted for 6,368 of the 6,977 firearm murders where the type was recorded. Federal law already restricts machine guns heavily, which the position retains with a path for highly vetted individuals.

## Drugs and Vice

#### Position
Marijuana is legal, though widespread use is not condoned. Hard drugs are decriminalized for use, though the position stands very close to banning them. Prostitution is legal, less clandestine, and regulated to prevent exploitation of prostitutes. Consensual, regulated sex work is distinct from trafficking, which is capital ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Human Trafficking|Human Trafficking]]). Gambling is regulated only to keep it from minors; adults may gamble. Driving while intoxicated or under the influence of hard drugs results in license revocation for a minimum of ten years, up to permanently. The drug war focuses less on domestic addicts and more on the offensive obliteration of drug cartels by force, treating them as terroristic entities endangering the country's safety. Joint operations with compliant Central and South American governments are best, and if they do not comply, war with those governments is justified to eradicate the cartel problem. Refusal to act against cartels operating against the United States is treated as hostility, consistent with [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Interventionism vs Non-Intervention|Foreign Policy & International Relations]].

> [!question]- "Is legalizing prostitution compatible with preventing exploitation?"
> Legal prostitution brings the trade into the open, where regulation can address exploitation directly. A clandestine trade leaves workers without protection. Regulation targets the rights violation, exploitation, rather than the voluntary exchange.

> [!question]- "Don't countries that legalize prostitution see more human trafficking?"
> The best cross-country evidence says they do. Cho, Dreher, and Neumayer found that countries with legalized prostitution report larger human trafficking inflows, apparently because a larger market outweighs the shift toward legal workers. Prostitution stays legal because consenting adults may trade, and the framework answers the trafficking risk directly: every business is strictly licensed, every worker's age, identity, and consent are verified, and trafficking is a capital crime ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Human Trafficking|Human Trafficking]]).

#### Key Elements
- Marijuana legal.
- Hard drugs decriminalized for use, with strong reservations.
- Prostitution legal and regulated against exploitation, with strict licensing and verification of every worker.
- Gambling legal for adults, kept from minors.
- DUI with intoxication or hard drugs: license revoked for ten years to life.
- Drug war aimed at cartels as terrorist entities; joint operations or war with non-compliant governments.

#### Rationale
Adult choices that harm only the chooser fall outside the state's coercive role, while driving impaired endangers others and is punished severely. Harsher DUI sanctions measurably reduce repeat offending (Washington State data show drops of up to 17% at the 0.08 threshold); the ten-years-to-life term is a protective judgment beyond what studies have tested. Cartels murder and corrupt on a scale that threatens national security, and the United States designated several cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations in 2025. The Supreme Court's decision in Murphy v. NCAA allowed states to legalize sports betting. Related positions are set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Drug Policy|Criminal Justice & Security]].

## Patriotism and National Identity

#### Position
A patriotic nation that is culturally cohesive in its values is the best result for cohesion. Multiculturalism can work only if the cultures share important common values; many legally immigrated Mexican and South American families are compatible because of shared family and religious values. Islamic and third-world African cultures are judged incompatible with Western society, clashing with values such as human rights and civic responsibility; individual immigrants are judged through values vetting. History is taught unmodified and objective. American exceptionalism rests not on America as such but on what America stands for: liberty, freedom, rights, and great financial opportunity.

> [!question]- "Does cultural compatibility contradict civic nationalism?"
> Civic nationalism unites people around shared values, and cultural compatibility is the condition for sharing them. Cultures that hold the same core values integrate; those that clash with them do not. The test is values, which is why compatible immigrant communities are welcomed.

## Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos

#### Position
Sentient animals within U.S. borders hold negative rights, and the government has a duty to protect them. Deliberately killing a sentient animal is murder, treated the same as in the human context: it goes on the capital track, and the same forfeitures that apply to human murder apply here. Killing an animal in self-defense against an actual attack is not illegal, but hunger or need is not self-defense; only killing outside of self-defense is murder. Animals are graded into classes based on sentience, ranging in severity of the offense. The capital class consists of the high-sentience animals. Lower-sentience animals do not classify as capital, but killing them is still punishable and counts as marks against the offender as a lesser offense. Hunting is murder and is classified as murder: it is an egregious rights violation and belongs on the capital track. Fishing that kills sentient animals is classified the same way. Animal agriculture is abolished, and lab-grown meat replaces it. Companion animals may be kept where their rights are respected. Zoos operate as sanctuaries: animals are kept for their own protection, not for display at their expense, and non-threatening predators held in sanctuary are fed lab-grown meat for the rest of their lives. Confined predators that remain violent and cannot be helped fall under the execution rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]].

> [!question]- "Isn't hunting necessary to control herbivore populations once predators are removed?"
> Removing predators does raise the question of herbivore populations, and the answer cannot be to kill the innocent animals the framework protects. Once predators are removed, herbivore populations are controlled. Predator removal starts with odd-order predators to avoid a trophic cascade that could harm the herbivores below them. Nature is herbivorized through bioengineering, with outcomes scientifically tested: infertility to stop predator species from breeding, bringing the environment fully under human dominion so wilderness no longer exists, and keeping animals in sanctuaries. Many of these methods are still in their infancy and need to be studied. Herbivorizing nature and reducing predation is central to the framework, because predation and sentient rights are so intertwined.

> [!question]- "What herbivorization methods are being proposed?"
> Many of these methods are still in their infancy and need to be studied. Methods proposed for study include:
> - Selective breeding of animals over generations for the ability to live on plants.
> - CRISPR gene drives to herbivorize predators.
> - Tunable gene drives that regulate fertility across species.
> - Single-sex gene drives, so predator species die out painlessly.
> - Supplementary feeding of herbivores to reduce competition.
> - Plants engineered so herbivores cannot overgraze them.
> - Sterilization.
> - Plant-based diets for predators.
> - A model sanctuary for wild animals.
> - AI monitoring of wild animals.
>
> No outside proposal has laid out an order for phasing predators out, so the odd-order predator approach above stands as the plan.

> [!question]- "What about ecosystem collapse, omnivores, and cats?"
> - **Ecosystem collapse.** Nature is not a standard; it is reordered by bioengineering to stop rights violations. Removing predators is paired with herbivore population control (fertility-regulating gene drives, sterilization, AI monitoring), so herbivores do not overshoot and starve.
> - **Omnivory.** Predation is defined by the act, not the species label. Herbivores that kill animals, such as deer eating nestling birds, fall under the same duty and the same herbivorization methods, weighted by the sentience of the animals they kill.
> - **Cats.** Obligate carnivores need specific nutrients, such as taurine, that synthetic or plant-based diets can supply. Each companion animal's diet is set by veterinary judgment of its health, and sentience science decides which prey species count.

> [!question]- "What is the long-term future of predator species?"
> The duty to stop predation applies instantly. Within U.S. borders, predation is a rights violation that the government must stop now, not someday ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]], [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]).
>
> **Realistic transition:** In a realistic world, many systems — like taxes, like education, like all the systems that would be changing — would have some gradual period of change for most of these changes to happen. Here that means the preference, not a mandate, to let predator species slowly go extinct rather than keep predators and constantly breed them, and openness to future science that changes predators' genetic makeup so they become herbivores instead of carnivores, in line with the gene-drive herbivorization methods under study and Pearce's proposal of "rewriting the vertebrate genome" (see Evidence: Herbivorization). That practical lag is a brief aside, not the main position, and it does not soften the duty.

> [!question]- "What about people who hunt or fish to feed their families?"
> No. Killing a sentient animal for food is murder, graded by sentience. Self-defense against an immediate attack remains, as for humans ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Section 1]]). But a predator that can be separated or contained may not be killed for being dangerous by nature; separation and containment come first. There is no subsistence exception, and no Indigenous exception whatsoever.

> [!question]- "How is animal cruelty punished?"
> Under [[#Animal Cruelty Law]]: cruelty to animals in the applicable sentience classes is capital if it causes [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|lasting injury]] and otherwise is egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); below that, cruelty is non-capital but tracked and counts toward the repeat-offender threshold. Companion animals are on the same tiers.

> [!question]- "What about the trade in wild and exotic animals?"
> Capturing wild animals to sell them, and the commercial trade in wild-caught animals, fall under the same ban as commercial breeding mills: they treat sentient beings as stock. Exotic-pet owners are in the same category as any other pet owner: as long as the animals are safe, they may foster and keep them. Anyone who takes an ownerless or wild animal under their responsibility may adopt it and becomes its guardian ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]). Most infectious diseases in people can spread from animals, so the trade also carries a public-health cost.

> [!question]- "Can people keep pets, and what about commercial breeding?"
> Companion animals may be kept where their rights are respected. Commercial breeding mills fall under the animal-agriculture ban: they are banned as the position. Killing a high-sentience companion animal for convenience is murder, and the cruelty tiers apply to companion animals the same as to any other sentient animal. Euthanasia is not murder only when it is for the animal's own sake, never for convenience, on a veterinarian's best-interest finding, and carried out by a veterinary professional, even at home ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]).
>
> > [!note]- Realistic transition
> > In a realistic world, many systems — like taxes, like education, like all the systems that would be changing — would have some gradual period of change for most of these changes to happen. Here that means commercial breeding mills follow the same five-to-ten-year wind-down as animal agriculture ([[9. Energy & Environment#Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat|Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat]]). That lag is a brief aside, not the position.

> [!question]- "Is pest control allowed?"
> Lethal control of sentient pests is allowed only to stop an actual or imminent attack on persons, or a clear disease or rights threat. Non-lethal methods are preferred. Pest control is graded by sentience class, like every other killing of a sentient animal, and preventive extermination of high-sentience animals is not allowed. This follows the self-defense rule above: killing in self-defense against an actual attack is not illegal, but hunger or need is not self-defense.

#### Key Elements
- Sentient animals within U.S. borders hold negative rights; the government's duty to protect covers them.
- Deliberately killing a sentient animal is murder on the capital track, with the same forfeitures as human murder.
- Killing an animal in self-defense against an actual attack is not illegal; hunger or need is not self-defense.
- No subsistence exception and no Indigenous exception whatsoever.
- Herbivore populations are controlled through bioengineering and predation reduction, starting with odd-order predators.
- Animals are graded into classes by sentience: the high-sentience capital class, and lower-sentience classes whose killing is not capital but is still punishable and is one mark, including fish unless the best available science places a species higher.
- Hunting is murder: an egregious rights violation on the capital track. Fishing that kills sentient animals is classified the same way.
- Animal agriculture is abolished; lab-grown meat replaces it.
- Companion animals may be kept where their rights are respected.
- Commercial capture and sale of wild animals banned; exotic-pet owners, like any pet owner, may foster and keep animals as long as they are safe.
- Commercial breeding mills fall under the animal-agriculture ban; killing a high-sentience companion animal for convenience is murder, and cruelty tiers apply.
- Euthanasia is not murder only when it is for the animal's own sake, on a veterinarian's best-interest finding, and carried out by a veterinary professional.
- Euthanasia outside those conditions: genuine non-vet cases with real suffering get a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty; cover cases are cruelty under the tiers; clear intent to harm is murder ([[#Animal Cruelty Law]]).
- Lethal pest control of sentient animals is allowed only to stop an actual or imminent attack on persons or a clear disease or rights threat; non-lethal methods are preferred, control is graded by sentience class, and preventive extermination of high-sentience animals is not allowed.

- Herbivorization methods proposed for study include selective breeding, gene drives, sterilization, supplementary feeding, engineered plants, plant-based diets for predators, a model sanctuary, and AI monitoring.

#### Rationale
Rights rest on sentience, not species ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]). Killing a sentient animal for sport or food violates its rights in the same way the framework treats any other killing of an innocent, so the same murder rules, self-defense exception, and forfeitures apply as in the human context. Grading by sentience sets the severity of the offense. Predation is treated as a rights violation even when the predator lacks agency, and within U.S. borders the government intervenes by placing non-threatening predators in sanctuary or confinement and feeding them lab-grown meat ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]). Sanctuary works for predators because their violence can be subdued: a predator can be contained, separated from other animals, and fed. A violent human, short of permanent sedation, requires far more maintenance and is much harder to manage, which is why violent humans face the forfeiture tracks rather than sanctuary. Herbivore populations and sanctuary lands receive priority protection ([[9. Energy & Environment#Regulation and Conservation|Energy & Environment]]). About 15.9 million people held paid hunting licenses in 2024, according to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, so ending hunting is a large change. The evidence for herbivorization is set out below.

#### Evidence: Herbivorization
Jeff McMahan argues that "we have reason to desire the extinction of all carnivorous species," provided herbivorous species replace them "without ecological upheaval involving more harm than would be prevented." David Pearce proposes "reprogramming or phasing out" predators: he writes that the abolitionist project "entails ecosystem redesign, cross-species immunocontraception, marine nanorobots, rewriting the vertebrate genome," and names "the extinction - or genetically-driven behavioural modification - of members of the cat family" as its most controversial case. Removing predators has already helped native species on islands: across 251 eradications of invasive mammals on 181 islands, 236 native species benefited and 7 were harmed, and four threatened species improved their IUCN category while none got worse (Jones et al. 2016). New Zealand's Predator Free 2050 program, which targets possums, rats, and mustelids, had over 2.5 million hectares under sustained suppression and 122,110 hectares free of all three target groups and defended against reinvasion as of June 2025; two of its seven 2025 goals were achieved. Contraception works on single herds but lowers numbers slowly: PZP immunocontraception of the Assateague horses was 92–100% effective each year and reached zero population growth within 2 years, but the herd fell only 22.8% (175 to 135) by year 11 because treated mares lived longer. No gene drive has been released into the wild in any vertebrate: the two most advanced vertebrate systems, both in house mice, have not yet reached full proof of concept in the laboratory, according to the 2026 Gene Drive Monitor survey. Without population control, removing predators shifts herbivores to food limitation: they increase, overgraze, and vegetation declines (Hairston, Smith & Slobodkin 1960). That is why herbivore control goes together with predator removal.

##### Case Study: Yellowstone Wolves
Wolves were eradicated from Yellowstone by the 1920s. Park managers held elk to about 3,000 by culling until 1969; after culling stopped, elk peaked at about 22,000 in 1994, and between 1995 and 1997, 41 wolves were reintroduced. A 21-year study of 113 aspen stands found that elk density was 8.6 to 9.5 times stronger than wolf predation risk as a driver of browsing, and about 20 to 28 times stronger for young aspen growing out of elk reach: herbivore numbers, not elk's fear of wolves, drove both the damage and the recovery (Brice et al. 2025). Recovery took more than a decade: browsing did not begin to decline until 2008. Lower elk numbers alone did not restore willows. In a 20-year experiment, willows reached the heights of a restored community only where simulated beaver dams were present and browsing was reduced, and a reorganized community of large herbivores, with bison as a relatively invulnerable prey species, suppressed willow growth (Hobbs et al. 2024). Yellowstone supports controlling the whole herbivore system: every herbivore species, not one, along with the conditions plants need to recover.

## Animal Cruelty Law

#### Position
Cruelty to a sentient animal is a rights violation and a crime. Animal cruelty is any of the following: 1. extreme violence; 2. intentional neglect, a chosen pattern of keeping it up; 3. beating without drawing blood; 4. psychological abuse, withholding food, water, or shelter, not out of neglect but as deliberate punishment. Beating means force beyond ordinary discipline: a closed fist, an object, or repeated strikes. A swat on the rear as ordinary discipline is not beating. If any of these acts is done to a human or to an animal in the higher sentience tier, it is capital if it causes [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|lasting injury]], physical or psychological; otherwise it is egregious, the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|egregious tier]]: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Marks are uniform across victims: the same act carries the same marks whether the victim is a person or a higher-sentience animal. Lesser cruelty, harm to a higher-sentience animal outside the four acts and without lasting injury, such as a single strike causing pain or abandonment, is two marks; cruelty to a lower-sentience animal is one mark. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is not capital; it counts as marks against the offender. The same intent test that governs crimes against humans applies: what matters is whether the offender acted voluntarily and knew what they were doing. Moral agency is irrelevant to whether a violation occurred, but where agency is absent, no forfeiture-based punishment follows. The same sentience-based classes used for killing animals apply: cruelty to high-sentience animals in the capital class is capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise is egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor), and cruelty to lower-sentience animals is a lesser offense that counts as marks against the offender. Animal cruelty counts toward the repeat-offender threshold, with its weight depending on severity. Extremely severe, violent cruelty that causes lasting injury is capital even when only one animal is harmed, just as violent torture of a single human is capital; it does not require large numbers. Torturing large numbers of animals is likewise capital outright, with no lesser classification. Mass neglect, meaning neglect of multiple animals at once or a sustained pattern, is capital when it causes lasting injury, physical or psychological, and otherwise egregious (three marks); intentional neglect of a single higher-sentience animal without lasting injury is egregious (three marks), the same as for a person, and neglect of a lower-sentience animal counts as marks. Ritual slaughter is banned, with no religious exemption. Euthanasia that meets the living-animal rule in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]] is not cruelty. Euthanasia that breaks those conditions is graded on a sliding scale: a genuine case of real suffering where the guardian skips the vet, the convenience being the vet trip and not the killing, carries a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty; a cover case with no real suffering is cruelty under these tiers, capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); clear intent to harm is murder.

Animals cannot consent to sexual activity. All sexual acts with sentient animals are banned; the ban itself makes no distinction between forced and non-forced acts. Forced acts are graded under the cruelty tiers; non-forced acts carry a separate, lesser penalty. The ban follows from the consent principle in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Age of Consent|Age of Consent]].

> [!question]- "Shouldn't cruelty to animals be punished less severely than violence against people?"
> Sentient animals hold the same negative rights as people, so the same cruelty acts get the same penalties against a higher-sentience animal as against a human: capital if they cause lasting injury, egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor) otherwise. Severity is graded by the animal's sentience, not by species membership.

> [!question]- "How is animal cruelty punished?"
> Any of the four cruelty acts (extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment) against a higher-sentience animal is capital if it causes lasting injury, even when only one animal is harmed, the same as against a human. Without lasting injury it is egregious, the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|egregious tier]]: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is non-capital but tracked in the system, and cruelty convictions count toward the repeat-offender threshold. This matches the murder rules in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]] and the threshold in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]], and replaces treatment of animal cruelty as an ordinary crime. Companion animals are included on the same tiers ([[#Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos]]).

> [!question]- "Why ban it if the animal isn't forced?"
> Animals cannot consent. With non-consenting people, the main problem is psychological damage, which can be severe, especially for the young. That harm dynamic is not exactly the same with animals. Even though animals can't consent, if they are not being sexualized in an abusive manner, the sexual activity itself does not create psychological danger to the animal, and sexual activity is a strong biological drive for them. So there is a difference between forced bestiality, where someone forces an animal, penetrative or otherwise, and cases where someone lets an animal do something to them. It is not as clear-cut as human sexual consent. This framework does not see a problem with the second category in itself. But societally, if any form were allowed, it would be too much of a gray area for people to abuse animals. So the ban is flat and covers all sexual acts with sentient animals.

> [!note]- Penalties
> Forced sexual acts with sentient animals are cruelty and are graded under the cruelty tiers above, by severity and sentience class. Non-forced acts are still banned and carry a separate, lesser penalty: one mark, a fixed-term labor sentence with mandatory rehabilitation and a known end date, a fine of about 5 percent of the offender's own existing assets, with a minimum of \$1,000, lighter than forced acts, which go through the cruelty tiers; the fine goes first to the animal's care, then to the victim restitution fund, and never to the seizing agency, and a ban on keeping animals. Fines can be substantial.
> Bestiality and neglect are separate harms: the sex act is one violation of the animal's dignity, and neglect is a failure of care on top of it. Marks stack only when both are charged, so the same suffering is not punished twice. Neglect is charged separately only when it is a distinct failure of care, not the harm of the act itself. Forced bestiality follows the same rule: the force is graded under the cruelty tiers (capital with lasting injury, otherwise egregious, three marks), and distinct neglect stacks on top.

#### Key Elements
- Animal cruelty is extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood (force beyond ordinary discipline), or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment. Any of these against a human or a higher-sentience animal is capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise is egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); against a lower-sentience animal it counts as marks.
- The intent test applies, as for crimes against humans.
- Where agency is absent there is no forfeiture-based punishment; executing a persistently insane, violent offender who makes no progress is removal of an unchangeable threat, not forfeiture ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Insanity Defense]]).
- Sentience-based classes: cruelty to the high-sentience class is capital if it causes lasting injury, otherwise egregious (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); cruelty to lower-sentience animals counts as marks against the offender.
- Animal cruelty counts toward the three-mark repeat-offender threshold, weighted by severity.
- Marks are uniform across victims: the same act carries the same marks against a person or a higher-sentience animal. Lesser cruelty to higher-sentience animals outside the four acts is two marks; cruelty to lower-sentience animals is one mark.
- Extremely severe, violent cruelty that causes lasting injury is capital even against a single animal; it does not require large numbers.
- Mass neglect, meaning neglect of multiple animals at once or a sustained pattern, is capital when it causes lasting injury, physical or psychological, and otherwise egregious (three marks); intentional neglect of a single higher-sentience animal without lasting injury is egregious (three marks), the same as for a person, and neglect of a lower-sentience animal counts as marks.
- Ritual slaughter banned; no religious exemption ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Ritual Slaughter and Animal Sacrifice|Religion & Secularism]]).
- Flat ban on all sexual acts with sentient animals, forced or not; animals cannot consent. Forced acts under the cruelty tiers; non-forced acts a separate, lesser penalty: one mark, fixed-term labor with mandatory rehabilitation, a fine of about 5 percent of the offender's own existing assets (minimum \$1,000) that goes first to the animal's care, then to the victim restitution fund, never the seizing agency, and a ban on keeping animals.
- Bestiality and neglect are separate harms: the sex act is one violation of the animal's dignity, and neglect is a failure of care on top of it. Marks stack only when both are charged, so the same suffering is not punished twice. Neglect is charged separately only when it is a distinct failure of care, not the harm of the act itself. Forced bestiality follows the same rule: the force is graded under the cruelty tiers (capital with lasting injury, otherwise egregious, three marks), and distinct neglect stacks on top.

#### Rationale
Sentient animals hold negative rights within U.S. borders, so deliberately inflicting suffering on one is an outward rights violation, which is what the framework's criminal law punishes ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]). Because those rights are the same rights humans hold, the same cruelty acts that are capital against a human are capital against a higher-sentience animal, with severity graded by sentience under [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]. Religious practice does not exempt anyone from rights protections ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Ritual Slaughter and Animal Sacrifice|Religion & Secularism]]). Federal law already criminalizes certain acts of animal crushing and torture under the Preventing Animal Cruelty and Torture Act (18 U.S.C. § 48).
