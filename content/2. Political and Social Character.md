## Civilizational Nationalism

#### Position
Priority is given to cultural cohesion, high-trust social conditions, national sovereignty, and strong borders. The state's primary duties are to its own citizens and to the long-term continuity of the political community. Immigration is selective and oriented toward compatibility with the shared Western value structure, judged at the country level and through values vetting of every immigrant, and cultural assimilation is paramount. The border is secured, with lethal force used when necessary against violent or armed crossings. "Western" names a value structure, not an ethnicity, so compatibility is open to anyone who holds those values.

> [!question]- "Isn't nationalism just ethnic exclusion?"
> No. The community is defined by shared values: individual negative rights, liberty, religious freedom with separation of church and state, the rule of law, civic responsibility, and economic opportunity. People of any ethnicity who hold those values are compatible, and compatibility judgments govern admission only; they impose no legal penalty for belief on anyone already within the community.

#### Key Elements
- Cultural cohesion, high trust, sovereignty, and strong borders.
- The state's first duties are to its own citizens.
- Selective immigration based on compatibility with Western values.
- Assimilation paramount.
- Lethal force when necessary against violent or armed crossings.
- Compatibility defined by values, not ethnicity.

#### Rationale
The detailed immigration position is set out in [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations]], border enforcement in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Border Security and Immigration Enforcement|Criminal Justice & Security]], and the definition of Western compatibility in [[#Western Values and Compatibility]]. Assimilation and national identity are developed in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Patriotism and National Identity|Cultural & Social Issues]], and English as the common civic language reflects Executive Order 14224, which designated English the official language of the United States in 2025.

##### Sources

>[!tldr]- Summary
> The country puts its own citizens first, keeps strong borders, and admits immigrants who share Western values and assimilate. Those values, not ethnicity, define who fits.

## Economic Orientation

#### Position
The economy is free-market leaning, with low and simple taxation built on a flat income tax with a large zero bracket. Mandatory welfare and large-scale redistribution are rejected in favor of an opt-in model. Subsidies to any business or industry require taxpayer opt-in, industrial policy is minimal and tightly justified, and the burden of proof rests on every regulation rather than on the people regulated. Budgets are balanced, with defense the top spending priority.

> [!question]- "Without mandatory welfare, what happens to people in need?"
> The welfare model is opt-in, and emergency protections remain, such as emergency shelters and emergency medical care set out in the detailed domestic policy section. What is rejected is compulsory redistribution as the default, not help for people in genuine emergencies.

#### Key Elements
- Free markets as the default.
- Flat income tax with a large zero bracket.
- Opt-in welfare instead of mandatory redistribution.
- No business subsidies without taxpayer opt-in.
- Burden of proof on regulation.
- Balanced budgets, with defense first.

#### Rationale
The tax, welfare, and regulatory positions are set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance|Domestic Policy & Governance]], including [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]], [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Infrastructure and Regulation|Infrastructure and Regulation]], and [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Fiscal Policy|Fiscal Policy]]. The no-subsidy rule is applied to energy in [[9. Energy & Environment#Subsidies and Mandates|Energy & Environment]]. The scale of business subsidy the rule removes is visible in agriculture: direct government payments to U.S. farm producers totaled about \$54 billion in 2020, measured in 2024 dollars, much of it pandemic assistance, according to USDA's Economic Research Service.

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Free markets, a flat tax with a large zero bracket, opt-in welfare, no business subsidies without opt-in, regulation that must justify itself, and balanced budgets with defense first.

## Cultural Stance

#### Position
The nuclear family and traditional social patterns are the preferred default, without legal mandates for private life; same-sex marriage is legal, and divorce is allowed. Identity politics and DEI frameworks are opposed, and people are treated on merit. Free speech is near-absolute, with narrow exceptions for true threats and incitement to imminent violence. Islamism and communism are condemned in the government's official voice, with no official platforms, public funding, or public honors, while the law restricts them only as it restricts any other ideology.

> [!question]- "How can the government condemn an ideology and still protect free speech?"
> Condemnation is the government speaking for itself; it is not a restriction on anyone else. Citizens keep full freedom to hold and express these views. What they do not get is official endorsement, public funding, or public honors.

#### Key Elements
- Nuclear family as the preferred default, not a legal mandate.
- Same-sex marriage legal; divorce allowed.
- Opposition to identity politics and DEI; treatment on merit.
- Near-absolute free speech, except true threats and incitement.
- Official condemnation of Islamism and communism without legal penalty for belief.

#### Rationale
The family, speech, and merit positions are set out in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Family and Gender|Family and Gender]], [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Free Speech and Censorship|Free Speech and Censorship]], [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Race and Diversity|Race and Diversity]], and [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]. The treatment of Islamism is developed in [[4. Religion & Secularism#Islam and Extremism|Religion & Secularism]]. The free speech exceptions track the narrow categories the Supreme Court has recognized; in *Brandenburg v. Ohio* (1969) the Court held that advocacy may be punished only when directed to inciting or producing imminent lawless action and likely to incite or produce such action.

## Order and Security

#### Position
The orientation on crime is hardline. Murder (including attempted murder), violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking carry a mandatory death penalty under a strict evidentiary standard, repeat offenders who reach three marks forfeit the right to life, with the victim choosing execution, experimentation, or life with labor (a judge decides when victims disagree, the victim has died, or the victim is an animal), sentences for first and second offenses are greatly increased in length, every custodial sentence is served as compelled labor, idle permanent incarceration is abolished, and cartels and chronic rights violators face decisive action. Substantial state coercive power is accepted as necessary to suppress recurrent predation and disorder, with independent oversight of police. Islamist and communist ideologies are treated as civilizational and security threats, monitored only through public data and lawful process, with no legal penalty for belief.

> [!question]- "Doesn't this much state power threaten the innocent?"
> The power is aimed at those who have violated rights, and it operates under due process: a strict evidentiary standard, compensation when convictions are vacated, and independent auditors of police conduct. Belief alone is never punished.

#### Key Elements
- Mandatory death penalty for murder (including attempted murder), violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking under a strict evidentiary standard.
- Forfeiture of the right to life for repeat offenders at three marks; the victim chooses execution, experimentation, or life with labor. When multiple victims disagree, when the victim has died, or when the victim is an animal, a judge makes the decision instead of the victim.
- Longer sentences served as compelled hard labor; grave abuse without lasting injury is egregious: three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor; lesser crimes carry one or two marks and a fixed term; no idle permanent incarceration.
- Decisive action against cartels and chronic violators.
- Independent oversight of police.
- Ideological threats monitored through public data only; no penalty for belief.

#### Rationale
These positions are set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security|Criminal Justice & Security]], including [[6. Repeat offending is common: among prisoners released from 34 states in 2012, 62% were rearrested within three years and 71% within five, according to the Bureau of Justice Statistics.

## The Future and Technology

#### Position
The orientation is techno-optimist within sentientist constraints. AI, genetic technologies, brain-computer interfaces, and privatized civilian spaceflight are supported, with private innovation preferred and government limited to regulating existential threats, rights violations, and national security. Data trading is opt-in only. Animal testing is banned, with experimentation redirected to condemned offenders whose rights are forfeited. Any emergent AI sentience is recognized under the ethical framework and receives full protection, but remains subject to decisive termination if it violates or imminently threatens rights.

> [!question]- "Is it consistent to recognize AI sentience and still allow termination?"
> Yes. The same forfeiture rules apply to every sentient being. A sentient AI holds rights like any other, and like any other it forfeits them if it violates or imminently threatens the rights of others.

#### Key Elements
- Support for AI, genetic technologies, BCIs, and private spaceflight.
- Private innovation preferred; regulation limited to existential, rights, and security risks.
- Opt-in data trading.
- Animal testing banned; experimentation redirected to forfeited offenders.
- Sentient AI protected, and subject to the same forfeiture rules.

## Western Values and Compatibility

"Western" names a value structure rather than an ethnicity or a geography: individual negative rights, liberty, religious freedom with separation of church and state, the rule of law, civic responsibility, and economic opportunity. Compatibility means holding those values. It is judged at the country level through a presumption against admitting migrants from places where Islamist movements govern or where survey data show majority support for state-enforced religious law, and at the individual level through values vetting of every immigrant (see [[4. Religion & Secularism#Islam and Extremism|Religion & Secularism]] and [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]). Compatibility judgments govern admission to the political community; they impose no legal penalty for belief on anyone already within it.

The values in that list are the ones the rest of the framework already depends on. Individual negative rights and liberty are the core of [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]. Religious freedom with separation of church and state is set out in [[4. Religion & Secularism#Role of Religion in Government|Religion & Secularism]]. The rule of law and civic responsibility appear in [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Due Process and Rule of Law|Authority & Civil Liberties]], and economic opportunity in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Economic System|Domestic Policy & Governance]]. A person who holds these values is compatible whatever their ancestry. A person who rejects them is not, whatever their ancestry.

Because the test is about values, it cuts in both directions. Many cultures outside Europe and North America are judged compatible; many legally immigrated Mexican and other Latin American communities are named as examples in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Patriotism and National Identity|Cultural & Social Issues]]. Cultures whose governing norms clash with these values, such as Islamist movements that seek state-enforced religious law, are judged incompatible. That judgment is aimed at the value structure, and it is applied to individuals through vetting rather than assumed from origin alone.

Christianity's historical role is acknowledged as part of how these values developed in the West, while scripture is never mandated ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Christianity and Western Heritage|Religion & Secularism]]). The standard is the values themselves, which a citizen can hold as a Christian, a member of another faith, or an atheist.

## Relation to Other Political Traditions

#### Position
I do not file this framework under an existing label, but it is fair to say where it stands. I agree with classical liberalism and libertarianism on negative rights, private property, free markets, and a state limited to protecting people from force and fraud ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]; [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance|Domestic Policy & Governance]]). I part from libertarianism in accepting far more coercive power against those who violate rights: the mandatory death penalty ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]), strict borders with lethal force against violent or armed crossings ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]), and decisive force against organized threats. National conservatism, as most people use the term, means being fiscally conservative, socially traditional, and pro-military, with an emphasis on cultural cohesion and national identity. I share that commitment to fiscal restraint, a strong military, assimilation, patriotism, and national interest in trade ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Patriotism and National Identity|Cultural & Social Issues]]; [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Global Institutions|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]), but I ground the shared culture in a structure of values open to people of any ethnicity rather than in religious or ancestral heritage, I keep church and state separate and tax churches ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Religious Exemptions|Religion & Secularism]]), and I recognize same-sex marriage ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|Cultural & Social Issues]]). What sets this framework apart from all three is sentientism: rights rest on sentience rather than species, which extends protection to animals and to any truly sentient artificial being ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]).

> [!question]- "Isn't this just national conservatism with a libertarian economic policy?"
> No. The movement's own statement of principles calls for public life rooted in Christianity in nations with a Christian majority and for public support of the traditional family. I keep church and state separate, tax churches, and recognize same-sex marriage. Grounding rights in sentience, meanwhile, has no counterpart in any of the three traditions.

#### Key Elements
- I align with libertarianism and national conservatism on fiscal restraint, the role of government, tradition, strong military, and cohesion.
- I align with classical liberalism on marriage freedom and the separation of church and state.
- I reject a mandatory welfare state, forced redistribution, tradition that violates sentient rights, and the progressive left's social agenda.
- I ground rights in sentience, which is unique.
- I am stricter on rights violations and crime than most of the political spectrum.
- No existing label fits the whole framework.

#### Rationale
The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes liberalism as a tradition in which liberals accord liberty primacy as a political value, with a presumption in favor of liberty that puts the burden of justification on anyone who would restrict it. That is the same burden I place on regulation ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Infrastructure and Regulation|Domestic Policy & Governance]]). The Encyclopedia describes libertarianism as a family of views built on strong rights of self-ownership and property and a correspondingly minimal state. In everyday usage, national conservatism names a fiscally conservative, socially traditional, pro-military outlook centered on cultural cohesion and national identity, and that everyday sense is the one I mostly share. The national conservative movement's formal Statement of Principles, published in 2022, goes further: it calls for public life rooted in Christianity in nations with a Christian majority and for public support of the traditional family, which is where I part ways. Measured against those definitions, my economic and rights positions sit closest to classical liberalism, my security and immigration positions closest to national conservatism, and my religious and sentientist positions apart from all three.

##### Sources
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)
- Edmund Burke Foundation – [National Conservatism: A Statement of Principles](https://nationalconservatism.org/national-conservatism-a-statement-of-principles/)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> I share the liberal and libertarian commitment to rights, property, and markets, but I accept far more force against criminals and at the border. I share national conservatism's emphasis on fiscal restraint, a strong military, assimilation, and patriotism, but I base culture on values rather than heritage and keep religion out of government. Grounding rights in sentience sets this framework apart from all three.
