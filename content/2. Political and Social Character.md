## Civilizational Nationalism

Priority is given to cultural cohesion, high-trust social conditions, national sovereignty, and strong borders. Immigration policy should be selective and oriented toward cultural compatibility. The state’s primary duties are to its own citizens and the long-term continuity of the political community.

## Economic Orientation 

Free-market leaning with a preference for low, simple taxation (including flat tax structures). Mandatory welfare and large-scale redistribution are viewed with skepticism (see [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance|Domestic Policy & Governance]] for the specific position). Subsidies require taxpayer opt-in, and industrial policy should be minimal and tightly justified.

## Cultural Stance

Preference for the nuclear family and traditional social patterns as the preferred default, without requiring legal mandates for private life. Strong opposition to identity politics and DEI frameworks. Free speech is treated as a near-absolutist value, with narrow exceptions for direct threats and incitement to imminent violence. Islamism and communism are condemned in the government's official voice, with no official platforms, public funding, or public honors, while the law restricts them only as it restricts any other ideology.

## Order and Security 

Hardline orientation on crime: expanded use of imprisonment, support for the death penalty in serious cases, and decisive action against cartels and chronic rights violators. Substantial state coercive power is accepted as necessary to suppress recurrent tendencies toward predation and disorder. Islamist and communist ideologies are treated as civilizational and security threats due to their systematic hostility to the Western rights-based framework; they are monitored only through public data and lawful process, with no legal penalty for belief.

## The Future and Technology 

Techno-optimist within the bounds of the prescribed sentientist constraints. Support for AI, genetic technologies, and related advances. Any emergent AI sentience is recognized under the ethical framework but remains subject to decisive termination if it violates or imminently threatens rights, posing a systemic threat to the rights-respecting order.

## Western Values and Compatibility

"Western" names a value structure rather than an ethnicity or a geography: individual negative rights, liberty, religious freedom with separation of church and state, the rule of law, civic responsibility, and economic opportunity. Compatibility means holding those values. It is judged at the country level through a presumption against admitting migrants from places where Islamist movements govern or where survey data show majority support for state-enforced religious law, and at the individual level through values vetting of every immigrant (see [[4. Religion & Secularism#Islam and Extremism|Religion & Secularism]] and [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]). Compatibility judgments govern admission to the political community; they impose no legal penalty for belief on anyone already within it.

## Relation to Other Political Traditions

#### Position
I do not file this framework under an existing label, but it is fair to say where it stands. I agree with classical liberalism and libertarianism on negative rights, private property, free markets, and a state limited to protecting people from force and fraud ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]; [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance|Domestic Policy & Governance]]). I part from libertarianism in accepting far more coercive power against those who violate rights: the mandatory death penalty ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]), strict borders with lethal force against violent or armed crossings ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]), and decisive force against organized threats. National conservatism, as most people use the term, means being fiscally conservative, socially traditional, and pro-military, with an emphasis on cultural cohesion and national identity. I share that commitment to fiscal restraint, a strong military, assimilation, patriotism, and national interest in trade ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Patriotism and National Identity|Cultural & Social Issues]]; [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Global Institutions|Foreign Policy & International Relations]]), but I ground the shared culture in a structure of values open to people of any ethnicity rather than in religious or ancestral heritage, I keep church and state separate and tax churches ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Religious Exemptions|Religion & Secularism]]), and I recognize same-sex marriage ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|Cultural & Social Issues]]). What sets this framework apart from all three is sentientism: rights rest on sentience rather than species, which extends protection to animals and to any truly sentient artificial being ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]).

> [!question]- "Isn't this just national conservatism with a libertarian economic policy?"
> No. The movement's own statement of principles calls for public life rooted in Christianity in nations with a Christian majority and for public support of the traditional family. I keep church and state separate, tax churches, and recognize same-sex marriage. Grounding rights in sentience, meanwhile, has no counterpart in any of the three traditions.

#### Key Elements
- I align with libertarianism and national conservatism on fiscal restraint, the role of government, tradition, strong military, and cohesion.
- I align with classical liberalism on marriage freedom and the separation of church and state.
- I reject a mandatory welfare state, forced redistribution, tradition that violates sentient rights, and the progressive left's social agenda.
- I ground rights in sentience, which is unique.
- I am stricter on rights violations and crime than most of the political spectrum.
- No existing label fits the whole framework.

#### Rationale
The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes liberalism as a tradition in which liberals accord liberty primacy as a political value, with a presumption in favor of liberty that puts the burden of justification on anyone who would restrict it. That is the same burden I place on regulation ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Infrastructure and Regulation|Domestic Policy & Governance]]). The Encyclopedia describes libertarianism as a family of views built on strong rights of self-ownership and property and a correspondingly minimal state. In everyday usage, national conservatism names a fiscally conservative, socially traditional, pro-military outlook centered on cultural cohesion and national identity, and that everyday sense is the one I mostly share. The national conservative movement's formal Statement of Principles, published in 2022, goes further: it calls for public life rooted in Christianity in nations with a Christian majority and for public support of the traditional family, which is where I part ways. Measured against those definitions, my economic and rights positions sit closest to classical liberalism, my security and immigration positions closest to national conservatism, and my religious and sentientist positions apart from all three.
