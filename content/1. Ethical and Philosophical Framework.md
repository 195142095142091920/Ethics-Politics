## **Normative Ethics**

#### Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)  

#### Axioms

1. I prescribe that subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) shall serve as the basis for moral status and the possession of negative rights.

2. Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer under this framework. Higher-order self-awareness is not required.

3. A being on an undisturbed developmental trajectory toward sentience (e.g., a healthy fetus in a living womb) shall be granted significant moral weight proportional to the robustness of that trajectory.

4. A being that once possessed sentience retains residual moral consideration. If sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority transfers to next-of-kin, guided by respect for the former being’s known wishes and dignity.

5. Practical determination of sentience shall follow the best available scientific consensus. Where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with these prescriptions.

6. Under this framework, sentient beings are to be granted strong negative rights against interference and harm. These rights function as side-constraints that may be overridden only when a clear threshold of rights violation by the agent has been crossed (rights forfeiture). The direct imposition of severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on others counts as crossing that threshold.

7. All moral and political rules within this system must remain consistent with the prescribed primacy of subjective experience. This framework is not claimed as an objective moral fact, but as a coherent system I choose to endorse and seek to enforce.

#### Summary

- **Sentientist:**
    1. Moral status and negative rights attach to beings capable of first-order subjective experience, or those on a clear developmental trajectory toward it.
    
       - Wikipedia - [Sentientism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentientism) 

- **Negative-rights focused:** 
    1. Rights function as side-constraints against interference and harm. Positive rights that compel others to provide goods or services are generally rejected. Minimal tax-funded basic services (education, public safety, infrastructure) are accepted as a narrow practical exception.
       
       - Wikipedia - [Negative and Positive Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_rights) 

- **Threshold deontologist:** 
    1. Core rules against violating sentient rights normally hold. They may be overridden only after a clear threshold of rights violation is crossed, at which point the violator forfeits corresponding protections. Fetal moral weight scales with proximity to sentience and is grounded primarily in the responsibility created by voluntary procreation. Duties to sustain another being arise only from voluntary responsibility; where no voluntary act created the dependency, as in rape, withdrawing support differs from killing.
       
       - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/)


#### Sentience Determination

Whether a being is sentient is determined by the best available scientific consensus (Axiom 5). Under Axiom 2, any being with first-order subjective experience is a rights-bearer, so innocent animals of every kind, including fish and insects, receive sentience protections. Within that protection, moral weight is applied on a sliding scale: it rises with the strength of the evidence for subjective experience and with the richness of that experience, so mammals and birds weigh more heavily than fish, and fish more heavily than insects. Everyday accidental harm cannot be policed, so enforcement targets deliberate, unnecessary killing: stepping on a bug is not the target, exterminating a hive for fun is. Placement on the scale is revised as the evidence changes, and artificial systems are assessed by the same standard.

Bioengineering of animals follows the stance already taken on predation and herbivores. Herbivores can live without harming other sentient beings, so their populations receive priority, and genetic modification of animals is carried out only to advance their sentient-rights protections, never to violate them ([[9. Energy & Environment#Regulation and Conservation|Energy & Environment]], [[10. Bioethics, Progress, & Technology#Genetic Engineering and CRISPR|Bioethics, Progress, & Technology]]).

The 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness found strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds and at least a realistic possibility of it in all vertebrates, including fishes, and in many invertebrates, including insects. The United Kingdom's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 extends its protections to all vertebrates other than humans, plus cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans.

- New York University - [The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024)](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration)
- UK Legislation - [Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022, section 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/22/section/5)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness/)

#### Wild-Animal Predation

Predation is a rights violation regardless of moral agency. A predator that kills a sentient animal violates that animal's rights even though the predator lacks the foresight and voluntary control of an agent. Agency governs responsibility and forfeiture, not whether a violation occurred: a being that lacks agency at the act does not forfeit its own rights by that act the way a deliberate agent does, but the victim's rights are violated all the same ([[#Minors and Incompetent Adults]]). Permanent unremediable dangerous incapacity is handled under [[#Free Will and Responsibility]] and [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Insanity Defense]]: confinement, then execution if no progress — not as classic act-forfeiture.

Within U.S. borders, the government has a duty to intervene against predation to protect all sentient animals. Carnivores that prey on innocent animals must be stopped, or, if non-threatening, placed in sanctuary or confinement and fed lab-grown meat for the rest of their lives. A confined predator that remains violent, shows no sign of change, and cannot be helped falls under the rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]: it deserves to die. Outside U.S. borders, intervening against predation is a virtue, not a duty. Herbivores, which can live without harming other sentient beings, continue to receive priority in conservation, habitat, and sanctuary policy ([[9. Energy & Environment#Regulation and Conservation|Energy & Environment]]).

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [The Moral Status of Animals](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/)

## **Animal Rights and Sentientism**

#### Position
Animal agriculture and aquaculture should be banned outright and instantly, because that is how the framework stays sentientist. The philosophy itself is not phased in. The same two-layer rule governs every animal topic on this site: the ban is primary; any real-world lag is secondary.

> [!note]- Realistic transition
> The ban is the position. In a realistic world, many systems — like taxes, like education, like all the systems that would be changing — would have some gradual period of change for most of these changes to happen. Existing animal-agriculture, wild commercial fishing, and aquaculture operations close on a five-to-ten-year wind-down, with new expansion banned immediately and transition support (job placement or retraining) for displaced workers, not for the industry. Grazing permits and operating licenses are not honored commitments. The step-by-step plans sit in [[9. Energy & Environment#Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat|Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat]] and [[9. Energy & Environment#Commercial Fishing and Aquaculture|Commercial Fishing and Aquaculture]].

Sentience exists on a gradient across species, and that gradient sets the moral weight of a being under the same scale used for murder in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]. Animals are graded into sentience classes: deliberately killing a member of a high-sentience class outside self-defense is murder, an egregious rights violation on the capital track with the same forfeitures as the murder of a human, and killing a member of a lower-sentience class counts as a mark against the offender. Fish follow the same gradient, with fish sentience determined by the best available science. Moral agency does not decide whether a rights violation occurred: predation by an animal violates its victim's rights even though the predator is not a moral agent.

Hunting is murder. There is no subsistence or Indigenous exception. Self-defense applies only against an actual attack.

Commercial fishing and aquaculture of sentient animals are banned outright and instantly, with rights protections for fish phased in as they are developed. Shellfish and bivalves are covered with no sentience exception. Bycatch and incidental marine kills are banned, and seafood imports are banned. The Section 9 fishing rules are an application of this topic, not a separate ethic.

Animal cruelty is equivalent to human torture and kidnapping and is graded by sentience class. Extremely severe, violent cruelty to applicable animals is capital, even when only one animal is harmed; it does not require large numbers. Cruelty below that threshold is not capital, but it is tracked and counts toward the repeat-offender threshold, weighted by severity. Ritual slaughter is banned outright.

The duty to protect covers all sentient animals. Within U.S. borders, the government has a duty to intervene to stop rights violations against animals; protecting animals elsewhere is a virtue but not a duty of the American government. Predation and herbivore population control are decided in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos|Cultural & Social Issues]] and in [[#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]; this topic is the authoritative statement those pages apply.

Animals cannot consent.

While an animal is alive, decisions about its care are made by a guardian acting for the animal, not by next-of-kin; the next-of-kin rule in Axiom 4 applies only after sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost. The owner is the guardian by default and decides routine vet care. Euthanasia and other major decisions are allowed only for the animal's own sake, such as ending suffering, never for convenience, and only on a veterinarian's finding that it is in that animal's best interest. Even at home, if the guardian believes the animal is suffering, euthanasia still requires a veterinary professional, just as assisted death for humans requires a medical professional ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Assisted Death|Assisted Death]]).

#### Key Elements
- Two-layer rule for all animal topics: outright, instant ban as the primary position; gradual real-world change as a brief aside (like taxes or education).
- Sentience on a gradient across species, on the same scale as the murder section; fish included.
- Deliberately killing a high-sentience animal outside self-defense is murder on the capital track; lower-sentience killings count as marks against the offender. Euthanasia is not murder only when all four conditions hold: for the animal's own sake, never convenience; on a veterinarian's best-interest finding; and carried out by a veterinary professional, even at home.
- Hunting is murder, with no subsistence exception.
- Commercial fishing and aquaculture banned outright and instantly; shellfish covered; bycatch and seafood imports banned.
- Animal cruelty tiered by severity: extremely severe violent cruelty is capital; lesser cruelty is tracked and counts toward repeat offending.
- Duty to protect animals within U.S. borders; predation and herbivore control decided in Section 7 and Wild-Animal Predation.
- Animals cannot consent.
- Living animals: decisions are made by a guardian, the owner by default for routine vet care; euthanasia and major decisions only for the animal's own sake, never convenience, on a veterinarian's best-interest finding, and carried out by a veterinary professional. Next-of-kin applies only after sentience is permanently lost.

## **Moral Psychology**

#### Theory of Agency 

A being becomes a full moral agent under this framework when it possesses sufficient first-order subjective experience combined with the capacity for intentional action and foresight of consequences. Agency is not binary in all cases; it scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication. Once agency is present, the being can generate duties through voluntary choices (most clearly seen in the responsibility trigger of voluntary procreation) and can forfeit rights through deliberate violations of others’ negative rights. Non-agent sentient beings (e.g., many animals or fetuses) retain moral status through sentience alone but do not bear the same responsibilities.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Agency](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agency/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/) 

#### Moral Motivation 

Because this system is anti-realist, moral motivation cannot rely on the belief that moral facts exist independently of human prescription. Motivation instead rests on two pillars: 
1. Rational recognition that the sentience-based negative-rights framework is the most coherent and stable system available for protecting subjective experience.
2. The personal cultivation of character through Stoicism. The agent chooses to uphold the framework because it is the best available coordination system and because living in accordance with it supports personal flourishing ([[#Stoic Eudaimonism]]). External enforcement remains necessary for those who reject the prescription.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Moral Motivation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-motivation/)


#### Weakness of Will (Akrasia) 

Weakness of will is acknowledged as a persistent human tendency. Knowing the correct action under the prescribed rules does not guarantee performance. Stoic discipline is the primary countermeasure: training judgment, focusing only on what is within one’s control, and repeatedly aligning impulse with reasoned principle. The framework itself does not excuse akrasia; failure to act rightly when one had the capacity remains a personal shortcoming, though the political system concerns itself primarily with outward rights violations rather than inner moral failure.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Weakness of Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)


#### Moral Psychology of Rights & Forfeiture 

Humans readily grasp the logic of negative rights and proportional response when the framework is stated clearly. Rights are understood as side-constraints against interference. When an agent knowingly and wantonly violates those constraints, especially through repeated or severe predation that erodes trust, forfeiture of corresponding rights becomes both intelligible and motivating to observers. The psychological force of forfeiture lies in reciprocity and desert: those who refuse to respect the basic negative rights of others cannot claim the full protection of those same rights. This supports decisive enforcement without requiring belief in objective moral truth—only recognition of the practical necessity of the prescribed rules.

- Christopher Heath Wellman - [The Rights Forfeiture Theory of Punishment](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/resmgr/Prizes_&_Awards/Wellman,_Christopher.pdf)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Retributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-retributive/)

#### Minors and Incompetent Adults

Rights rest on sentience, not on agency, so children and cognitively impaired adults hold full negative rights. Responsibility rests on agency, which scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication; only an agent can generate duties through voluntary choice or forfeit rights through deliberate violation, and a being that lacks agency at the act does not forfeit by that act the way a deliberate agent does. Permanent unremediable dangerous incapacity is handled under [[#Free Will and Responsibility]] and [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Insanity Defense]]: confinement, then execution if no progress — not as classic act-forfeiture. Minors cannot consent to irreversible decisions about their bodies. Where sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority passes to next-of-kin, guided by the being's known wishes (Axiom 4). For adult offenders, mental competence or intellectual capacity does not reduce the sentence for a deliberate violation, because the framework's political concern is the outward violation ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]). Liability for minors scales with the severity of the crime ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Juvenile Offenders|Juvenile Offenders]]).

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/)
- Roper v. Simmons (2005) - [Death penalty barred for offenses committed under 18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/543/551)

#### General Rules of Forfeiture

Forfeiture is triggered only when an agent crosses the threshold set in Axiom 6: violating another's negative rights through force or fraud, or directly imposing a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm. What is forfeited corresponds to the violation and is applied proportionally. Forfeiture is found by a court under due process, never by executive declaration, and its duration follows the sentence: rights forfeited for a finite sentence are forfeited for its duration, and past the repeat-offender threshold or for capital offenses they are forfeited for life. Some consequences outlast the sentence by rule, such as the bar on felons voting. The same logic scales to organized groups that systematically violate rights ([[#Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale]]).

Duration matters because repeat offending is common. Among prisoners released from 34 states in 2012, 62% were rearrested within three years and 71% within five, and property offenders had the highest rate, 78.3% within five years.

- Christopher Heath Wellman - [The Rights Forfeiture Theory of Punishment](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/resmgr/Prizes_&_Awards/Wellman,_Christopher.pdf)
- Bureau of Justice Statistics (Durose & Antenangeli) - [Recidivism of Prisoners Released in 34 States in 2012: A 5-Year Follow-Up Period (2012–2017)](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/recidivism-prisoners-released-34-states-2012-5-year-follow-period-2012-2017)

#### Free Will and Responsibility

Responsibility does not wait on a resolution of the metaphysical free-will debate. An act counts as a knowing, voluntary violation when the agent understood what it was doing, foresaw that it would harm another, and was not physically compelled. Desires, upbringing, temperament, and mental illness shape choices without erasing them; an agent who chose under their influence still chose. This is why a diagnosed condition does not by itself exempt an offender from the penalties set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]].

Responsibility is absent only where agency itself is absent: a person physically forced by another, a person who could not understand the nature of the act, or a being without the cognitive capacity for agency at all. These are the incompetence cases covered in [[#Minors and Incompetent Adults]]. Between full agency and none, the question is factual: whether this agent, at this time, knew what they were doing. Incompetence alone does not lead to execution. A person who was completely unaware and incompetent has no agency and is removed from society, imprisoned or confined, because they are too dangerous to be around. But a person who cannot be changed from incompetence and remains a threat, violent and showing no sign of change, is a threat to society and is not kept alive merely for the sake of keeping them alive; they deserve to die, as set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Criminal Justice and Security]].

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Compatibilism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/compatibilism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/)

#### Conflicts Between Rights-Holders

Some conflicts arise where no one has forfeited anything. Negative rights are side-constraints, so the default is that an innocent person may not be harmed to benefit another, even when the numbers favor it. Self-defense applies to the threat itself: force may be used to stop an attack in proportion to the danger, consistent with [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Self-Defense and Defense of Others|Authority & Civil Liberties]], and the person defending is not required to absorb serious harm because the attacker lacks culpability.

Scarcity of rescue, such as the allocation of emergency medical care, is a question of positive aid rather than of violation. Allocating a limited resource by a fair, publicly known rule does not violate the rights of those who go without, because no one held a negative right to the resource. What remains forbidden is actively killing or injuring a bystander as a means to an end.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Self-Defense](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-defense/)
- Robert Nozick - [Anarchy, State, and Utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy,_State,_and_Utopia)

## **Character Ethics**

#### Stoic Eudaimonism

I adopt Stoic eudaimonism as my personal operating system for character and daily conduct. Key elements I take from it:

- Focus only on what is within my control (my judgments, actions, and responses).
- Accept external events, including the actions of others and outcomes of politics, without being ruled by them emotionally.
- Cultivate the four cardinal virtues as practical guides: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.
- Treat adversity and the hostility of others as material for practicing virtue rather than as reasons for despair or rage.
- Maintain inner composure while still acting decisively in the world according to the prescribed sentientist principles.
- Flourish through virtue and rational living, pursued within the constraints of the prescribed rights framework

Stoic eudaimonism is not a replacement for my political or inter-personal rights framework. It is the personal discipline that allows me to live and act consistently with that framework without being destroyed by anger, resentment, or attachment to outcomes I cannot fully control.

## **Political Philosophy**

#### Purpose of the State 

The legitimate function of the state is the protection of negative rights and the security of the political community that sustains them. The state exists first to prevent and punish violations of life, liberty, and property, and to defend the community against external and internal threats that would destroy the conditions for those rights. It is not primarily an engine for equalizing outcomes, enforcing comprehensive moral visions, or guaranteeing positive goods.

- Robert Nozick - [_Anarchy, State, and Utopia_](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP641/%CE%95%CE%BD%CF%8C%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%84%CE%B1%205%CE%B7:%20%CE%A6%CE%AC%CE%BA%CE%B5%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%82%20%CE%9C%CE%B1%CE%B8%CE%AE%CE%BC%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%82/Robert%20Nozick%20Anarchy,%20State,%20and%20Utopia%202001.pdf)
  
#### Political Authority and Coercion

Political authority is justified only insofar as it is necessary to secure negative rights and collective security. Coercive power is a tool, not an intrinsic good. It must remain bounded by the same side-constraints that apply to individuals, except where an agent has forfeited rights through serious violation. Expansive state power is accepted only when required to suppress recurrent patterns of rights violation or to defend the community against organized threats.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Authority](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/authority/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Political Legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)


#### Justice 

Justice consists primarily in the reliable protection of negative rights and the impartial application of rules that respect agency and responsibility. A just order secures the conditions under which sentient beings can exercise agency without predation, enforces rights forfeiture against violators, and refrains from using state power to engineer equal outcomes. Distributive patterns are not themselves the measure of justice; the protection of side-constraints and the maintenance of a high-trust framework for cooperation are.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Distributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-distributive/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)


#### Philosophy of Law

Law is a practical instrument for securing negative rights and coordinating behavior within a political community. Its authority derives from its necessity in protecting the conditions for rights-respecting order, not from any claim to embody objective moral truth. Valid law should track responsibility and agency, apply rights forfeiture proportionally, and remain limited in scope. When law systematically violates negative rights or abandons impartial enforcement, it loses legitimacy and may justly be resisted.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Legal Positivism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-positivism/)

#### Sovereignty and Borders

A political community has the right to maintain its sovereignty and to control its borders. Because high-trust societies better protect negative rights in practice, and that trust rests on shared norms together with impartial enforcement of law, a people may legitimately prefer cultural continuity and selective immigration that preserves the conditions for mutual cooperation and rights-respecting order. Open-ended demographic transformation that erodes those conditions ought be avoided.

#### National Interest and Foreign Relations 

The state retains a primary duty to its own citizens. Alliances, interventions, and international commitments are evaluated according to whether they advance the security and long-term interests of the political community. No foreign tribunal or external authority holds legitimate veto power over decisions necessary for national survival or the protection of citizens’ rights.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Political Realism in International Relations](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/realism-intl-relations/)

#### Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale 

The same logic of rights and forfeiture that applies to individuals scales to political life. Agents or organized groups that systematically violate the negative rights of others, or that seek to subvert the rights-respecting order itself, may be met with decisive force. Reciprocity and the practical necessity of deterrence justify withholding full rights protections from those who reject the basic framework of non-aggression and mutual restraint.

- Christopher Heath Wellman - [Rights Forfeiture Theory of Punishment](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/resmgr/Prizes_&_Awards/Wellman,_Christopher.pdf)

#### Limits of Politics 

Politics cannot create virtue, guarantee flourishing, or eliminate all human conflict. The state’s role is deliberately narrower: to maintain the external conditions under which individuals can pursue their own ends, including character development and personal flourishing, without being subjected to predation or chaos. Over-extension of political power beyond this function tends to generate new rights violations rather than resolve them.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Liberalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liberalism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Perfectionism in Moral and Political Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/perfectionism-moral/)

## **Corporations as Moral Entities**

#### Position
Corporations are legal tools, not moral agents. Rights violations attach to the human agents who authorize them, such as officers and controlling shareholders. Limited liability stays for ordinary contract and tort; the veil is pierced for deliberate rights violations, fraud, or knowing extreme-risk conduct.

#### Key Elements
- Corporations are legal tools, not moral agents.
- Rights violations attach to the human agents who authorize them.
- Limited liability for ordinary contract and tort.
- Pierce for deliberate rights violations, fraud, or knowing extreme-risk conduct.

#### Rationale
Agency and responsibility scale to persons, so a corporation cannot itself hold moral guilt; the officers and controlling shareholders who authorize a violation do. Organized groups that systematically violate rights meet decisive force under Rights at Scale in [[#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]. U.S. courts already pierce the corporate veil only for serious misconduct, such as fraud or abuse of the corporate form.

##### Sources
- Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute – [Piercing the Corporate Veil](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/piercing_the_corporate_veil)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Corporations are tools, not moral agents. The people who authorize rights violations are liable. Limited liability stays for ordinary business, but not for deliberate violations, fraud, or knowing extreme risk.

## **Duties to Future Generations**

#### Position
Present agents must not impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings. Public debt must not bind future people to fund rights violations or permanent subsidy schemes. Debt for rights-protection and honored transition commitments is allowed; pay-as-you-go and opt-in are preferred for non-rights spending.

#### Key Elements
- No extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction imposed on future sentient beings.
- No debt binding future people to fund rights violations or permanent subsidy schemes.
- Debt allowed for rights-protection and honored transition commitments.
- Pay-as-you-go and opt-in preferred for non-rights spending.

#### Rationale
Side-constraints bind across time where the harmed party will be sentient, extending the capital-track extreme-risk rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]] forward in time. The honored-commitments rule already protects current retirees during transition in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]. GAO projects publicly held debt reaching 123% of the economy in 2036, and reports that net interest spending in FY 2025 exceeded federal spending on national defense.

## **Aesthetics**

#### Beauty

Beauty in the fullest sense is found in clarity, competence, and order. These are valuable because they are often the conditions under which sentient beings can live well and exercise agency without constant friction. Rights-respecting order - expressed through law, discipline, high agency, and care - generates a deeper form of beauty that frequently produces visible beauty in objects and environments. Natural visual beauty can exist independently of ethics: nature may be visually striking while remaining ethically indifferent or abominable.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Beauty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauty/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Environmental Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/environmental-aesthetics/)

#### Artistry

A culture that rewards rights-respecting order, high agency, developed skill, and merit tends to produce works of lasting aesthetic quality. A culture that treats all output as equal, or that elevates works primarily for therapeutic or political reasons while neglecting competence and clarity, tends toward aesthetic decline.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Aesthetic Judgment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aesthetic-judgment/)

## **Meaning**

#### Purpose

Purpose is not discovered as an objective cosmic fact, nor is it purely invented in a vacuum. It is realized through the consistent exercise of agency in accordance with virtue and the prescribed ethical constraints. A meaningful life is one in which the agent develops character, acts rightly toward other sentient beings, and pursues long-term projects that express competence and care. [[#Stoic Eudaimonism]] provides the personal aim: flourishing through rational activity and virtue. Meaning is not a separate mystical property; it is what a well-lived life feels like from the inside when one is meeting the demands of reason and character over time.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [The Meaning of Life](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life-meaning/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)

#### Legacy and Mortality

Mortality is a biological fact, not a moral tragedy that requires supernatural compensation. The appropriate response is clear-eyed acceptance combined with the effort to leave the conditions for other sentient lives better rather than worse. Legacy matters insofar as it reflects actual causal impact - character transmitted, rights-respecting order maintained, useful work left behind - not as a demand for eternal remembrance. Purpose is local and agent-centered: to live well within the constraints, to protect what deserves protection, and to avoid adding unnecessary suffering or chaos.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Death](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/death/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy - [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)
