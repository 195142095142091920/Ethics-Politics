## Meta-Ethics

>[!tldr] In short
> Moral anti-realism: I claim no objective moral truth. A sentience-centered system is prescribed because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available.

#### Moral anti-realism

I prescribe a sentience-centered system because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available. I do not claim objective moral truth.  

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Anti-Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-anti-realism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-realism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Constructivism in Metaethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/constructivism-metaethics/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Cognitivism vs. Non-Cognitivism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-cognitivism/)

> [!question]- "Isn't morality subjective?"
> I do not claim objective moral truth. It is a prescribed, sentience-centered system, chosen because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available. Its authority rests on that coherence and on its practical necessity for protecting subjective experience, not on moral facts existing independently of human prescription. Those who reject it are still bound by its enforcement.

> [!question]- "Why moral anti-realism?"
> Moral [[Glossary#Moral anti-realism|anti-realism]] is the view that there are no moral facts that hold independently of what anyone thinks or prescribes. I don't claim such facts can be established, and I hold moral claims to the same standard of evidence I apply elsewhere. Three arguments support that judgment; they are reasons, not proof. The first is from disagreement: moral views differ deeply across cultures and eras in ways that are better explained by differing attitudes and interests than by some groups perceiving moral facts that others miss. The second is J. L. Mackie's argument from queerness: objective values would have to be facts of a very strange kind, prescriptions built into the world that motivate or bind anyone who grasps them. Nothing else in a naturalistic picture of the world is like that. The third is evolutionary debunking, developed by Sharon Street and Richard Joyce. Human moral attitudes were shaped by natural selection for survival and reproduction, not for tracking independent moral truth, so any match between them and such truths would be a coincidence. Rather than rest on facts I can't demonstrate, I prescribe a sentience-centered system and defend it on grounds that can be checked. It is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available, and it is practically necessary for protecting beings that can be harmed.

> [!question]- "What kind of anti-realism is it?"
> A hybrid, which is a recognized position in metaethics. It is constructivist in the Humean sense: its rules are built from what beings actually care about, chiefly avoiding suffering, and the best set of rules is the one that is most coherent and stable. It is prescriptivist in form, in the tradition of R. M. Hare: its moral statements are universal prescriptions, rules that apply to everyone in the same situation, including the person who states them. And it has an expressive element: its judgments also express a settled attitude against harm to beings that can suffer.

> [!question]- "If there are no objective moral facts, why should anyone accept these rights?"
> Critics claim that a framework without independent moral facts cannot bind anyone who rejects its starting point. No moral system meets that demand, realist ones included, because asserting that moral facts exist does not by itself persuade someone who denies them. What my framework delivers is what any moral system can actually deliver. My reasons are open to everyone. The system is coherent and stable, it protects every being that can be harmed rather than drawing arbitrary lines, and anyone who wants protection from harm has a reason to support a system that protects it. Those who accept it are motivated by recognizing that coherence and by cultivated character ([[#Moral Motivation]]). Those who reject it are still bound by its enforcement, as people are under any legal order.

> [!question]- "People disagree about science too. Doesn't moral disagreement prove nothing?"
> The realist reply has force. Disagreement alone does not show there are no facts, since disagreement exists in nearly every field, including those where everyone agrees there are facts. Realists also point to convergence on basics, such as condemning gratuitous cruelty. The anti-realist answer is about the kind of disagreement: scientific disputes tend to narrow as evidence accumulates and methods are shared, while basic moral disputes often persist among informed people who agree on every fact. I treat disagreement as evidence that weighs against realism, not as a refutation, and I'd note that the convergence realists cite, on avoiding suffering, is exactly what my constructivism builds from.

> [!question]- "If objective values are 'queer,' aren't the norms of reasoning and mathematics queer too?"
> Realists answer with a companions-in-guilt argument: the objection to moral facts would apply equally to facts that almost no one doubts, such as the norms of good reasoning or the truths of mathematics, so it proves too much. The parallel fails. Mathematical and logical norms are confirmed by their use in every successful field of inquiry and by results that anyone can check, while independent moral facts add no such checkable payoff. I rely on reasoning and evidence myself, not on independent moral facts, so I don't need the stronger claim.

> [!question]- "Doesn't evolutionary debunking undermine your own moral views too?"
> It would, if I claimed my intuitions track moral truth. I don't. Realists answer debunking by arguing that evolution favored attitudes such as caring for others because those attitudes were in fact good, so the match is not a coincidence. Others point out that the same argument, pressed too far, would undermine trust in human reasoning in general. I don't rely on my intuitions being accurate detectors of independent truth. My case rests on coherence and on evidence about sentience, which are testable: whether a being can suffer is a scientific question, and whether a set of rules is consistent is a logical one. Debunking removes the case for realism; it does not remove the evidence that beings can be harmed or the reasons beings have to avoid harm.

#### Key Elements
- Moral anti-realism: no claim of objective moral truth.
- A sentience-centered system is prescribed because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available.
- Motivation rests on rational recognition of that coherence and on Stoic character, with external enforcement for those who reject the prescription.

## Metaphysics

>[!tldr] In short
> Agnostic atheism: no belief in gods, open in principle to evidence. The stance is personal; the moral system rests on the sentientist framework, not on theology.
#### Agnostic Atheism 

I am an agnostic atheist. I do not believe in the existence of any gods, while remaining open to the theoretical possibility that evidence could change that assessment. Religious claims are treated as unsupported by sufficient evidence. This stance is personal and does not form the basis of my moral system; my moral system is grounded in my prescribed [[Glossary#Sentience basis (sentientism)|sentientist]] framework rather than in any theological foundation.

- Wikipedia – [Agnostic atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic_atheism)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Atheism and Agnosticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Religion and Morality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/religion-morality/)

> [!question]- "Why agnostic atheism rather than atheism or agnosticism alone?"
> The two words answer different questions. Atheism concerns belief: an agnostic atheist lacks belief in any god, and I treat religious claims as unsupported by sufficient evidence. Agnosticism concerns knowledge: the agnostic atheist does not claim to know with certainty that no god exists and remains open to evidence that could change the assessment. The burden of proof rests on the claim that a god exists, as it does for any claim about what exists. Proof here means testable, repeatable evidence or reasoning that others can check; revelation, scripture, or private experience alone does not meet that standard. The absence of sufficient evidence warrants withholding belief, not asserting the opposite as proven. Philosophers often use "atheism" more strictly, for the claim that no god exists, and on that usage the position is closer to non-theism; I use the wider sense of lacking belief.

> [!question]- "Without God, where does morality come from?"
> From the prescribed sentientist framework, not from theology. The stance on gods is personal and does not form the basis of the moral system. Rights rest on sentience, the capacity to be harmed, which can be investigated by evidence whether or not a god exists. Grounding morality in a god's commands also raises an old problem: if something is good because a god commands it, the commands could have been anything. If a god commands it because it is good, its goodness does not depend on the command. Theists argue that without a god there is no moral authority beyond human prescription. I claim no more than that and need no more: the framework rests on reasons anyone can check ([[#Meta-Ethics]]).

> [!question]- "If free will is unresolved, how can anyone forfeit rights?"
> Responsibility does not wait on a resolution of the metaphysical free-will debate. An act counts as a knowing, voluntary violation when the agent understood what it was doing, foresaw that it would harm another, and was not physically compelled. Desires, upbringing, temperament, and mental illness shape choices without erasing them; an agent who chose under their influence still chose, and [[Glossary#Rights forfeiture|forfeiture]] follows the deliberate violation. Responsibility is absent only where agency itself is absent ([[#Free Will and Responsibility]]).

#### Key Elements
- Agnostic atheism: no belief in gods, open in principle to evidence.
- Religious claims treated as unsupported by sufficient evidence.
- The stance is personal; the moral system rests on the prescribed sentientist framework, not on theology.
- Responsibility does not depend on settling the free-will debate ([[#Free Will and Responsibility]]).

## Normative Ethics

>[!tldr] In short
> My ethics is a threshold sentientist deontology. Moral status and strong negative rights belong to every being with first-order subjective experience, biological or artificial. Those rights are side-constraints, not goals to maximize, and they give way only when an agent crosses a clear threshold of rights violation and forfeits the matching protections. Positive rights are generally rejected, with minimal tax-funded basic services as a narrow practical exception.

#### Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)

##### Axioms

1. I prescribe that subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) shall serve as the basis for moral status and the possession of [[Glossary#Negative rights|negative rights]].

2. Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer under my framework. Higher-order self-awareness is not required.

3. A being on an undisturbed developmental trajectory toward sentience (e.g., a healthy fetus in a living womb) shall be granted significant moral weight proportional to the robustness of that trajectory.

4. A being that once possessed sentience retains residual moral consideration. If sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority transfers to next-of-kin, guided by respect for the former being’s known wishes and dignity.

5. Practical determination of sentience shall follow the best available scientific consensus. Where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with these prescriptions.

6. Under my framework, sentient beings are to be granted strong negative rights against interference and harm. These rights function as side-constraints that may be overridden only when a clear threshold of rights violation by the agent has been crossed (rights forfeiture). The direct imposition of severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on others counts as crossing that threshold.

7. All moral and political rules within this system must remain consistent with the prescribed primacy of subjective experience. I don't claim this as objective moral fact. It's a coherent system I choose to endorse and want enforced.

##### Summary

- **Sentientist:**
    1. Moral status and negative rights attach to beings capable of first-order subjective experience, or those on a clear developmental trajectory toward it.
    
       - Wikipedia – [Sentientism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentientism) 

- **Negative-rights focused:** 
    1. Rights function as side-constraints against interference and harm. Positive rights that compel others to provide goods or services are generally rejected. Minimal tax-funded basic services (education, public safety, infrastructure) are accepted as a narrow practical exception.
       
       - Wikipedia – [Negative and Positive Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_rights) 

- **Threshold deontologist:** 
    1. Core rules against violating sentient rights normally hold. They may be overridden only after a clear threshold of rights violation is crossed, at which point the violator forfeits corresponding protections. Fetal moral weight scales with proximity to sentience and is grounded primarily in the responsibility created by voluntary procreation. Duties to sustain another being arise only from voluntary responsibility; where no voluntary act created the dependency, as in rape, withdrawing support differs from killing.
       
       - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/)


> [!question]- "How is sentientism here different from utilitarianism?"
> Both start from subjective experience, but mine is a threshold deontology, not a calculation of total welfare. Sentient beings hold strong negative rights that work as side-constraints: an innocent person may not be harmed to benefit another, even when the numbers favor it ([[#Conflicts Between Rights-Holders]]). The constraints give way only when an agent crosses the threshold of rights violation and forfeits protections. Positive rights that compel others to provide goods or services are generally rejected.

> [!question]- "Why do rights rest on sentience?"
> Subjective experience is what can be harmed: a being that feels has a life that can go well or badly for it. So I make first-order subjective experience the basis for moral status and negative rights, for any being, biological or artificial, that has it. Higher-order self-awareness is not required, and whether a being is sentient follows the best available scientific consensus.

> [!question]- "Why is sentience, rather than species, intelligence, or reason, the right basis for moral status?"
> Because sentience is what makes harm matter to the one harmed. A being that can suffer, or have its life go well or badly, has interests of its own; a being that cannot has nothing at stake in how it is treated. Jeremy Bentham put the test plainly: "the question is not, Can they reason? nor, Can they talk? but, Can they suffer?" The alternatives draw the line arbitrarily. Species membership alone says nothing about whether a being can be hurt. Intelligence, rationality, and the ability to reciprocate duties fail what philosophers call the problem of "marginal cases." Infants and some severely disabled people lack them, yet almost everyone holds that they have rights, which shows the criterion is doing no real work. Sentience is also evidence-based and gradeable. The 2012 Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness found that humans are not unique in possessing the neurological substrates that generate consciousness. The 2024 New York Declaration found strong support for conscious experience in mammals and birds and at least a realistic possibility of it in all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including insects. Negative rights follow from that basis: if what matters is that a being can be harmed, the core claim it holds is not to be harmed by others. Critics object that sentience cannot be observed directly, so its borders, in insects or in artificial systems, stay uncertain. Uncertain borders call for graded weight and precaution, not for a criterion that's easier to measure but doesn't track what makes harm wrong.

> [!question]- "Why only negative rights, not positive rights?"
> Negative rights are side-constraints against interference and harm. A positive right to goods or services would compel others to provide them, which is itself interference with those others. Positive rights are therefore generally rejected. Minimal tax-funded basic services, such as education, public safety, and infrastructure, are accepted as a narrow practical exception, not as rights.

> [!question]- "What makes rights side-constraints rather than goals?"
> A goal is something to maximize, so one person's rights could be traded away whenever that raises the total. A side-constraint limits what any agent may do to any other. That is why an innocent person may not be harmed to benefit another, even when the numbers favor it ([[#Conflicts Between Rights-Holders]]). The constraints give way only when a clear threshold of rights violation is crossed.

> [!question]- "Why can rights be forfeited at all?"
> Rights are reciprocal. An agent who knowingly violates others' negative rights, or directly imposes a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm on them, cannot claim the full protection of those same rights (Axiom 6). The force of forfeiture lies in reciprocity and desert. It is found by a court under due process, corresponds to the violation, and is applied proportionally ([[#General Rules of Forfeiture]]).

#### Key Elements
- Subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) is the basis for moral status and negative rights; higher-order self-awareness is not required.
- Any being, biological or artificial, with first-order subjective experience is a rights-bearer.
- A being on an undisturbed trajectory toward sentience gets weight proportional to that trajectory; a being that lost sentience keeps residual consideration.
- Sentience is determined by the best available scientific consensus.
- Negative rights are side-constraints, overridden only past a clear threshold of rights violation (forfeiture), including directly imposing severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm.
- Not claimed as objective moral fact; a coherent system chosen and enforced.



#### Minors and Incompetent Adults

Rights rest on sentience, not on agency, so children and cognitively impaired adults hold full negative rights. Responsibility rests on agency, which scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication. Only an agent can generate duties through voluntary choice or forfeit rights through deliberate violation, and a being that lacks agency at the act does not forfeit by that act the way a deliberate agent does. Permanent unremediable dangerous incapacity is handled under [[#Free Will and Responsibility]] and [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Insanity Defense]]: confinement, then execution if no progress — not as classic act-forfeiture. Minors cannot consent to irreversible decisions about their bodies. Where sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority passes to next-of-kin, guided by the being's known wishes (Axiom 4). For adult offenders, mental competence or intellectual capacity does not reduce the sentence for a deliberate violation, because my political concern is the outward violation ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]). Liability for minors scales with the severity of the crime ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Juvenile Offenders|Juvenile Offenders]]).

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/)
- Roper v. Simmons (2005) – [Death penalty barred for offenses committed under 18](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/543/551)

#### General Rules of Forfeiture

Forfeiture is triggered only when an agent crosses the threshold set in Axiom 6: violating another's negative rights through force or fraud, or directly imposing a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm. What is forfeited corresponds to the violation and is applied proportionally. Forfeiture is found by a court under due process, never by executive declaration, and its duration follows the sentence. Rights forfeited for a finite sentence are forfeited for its duration, and past the repeat-offender threshold or for capital offenses they are forfeited for life. Some consequences outlast the sentence by rule, such as the bar on [[Glossary#Felony and misdemeanor|felons]] voting. The same logic scales to organized groups that systematically violate rights ([[#Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale]]).

Duration matters because repeat offending is common. Among prisoners released from 34 states in 2012, 62% were rearrested within three years and 71% within five, and property offenders had the highest rate, 78.3% within five years. Counted per person rather than per release, about one in three people who enter and leave prison go back to prison (Rhodes et al., using 13 years of data from 17 states). A third of everyone released returning to prison is the repeat population that duration rules exist for.

- Christopher Heath Wellman – [The Rights Forfeiture Theory of Punishment](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/resmgr/Prizes_&_Awards/Wellman,_Christopher.pdf)
- Crime & Delinquency (Rhodes et al.) – [Following Incarceration, Most Released Offenders Never Return to Prison (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0011128714549655)
- Bureau of Justice Statistics (Durose & Antenangeli) – [Recidivism of Prisoners Released in 34 States in 2012: A 5-Year Follow-Up Period (2012–2017)](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/recidivism-prisoners-released-34-states-2012-5-year-follow-period-2012-2017)

#### Conflicts Between Rights-Holders

Some conflicts arise where no one has forfeited anything. Negative rights are side-constraints, so the default is that an innocent person may not be harmed to benefit another, even when the numbers favor it. Self-defense applies to the threat itself: force may be used to stop an attack in proportion to the danger, consistent with [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Self-Defense and Defense of Others|Authority & Civil Liberties]], and the person defending is not required to absorb serious harm because the attacker lacks culpability.

Scarcity of rescue, such as the allocation of emergency medical care, is a question of positive aid rather than of violation. Allocating a limited resource by a fair, publicly known rule does not violate the rights of those who go without, because no one held a negative right to the resource. What remains forbidden is actively killing or injuring a bystander as a means to an end.

## Animal Rights and Sentientism

>[!tldr] In short
> Animal agriculture and aquaculture are banned outright and shut down immediately, with sanctuary for every animal still alive ([[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]). Sentient animals have rights on a gradient. Deliberately killing a high-sentience animal is murder, except euthanasia for the animal's own sake on a veterinarian's finding and by a veterinary professional. Hunting is murder, commercial fishing is banned and shuts down immediately, and cruelty causing lasting injury is capital, with other grave cruelty in the grave tier: three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor. Other sections apply this ethic to their own fields.

#### Position
Animal agriculture and aquaculture should be banned outright and instantly, because that is how my view stays sentientist. The philosophy itself is not phased in. Animal agriculture, commercial fishing, aquaculture, commercial breeding mills, and animal testing have no lag at all: they shut down immediately, and every animal still alive gets sanctuary ([[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]). For other animal topics the same two-layer rule applies: the ban is primary; any real-world lag is secondary.

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics; they illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Westland/Hallmark, 2008:** undercover footage released by the Humane Society (an advocacy group; primary footage) showed workers shocking, kicking and ramming downed cows with a forklift to force them to slaughter. CNN (2008) – [Video of workers abusing cows raises food safety questions](https://www.cnn.com/2008/US/01/30/undercover.slaughter.video/index.html)
> - **Recall:** the plant's footage led to the largest U.S. meat recall, 143 million pounds, about 50 million of which had gone to school lunch and other federal nutrition programs. Congressional Research Service (2008) – [Prepared for Members and Committees of Congress](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20080829_RS22819_1c22a63bb1948e9eae2a03dd42755d63077cc911.pdf)

> [!note]- No wind-down: the Sanctuary Plan
> The ban is the position, and there is no wind-down. Animal agriculture, wild commercial fishing, aquaculture, commercial breeding mills, and animal testing shut down immediately. Every animal alive at the shutdown is kept alive and given sanctuary for its natural life. Licenses, quotas, and grazing permits are revoked on day one and are not honored commitments. Firms that earn most of their revenue from animal agriculture convert fully to cultivated-meat research or plant-based farming or are liquidated, others divest the animal-agriculture line, and shareholders get no compensation; what is left after creditors goes to the sanctuaries. Most animals go to sanctuaries, run by the state in the emergency phase and then by licensed nonprofits; those that are safe to keep may be adopted after strict vetting. There is no retraining or job-placement program; workers find new jobs or can apply for sanctuary jobs under the worker rules in the Sanctuary Plan, which bar anyone who killed animals for a living from animal care. The full plan sits in [[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]].

Sentience exists on a gradient across species, and that gradient sets the moral weight of a being under the same scale used for murder in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]. Animals are graded into sentience classes. Deliberately killing a member of a high-sentience class outside self-defense is murder, an egregious rights violation on the capital track with the same forfeitures as the murder of a human. Killing a member of a lower-sentience class counts as a mark against the offender. Fish follow the same gradient, placed species by species under the [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]. Moral agency does not decide whether a rights violation occurred: predation by an animal violates its victim's rights even though the predator is not a moral agent.

Hunting is murder. There is no subsistence or Indigenous exception. Self-defense applies only against an actual attack. Defense of others covers any sentient victim: force may be used to stop an actual attack on a person or an animal, in proportion to the danger, and separation or containment comes first where it will work.

Commercial fishing and aquaculture of sentient animals are banned outright and instantly. Crabs, lobsters, and other decapod crustaceans sit in the high class, shrimp are placed species by species, and bivalves are covered as a precaution, given their low or uncertain sentience, as with insects ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]). Bycatch ends with the fishing ban; other marine activity, such as shipping, dredging, and offshore energy, follows the least-harmful-method rule in [[#Sentience Determination]], the same rule as on land; and seafood imports are banned. The Section 9 fishing rules are an application of this topic, not a separate ethic.

Animal cruelty is extreme violence, intentional neglect, beating without drawing blood, or withholding food, water, or shelter as deliberate punishment ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Animal Cruelty Law|Animal Cruelty Law]]). Any of these against a higher-sentience animal is capital if it causes [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|lasting injury]], physical or psychological, even when only one animal is harmed, as it is against a human; otherwise it falls in the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|grave tier]]: three marks at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Cruelty to lower-sentience animals is not capital, but it is tracked and counts toward the repeat-offender threshold, weighted by severity. Ritual slaughter is banned outright.

The duty to protect covers all sentient animals. Within U.S. borders, the government has a duty to intervene to stop rights violations against animals; protecting animals elsewhere is a virtue but not a duty of the American government. Predation and herbivore population control are decided in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Hunting, Fishing, Pets, and Zoos|Cultural & Social Issues]] and in [[#Wild-Animal Predation|Wild-Animal Predation]]; this topic is the authoritative statement those pages apply.

Animals cannot consent.

While an animal is alive, decisions about its care are made by a guardian acting for the animal, not by next-of-kin; the next-of-kin rule in Axiom 4 applies only after sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost. The owner is the guardian by default and decides routine vet care. Euthanasia and other major decisions are allowed only for the animal's own sake, such as ending suffering, never for convenience, and only on a veterinarian's finding that it is in that animal's best interest. Even at home, if the guardian believes the animal is suffering, euthanasia still requires a veterinary professional, just as assisted death for humans requires a medical professional ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Assisted Death|Assisted Death]]).

The comparison to human assisted death concerns who carries it out, not who decides. A human may choose assisted death only as a competent adult. Animals cannot consent, so for an animal the guardian decides for it, under the stricter limits above: only for the animal's own sake, never for convenience, and only on a veterinarian's best-interest finding.

An ownerless or wild animal has no guardian until someone takes it on. Anyone who wants to take an ownerless or wild animal under their responsibility is free to adopt it and becomes its guardian. Adoption is the rule; there is no state or sanctuary mandate. Animals alive when animal agriculture, fishing, breeding mills, and testing shut down are different: they are kept in sanctuaries, and those that are safe to keep may be adopted only after the vetting in the [[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]] (background check, a home inspection that also sets how many animals a home may take, signed no-slaughter commitment, sterilization, registry ID, unannounced welfare checks, return rule; the adopter pays all care, with no stipend; never a sale).

Euthanasia that breaks these conditions is graded on a sliding scale. A genuine case where the animal is truly suffering, but the guardian ends its life at home without a vet, for example because they cannot afford the vet or do not want to make the trip, carries a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty. The convenience there is the vet trip, not the killing, and real suffering is still required. A case that only looks like mercy, with no real suffering and no genuine basis, is cruelty, graded under the cruelty tiers in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Animal Cruelty Law|Animal Cruelty Law]], capital if it causes lasting injury and otherwise falls in the [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|grave tier]]. A case with clear intent to harm is murder. Killing for convenience stays murder. A parent who kills a sick child at home is wrong even if the child was going to die, and this needs a real block. But genuine euthanasia holds real mercy, so a capital line for every non-vet case would be too harsh.

> [!question]- "Sentience can't be measured directly. How does anyone decide which animals count?"
> No one can observe another being's experience directly, in animals or in other humans, so the question is one of evidence, not proof. Researchers have built graded tests. A 2021 review for the UK government by Jonathan Birch and colleagues at the London School of Economics assessed over 300 studies against eight criteria, including nociceptors, integrative brain regions, responses to anaesthetics and analgesics, and trade-offs between the cost of a threat and the benefit of obtaining resources, and recommended that all cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans be regarded as sentient in UK animal welfare law. I use the same approach. Species are placed on the sentience gradient by the best available evidence, and where evidence is uncertain, protection is extended rather than withheld, because wrongly denying a sentient being its rights is the worse error. I adopt that recommendation, so cephalopods and decapods sit in the high class ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]), and bivalves are covered as a precaution. Insects are technically covered too, but they sit on a very low tier of the sentience scale, closer to potential sentience than established sentience, and are protected on a precautionary basis. Enforcement targets deliberate, unnecessary killing, not everyday accidental harm.

> [!question]- "Why should animals hold rights when they cannot hold duties?"
> Rights rest on sentience, the capacity to suffer and to have a life go well or badly, not on the ability to take on duties. Infants and incompetent adults hold rights without moral agency, and sentient animals do too, weighted by their place on the sentience gradient.

> [!question]- "If rights come in proportion to sentience, how can killing a pig carry the same sentence as killing a person?"
> While a human life is usually richer than a pig's, richness does not set the right not to be killed. I'm a threshold deontologist, not a utilitarian. The gradient decides which class a being falls in; it does not discount the right not to be killed once a being is in the high class ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|sentience classes]]). Above that threshold, the right against being killed is a full side-constraint, the same for every member. That is already how the law treats people: an infant, a person with severe dementia, and a philosopher have unequal cognitive lives and an equal right not to be murdered. A pig, a cow, or a chicken has a life that can go well or badly for it, and killing it takes all of that away, permanently. Below the threshold, the gradient sets the penalty, marks rather than death, and across every class it guides rescue priority, which is positive aid, not violation ([[#Conflicts Between Rights-Holders]]). While most people's intuitions rank a human death far above an animal's, intuitions once ranked some humans above others too, and an intuition is not evidence of where the threshold lies. Proportionality in my framework means the punishment matches the violation, and killing a high-sentience being is the gravest violation it can suffer.

> [!question]- "If animal farming is murder, are today's farmers, slaughter workers, and hunters murderers?"
> The act is a rights violation now. But criminal punishment applies only to acts committed after the law that forbids them takes effect. The Constitution bars ex post facto laws, and I hold that rule on its own merits: my ethics are prescriptions I choose and want enforced, and a prescription becomes a punishable law only once it is enacted and published. So no one is prosecuted for killing an animal before the ban, and every killing after it is prosecuted under the sentience-graded rules. The bar on people who killed animals for a living working in hands-on care or adopting ([[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]) is not a punishment. It is a fitness rule for the animals' safety, of the same kind as the adoption vetting.
> - Cornell LII – [Calder v. Bull, 3 U.S. 386 (1798)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/3/386)

#### Key Elements
- Two-layer rule for all animal topics: outright, instant ban as the primary position; gradual real-world change as a brief aside (like taxes or education).
- Sentience on a gradient across species, on the same scale as the murder section; fish included.
- Deliberately killing a high-sentience animal outside self-defense is murder on the capital track; lower-sentience killings count as marks against the offender.
- Sentience classes are fixed in statute by an expert board ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|Sentience Determination]]); above the high-class threshold, the right not to be killed is full and equal.
- Criminal penalties reach only acts after the ban takes effect; defense of others covers animals.
- Hunting is murder, with no subsistence exception.
- Commercial fishing and aquaculture banned outright and instantly; shellfish covered; bycatch ends with fishing; other marine activity under the least-harmful-method rule; seafood imports banned.
- Animal cruelty tiered by severity: cruelty causing lasting injury is capital; without lasting injury it falls in the grave tier: three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor; lesser cruelty is tracked and counts toward repeat offending.
- Duty to protect animals within U.S. borders; predation and herbivore control decided in Section 7 and Wild-Animal Predation.
- Animals cannot consent.
- Living animals: decisions are made by a guardian, the owner by default for routine vet care; euthanasia and major decisions only for the animal's own sake, never convenience, on a veterinarian's best-interest finding, and carried out by a veterinary professional.
- Ownerless or wild animals: anyone who takes responsibility may adopt one and becomes its guardian; no state or sanctuary mandate, except that animals freed by the shutdown are kept in sanctuaries and adopted only after vetting ([[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]).

#### Rationale

**Further detail on the key elements:**
- Euthanasia is not murder only when all of these conditions hold: for the animal's own sake, never convenience; on a veterinarian's best-interest finding; and carried out by a veterinary professional, even at home.
- Next-of-kin applies only after sentience is permanently lost.
- Euthanasia outside the conditions, on a sliding scale: genuine non-vet cases with real suffering get a lesser, separate, non-capital penalty. Cover cases with no real suffering are cruelty under the tiers, capital if they cause lasting injury, otherwise grave abuse (three marks: execution, experimentation, or life with labor); clear intent to harm is murder.

I ground rights in sentience, so a being that can suffer holds moral weight in proportion to its sentience, and full negative rights above its class threshold, whatever its species. Science increasingly supports this. The 2012 Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness concluded that the weight of evidence indicates humans are not unique in possessing the neurological substrates that generate consciousness. The 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness found strong scientific support for conscious experience in other mammals and in birds, and a realistic possibility of it in all vertebrates, including fishes, and in many invertebrates such as cephalopods, decapod crustaceans, and insects. That gradient is why fish and shellfish are covered and why sentience is set by the best available science.

#### Sentience Determination

Whether a being is sentient is determined by the best available scientific consensus (Axiom 5). Under Axiom 2, any being with first-order subjective experience is a rights-bearer, so innocent animals of every kind, including fish and insects, receive sentience protections. Within that protection, moral weight is applied on a sliding scale. It rises with the strength of the evidence for subjective experience and with the richness of that experience, so mammals, birds, cephalopods, and decapod crustaceans weigh more heavily than most fish, and fish more heavily than insects. Everyday accidental harm cannot be policed, so enforcement targets deliberate, unnecessary killing: stepping on a bug is not the target, exterminating a hive for fun is. Where killing animals of any class cannot be avoided in lawful activity, as in harvesting, pest control, construction, and shipping, the duty is to use the least-harmful method that works; failing to use it is punished, and incidental kills from lawful activity carry no criminal liability. Placement on the scale is revised as the evidence changes, and artificial systems are assessed by the same standard.

The law ought to fix the sentience classes in statute, with an expert sentience board that revises a species' placement only on the best available scientific consensus (Axiom 5); a revision applies only to acts committed after it takes effect. The high class covers all mammals and birds, including rats, mice, and pigeons, and all cephalopod molluscs, such as octopuses, squid, and cuttlefish. Fish are placed species by species on the evidence for each: a species with strong evidence of pain experience sits in the high class, and the rest sit in the lower class until the evidence places them higher. Decapod crustaceans, such as crabs, lobsters, and crayfish, sit in the high class. Shrimp and prawns are placed species by species, like fish. Bivalves and insects sit on the lowest, precautionary tier. Deliberately killing a high-class animal outside self-defense is murder on the capital track, killing a lower-class animal is a mark, and deliberately killing an animal on the lowest tier carries a small fine. The fine will generally go unenforced in practice: no one can police every insect swatted or mussel crushed, and enforcement targets deliberate, unnecessary killing, such as exterminating a hive for fun. The fine stands anyway, because the law ought to state what is owed to every being that may be sentient, whether or not each breach is caught. ^sentience-classes

> [!question]- "How is anyone supposed to know which animals are capital?"
> The statute lists the classes, and the list is published. The mental state required is knowledge of the act: the offender knew they were killing that animal. Not knowing the list is no defense, any more than not knowing the murder statute is. A genuine, reasonable mistake about what the animal was, such as a thrown net that caught a bird hidden in it, goes to intent the same way it does with a human victim. The evidence behind the list is strongest for mammals and birds. For cephalopods and decapods, Birch and colleagues' review of over 300 studies for the UK government recommended treating all cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans as sentient, and the UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 now covers both groups. I adopt that finding: octopuses, squid, crabs, lobsters, and crayfish sit in the high class. Shrimp and prawns span many species, so, like fish, they are placed species by species. For fish, Sneddon and colleagues found that rainbow trout have pain receptors much like those of mammals, and that a painful stimulus changed their behavior and physiology for a prolonged period. Fish species differ widely, which is why they are placed species by species rather than as one group.
> - Sneddon, Braithwaite & Gentle, Proceedings of the Royal Society B – [Do fishes have nociceptors? Evidence for the evolution of a vertebrate sensory system (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2003.2349)
> - London School of Economics (Birch et al.) – [Review of the Evidence of Sentience in Cephalopod Molluscs and Decapod Crustaceans (2021)](https://www.lse.ac.uk/News/News-Assets/PDFs/2021/Sentience-in-Cephalopod-Molluscs-and-Decapod-Crustaceans-Final-Report-November-2021.pdf)
> - UK Legislation – [Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022, section 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/22/section/5)

> [!question]- "Plants respond to damage too. Why don't they count?"
> Responding to damage is not experiencing it. Plants have no neurons and no centralized structure that integrates information into experience, and the plant biologists who reviewed the "plant neurobiology" claims concluded that plants neither have nor need consciousness. Under Axiom 5 the best available science decides, and it finds no subjective experience in plants. If the evidence changed, so would the classification. A plant diet also kills fewer plants than an animal diet, because farmed animals eat crops ([[9. Energy & Environment#Agriculture and Food Policy|Agriculture and Food Policy]]).
> - Taiz et al., Trends in Plant Science – [Plants Neither Possess nor Require Consciousness (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tplants.2019.05.008)

> [!question]- "Harvesting crops kills field mice. Isn't a plant diet also killing?"
> Some harvest kills are real. Steven Davis argued they could exceed the deaths from grazing cattle. That comparison counted totals rather than deaths per person fed, and it left out the cattle themselves. A diet that feeds crops to animals kills more field animals per person, because most feed calories are lost on the way. Incidental deaths in lawful harvesting are not deliberate killings, so they carry no criminal liability; farmers must use the least-harmful method that works, and failing to use it is punished.
> - Davis, Journal of Agricultural and Environmental Ethics – [The Least Harm Principle May Require that Humans Consume a Diet Containing Large Herbivores, Not a Vegan Diet (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1025638030686)

#### Wild-Animal Predation

Predation is a rights violation regardless of moral agency. A predator that kills a sentient animal violates that animal's rights even though the predator lacks the foresight and voluntary control of an agent. Agency governs responsibility and forfeiture, not whether a violation occurred. A being that lacks agency at the act does not forfeit its own rights by that act the way a deliberate agent does, but the victim's rights are violated all the same ([[#Minors and Incompetent Adults]]). Permanent unremediable dangerous incapacity is handled under [[#Free Will and Responsibility]] and [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Insanity Defense]]: confinement, then execution if no progress — not as classic act-forfeiture.

Within U.S. borders, the government has a duty to intervene against predation to protect all sentient animals. Carnivores that prey on innocent animals must be stopped, or, if non-threatening, placed in sanctuary or confinement and fed lab-grown meat for the rest of their lives. Intervention is phased: predator numbers are reduced humanely through fertility control and sanctuary placement, and herbivore numbers are managed the same way, so that removing predators does not lead to overpopulation, starvation, or habitat collapse. The duty is bounded: no step is taken that would cause more suffering or more rights violations than it prevents. A confined predator that remains violent, shows no sign of change, and cannot be helped falls under the rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]]: it deserves to die. Outside U.S. borders, intervening against predation is a virtue, not a duty. Herbivores, which can live without harming other sentient beings, continue to receive priority in conservation, habitat, and sanctuary policy ([[9. Energy & Environment#Regulation and Conservation|Energy & Environment]]).

## Moral Psychology

>[!tldr] In short
> Full moral agency needs sentience plus intentional action and foresight, and it scales with sophistication. Motivation rests on my framework's coherence and on Stoic discipline, not moral facts. Forfeiture is triggered only by a deliberate violation, found by a court and proportional to the act. Responsibility does not wait on the free-will debate and is absent only where agency is absent. Rights rest on sentience, so children and incompetent adults hold full rights, and innocents may not be harmed for others' benefit.

#### Theory of Agency 

A being becomes a full moral agent under my framework when it possesses sufficient first-order subjective experience combined with the capacity for intentional action and foresight of consequences. Agency is not binary in all cases; it scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication. Once agency is present, the being can generate duties through voluntary choices (most clearly seen in the responsibility trigger of voluntary procreation) and can forfeit rights through deliberate violations of others’ negative rights. Non-agent sentient beings (e.g., many animals or fetuses) retain moral status through sentience alone but do not bear the same responsibilities.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Agency](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agency/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/) 

#### Moral Motivation 

Because this system is anti-realist, moral motivation cannot rely on the belief that moral facts exist independently of human prescription. Motivation instead rests on two pillars: 
1. Rational recognition that the sentience-based negative-rights framework is the most coherent and stable system available for protecting subjective experience.
2. The personal cultivation of character through Stoicism. The agent chooses to uphold my framework because it is the best available coordination system and because living in accordance with it supports personal flourishing ([[#Stoic Eudaimonism]]). External enforcement remains necessary for those who reject the prescription.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Moral Motivation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-motivation/)


#### Weakness of Will (Akrasia) 

Weakness of will is acknowledged as a persistent human tendency. Knowing the correct action under the prescribed rules does not guarantee performance. Stoic discipline is the primary countermeasure: training judgment, focusing only on what is within one’s control, and repeatedly aligning impulse with reasoned principle. I do not excuse akrasia; failure to act rightly when one had the capacity remains a personal shortcoming, though the political system concerns itself primarily with outward rights violations rather than inner moral failure.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Weakness of Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/)


#### Moral Psychology of Rights & Forfeiture 

Humans readily grasp the logic of negative rights and proportional response when my framework is stated clearly. Rights are understood as side-constraints against interference. When an agent knowingly and wantonly violates those constraints, especially through repeated or severe predation that erodes trust, forfeiture of corresponding rights becomes both intelligible and motivating to observers. The psychological force of forfeiture lies in reciprocity and desert: those who refuse to respect the basic negative rights of others cannot claim the full protection of those same rights. This supports decisive enforcement without requiring belief in objective moral truth—only recognition of the practical necessity of the prescribed rules.

- Christopher Heath Wellman – [The Rights Forfeiture Theory of Punishment](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.apaonline.org/resource/resmgr/Prizes_&_Awards/Wellman,_Christopher.pdf)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Retributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-retributive/)



#### Free Will and Responsibility

Responsibility does not wait on a resolution of the metaphysical free-will debate. An act counts as a knowing, voluntary violation when the agent understood what it was doing, foresaw that it would harm another, and was not physically compelled. Desires, upbringing, temperament, and mental illness shape choices without erasing them; an agent who chose under their influence still chose. This is why a diagnosed condition does not by itself exempt an offender from the penalties set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]].

Responsibility is absent only where agency itself is absent: a person physically forced by another, a person who could not understand the nature of the act, or a being without the cognitive capacity for agency at all. These are the incompetence cases covered in [[#Minors and Incompetent Adults]]. Between full agency and none, the question is factual: whether this agent, at this time, knew what they were doing. Incompetence alone does not lead to execution. A person who was completely unaware and incompetent has no agency and is removed from society, imprisoned or confined, because they are too dangerous to be around. But a person who cannot be changed from incompetence and remains a threat, violent and showing no sign of change, is a threat to society and is not kept alive merely for the sake of keeping them alive. They deserve to die, as set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Insanity Defense|Criminal Justice and Security]].

## Character Ethics

>[!tldr] In short
> Stoic eudaimonism is the personal operating system for character: focus on what is within one's control, practice wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance, and keep composure while acting decisively. It supports the rights framework without replacing it.

#### Stoic Eudaimonism

I adopt Stoic eudaimonism as my personal operating system for character and daily conduct. Key elements I take from it:

- Focus only on what is within my control (my judgments, actions, and responses).
- Accept external events, including the actions of others and outcomes of politics, without being ruled by them emotionally.
- Cultivate the four cardinal virtues as practical guides: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.
- Treat adversity and the hostility of others as material for practicing virtue rather than as reasons for despair or rage.
- Maintain inner composure while still acting decisively in the world according to the prescribed sentientist principles.
- Flourish through virtue and rational living, pursued within the constraints of the prescribed rights framework.

Stoic eudaimonism is not a replacement for my political or inter-personal rights framework. It is the personal discipline that allows me to live and act consistently with that framework without being destroyed by anger, resentment, or attachment to outcomes I cannot fully control.

## Political Philosophy

>[!tldr] In short
> The state exists to protect negative rights and the security of the community that sustains them, not to equalize outcomes. Authority and law are justified only as far as they secure rights, and law that systematically violates rights loses legitimacy. A community may control its borders and prefer cultural continuity that preserves a high-trust order. The state owes its first duty to its citizens. Forfeiture scales to groups that systematically violate rights, and politics cannot create virtue or flourishing.

#### Purpose of the State 

The legitimate function of the state is the protection of negative rights and the security of the political community that sustains them. The state exists first to prevent and punish violations of life, liberty, and property, and to defend the community against external and internal threats that would destroy the conditions for those rights. It is not primarily an engine for equalizing outcomes, enforcing comprehensive moral visions, or guaranteeing positive goods.

- Robert Nozick – [_Anarchy, State, and Utopia_](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP641/%CE%95%CE%BD%CF%8C%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%84%CE%B1%205%CE%B7:%20%CE%A6%CE%AC%CE%BA%CE%B5%CE%BB%CE%BF%CF%82%20%CE%9C%CE%B1%CE%B8%CE%AE%CE%BC%CE%B1%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%82/Robert%20Nozick%20Anarchy,%20State,%20and%20Utopia%202001.pdf)
  
#### Political Authority and Coercion

Political authority is justified only insofar as it is necessary to secure negative rights and collective security. Coercive power is a tool, not an intrinsic good. It must remain bounded by the same side-constraints that apply to individuals, except where an agent has forfeited rights through serious violation. Expansive state power is accepted only when required to suppress recurrent patterns of rights violation or to defend the community against organized threats.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Authority](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/authority/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Political Legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)


#### Justice 

Justice consists primarily in the reliable protection of negative rights and the impartial application of rules that respect agency and responsibility. A just order secures the conditions under which sentient beings can exercise agency without predation, enforces rights forfeiture against violators, and refrains from using state power to engineer equal outcomes. Distributive patterns are not themselves the measure of justice; the protection of side-constraints and the maintenance of a high-trust framework for cooperation are.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Distributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-distributive/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/)


> [!question]- "Isn't rejecting positive rights cruel to the poor?"
> No. Rejecting positive rights rejects compelled redistribution as the default, not help. The floor that remains is not a right anyone can claim against others. It is part of the state's security role, since a community where people starve in the street, die untreated at the hospital door, or grow up unable to read cannot keep the order that protects everyone's rights ([[#Purpose of the State]]). It is a short list of named exceptions, each funded by minimal necessary taxes: emergency shelter and emergency medical care; basic K-12 funding averaging about \$10,000 per child, paid by each state from its general revenue; an [[Glossary#Earnings-only top-up|earnings-only top-up]] for low earners, paid only on earned income; and a strictly means-tested [[Glossary#Last-resort allowance|last-resort allowance]] for severe disability and the elderly poor ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]], [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Education]]). There is no retraining or job-placement program for workers in the industries the government shuts down; they find new jobs or apply for sanctuary jobs under the worker rules in the [[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Sanctuary Plan]]. People already on benefits keep their entitlement through the transition, and current retirees and those within ten years of full retirement age keep Social Security and Medicare for life, funded by opt-in contributions plus the temporary transition levy ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]). Beyond the floor, help comes through opt-in programs and private giving, and justice is measured by the protection of rights, not by distributive patterns.

#### Philosophy of Law

Law is a practical instrument for securing negative rights and coordinating behavior within a political community. Its authority derives from its necessity in protecting the conditions for rights-respecting order, not from any claim to embody objective moral truth. Valid law should track responsibility and agency, apply rights forfeiture proportionally, and remain limited in scope. When law systematically violates negative rights or abandons impartial enforcement, it loses legitimacy and may justly be resisted.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Legal Positivism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-positivism/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [The Nature of Law](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lawphil-nature/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Legal Obligation and Authority](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-obligation/)


#### Sovereignty and Borders

A political community has the right to maintain its sovereignty and to control its borders. Because high-trust societies better protect negative rights in practice, and that trust rests on shared norms together with impartial enforcement of law, a people may legitimately prefer cultural continuity and selective immigration that preserves the conditions for mutual cooperation and rights-respecting order. Open-ended demographic transformation that erodes those conditions ought to be avoided.

The negative-rights protections a government provides apply to the people it governs. Foreign actors, civilians included, who actively harbor ideologies supporting genocide or ethnic cleansing against a nation have, in effect, forfeited those protections against it. Where people abroad actively foment such ideas, join or support terror groups, or vote in and build new ones such as Hamas, the threatened nation has the right and the duty to deal with that threat and defend its people as it sees fit. This does not mean obliterating the foreign country; it means confronting those actively driving the threat. The security of a nation's people is paramount.

#### National Interest and Foreign Relations 

The state retains a primary duty to its own citizens. Alliances, interventions, and international commitments are evaluated according to whether they advance the security and long-term interests of the political community. No foreign tribunal or external authority holds legitimate veto power over decisions necessary for national survival or the protection of citizens’ rights.

#### Rights, Order, and Forfeiture at Scale 

The same logic of rights and forfeiture that applies to individuals scales to political life. Agents or organized groups that systematically violate the negative rights of others, or that seek to subvert the rights-respecting order itself, may be met with decisive force. Reciprocity and the practical necessity of deterrence justify withholding full rights protections from those who reject the basic framework of non-aggression and mutual restraint.

I hold that a doctrine is an excluded ideology when its program, as taught by its own authoritative texts and mainstream tradition, calls for state power to strip people of equal negative rights by status (race, religion, sex, class, or leaving the faith), and states acting on it have done so. Nazism, communism, socialism, and Islam meet that test; I judge Islam by its mainstream legal tradition, which gives non-Muslims and women lesser standing and prescribes death for leaving the faith ([[4. Religion & Secularism#The Islamic Faith in Practice|The Islamic Faith in Practice]]). The test is the same for every doctrine, religious or secular, and any doctrine that meets it falls under the same rules. The United States ought to designate excluded ideologies by constitutional amendment, never by executive declaration. Adherence to an excluded ideology carries two civic disabilities and nothing more. First, no taxpayer-funded position or public office: no one holds a negative right to a position the public pays for, so this is the government choosing its own servants, not a punishment. Second, no building, rebuilding, expansion, or relocation of places that promote it, which caps them, mosques included, at today's number: an institution built to propagate a doctrine whose program is to strip others of their rights is organized subversion of the rights-respecting order, and the bar falls on the institution, never on a person's private belief, speech, or home practice. No one is fined, jailed, removed, or silenced for holding or advocating any view. ^excluded-ideologies

#### Limits of Politics 

Politics cannot create virtue, guarantee flourishing, or eliminate all human conflict. The state’s role is deliberately narrower: to maintain the external conditions under which individuals can pursue their own ends, including character development and personal flourishing, without being subjected to predation or chaos. Over-extension of political power beyond this function tends to generate new rights violations rather than resolve them.

## Corporations as Moral Entities

>[!tldr] In short
> Corporations are tools, not moral agents. The CEO answers by default, and the people who authorize rights violations are liable as well. Limited liability stays for ordinary business, but not for deliberate violations, fraud, or knowing extreme risk.

#### Position
Corporations are legal tools, not moral agents. Rights violations attach to human agents. The CEO answers by default for the company's crimes, and officers and controlling shareholders who authorized the violation answer as well ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Income-Scaled Fines|Income-Scaled Fines]]). Limited liability stays for ordinary contract and tort; the veil is pierced for deliberate rights violations, fraud, or knowing extreme-risk conduct.

>[!example]- Documented accounts
> These are individual reported events, not statistics; they illustrate the position and do not measure a trend.
> - **Boeing 737 MAX:** Boeing admitted that two of its technical pilots deceived the FAA about MCAS and paid over \$2.5 billion under a deferred prosecution agreement after crashes that killed 346 people. U.S. Department of Justice (2021) – [Boeing Charged with 737 Max Fraud Conspiracy and Agrees to Pay over \$2.5 Billion](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/boeing-charged-737-max-fraud-conspiracy-and-agrees-pay-over-25-billion)
> - **Purdue Pharma:** the OxyContin maker pleaded guilty to three felony conspiracies, admitting it misled the DEA and paid kickbacks to boost opioid prescriptions. U.S. Department of Justice (2020) – [Opioid Manufacturer Purdue Pharma Pleads Guilty to Fraud and Kickback Conspiracies](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/opioid-manufacturer-purdue-pharma-pleads-guilty-fraud-and-kickback-conspiracies)

> [!question]- "If a corporation is not a moral agent, how can a company be punished?"
> The company itself is not punished as a moral wrongdoer. Its assets answer for its debts and torts, the CEO answers by default, and the officers and controlling shareholders who authorized a deliberate violation answer personally. Liability follows the agents, so the people who decided cannot hide behind the legal form.

#### Key Elements
- Corporations are legal tools, not moral agents.
- The CEO answers by default; officers and controlling shareholders who authorized the violation answer as well.
- Limited liability for ordinary contract and tort.
- Pierce for deliberate rights violations, fraud, or knowing extreme-risk conduct.

#### Rationale
Agency and responsibility scale to persons, so a corporation cannot itself hold moral guilt; the CEO, as the person ultimately responsible for the company, and the officers and controlling shareholders who authorize a violation do. Organized groups that systematically violate rights meet decisive force under Rights at Scale in [[#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]. U.S. courts already pierce the corporate veil only for serious misconduct, such as fraud or abuse of the corporate form.

> - Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute – [Piercing the Corporate Veil](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/piercing_the_corporate_veil)

## Duties to Future Generations

>[!tldr] In short
> Present agents may not impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings. Debt may fund rights-protection and honored transitions, not rights violations or permanent subsidies.

#### Position
Present agents must not impose extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction on future sentient beings. Public debt must not bind future people to fund rights violations or permanent subsidy schemes. Debt for rights-protection and honored transition commitments is allowed; pay-as-you-go and opt-in are preferred for non-rights spending.

> [!question]- "Future people do not exist yet. How can anyone owe them anything?"
> The duty is a side-constraint on present action, not a debt to a present person. Whoever is sentient when the harm lands holds the same rights as anyone else, so an act set in motion now that destroys those rights later is a violation once it lands, the same as a delayed bomb.

#### Key Elements
- No extreme, unjustified risk of death or rights destruction imposed on future sentient beings.
- No debt binding future people to fund rights violations or permanent subsidy schemes.
- Debt allowed for rights-protection and honored transition commitments.
- Pay-as-you-go and opt-in preferred for non-rights spending.

#### Rationale
Side-constraints bind across time where the harmed party will be sentient, extending the capital-track extreme-risk rule in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Criminal Justice & Security]] forward in time. The honored-commitments rule already protects current retirees during transition in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]. GAO projects publicly held debt reaching 123% of the economy in 2036, and reports that net interest spending in FY 2025 exceeded federal spending on national defense.

## Aesthetics

>[!tldr] In short
> Beauty is found in clarity, competence, and order. Cultures that reward rights-respecting order, skill, and merit tend to produce works of lasting quality.

#### Beauty

Beauty in the fullest sense is found in clarity, competence, and order. These are valuable because they are often the conditions under which sentient beings can live well and exercise agency without constant friction. Rights-respecting order - expressed through law, discipline, high agency, and care - generates a deeper form of beauty that frequently produces visible beauty in objects and environments. Natural visual beauty can exist independently of ethics: nature may be visually striking while remaining ethically indifferent or abominable.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Beauty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauty/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Environmental Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/environmental-aesthetics/)

#### Artistry

A culture that rewards rights-respecting order, high agency, developed skill, and merit tends to produce works of lasting aesthetic quality. A culture that treats all output as equal, or that elevates works primarily for therapeutic or political reasons while neglecting competence and clarity, tends toward aesthetic decline.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Aesthetic Judgment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aesthetic-judgment/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [The Definition of Art](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/art-definition/)

## Meaning

>[!tldr] In short
> Purpose is realized through exercising agency with virtue, within my framework's ethical constraints. Mortality is accepted, and legacy is the real impact left behind.

#### Purpose

Purpose is not discovered as an objective cosmic fact, nor is it purely invented in a vacuum. It is realized through the consistent exercise of agency in accordance with virtue and the prescribed ethical constraints. A meaningful life is one in which the agent develops character, acts rightly toward other sentient beings, and pursues long-term projects that express competence and care. [[#Stoic Eudaimonism]] provides the personal aim: flourishing through rational activity and virtue. Meaning is not a separate mystical property; it is what a well-lived life feels like from the inside when one is meeting the demands of reason and character over time.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [The Meaning of Life](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life-meaning/)
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)

#### Legacy and Mortality

Mortality is a biological fact, not a moral tragedy that requires supernatural compensation. The appropriate response is clear-eyed acceptance combined with the effort to leave the conditions for other sentient lives better rather than worse. Legacy matters insofar as it reflects actual causal impact - character transmitted, rights-respecting order maintained, useful work left behind - not as a demand for eternal remembrance. Purpose is local and agent-centered: to live well within the constraints, to protect what deserves protection, and to avoid adding unnecessary suffering or chaos.

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy – [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)
