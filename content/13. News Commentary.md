## Case: France riots, 17 October 2026

**Status as of Oct 23, 2026:** Open.

>- France, 17 Oct 2026: a nationwide day of protest against the cost of living and school conditions (authorities estimated about 550,000 marchers) turned violent in several cities. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said about 1,800 people were arrested, including a "record" 855 in the Paris region. Masked groups looted shops in Paris, Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice and Strasbourg, and Limoges's 19th-century town hall was destroyed by a fire the prefect said was set deliberately. >[!example]- Data: immigration and France's public finances
> Figures as reported by the OECD and by Chojnicki, Ragot & Sokhna; they bear on the fiscal point in my view below.
>- In the OECD's International Migration Outlook 2021 (Table 4.1, 2006–18 average), immigrants' net contribution in France is −0.85% of GDP when pure public goods such as defence and debt interest are shared per head, +0.25% when they are left out, and +1.02% counting only individual taxes, benefits, health and education. Defenders of France's immigration record point out that natives come out at −3.25% in the same accounting and that the total stays within about 1% of GDP. Per head, the picture runs the other way: in France each immigrant pays 12% less than a native-born resident while public spending per immigrant is only 6% lower, and the OECD singles out France for a relative fall in immigrants' fiscal ratio in the latest years. ## Case: Massachusetts abortion law, Chapter 188, 2026

**What happened (sourced):** Before Chapter 188, Massachusetts allowed an abortion at or after 24 weeks only if it was needed to preserve the patient's life or physical or mental health, or for a lethal fetal anomaly or a grave fetal diagnosis incompatible with sustained life outside the uterus, and only in a hospital. Chapter 188 replaces those four reasons with "the professional judgment of the physician," bars any medical review process from overriding that judgment, and drops the hospital requirement, so licensed clinics may perform these abortions. Abortion before 24 weeks and written informed consent are unchanged. It takes effect 8 November 2026 unless the referendum petition suspends it. Massachusetts Legislature – [Chapter 188 of the Acts of 2026](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2026/Chapter188)

**My view:** This is the drift I describe in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Abortion and Bioethics|Abortion and Bioethics]]. A state that allowed abortion after 24 weeks only for set reasons now leaves it to the physician's judgment, at a stage inside the range where expert estimates place the onset of sentience. That is why I support pro-life policy in full as a practical safeguard, separate from the sentience line itself.
