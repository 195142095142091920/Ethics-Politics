## Core Principles
Strong support for technological advancement and transhumanism, constrained only by sentient rights. Free-market and private innovation preferred; government role limited to regulation against existential threats, rights violations, and national security. Opt-in privacy and data rules; animal testing redirected to forfeited rights holders.

## AI and Automation

#### Position
AI is monitored and regulated as part of the government's role in protecting citizens' rights and security, limited to existential and rights-violation risks. If AI is observed becoming an existential threat, a risk serious enough to prepare for, the government focuses on regulating its development and preparing to defend against it. A citizen dividend is on the table as a response to heavy automation: a percentage of what the country produces as a nation, given back to citizens, rather than income taken through taxation. The position is pro-AI, and a defensive AGI will likely be needed to combat any dangerous AGI that emerges.

> [!question]- "Does a pro-AI stance conflict with treating AI as an existential threat?"
> No. AI offers enormous benefits and also serious risks. Supporting development while regulating against existential danger and building defensive capability addresses both.

#### Key Elements
- Regulate AI as part of government's protective duty.
- Treat existential risk seriously.
- Prepare a defensive AGI against dangerous AGI.
- Citizen dividend open as a response to automation-driven job loss: a percentage of national product given back to citizens, not income taken through taxation.
- Pro-AI overall.

#### Rationale
Protecting citizens from catastrophic threats is a core government function, and advanced AI may become one. In a 2023 survey of 2,778 AI researchers, the median estimate of the chance of extremely bad outcomes such as human extinction was 5%. Federal policy currently aims to promote American AI leadership. A defensive capability ensures that if dangerous AI emerges, it can be countered. Measured job losses from automation have so far been modest (0.2 percentage points of employment per added robot per 1,000 workers), which is why the citizen dividend stays conditional.

> [!info]- What is AGI?
> Artificial general intelligence refers to AI systems able to perform most intellectual tasks at or beyond human level across domains, rather than within narrow tasks.

##### Sources
- The White House – [Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence/)
- Grace et al. – [Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)
- Acemoglu & Restrepo, Journal of Political Economy – [Robots and Jobs: Evidence from US Labor Markets (2020)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/705716)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> The position is pro-AI but treats it as a serious existential risk, so government regulates it and prepares a defensive AGI. A citizen dividend, a share of what the nation produces given back to citizens, is an option if automation displaces large numbers of workers.

## Pandemics and Biosecurity

#### Position
Vaccine mandates are rejected; only opt-in vaccines. If you choose to get vaccinated, you immunize yourself and help limit spread to others; if you don't, that's your choice. There is no exception for severe cases. Engineered pandemic pathogens are an existential risk, which falls within the government's role in regulating technology, so research that makes dangerous pathogens more transmissible or lethal is regulated accordingly.

> [!question]- "Doesn't regulating research slow medicine?"
> Regulation is limited to research that creates existential risk. Most biomedical research is untouched.

#### Key Elements
- Vaccine mandates rejected.
- Only opt-in vaccines.
- No severe-case exception.
- Engineered pathogens as existential risk.
- Regulation of dangerous gain-of-function research.

#### Rationale
Executive Order 14292 (May 5, 2025) ended federal funding for dangerous gain-of-function research in countries of concern and paused other federally funded such research pending new policy. Under it, the USG Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research, announced in NIH notice NOT-OD-26-101 on July 28, 2026, prohibits federal support for dangerous gain-of-function research and sets risk-based oversight for other life-sciences research. Emergency powers are limited as set out in [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Emergency Powers|Authority & Civil Liberties]].

##### Sources
- The White House – [Improving the Safety and Security of Biological Research (EO 14292)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/improving-the-safety-and-security-of-biological-research/)
- National Institutes of Health – [NOT-OD-26-101: USG Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-26-101.html)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Vaccine mandates are rejected; only opt-in vaccines. Dangerous gain-of-function research is regulated as an existential risk.

## Genetic Engineering and CRISPR

#### Position
Where genetic editing can modify against negative traits, it is done. Designer babies are acceptable as long as sentient rights are not violated post-sentience. Genetic engineering of animals is done only to benefit their sentient-rights protections, never to violate them.

> [!question]- "Are designer babies a step toward eugenics?"
> Eugenics historically meant coercive state control of reproduction. Genetic editing chosen to remove negative traits, with rights respected after sentience, involves no coercion and benefits the individual.

#### Key Elements
- Support editing out negative traits.
- Designer babies acceptable if post-sentience rights are respected.
- Animal genetic engineering only when it advances their sentient rights.

#### Rationale
Reducing suffering from genetic disease benefits sentient beings. Heritable human editing is currently restricted in many countries, and some scientists have called for a moratorium, which shows where the debate stands. The sentient-rights test sets the limit.

> [!info]- What is CRISPR?
> CRISPR is a gene-editing technology that allows precise changes to DNA in living organisms.

##### Sources
- Lander et al., Nature – [Adopt a moratorium on heritable genome editing](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-00726-5)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Genes should be edited to remove negative traits, and designer babies are fine if rights are respected after sentience. Animals may be genetically modified only for their own benefit.

## End-of-Life Care and Lost Sentience

#### Position
While a person remains sentient, their own decisions govern their care, including refusing treatment. Advance directives and other known wishes are honored. When sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, the being keeps residual moral consideration, and decision-making authority, including whether to continue life support, passes to next-of-kin, guided by the person's known wishes and dignity (Axiom 4). Whether sentience is irreversibly lost is decided by the best available scientific consensus and accepted medical standards (Axiom 5).

> [!question]- "Who decides when a patient can no longer speak and has left no instructions?"
> Next-of-kin decide, guided by what is known of the person's wishes and by respect for their dignity. Where the person left an advance directive, it governs. The framework places the decision with the family rather than with the state, which intervenes only to protect a still-sentient person's rights.

#### Key Elements
- A sentient patient's own decisions govern their care, including refusal of treatment.
- Advance directives and known wishes are honored.
- After permanent loss of sentience, next-of-kin decide, guided by known wishes and dignity.
- Irreversible loss is determined by scientific consensus and medical standards.
- Residual moral consideration remains after sentience is lost.

#### Rationale
Axiom 4 already sets the rule; this topic applies it to medical care. Current law offers the working standard. The Uniform Determination of Death Act defines death as the irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory functions, or of all functions of the entire brain including the brain stem, determined by accepted medical standards. In *Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health* (1990), the Supreme Court allowed a state to require clear and convincing evidence of an incompetent patient's wishes before life support is withdrawn. That shows why recorded wishes matter in practice. Assisted death for competent adults is covered in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Assisted Death|Cultural & Social Issues]].

##### Sources
- Uniform Law Commission – [Uniform Determination of Death Act (1981)](https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=38420c48-ebbd-531d-da32-e6cc175ebdb1)
- Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health (1990) – [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/497/261)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Patients decide their own care while they are conscious, and their written wishes are followed. Once consciousness is permanently gone, the family decides, guided by what the person wanted. Medical science decides when that point has been reached.

## Reproductive Technology

#### Position
IVF, embryo screening, and genetic editing against negative traits are supported, and designer babies are acceptable as long as sentient rights are respected after sentience. Whoever creates an embryo holds responsibility and stewardship for it, following the responsibility created by voluntary procreation. Before sentience, an embryo receives provisional protection of the kind given in animal welfare, not the full rights of a sentient being. Full rights attach at sentience. The same caution applied to abortion governs here: protection starts earlier than the moral line as a safeguard against political drift, and moral weight rises gradually as development approaches sentience. Keeping an embryo in frozen storage indefinitely does not pause the creator's duty.

> [!question]- "If an embryo isn't sentient, why protect it at all?"
> Because creating it was a voluntary act, and voluntary procreation creates responsibility. Before sentience the protection is provisional, in the animal-welfare register rather than the register of full rights. The caution that leads legal limits on abortion to start before the moral line applies here too, so protection does not wait until the moment of sentience. Freezing an embryo indefinitely does not suspend that responsibility.

#### Key Elements
- IVF, embryo screening, and editing against negative traits are supported.
- Designer babies are acceptable if rights are respected after sentience.
- The creator of an embryo holds responsibility and stewardship.
- Pre-sentience: provisional protection in the animal-welfare register.
- Full rights at sentience.
- Protection starts earlier than the moral line, and moral weight rises gradually toward sentience.
- Indefinite frozen storage does not pause the creator's duty.

#### Rationale
Assisted reproduction is now a routine part of family formation. In 2022, U.S. clinics reported 435,426 ART cycles resulting in 98,289 live-born infants, about 2.6% of all U.S. births, and 184,423 of those cycles were banking cycles in which all eggs or embryos were frozen for later use.[^cdc-art] Large numbers of stored embryos make the question of stewardship practical rather than theoretical. The responsibility and sentience framework in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Abortion and Bioethics|Cultural & Social Issues]] supplies the rule, and editing and selection follow [[#Genetic Engineering and CRISPR]].

[^cdc-art]: Figures taken from a search-engine copy of the CDC's ART Surveillance page; the CDC site blocked direct retrieval for this draft. A future data-overhaul pass will verify them against the CDC source.

##### Sources
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention – [ART Surveillance](https://www.cdc.gov/art/php/surveillance/index.html)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> IVF, embryo screening, and gene editing are supported. Whoever creates an embryo is responsible for it: before sentience it gets provisional protection like animal welfare, with protection starting early as a safeguard, and full rights at sentience. Freezing an embryo indefinitely does not end that responsibility.

## Organ Donation

#### Position
Organ donation and organ sales are opt-in. Any government payment related to organ donation or sales requires opt-in funding. It is always opt-in, even after death: it reflects the wishes of the person in life, not a default that takes without asking. Organs from offenders sentenced to death, whose rights are forfeited, may be used under the same logic as the experimentation track. They are taken either at execution, which still takes place on the sentencing date under [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Capital Trials and the Abolition of Death Row|Section 6]], or from offenders the state has assigned to the experimentation track, as set out in [[#Animal Rights and Sentience Technology]].

> [!question]- "Isn't taking organs from executed offenders abuse?"
> Under forfeiture, a condemned violent offender has given up the rights that would otherwise protect them, just as with experimentation. Innocent people's organs are taken only with consent.

#### Key Elements
- Organ donation and organ sales are opt-in.
- Government payments related to donation or sales require opt-in funding.
- Always opt-in, even after death; no default that takes without asking.
- Forfeited offenders' organs may be used.
- Same logic as the experimentation track.

#### Rationale
As of July 30, 2026, 109,817 people were on the U.S. national transplant waiting list, according to the Health Resources and Services Administration. The experimentation track is set out in [[#Animal Rights and Sentience Technology]].

##### Sources
- Health Resources and Services Administration – [Organ Donation Statistics](https://www.organdonor.gov/learn/organ-donation-statistics)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Organ donation and sales are always opt-in, even after death, and any government payment needs opt-in funding. Organs of condemned offenders may be used under forfeiture.

## Animal Rights and Sentience Technology

#### Position
Lab-grown meat is viewed as humanity's best hope for a sentientist world and for vastly diminishing animal agriculture. AI sentience can be allowed, and if artificial systems give rise to true sentience, that sentience receives the protections of sentient rights, as biological beings do, weighted on the sliding scale in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Section 1]], and is subject to the same forfeiture rules. Animal testing is completely banned. Scientific experimentation of any kind, including cosmetic, pharmaceutical, and technological testing, is redirected to violent criminal offenders sentenced to death, whose rights have been forfeited through their egregious violations. Same-day execution remains the default; as an alternative selected case by case, the state may assign or reassign an offender sentenced to death to an experimentation track at any time based on research needs, Offenders on the experimentation track have forfeited their rights; they are experimented on for as long as the institution or research agency deems appropriate, and are then executed at some point or put down in the course of an experiment.

> [!question]- "Is experimentation on condemned prisoners compatible with sentient rights?"
> Under the forfeiture principle, those who commit egregious violations of others' rights forfeit their own. Innocent animals hold their rights intact; condemned violent offenders do not. The burden of experimentation shifts from those who did nothing wrong to those who did.

> [!question]- "Can AI and computer modeling replace experimentation on living subjects?"
> Not yet. AI speeds discovery and improves early safety results, but it has not improved how often drugs work in patients, no AI-discovered drug has been approved end to end, and regulators in the United States and Europe still require testing on living subjects where validated alternatives do not exist.

#### Key Elements
- Lab-grown meat as the best path to a sentientist world.
- True AI sentience receives full sentient-rights protections.
- Animal testing completely banned.
- Any form of scientific experimentation redirected to violent offenders sentenced to death, through an experimentation track the state may assign or reassign case by case in place of same-day execution; experimentation continues for as long as the institution or research agency deems appropriate, followed by execution or death in the course of an experiment. Sentencing and timing rules are set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Capital Trials and the Abolition of Death Row|Section 6]].

#### Rationale
Cultivated-meat costs remain unproven at industrial scale and investment fell in 2024, so the path depends on private innovation reaching price parity. Animal testing inflicts suffering on sentient beings who have violated no one's rights. Federal law now permits alternatives to animal testing for drug approval under the FDA Modernization Act 2.0. Forfeiture theory, set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Criminal Justice & Security]], grounds the redirection of experimentation. Computational methods do not yet replace testing on living subjects. AI-discovered drugs pass early safety trials 80 to 90 percent of the time, against a historical 40 to 65 percent, but succeed in about 40 percent of trials that test effectiveness in patients, roughly the historical average, and no drug discovered end to end by AI has been approved. AI-discovered candidates from BenevolentAI, Exscientia, and Recursion have failed in human trials, and AlphaFold3 cannot reliably predict how strongly drugs bind to their targets. Animal models also predict human results poorly: over 90 percent of drugs that pass animal studies are never approved. The FDA began phasing out animal testing in 2025, starting with monoclonal antibodies, only where equivalent or better alternatives exist; the European Medicines Agency accepts AI only as validated support, not as a sole basis for approval; and non-animal methods cannot yet replace whole-body or long-term safety testing. Experimentation on living subjects therefore remains necessary, and the experimentation track places that burden on condemned offenders rather than on animals.

## AI Sentience Determination

#### Position
Whether an artificial system is sentient is determined by the best available scientific consensus, as for any being. This follows Section 1: if AI is sentient, it is given sentient rights and treated with the same level of forfeiture if necessary. Consensus decides sentience.

> [!question]- "How could anyone tell if an AI is sentient?"
> Through the best available science, the same standard used for animals. Researchers have proposed indicators drawn from theories of consciousness.

#### Key Elements
- Scientific consensus decides.
- Sentient-rights protections if sentient, weighted on the Section 1 sliding scale.
- Same forfeiture rules.

#### Rationale
Butlin, Long, and colleagues (2023) proposed assessing AI systems against indicator properties derived from scientific theories of consciousness. Sentience rules are in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Sentience Determination|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]].

##### Sources
- Butlin, Long, et al. – [Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> AI sentience is decided by scientific consensus, and sentient AI gets full rights.

## Surveillance and Digital Technology

#### Position
All data privacy is respected, and all data trading is opt-in only. Government regulates this and punishes any transgressions. Data acquired in public spaces carries no expectation of privacy protection and is used for public protection and the best interest of the people, and by companies only with notice and opt-in. Government regulates any private company trading or accessing this encrypted data.

> [!question]- "If public data has no privacy expectation, why regulate companies' use of it?"
> Public data may be used for public protection. Private trading of that data for commercial gain is a separate act, and regulation keeps it within consumer protections.

#### Key Elements
- Data privacy fully respected.
- All data trading opt-in only.
- Government regulates and punishes violations.
- Public-space data carries no privacy expectation; used for public protection, and by companies only with notice and opt-in.
- Private companies accessing or trading such data remain regulated.

#### Rationale
Personal data is an extension of the person, and its sale without consent is a rights violation. Public spaces carry no expectation of privacy, so public-safety use is permitted. These rules match the privacy and surveillance positions in [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Surveillance State|Authority & Civil Liberties]].

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [Fourth Amendment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/fourth_amendment)
- Supreme Court of the United States – [Carpenter v. United States, 585 U.S. 296 (2018)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/16-402)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Data privacy is respected and all data trading is opt-in. Public-space data can be used for public safety, and companies handling it are regulated.

## Deepfakes and Synthetic Media

#### Position
Deepfakes are free speech, protected under [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Free Speech and Censorship|Section 7]]. Identity fraud using deepfakes is a crime under existing fraud law, not a speech restriction. Using someone's likeness for profit without consent is a property claim. Non-commercial use (parody, art, political commentary) is protected; the line is commercial use, not any use. Deepfakes are impossible to regulate, so the approach stays narrow: protect speech, punish fraud, don't ban the tech.

> [!question]- "Doesn't leaving deepfakes legal invite abuse?"
> Fraud is punished under existing fraud law, and commercial use of a person's likeness without consent is a property claim. Everything else is speech.

#### Key Elements
- Deepfakes are free speech.
- Identity fraud using deepfakes prosecuted under existing fraud law.
- Commercial use of likeness without consent is a property claim.
- Non-commercial use (parody, art, political commentary) protected.
- No ban on the technology.

#### Rationale
Federal wire fraud law already punishes schemes to defraud carried out by electronic communication, whatever the medium of deception (18 U.S.C. § 1343). Speech limits are set out in [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Free Speech and Censorship|Cultural & Social Issues]].

##### Sources
- Cornell LII – [18 U.S.C. § 1343: Fraud by wire, radio, or television](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1343)

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Deepfakes are free speech. Fraud is punished under fraud law, commercial use of a likeness without consent is a property claim, and the technology is not banned.

## Space and Exploration

#### Position
Human spaceflight is completely privatized. The only government space program that is funded is militarized, focused on advancing technology to compete with any foreign power. Private companies conduct research and development for civilian spaceflight. Government may purchase from or contract private companies for military research or security programs.

> [!question]- "Can private companies lead in space without government funding?"
> Private firms already build launch vehicles and spacecraft under commercial models. The government remains a customer for security needs rather than a competitor.

#### Key Elements
- Human spaceflight fully privatized.
- Government space efforts limited to militarized programs and competition with foreign powers.
- Private companies handle civilian R&D.
- Government contracts private firms for military and security needs.

#### Rationale
Private enterprise innovates faster and at lower cost than government programs. NASA already relies on commercial partners for crew and cargo transport. Space is a strategic domain, which justifies a military program.

##### Sources

>[!tldr]- Summary
> Human spaceflight is left to private companies. Government funds only military space programs and hires private firms when needed.

#### Rationale
Open sharing accelerates innovation, while protection preserves incentives for those who invest in creation. Patent and copyright law provide the framework for those who opt in.
