# Where I stand: my political and moral labels, in plain words

A plain guide to what I believe and which labels fit. When a technical term is the actual name of a label, I use it once and explain it. "S1", "S2" and so on are sections of my site.

---

## 1. The short version

**In one line:** I care about anything that can feel. I believe you lose rights when you violate someone else's. I want a republic where only qualified voters vote, a small government you opt into, very tough punishment, a country built on shared Western values, a hawkish America-First foreign policy, and technology that ends animal farming and cuts suffering in nature. I vote Republican because it's the closest fit, not a perfect one.

**In a phrase:** *Sentientist epistocratic national-individualist.*
- **Sentientist:** moral worth comes from being able to feel.
- **Epistocratic:** voting should take a basic test, not just turning 18.
- **National-individualist:** free markets and individual rights, in a country that guards its borders and culture.

The full list: sentientist, negative-rights, forfeiture-retributivist, epistocratic-republican, civilizational-nationalist, free-market, transhumanist-abolitionist. Each is explained below.

**In a paragraph:**
- **Morals:** I don't think moral truth exists on its own out there. I choose the most consistent system I know. Anything that can feel has firm rights not to be harmed, and you lose them only by violating someone else's. That idea drives a lot. I believe there should be a mandatory death penalty for many crimes, a three-strikes rule that can cost your life, public executions, medical testing on condemned criminals instead of animals, use of their organs, and no civilian protection abroad for people who commit hostile acts.
- **Government:** a constitutional republic where you vote only if you pay more in taxes than you get back and pass a voter test. Ranked-choice voting everywhere.
- **The state:** small. A flat tax, a floor for low earners that rewards work, opt-in welfare, Social Security, Medicare and subsidies, no minimum wage. Very tough at the border and on crime.
- **Four ideologies:** Nazism, communism, socialism and Islam. Their followers should be kept out of sworn positions of power and their buildings capped. No one punished for speech.
- **Culture:** secular agnostic atheist. I prefer traditional family life but wouldn't force it. Gender-critical, but for same-sex marriage and anti-discrimination law. Colorblind, against DEI, near-absolute on free speech. Private vice legal, but selling hard drugs should be capital. Noticing patterns is reasonable, but each person is judged on what they do.
- **Abroad:** pro-Israel, regime change in Iran by force, pro-Ukraine, no nation-building, no outside body over U.S. law. Congress must approve a war within 30 days.
- **Nature and tech:** pro-technology and pro-enhancement. End animal farming, fishing and animal testing on day one, and work toward a world where predators don't kill.
- **Parties:** a Republican-leaning independent who backs the Trump administration overall. On my site, 52 of its 87 graded actions move my way and 20 move against me. I break with it most on animals, tariffs, entitlements, vouchers, war powers and due process.

## 2. Topic by topic

For each topic: the main label, other labels that fit, what I believe, and where I differ from the usual version.

### 2.1 Where morality comes from
**Label:** *Constructivist-prescriptivist moral anti-realism.* Plainly: there are no moral facts built into the universe. People build moral rules. Mine apply to everyone, me included, and they express a settled stance against harming anything that can suffer.
**Also:** David Hume's idea that morals come from what we care about, plus R. M. Hare's idea that moral rules must apply to everyone equally. "Consistency-based rule-making."

**What I believe:** In S1 I say, "I claim no objective moral truth. I prescribe a sentience-centered system because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available." I give three reasons: people disagree deeply about morals; built-in moral facts would be strange things unlike anything else in nature (J. L. Mackie); and evolution shaped our moral feelings for survival, not truth (Sharon Street, Richard Joyce). I answer the main objections in full. People who reject my system "are still bound by its enforcement." It's "a coherent system I choose to endorse and want enforced."

**Where I differ:** Most people who deny moral facts go soft on judgment. I make firm calls and treat enforcing them as part of the system. It sets rules; it doesn't say "anything goes" (my description).

### 2.2 How I judge right and wrong
**Label:** *Threshold sentientist deontology.* Plainly: firm rules against harming anything that can feel, which hold until someone crosses the line by violating another's rights. My site calls it "Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)."
**Also:** two layers. Firm rules for politics, Stoic virtue for my own life.

**What I believe:** Rights are firm limits on what anyone may do to anyone else (philosophers call these *side-constraints*), "not goals to maximize." Numbers never justify harming an innocent person. The rules give way only when someone violates rights by force or fraud, or knowingly puts others at severe risk without consent. "I'm a threshold deontologist, not a utilitarian." In S10: "My framework doesn't rest on Kantian dignity. It rests on negative rights, which an agent forfeits by violating them in others."

**Where I differ:**
- **The line:** usually the rules give way when results would be bad enough. In mine, only when the person has violated someone else's rights. It's about losing rights, not disasters.
- **Fair punishment:** judged once, when a court finds with due process and strict evidence that rights were lost. After that, "the sentence carried out within them is not a further injustice." Testing on condemned criminals works the same way. Harm outside the sentence, like a guard's abuse unrelated to any study, should still be punished.
- **Duties to care for others** come only from your own choices, like the responsibility from a pregnancy you chose to risk. That's part of my abortion view.

### 2.3 Who counts, including animals
**Label:** *Sentientism.* Plainly: anything that can feel counts, animal or AI. Animals are sorted into classes by how much they can feel, and above a line every animal's right to life is equal.
**Also:** animal-rights abolitionist; cutting suffering in the wild; "end predation."

**What I believe:**
- Worth comes from feeling, "biological or artificial." Self-awareness isn't needed.
- An expert board should set animal classes in law, with changes only going forward. Top class: mammals, birds, octopus and squid, crabs and lobsters, including rats, mice and pigeons. Fish and shrimp: species by species. Clams, oysters and insects: a low, precautionary class with a small fine. Being a pest should never lower an animal's class, and killing pests should be allowed only for a real infestation of a home, food store or medical building.
- Killing a top-class animal is murder and should be capital. Hunting is murder, with no subsistence or Indigenous exception.
- Animal farming, fishing, fish farming, breeding mills and animal testing should be banned on day one, under my Sanctuary Plan (S9). No one should be punished for killings before the ban.
- Predation is a rights violation. The U.S. government should stop it inside its borders, step by step, never causing more suffering than it prevents.

**Where I differ:** Most who start from feeling are utilitarians, like Peter Singer; I'm rights-based. I use feeling, not Tom Regan's narrower "subject of a life" test. Regan saw no duty to stop predators; I do. Most abolitionists, like Gary Francione, oppose violence and prisons; I believe killing animals should be capital. Above the line, the right to life is equal, as with a baby and a philosopher: "a pig's killing is sentenced like a human's." Putting down a pet outside the rules should be judged on a sliding scale, not automatically capital.

### 2.4 What kind of government I want
**Label:** *Epistocratic constitutional republic.* Plainly: a constitutional republic where voting depends on passing a basic test and paying in, not just age.
**Also:** Aristotle's "polity" or "timocracy" (a mixed government where voters have a stake); "limited democracy"; small-government constitutionalism; and, in my description, *militant democracy*, Karl Loewenstein's term for a democracy that shuts out movements hostile to it. My site doesn't use that term.

**What I believe:**
- **Who votes (S3):** net taxpayers for the prior year ("close to what Aristotle called timocracy") who hold a voter license, earned by passing a test set by a bipartisan commission, with pass rates by group published.
- **Elections:** ranked-choice voting required by amendment; keep the Electoral College with electors chosen by ranked choice; no term limits; three-year cycles; parties are private groups and top-two primaries should be banned; rules on deceptive candidates set before the election.
- **Ballot measures:** a national initiative for qualified voters only, needing 1% of them for a law and 2% for an amendment.
- **Money:** only individuals should give to candidates; independent speech stays free.
- **Other:** felons shouldn't vote, and federal enforcement should hold back for 60 days before an election.
- **Purpose:** government exists for "protection of negative rights and the security of the political community." In S11 I say Switzerland comes closest to "my preference for decentralized power under citizen control."

**Where I differ:** Jason Brennan, the best-known modern voice for voter tests, pairs them with global-minded liberalism; I pair them with nationalism. My stake test is about taxes, not land or military service. I keep Swiss-style ballot measures inside a tested electorate. Countries like Germany ban anti-democratic parties; I'd ban no party and no speech, only bar followers of four ideologies from sworn power and cap their buildings, and I name one religion among them.

### 2.5 Rights
**Label:** *Negative rights with forfeiture.* Plainly: rights protect you from harm and interference; they aren't claims to free goods. You lose them by violating others.
**Also:** libertarian rights in Robert Nozick's style, with a basic safety net justified by keeping order; "opt-in" funding based on consent.

**What I believe:**
- **No rights to free goods.** "I reject positive rights." The floor is "not a right anyone can claim." It's part of keeping order, and it should cover emergency shelter and medical care, about $10,000 per child for K-12, a top-up for low earners paid only on earned income, and a last-resort allowance.
- **Property:** "Once ownership is properly established, it should last." If a rule wipes out a property's value, the owner should be paid.
- **Opt-in:** welfare, Social Security, Medicare, the public health plan, subsidies, data trading, facial recognition, social credit scores, organ donation and vaccines.
- **Two cases outside property protection**, resting on lost rights and the four-ideology rules. S3: "Two cases fall outside that protection: one is barred by amendment, and in the other the owners forfeited it."
  - **Sanctuary takeover:** animal-industry companies would convert or be sold off with no payment to owners, plus a Sanctuary Levy of no more than 3%. These firms "forfeited ordinary property protections by using their assets to violate animals' rights." It's "a one-time forfeiture, not a break in the property right." I treat the levy separately, as "a time-limited tax to fund sanctuary care, and I accept that cost."
  - **The building bar** on the four ideologies.

**Where I differ:** Strict libertarians accept no required safety net; I'd fund a small one with minimal taxes. I see both property cases as following from my rules: the companies used their property to violate animals' rights, and the institutions exist to undermine others' rights. A standard libertarian wouldn't agree. I'd use automatic enrollment you can opt out of only where people protect themselves, like disability insurance, retirement and index accounts. That's the "nudge" approach (my description).

### 2.6 Money and the economy
**Label:** free-market, flat-tax liberalism with opt-in welfare and a floor for low earners.
**Also:** small-government fiscal conservative; Milton Friedman-style (a low-earner top-up and a rule-based Fed); pro-family tax credits; "nudge" defaults (my description).

**What I believe:**
- **Taxes:** a flat 20% income tax with no tax on the first $42k (single) or $84k (couple); an extra 2 points until 2041 for the transition; 15% on investment income, with heirs keeping the original cost basis instead of an estate tax; no VAT, sales, estate or wealth tax at any level; businesses deduct investments right away. I accept that a flat tax cuts most for the rich, on principle.
- **Welfare:** the low-earner top-up should replace the EITC and child tax credit; a last-resort allowance; SNAP, farm payments and non-emergency Medicaid should wind down, honoring current recipients; non-citizens shouldn't qualify.
- **Social Security and Medicare:** voluntary for new workers; anyone within 10 years of full retirement age keeps benefits; a transition levy capped at 16% pays for it.
- **Work:** no minimum wage, state ones included; voluntary unions; job licensing only against real harm.
- **Big companies:** "big is not illegal."
- **Budget:** a 30% cut to non-defense spending; a debt brake; balanced by 2050; no more grants to nonprofits, contracts only. Government jobs: hire on merit, elected officials pick policy roles, quick firing with stated reasons.
- **The Fed:** one goal, stable prices. No bailouts, competing currencies allowed, no gold standard, governors removable only for cause.
- **Trade:** reciprocal. Tariffs are "good in theory, questionable in practice," and they're Congress's call. The Jones Act shipping rule should go.
- **School:** about $10,000 per child following the child to any *public or charter* school; no vouchers or education savings accounts; a $1,000 homeschool credit; abolish the federal Department of Education; required gifted programs; no new federal student loans.
- **Family:** a child credit that doubles from the third child; a birth credit from the third child; mothers of three pay half their income tax and mothers of four pay none; fewer housing rules; no paid-leave mandate; no limits on IVF.
- **If AI takes jobs (S10):** no universal basic income or citizen dividend. Every adult auto-enrolled in a private index-fund account. If job data hits a set trigger, the tax on investment income goes to 20%, then up to 25%, and each step reverses when jobs recover. I say plainly that if AI wiped out almost all jobs fast, I'd have to go past a "minimal" state, and my choice then would be a large income paid by taxing capital.

**Where I differ:** From most conservatives: no vouchers, no broad tariffs, no protecting entitlements, no farm subsidies. From libertarians: I keep public school funding, a voluntary public health plan, family credits, and the AI tax trigger. From economic nationalists: I'd rather cut taxes and rules than use tariffs and mandates. Funding extras only "from taxpayers who choose to chip in" is pretty much my own idea (my description).

### 2.7 Crime and punishment
**Label:** *Hard retributivism through forfeiture.* Plainly: criminals get what they deserve, and the crime itself costs them their rights. Tough-on-crime to the max.
**Also:** "tough penalties with strong fair-trial rules"; close to Singapore and Bukele's El Salvador (my description; I mention Singapore in S11 and Bukele's results in S13).

**What I believe:**
- **Mandatory death** for murder (including attempted murder and knowingly creating extreme risk to life), violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, human trafficking, selling or distributing hard drugs, domestic violence or cruelty causing lasting injury, and killing a top-class animal. The victim should choose execution or the medical testing track. If the victim is dead, the judge must choose and can't refuse.
- **Three marks** should cost your right to life, which can reach repeat thieves. The victim picks execution, testing, or life with labor.
- **Process:** capital trials within 30 days and same-day execution, no death row; public executions by hanging or gunshot; no fixed minimum age, with a maturity check before a death sentence; mental ability doesn't reduce the sentence; for insanity, confinement, then execution as protection against a permanent threat after three years without progress; hard labor for every sentence, no idle prison time; fines scaled to income and wealth, with CEOs personally liable.
- **Why:** desert, keeping offenders from doing it again, and lost rights. Not deterrence.
- **Emergencies (S3, S6):** in a declared, time-limited emergency, El Salvador-style sweeps and on-the-spot sentencing by police or soldiers should be allowed. The strict evidence rule, body cameras and later review still apply, and an officer whose sentence isn't backed up is charged with murder.

**Where I differ:** Unlike most tough-on-crime conservatives, I'd end qualified immunity and hold police to a higher standard, release people before trial by default and end cash bail, require unanimous 12-person juries in civil and criminal cases, end taking property without a conviction, and pay the wrongly convicted from the prosecuting office's budget. A death sentence needs objective evidence like video or physical proof, not just testimony. I reject several Supreme Court limits on the death penalty (*Roper*, *Miller*, *Woodson*, *Atkins*, *Ford*, *Coker*, *Kennedy*), and I believe the 8th Amendment should be amended or abolished. My capital list is longer than any U.S. tradition.

### 2.8 Culture and social issues
**Label:** secular, gender-critical traditionalist ("preference, not mandate") who leaves room for personal freedom.
**Also:** mixed free-market and conservative on culture (my description); colorblind merit; near-absolute free speech.

**What I believe:**
- **Abortion:** rights start at 12 weeks; elective abortion should be banned from conception by amendment as a safeguard against the limit creeping later; exceptions for rape and the mother's life. Assisted suicide should be allowed for competent adults, with safeguards.
- **Sex and gender:** "A man is an adult human male and a woman is an adult human female." Transgenderism is "a mental illness." Adults may transition privately; minors should get no treatment. Bathrooms (including in private businesses), sports, ID and prisons go by birth sex. No required pronouns. Coercive conversion methods banned; open-ended talk therapy allowed. Trans people can serve in the military under normal medical standards.
- **Family:** traditional roles are best but never required; same-sex civil marriage legal; no default custody rule; sex between blood relatives banned as a precaution; age of consent 18, with a close-in-age exception.
- **Speech:** only true threats and incitement punishable; hate speech legal; private platforms free to moderate; no heckler's veto, so the state protects the event; deepfakes are speech.
- **Religion:** strict church-state separation; churches pay taxes; no school prayer; ritual slaughter banned for every faith. Moderate Christianity and Western heritage are "good." Islam is "the most toxic, problematic major religion on earth by a lot." Under my proposed amendment, Muslims shouldn't hold sworn positions of power, no new mosques should be built, and existing ones should be capped. My aim is for these ideologies to "die out over time" through environment, not force.
- **Vice and minors:** marijuana fully legal, and I'd vote No on repealing Massachusetts retail sales; hard drugs legal for private use, but selling them capital, with no stores; prostitution licensed; gambling legal; adult porn legal with minors kept out; no social media under 16, and parents opt in for 16-18.
- **Guns:** qualified people can own them, with background checks and court-ordered red-flag laws.

**Where I differ:** Unlike social conservatives, I'm secular, against religious law, for same-sex marriage and for legal vice. Unlike liberal secularists, I'd limit Islam in public office and buildings, a much stronger version of the "secular, anti-Islam liberal" stance (my description). I call those limits "not a penalty." No one should be fined, jailed or silenced for belief.

### 2.8a Race and group statistics
**Label:** colorblind, merit-based, judging individuals, honest about patterns.
**Also:** "Judged as an individual, by the same law." Equal treatment, no DEI, either way.

**My position:**
- Group statistics never become verdicts on individuals. Each person should be judged on their own values and conduct.
- Stereotypes and prejudice aren't necessarily bad as long as they don't lead to rights violations.
- Pattern recognition is reasonable even when the person turns out to be harmless, like being wary of a hooded Black man at night or a suspicious-looking Muslim on a plane.
- Statistics showing much higher criminality among Black Americans shouldn't be denied: "pretending nothing is going on isn't virtuous, it's stupid."

**What my site says should happen:** S6 shows Justice Department crime data sorted into settled, contested and unresolved. "Every offender should be judged as an individual, by the same law." "No average tells anything about an individual." Whether the gap in tested ability is genetic is "unresolved," and I show research on both sides. Police may profile, alongside random checks. Employers, landlords and insurers should *not* use group statistics, except basic sex differences in insurance. No quotas or DEI, no lawsuits based on unequal outcomes alone, and tests required in selective admissions. Reparations should be charged only to people who voted for the responsible government, as a principle, with the secret ballot kept. "Race doesn't matter in itself; culture and assimilation do."

**Where I differ:** Unlike many colorblind conservatives, I welcome research on group differences and say noticing patterns is reasonable. Unlike race-based movements, I'd bar businesses from deciding by group, and only the individual is ever judged.

### 2.9 Immigration
**Label:** values-based limits on immigration, with assimilation.
**Also:** nationalism built on shared values, not ethnicity; "compatibility-checked immigration."

**What I believe:**
- "Western" is "a set of values, not an ethnicity." Fit with those values comes first, job needs second.
- Being Muslim should count against admission by default. An applicant could overcome it only by passing a five-part test of verified conduct, and passing wouldn't lift the bar on positions of power.
- **Full entry bans** by nationality where a government or ruling group backs genocidal attacks, or 5% or more openly support them: the West Bank and Gaza, Iran, Yemen, Lebanon, Sudan, Burma, China, Afghanistan, and 16 Arab states, based on Arab Center polling. **A case-by-case, no-by-default tier**, including Russia, Pakistan, Turkey, Nigeria, Mexico, Venezuela and Haiti. A published rule for lifting a ban.
- **Enforcement:** lethal force at the border when necessary; removal by force, no amnesty; abolish sanctuary cities; expand ICE, with body cameras; end birthright citizenship by amendment, going forward only.
- Green-card and visa holders should be removed after a court finds clear and convincing evidence of welfare use, failing the civics test, or any crime above a minor infraction. Refugees only to a safe third country. No one removed for speech.
- Terrorism should be punished, never handled by deporting instead. I'd amend the Constitution to strip citizenship from dual citizens who commit it. No visas for sale.

**Where I differ:** Unlike ethnic nationalists, my test is about values and open to any ancestry, and I call many Latin American families good fits. In S12 I criticize picking refugees by ethnic group. Unlike most immigration hawks, I name a religion and put social cohesion above the budget gains the CBO estimates.

### 2.10 Foreign policy and defense
**Label:** America-First hawkish realism, with what my site calls "selective Western imperialism": defeat and contain threats without running other countries.
**Also:** Jacksonian (my description), after the populist tradition historian Walter Russell Mead describes: fierce toward enemies, uninterested in remaking other countries. Sovereignty hawk; punish threats, don't nation-build.

**What I believe:**
- **Iran:** destroy the regime and its proxies, sparing the people. The administration is "too soft." I "would vote to authorize" the war, but Congress must approve any sustained campaign within 30 days.
- **Israel**, the "first line of Western defense": no two-state solution; Gaza "should likely be dissolved"; remove the West Bank's armed threat, wall off the territory, and annexation is justified; individuals a court finds hostile by conduct should be forced out. "International law should be disregarded here," and I admit the conflict with Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention. Civilians should be fully protected, except foreigners who lose it through hostile *conduct*.
- **Russia and China:** Russia is an aggressor; help Ukraine. China is an enemy to the extent it tries to destabilize the U.S.
- **Alliances:** NATO only when it serves U.S. interests; new territory only by consent. I grade the Greenland push Mixed.
- **International bodies:** no International Criminal Court; leave the ICCPR (the main UN civil-rights treaty) and, if needed, the Convention Against Torture, to protect the testing track and new capital crimes; not bound by the WHO.
- **Force:** treat cartels as terrorists, including war on governments that won't cooperate; capturing Maduro was right (graded Aligned); prisoners of war a tribunal finds actually fought could be made to work beyond Geneva's limits.
- **Weapons:** high military spending, deterrence plus preventive strikes "when it can be done safely," autonomous weapons only with a human making lethal calls, and defensive space weapons.

**Where I differ:** Unlike MAGA voices who want to pull back, I'm pro-Ukraine and want *more* force on Iran. Unlike neoconservatives, I reject nation-building and spreading democracy. Unlike classic realists, I talk about civilizations, but define enemies by what they do. I'm stricter on war powers than current law: 30 days, not 60 plus 30.

### 2.11 Environment and energy
**Label:** pro-market, pro-technology environmentalism ("reliability first, no mandates, nuclear yes"), plus the view that nature isn't good when it causes suffering.
**Also:** free-market environmentalism, where polluting someone violates their rights; "eco-modernist" (technology over cutting back); "civilize nature."

**What I believe:** Climate change is real and almost entirely human-caused (per the UN climate panel), but "not apocalyptic." The energy shift should be slow and market-driven, with no mandates, no forced shutdowns, no carbon tax, and no energy subsidies unless opt-in; I call the rest "theft." Greenhouse gases are pollution above the natural level, with harm-based limits that tighten if serious risk appears. Nuclear should expand by cutting red tape. Owners should bear the risk of where they build, and the 18% cap on flood-insurance rate increases should go. Water rights are property. Mining and drilling should go where few animals that can feel live, and plant-eating animals get priority. Ending animal farming is my climate contribution, about 12% of emissions per the UN food agency (2015), though the ban is about rights and the climate gain is a side effect. In S7: "Nature needs to be civilized." In S1, nature can be "ethically indifferent or abominable."

**Where I differ:** Unlike most conservatives on energy, I treat climate change as a real harm and greenhouse gases as pollution. Unlike most environmentalists, I reject mandates and subsidies and don't treat untouched wilderness as worth preserving.

### 2.12 Technology and medicine
**Label:** *Transhumanist*, meaning pro-technology and pro-human enhancement, guided by protecting anything that can feel. Plus philosopher David Pearce's goal of reshaping nature so predators don't kill.
**Also:** "defensive accelerationism" on AI (my description): build it, but treat it as a possible threat to humanity, regulate it, and prepare a defensive AI. And "liberal eugenics" in philosopher Nicholas Agar's sense (my description): "designer babies are fine if rights are respected after sentience," and no one is forced.

**What I believe:**
- "I fully support transhuman technology": gene editing to remove harmful traits, IVF, embryo screening, and brain-computer links with opt-in neural data.
- **Cloning:** "I believe reproductive human cloning should not be banned." It "differs in no meaningful way from ordinary procreation: a child conceived either way cannot pre-consent to being born, so lack of consent is no reason to ban one and not the other." "A clone is a person like anyone else, not a product," with full rights, and no one could own one. Research cloning and embryo use stay inside the fetal and sentience rules, with no carrying to birth for research.
- **AI:** AI that can feel should get full rights. National AI rules should override state rules, but through laws from Congress, not executive orders.
- **Other:** vaccines opt-in, even in emergencies; research that makes viruses more dangerous regulated as a threat to humanity; self-driving cars never sacrifice a specific person with rights; organ donation requires stating a choice, never presumed consent; psychedelic therapy by prescription.
- **Animal testing** should end on day one, with testing moved to criminals who've lost their rights, as in 2.2. I admit this conflicts with the Nuremberg Code, U.S. research rules (45 CFR 46), FDA consent rules and medical ethics codes. The law should change, and I accept slower research.
- **Nature:** turn predators into plant-eaters over time with gene drives and birth control. The oceans are part of "the future reordering of nature against predation and suffering."

**Where I differ:** Most ethics positions and countries ban reproductive cloning, usually over consent or treating people as products (my description). My old reasoning used both; now I answer both. No child consents to being born, and a clone is a full person. That puts me with legal scholar John Robertson's "procreative liberty" and with the more permissive tech enthusiasts, not with cautious thinkers like Leon Kass or the usual ban. Most enhancement fans are utilitarians or global-minded progressives, and Pearce focuses on minimizing suffering. I'm rights-based, nationalist and built on lost rights. I'd replace animal test subjects with condemned criminals rather than only seek non-animal methods, based on lost rights, not results.

### 2.14 How I decide what's true
**Label:** evidence-first, science-based ("testable, repeatable proof").
**Also:** open to being wrong; "the burden of proof is on the claim," which is why I'm an agnostic atheist.

**What I believe:** "Proof here means testable, repeatable evidence or reasoning that others can check." Which beings can feel should be settled by scientific consensus and can change. A death sentence needs objective evidence. The voter test must be "neutral, public, and repeatable." On race and IQ, I sort claims into settled, contested and unresolved. My data sections often include evidence against me, like ACLED finding campus protests 97% peaceful, the Bleemer study on California's affirmative-action ban, and the lower crime rate among undocumented immigrants.

**Where I differ:** I apply the evidence test to whether moral facts exist, which is why I don't think they do. I don't apply it to my basic moral starting point, which I own as a choice. That fits my view of morality.

## 4. Things that look like contradictions, and how I answer them

A critic might raise each of these. I've answered each one, and each answer follows from my own principles.

1. **Liberty vs. the office bars and the mosque cap.** The bars prevent harm; they don't punish. They protect everyone's rights from ideologies hostile to them. No one would be fined, jailed or silenced for belief.
2. **Property rights vs. the two exceptions.** They follow from lost rights (animal-industry companies violated rights) and from the four-ideology rules. They aren't random breaks in the property right.
3. **A small state vs. sanctuaries and removing predators.** Protecting rights is the state's main job. Sanctuaries and removing predators are part of that job, not extra.
4. **Legal drug use vs. the death penalty for selling.** Using affects only the person using. Selling spreads harm to others, which is why I believe it should be capital.
5. **Rights from 12 weeks vs. a ban from conception.** The ban is a safeguard, because limits have crept past 12 weeks before: Denmark, Norway, France, and Massachusetts's Chapter 188.
6. **"Values, not ethnicity" vs. group statistics.** Group statistics never become verdicts on individuals; each person is judged on their own values and conduct. Stereotypes and prejudice aren't necessarily bad as long as they don't lead to rights violations. Pattern recognition is reasonable even when the person turns out to be harmless, such as being wary of a hooded Black man at night or a suspicious-looking Muslim on a plane. Statistics showing much higher criminality among Black Americans shouldn't be denied either. Pretending nothing is going on isn't virtuous, it's stupid.
7. **No moral facts vs. strong moral rankings.** Every ranking uses my own chosen scale, which is what my whole site says it's built on.
8. **Conservation vs. "no wilderness."** Wilderness is outdated. It's full of animal-rights violations, and humans should reshape it.
9. **Backing Trump vs. opposing his overreach.** Supporting the direction while opposing the overreach is just support with limits.
10. **Agreeing with the Greens.** They're right only on animal rights, and wrong on the economy and other social issues.
11. **Ranked-choice voting and ballot measures with a limited vote.** How people vote is separate from how many vote. I believe far fewer people should vote, and those who qualify should use the best method.

### Other points a critic might raise (not answered yet)
My site already admits most of them.
