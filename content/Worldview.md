## 2. Axis by axis

### 2.1 Metaethics
1. **Primary:** Constructivist-prescriptivist moral anti-realism. **Alternatives:** Humean constructivism plus Harean universal prescriptivism, with an expressivist element. "Coherentist prescriptivism."
2. **Why:** [Site] S1 Meta-Ethics: "I claim no objective moral truth. I prescribe a sentience-centered system because it is the most coherent and stably consistent framework available." My Q&As describe a hybrid: "constructivist in the Humean sense… prescriptivist in form, in the tradition of R. M. Hare… an expressive element." They also do the following:
   - give three supporting arguments: disagreement, Mackie's queerness argument, and Street and Joyce's evolutionary debunking;
   - answer the realist replies, companions-in-guilt and self-debunking, in full;
   - state that those who reject my system "are still bound by its enforcement" (S1, Axiom 7: "a coherent system I choose to endorse and want enforced").
3. **Where I depart from the standard version:** Most anti-realists hold back from strong first-order moral confidence. I draw hard verdicts and treat enforcement as part of my system's authority. [Inference] Mine is a *legislative* or *prescriptive* anti-realism, not a relativist one.

### 2.2 Normative ethics
1. **Primary:** Threshold sentientist deontology: negative-rights side-constraints plus forfeiture. **Alternatives:**
   - "Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)," my site's own title.
   - A two-level view: deontology for politics and Stoic eudaimonism for personal character.
2. **Why:** [Site] S1 Axioms 1-7. I believe rights are side-constraints, "not goals to maximize," and that the numbers never license harming an innocent (S1 Conflicts Between Rights-Holders). Rights give way only when an agent crosses the Axiom 6 threshold: violating rights by force or fraud, or directly imposing severe non-consensual risk. "I'm a threshold deontologist, not a utilitarian" (S1). In S10 I add: "My framework doesn't rest on Kantian dignity. It rests on negative rights, which an agent forfeits by violating them in others."
3. **Where I depart from the standard version:**
   - **The threshold.** In standard threshold deontology, a threshold of bad *consequences* overrides the constraints (Moore). In my view the threshold is the *agent's own violation*, so mine is a forfeiture theory more than a catastrophe-threshold theory.
   - **Proportionality.** [Site] I believe it should be applied once, at the forfeiture decision: a court finds, under due process and at the capital evidentiary standard, that the threshold was crossed (S1 General Rules of Forfeiture; S10). Once rights are forfeit, "the sentence carried out within them is not a further injustice." The experimentation track works the same way: the offender lost those protections by violating another's rights. I believe harm outside the sentence, such as guard abuse unrelated to any study, should still be punished.
   - **Duties to sustain others:** I believe these arise only from voluntary acts, as in the procreation-responsibility basis for my abortion position.

### 2.3 Moral status and animal ethics
1. **Primary:** Sentientism that is substrate-neutral and graded, with equal rights above a class threshold. **Alternatives:** Abolitionist animal rights. Wild-animal-suffering interventionism. "Predation abolitionism."
2. **Why:** [Site]
   - **Basis:** S1 Axioms 1-2. I believe moral status rests on first-order phenomenal consciousness, "biological or artificial," with no self-awareness requirement.
   - **Sentience classes:** I believe they should be set in statute by an expert board and revised only for acts committed after a revision.
     - High class: mammals, birds, cephalopods, and decapods, including rats, mice, and pigeons.
     - Fish and shrimp: placed species by species.
     - Bivalves and insects: a precautionary tier, with a small fine.
     - Pest status should never demote a species. Lethal control should be allowed only for a defined infestation of a dwelling, food store, or medical facility.
   - **Killing:** I believe killing a high-class animal is murder and should be on the capital track. My view is that hunting is murder, with no subsistence or Indigenous exception.
   - **Day-one bans:** I believe animal agriculture, fishing, aquaculture, breeding mills, and animal testing should be banned on day one, under the Sanctuary Plan (S9).
   - **Ex post facto:** I believe no one should be punished for killings before the ban (S1).
   - **Predation:** I believe it is a rights violation that the U.S. government should stop within its borders, in phases, with no step that causes more suffering than it prevents (S1, S7).
3. **Where I depart from the standard version:**
   - Sentientism is usually utilitarian (Singer). Mine is rights-based.
   - I use sentience, not Regan's "subject-of-a-life."
   - Regan denied any duty to stop predation. I believe there is one.
   - Francione-style abolitionists are nonviolent and anti-carceral. I believe animal killing belongs on the capital track.
   - Above the class threshold, I believe the right to life is equal. In S1 I make the infant-and-philosopher analogy: "a pig's killing is sentenced like a human's."
   - I believe euthanasia that breaks the rules should be graded on a sliding scale, not treated as automatically capital (S1).

## 4. Apparent tensions and my resolutions

A critic might raise each of these. I have answered each one, and in each case my answer follows from my framework.

1. **Liberty versus the office bars and the mosque cap.** The bars are prophylaxis, not punishment. They protect the rights order from ideologies that are hostile to it. No one would be fined, jailed, or silenced for belief.
2. **Property versus the two exceptions.** The exceptions follow from forfeiture (animal-industry firms violated rights) and from the excluded-ideology rules. They are not ad hoc breaks in the property right.
3. **A minimal state versus the sanctuary system and predator removal.** Protecting rights is the state's core duty. The sanctuary system and predator removal are part of that job, not an expansion of it.
4. **Legal drug use versus capital punishment for supply.** Personal use affects only the person using. Selling spreads harm to others, which is why I believe it should be a capital crime.
5. **A 12-week rights claim versus a ban from conception.** The ban from conception is a safeguard against documented drift past 12 weeks: Denmark, Norway, France, and Massachusetts Chapter 188.
6. **Values, not ethnicity, versus group statistics.** Group statistics never become verdicts on individuals; each person is judged on their own values and conduct. Stereotypes and prejudice aren't necessarily bad as long as they don't lead to rights violations. Pattern recognition is reasonable even when the person turns out to be harmless, such as wariness of a hooded Black man at night or a suspicious-looking Muslim on a plane. Statistics showing much higher criminality among Black Americans shouldn't be denied either. Pretending nothing is going on isn't virtuous, it's stupid.
7. **Anti-realism versus strong moral rankings.** Every ranking runs on my own prescribed scale, which is the stated basis of my whole site.
8. **Conservation versus "no wilderness."** Wilderness is obsolete. It is full of animal-rights violations and should be engineered by humankind.
9. **Backing Trump versus opposing his oversteps.** Supporting the administration's direction while opposing its oversteps is ordinary support with limits.
10. **Shared ground with the Greens.** The Greens are right only on animal rights. They're wrong on economics and on the other social issues.
11. **Ranked choice and initiatives inside a restricted vote.** How people vote is separate from how many vote. I believe the electorate should be drastically reduced, and those who qualify should vote by the best method.
