## **Core Ethical Framework:**

### Prescriptive Threshold Sentientist Deontology (Negative-rights focused)  

#### Axioms:

1. I prescribe that subjective experience (first-order phenomenal consciousness) shall serve as the basis for moral status and the possession of negative rights.

2. Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer under this framework. Higher-order self-awareness is not required.

3. A being on an undisturbed developmental trajectory toward sentience (e.g., a healthy fetus in a living womb) shall be granted significant moral weight proportional to the robustness of that trajectory.

4. A being that once possessed sentience retains residual moral consideration. If sentience is permanently and irreversibly lost, decision-making authority transfers to next-of-kin, guided by respect for the former being’s known wishes and dignity.

5. Practical determination of sentience shall follow the best available scientific consensus. Where science remains unclear, decisions should err on the side of consistency with these prescriptions.

6. Under this framework, sentient beings are to be granted strong negative rights against interference and harm. These rights function as side-constraints that may be overridden only when a clear threshold of rights violation by the agent has been crossed (rights forfeiture).

7. All moral and political rules within this system must remain consistent with the prescribed primacy of subjective experience. This framework is not claimed as an objective moral fact, but as a coherent system I choose to endorse and seek to enforce.

#### Summary:

- **Sentientist:**
    1. Moral status and rights attach to beings capable of sentience (or on a clear trajectory toward it under a graduated potentiality + responsibility framework). First-order subjective experience is the basis for moral status.
       
       https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentientism

- **Negative-rights focused:** 
    1. Rights primarily function as side-constraints against interference, harm, or rights violations by others (including the state). I generally reject positive rights that force others to provide goods or services. Negative rights are strong side-constraints.
       
       https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_rights

- **Threshold deontologist:** 
    1. I hold firm moral rules (do not violate sentient rights, do not kill the innocent, respect voluntary agreements) that normally override consequentialist calculations — until a sufficiently high threshold of harm or rights violation is reached. At that point, stronger action (including force, forfeiture of the violator’s rights, or intervention) becomes permissible or required. Simply, rights may be overridden only after a clear violation threshold; violators forfeit rights. Fetal moral weight scales with approaching sentience and is primarily grounded in the responsibility trigger of voluntary creation.
       
       https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/

## **Personal Ethical Practice:**

I adopt Stoicism as my personal operating system for character and daily conduct. Key elements I take from it:

- Focus only on what is within my control (my judgments, actions, and responses).
- Accept external events, including the actions of others and outcomes of politics, without being ruled by them emotionally.
- Cultivate the four cardinal virtues as practical guides: wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.
- Treat adversity and the hostility of others as material for practicing virtue rather than as reasons for despair or rage.
- Maintain inner composure while still acting decisively in the world according to the prescribed sentientist principles.

Stoicism is not a replacement for my political or inter-personal rights framework. It is the personal discipline that allows me to live and act consistently with that framework without being destroyed by anger, resentment, or attachment to outcomes I cannot fully control.

# **Foreign Policy & International Relations**

## **Core Principle Summary:** 

America First realism with selective Western imperialism. Prioritize U.S. sovereignty, security, cultural cohesion, and manufacturing strength. Engage forcefully against direct threats (especially Iran and extremist Islam) while avoiding open-ended nation-building. Participate in alliances and institutions only when they clearly serve American interests.

### Interventionism vs Non-Intervention

- Direct intervention justified in areas of clear U.S. strategic interest, particularly against direct enemies such as Iran.

- Skeptical of nation-building and regime change due to poor historical track records.

- Favors Western imperialism/colonialism against openly hostile, ethically inferior cultures. Non-hostile cultures should generally be left alone.

### Alliances & NATO

- Reject any alliance that erodes U.S. sovereignty or harms American interests.

- Participate only when it clearly strengthens U.S. security.

- Maintain diplomacy, but never capitulate to allies’ demands or submit to foreign tribunals.

### Trade & Economics

- Prioritize and incentivize American manufacturing.

- Imports should ideally be structured with reciprocal export benefits.

- Tariffs are good in theory but questionable in practice.

- Prefer domestic trade and place regulations/restrictions on outsourced labor.

- Treat China as a dangerous strategic rival; trade may continue under the above reciprocal and national-interest conditions.

### Immigration & Borders

- Cultural homogeneity is ideal for societal strength. Diversity has generally proven negative in the short-to-mid term by fostering tribalism and value clashes.

- Immigration is not inherently virtuous. Strictly vet and admit only those who fit the shared Western value structure; cultural assimilation is paramount.

- Significantly stronger border security, including lethal force as a deterrent in some cases.

- Forceful removal of illegal immigrants (incentives secondary).

- Abolish birthright citizenship prospectively (existing legal citizenships unaffected).

### Israel & Middle East

- Strong support for Israel as the frontline defense against the Iranian regime and extremist Islam.

- Aggressive policy toward Iran: obliterate the Islamic regime and its state-sponsored terror allies while sparing civilian populations where possible.

- Goal of eradicating extremist Islam as a global force.

### Russia, Ukraine, China/Taiwan

- Russia is an aggressor; support nations fighting it.

- Treat Russia and China as strategic adversaries seeking to destabilize the U.S.

- Prioritize intelligence, cybersecurity, and strength-based responses.

### Global Institutions (UN, WHO, ICC, climate accords)

- Participate only if they directly benefit U.S. interests.

- Never submit to international tribunals, global courts, or standards that override U.S. sovereignty.

- Follow American law and national interest above globalist bureaucracy.

### Military Spending & Projection

- Current high spending is acceptable; increase if needed for capability gaps.

- Homeland defense is priority #1, but global power projection and offensive deterrence are often necessary extensions of that goal.

### Nuclear & WMD

- Proliferation cannot be fully reversed.

- Rely on mutual assured deterrence.

- Prevent enemy proliferation through military means when safely possible.

- Maintain a strong U.S. nuclear posture.

  
  


### Interventionism vs Non-Intervention: 

  
***Should the US engage in foreign military interventions, nation-building, or regime change? When (if ever) is it justified?***  
  
The United States should intervene militarily when it directly advances American security and national interest (example: confronting Iran as a hostile, extremist regime that attacks U.S. interests and allies). Nation-building and idealistic regime-change projects should be avoided because they have a poor track record. Against openly hostile cultures that reject Western norms and threaten the West, decisive force, including forms of imperialism, can be justified. Non-hostile societies should generally be left alone.



**1\. National interest comes first**  
  
Foreign policy exists to protect the security, prosperity, and continuity of the United States and its people. Interventions are tools, not moral crusades. Realist thinking holds that great powers should act when core interests are at stake and stay out when they are not. Spreading democracy or “fixing” other societies is secondary and often counterproductive.



**2\. Nation-building and open-ended regime change have failed repeatedly**  
  
Recent major U.S. efforts show a clear pattern:

- Military victory is often achievable.

- Turning the conquered country into a stable, friendly, functional democracy almost never is.

- The result is usually prolonged insurgency, state collapse, massive cost in blood and money, and sometimes a worse strategic outcome (power vacuums filled by enemies).



**3\. Iran is a direct, ongoing threat**  
  
Iran funds, arms, and directs proxy forces that have attacked U.S. troops, shipping, and allies. It pursues nuclear capabilities, plots assassinations (including on U.S. soil), and openly calls for the destruction of Israel, a key partner. This is not a distant humanitarian issue; it is a concrete security problem.



**4\. Cultural and civilizational reality matters**  
  
Some societies and regimes are structured around values (theocratic absolutism, jihadist expansion, total rejection of reciprocal norms) that make peaceful coexistence or successful liberal nation-building extremely difficult. When such forces are actively hostile, containment or decisive weakening can be more realistic than transformation.



**5\. Non-hostile societies should be left alone**  
  
There is no national-interest justification for reconstructing societies that are not attacking or meaningfully threatening the United States. Restraint preserves resources and avoids unnecessary enemies.

**Realist framework**

- Classical and modern realist thinkers (and contemporary realist policy writing) argue that great powers should balance against genuine threats in important regions and avoid open-ended projects in secondary areas. Selective, interest-driven intervention is distinguished from both isolationism and liberal interventionism.

- Post-colonial outcomes are mixed and heavily debated. What is less debated is that attempts to rapidly liberalize or democratize societies with deep theocratic, tribal, or anti-Western structures have high failure rates when the local elite and population do not already share core institutional norms. This underpins the distinction between “hostile and incompatible” versus “non-hostile.”

#### Conclusion

This position is consistent with negative-rights realism applied internationally: the U.S. government exists first to protect the rights and security of its own citizens. It may use force against external actors who systematically violate those interests. It has no general duty to reconstruct foreign societies, and history shows that attempting to do so often produces more rights violations (chaos, terrorism, refugee flows, wasted resources) than it prevents.

# Domestic Policy & Governance

  
Core Principle: Limited government, individual responsibility, low taxation, cultural/economic freedom with minimal safety nets. Prioritize free markets, personal choice, and fiscal restraint while allowing opt-in social programs and basic public services.

Economic System

- Strongly pro-capitalism for proven results in quality of life, abundance, and innovation.

- Skeptical of unrestricted capitalism due to potential exploitation/safety issues.

- Favor limited regulations for consumer protection, health, and safety - otherwise, minimal to avoid stifling growth.

- Government role: Minimal - defense, police, fire, roads, parks, basic infrastructure.

- Minimum wage: Some inflation-adjusted floor, but raises ultimately increase costs.

- Welfare: Heavily cut. No mandatory redistribution. Opt-in taxes for social programs (SNAP, welfare, healthcare) preferred.

- UBI: Open to it as repayment of contributions (especially with automation), not pure redistribution.

- No corporate subsidies with taxpayer money.

Taxation

- Flat tax preferred - no punishment for success.

- Remove many taxes; keep only minimal for essential services (education, police, fire, emergency, infrastructure).

- Low overall tax burden so people control their own money.

- Taxation often equates to theft except for narrow, necessary functions.

Welfare & Entitlements

- Cut programs aggressively to bare-minimum safety nets.

- Opt-in funding model ideal.

- Benefits should provide only bare necessities (e.g., SNAP limited to basic plant-based staples like oats, rice, beans, vegetables - no comfort items).

- No glamorous or expansive entitlements. Reform/privatize where possible (Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, food stamps).

Healthcare

- Opt-in system: Choose to fund socialized options or buy private insurance.

- Freedom of choice over mandates.

Education

- Publicly funded basic education with strong school choice, vouchers, and full homeschooling rights.

- Local/state control primary; federal role limited to basic child protections (PII security, training).

- Curriculum: Objective facts and knowledge only - remove ideological biases (e.g., against CRT or forced patriotism).

Infrastructure & Regulation

- Government maintains core infrastructure (roads, etc.) to safe functional standards using tax funds.

- Business regulation: Limited to genuine safety, environmental harm, worker exploitation, and anti-consumer practices. Otherwise, free market.

- No excessive labor, financial, or environmental rules that hinder growth.

Fiscal Policy

- Pursue balanced budget.

- Minimize deficit spending.

- Work toward reducing/eliminating national debt.

Energy Policy

- Slow, gradual shift to cleaner sources.

- Encourage (but do not mandate) renewables.

- Incentivize nuclear.

- Free market competition for best consumer options.

- No heavy subsidies or mandates.

Housing & Urban Policy

- Regulate against predatory practices (corporate hoarding, price gouging, out-of-state landlords).

- Public housing: Minimal, bare necessities only in targeted areas.

- No rent control.

- Zoning reform implied for market efficiency.

  
  
Economic System:  
  
Capitalism, socialism, mixed economy, or something else? Role of government in the economy (regulation, taxes, welfare, minimum wage, universal basic income)?  
  
I believe that capitalism has demonstrably given the highest quality of life, surplus, and abundance. I am skeptical of completely unrestricted capitalism that can lead to exploitation and health/safety concerns - I am in favor of certain regulations for safety and consumer protection, but otherwise not, as it stifles growth. The duty of the government should be minimal, focusing on the safety of people (defense), and providing basic services, like taxes to pay for police, fire departments, and public places like roads and parks and their upkeep. Minimum wage seems to simply raise all costs each time it is raised, though I understand there should be some level of floor matching the level of inflation. Welfare should be heavily cut. No one should be obligated to pay for others. Though idealistic, I believe extra taxes that would fund social programs like SNAP/food stamps, welfare, and healthcare should be opt-in, not mandatory. In a high-trust society, it would seem likely that charitable organizations and philanthropic endeavors would emerge to willingly fund programs such as these, and that is virtuous. I just don't believe it should be the burden of the taxpayer to pay for things they does not wish to pay for. This same belief is applied to government subsidies. My tax money should not be going to subsidize corporations that I have no desire to subsidize. Universal basic income is an interesting concept, and pressing in our time of advancing automation. I am open to this concept because I am getting repaid on what I pay into it, it's not simply redistributing my wealth to others away from me.  
  
  
Taxation:  
  
Progressive, flat, consumption-based, or abolish certain taxes? Optimal overall tax burden?  
  
I believe in a flat tax. One should not be punished with more taxes as their wealth increases. Many taxes should be removed, as stated prior. Taxation is definitionally theft in most cases, though I do see minimal taxes that pay for education, police, fire, emergency services, and infrastructure upkeep as necessary. The tax burden should be low, and people should be able to use their money as they see fit. Money generally should not be redistributed out of my pocket at the behest of government.  
  
  
Welfare & Entitlements:  
  
Reform, expand, cut, or privatize Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, food stamps, etc.?  
  
Cut welfare programs to minimums. One should not be forced to pay for others. I believe these safety nets should be opt-in to fund. I do wish there to be some small level of safety net, but it should not be glamorous, and it should provide the bare necessities for life, nothing extra. For example, SNAP should only be eligible for plant-based basics like oats, rice, beans, vegetables, pasta, bread, and fruit - it should not be comfortable.  
  
  
Healthcare:  
  
Market-based, single-payer, or hybrid? Mandate or freedom of choice?  
  
I believe that these programs should be opt-in. If you opt-in to fund socialized programs, you can benefit from them and help others that qualify. If you do not choose to opt-in, you can purchase your own private options.  
  
  
Education:  
  
School choice/vouchers, homeschooling, federal role vs local/state control, curriculum standards (e.g., critical race theory, patriotism)?  
  
There should be some level of publicly funded education, regulated to avoid administrative bloat and waste. Private schools should have the full right to exist. Homeschooling should be fully allowed. Control should be at local level, with state and federal laws for child protections - like PII security, and childcare training and overwatch. Curriculum standards should exist but should seek to carve away biases of any sort - teaching should be entirely objective about facts and subject knowledge, not ideological.  
  
  
Infrastructure & Regulation:  
  
Government role in building/maintaining infrastructure? Level of business regulation (environmental, labor, financial)?  
  
Infrastructure should be maintained with our tax money to a functional level of safety standard. Businesses should be regulated if they cause danger to wildlife, exploit workers and have unsafe work environments, and have anti-consumer practices, but otherwise should be left alone to operate freely.  
  
  
Fiscal Policy:  
  
Balanced budget amendment? Deficit spending philosophy? National debt approach?  
  
I believe it is wise to try to balance our budget. We should try to not overspend on the deficit. We should try to eliminate our national debt.  
  
  
Energy Policy:  
  
Fossil fuels, nuclear, renewables? Subsidies or free market?  
  
I believe there should be a slow and gradual shift to cleaner energy sources. Renewables should be encouraged but not overtly mandated. Nuclear should be incentivized. There should be a free market for energy, providing the best options to the consumer.  
  
  
Housing & Urban Policy:  
  
Zoning reform, public housing, rent control?  
  
Housing should be regulated to protect consumers from predatory and anti-consumer practices like corporations buying up properties and gouging prices, or out-of-state landlords. Public housing can exist in certain districts, provided with bare necessities, but minimal. There should not be rent control.

# Cultural & Social Issues

Core Principle: Traditional nuclear family preference, free speech absolutism (with narrow exceptions), meritocracy, cultural compatibility for cohesion, and personal responsibility. Strong skepticism of identity politics, mandates, and progressive social engineering.

Family & Gender

- Ideal: Nuclear family (married mother + father, 2–3 children) as healthiest structure.

- Divorce allowed; no automatic custody default - neutral court decides based on child’s best interest (including child’s preference).

- Traditional gender roles (male provider, female homemaker) superior for society, but not mandated. Individuals free to choose.

Abortion & Bioethics

- Sentientist with fetal rights. Primary cutoff at sentience; overall pro-life due to societal domino effects of permissiveness.

- No termination of fetuses from voluntary decisions.

- Rape exception: Mother freed of responsibility (no consent/agreement).

- Builds on earlier responsibility framework (voluntary unprotected sex triggers duties).

LGBTQ+ & Identity Issues

- Same-sex marriage legal, but ideal outcome is biological male-female pairing. Concerns over male-male adoption (potential abuse risks) and incomplete fulfillment of traditional parenting dynamic.

- Transgenderism should not be encouraged; gender dysphoria as mental illness - treat root causes rather than affirm/transition.

- Adults free to transition privately, but no participation in opposite-sex sports.

- Minors: No medical transition.

- Bathrooms: Biological sex only.

- Pronouns: Voluntary respect only - never mandated.

- Conversion therapy: Opposed.

Free Speech & Censorship

- Absolutist: Only restrict direct threats/calls to violence violating sentient rights.

- Private platforms free to set their own TOS.

- Misinformation: Debunk via private actors or individuals, not government.

Race & Diversity

- Zero race/gender quotas or affirmative action - fundamentally discriminatory. Abolish DEI.

- Meritocracy only for hiring/admissions. Equality of opportunity, not outcome.

- Reparations: Only from direct offending governments; liability tied to voter records; generational sunset clause.

- Need unbiased research on group differences (e.g., IQ). Reduce focus on race as divider; emphasize shared values and color-blindness.

Gun Rights

Revisions  


Hardened Economics

Core Goals Retained

Low overall tax burden, minimal mandatory redistribution, strong security capacity, free-market orientation, fiscal sustainability, and long-term reduction in state dependency - pursued pragmatically over 15–20 years rather than through sudden purity.

### Tax System

- Permanent structure: Large zero-tax bracket (roughly first $40–50k individual / $80–100k joint) + single flat rate of 18–22% on income above it. Preferential but still flat treatment of capital gains/dividends. Most deductions and credits eliminated.

- Transitional bridge: Temporary high-end surtax or modest broad-based consumption tax (5–8%) with explicit sunset, lasting 10–15 years to cover the revenue gap while mandatory programs and education are restructured.

### Spending Hierarchy

1. National defense, border security, intelligence, and core domestic public safety.

2. Debt service.

3. Basic infrastructure and core public education funding floor.

4. Residual austere, work-conditioned safety net.

5. All other spending is subordinate and subject to reduction or elimination.

### Education

- Maintain a public funding obligation for basic K-12, but convert it to portable funding (vouchers or education savings accounts) so money follows the student.

- Parents may direct funds to public, charter, private, or accountable homeschool options.

- Strict focus on core skills (literacy, numeracy, science, civic knowledge); eliminate ideological and administrative bloat.

- Prefer state and local funding responsibility; limit federal role to narrow civil-rights and data functions.

- Higher education: reduce open-ended subsidies and lending that inflate costs; increase institutional accountability and alternative credential pathways.

### Safety Net

- Small, mandatory, time-limited, work-conditioned floor (basic food, emergency shelter, catastrophic medical) kept deliberately austere.

- Anything above the floor relies on voluntary charity, mutual aid, and community systems.

- Future automation/productivity gains may support a separate, modest citizen dividend framed as a share of national output rather than classic redistribution.

### Mandatory Program Reform Path

- Social Security and Medicare moved gradually toward higher retirement ages, means-testing for high earners, and more contribution-linked or defined-contribution designs over 15–20 years.

- Other welfare consolidated into the austere floor described above.

- No cliff-edge abolition; phased restraint to avoid fiscal and political shock.

### Transition Horizon

- Years 1–5: Implement hybrid flat tax + temporary revenue bridge. Freeze or slow mandatory growth. Expand security capacity. Begin portable education pilots and work-conditioned safety-net reforms.

- Years 6–12: Deepen means-testing and age adjustments. Expand education portability. Reduce transitional taxes as growth and spending discipline improve. Debt-to-GDP stabilizes then declines.

- Years 13–20: Approach long-term target of lower permanent flat rate, minimal redistribution, strong security capacity, portable core education, and a small residual safety floor.

### Accepted Compromises for Realism

- Permanent tax burden higher than a pure ultra-low flat tax ideal, especially during transition.

- Continued (but tightly limited) mandatory safety floor and public education funding obligation.

- Full debt elimination unlikely; realistic goal is stabilizing then reducing debt-to-GDP.

- Some targeted support for defense-critical industry may persist.

- Sustained real economic growth is required for the arithmetic to close.
