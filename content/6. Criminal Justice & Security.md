## Core Principles
Retribution, incapacitation, and the protection of victims' sentient rights take priority over rehabilitation. Those who murder, commit violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, or human trafficking, or who repeatedly violate the rights of others, forfeit the protections they denied their victims. Punishment is swift, certain, and public, and capital punishment applies only where guilt is established beyond any real doubt. Enforcement is heavy, with military-style action against cartels and high-crime threats.

## Evidentiary Standard

>[!tldr] In short
> Nobody is executed unless the proof is overwhelming and backed by objective evidence such as video and physical evidence, not just testimony. Because there is no long appeals process, the trial itself has to get it right.

#### Position
Execution happens only when guilt is absolutely clear, without a shadow of a doubt. Ordinary proof beyond a reasonable doubt is not enough for a capital outcome. The standard requires corroborating objective evidence: body-camera or surveillance footage, multiple eyewitnesses backed by camera footage, physical evidence such as a body, and similar proof that does not depend on a single witness or on inference alone. Cases that fall short of this bar do not go to the capital track.

> [!question]- "Innocent people have been sentenced to death. Doesn't any death penalty guarantee that the state will eventually kill an innocent person?"
> The risk is real and the evidence for it is serious. Gross and colleagues (2014) estimated that at least 4.1 percent of defendants sentenced to death in the United States are likely innocent. That figure is the strongest argument against capital punishment as it currently operates, and it is the reason for this standard. Most known wrongful capital convictions rested on mistaken eyewitness identification, false confessions, unreliable informants, or flawed forensic testimony. Those are exactly the kinds of evidence a corroboration requirement excludes as a sufficient basis. Wrongful execution is treated as a rights violation by the state, with full accountability for those responsible. No human process reaches zero error. The claim here is narrower: restricting execution to cases with objective corroboration reduces the error rate far below the current system's.

#### Key Elements
- The capital standard is higher than proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Objective, corroborated evidence is required, not testimony or inference alone.
- Cases that fail the standard leave the capital track.
- Wrongful execution is treated as a rights violation by the state.

#### Rationale
A swift system with no long appeals is defensible only if guilt is certain at the front end. The longer the period between sentence and execution, the more the system relies on appeals to catch mistakes. This framework removes that period, so the burden moves to the trial itself. The evidentiary bar does the work that decades of appeals were meant to do.

Corroboration matters because the main sources of wrongful conviction are subjective. A recording, a body, and independent witnesses who agree with the recording are much harder to fabricate or misread than a single identification made under stress. The bar is deliberately strict. Its purpose is to make the capital track narrow, reliable, and limited to cases where the facts are not in serious dispute.

> [!info]- What counts as clear-cut evidence?
> Evidence that establishes both the act and the identity of the offender without depending on a single uncorroborated source. Examples include body-camera or surveillance footage of the act, multiple eyewitnesses whose accounts are confirmed by camera footage, and physical evidence such as a body. A confession alone, a single eyewitness, or circumstantial inference alone does not meet the standard.

>[!quote]- Sources
> - Gross, O'Brien, Hu & Kennedy – [Rate of False Conviction of Criminal Defendants Who Are Sentenced to Death](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1306417111) (PNAS, 2014)

## Statutes of Limitations

>[!tldr] In short
> Murder, violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking can be prosecuted no matter how much time has passed, as long as the strict evidence standard is met. Old convictions always count toward the repeat-offender threshold, and restitution and civil claims never expire: restitution is owed until it is paid. Other non-capital crimes have tiered limits of 3, 6, or 20 years: under \$500, \$500 up to \$10,000, and \$10,000 and up.

#### Position
There is no statute of limitations for murder, violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, or human trafficking. All people are held liable for their actions, and the passage of time does not restore rights that were forfeited by a deliberate violation. Old convictions count toward the repeat-offender threshold indefinitely; there is no time limit on the look-back. There is no time limit on restitution or civil claims: restitution is owed forever until it is paid. Theft, fraud, and other non-capital crimes have limitation periods that scale with severity and the value involved. Minor offenses (under \$500) may be prosecuted within 3 years, mid-level offenses (\$500 up to \$10,000) within 6 years, and major offenses (\$10,000 and up) within 20 years. The thresholds may be adjusted each year to rise with the national average wage.

> [!question]- "Doesn't evidence degrade over time, risking wrongful convictions in old cases?"
> The protection against that risk is the evidentiary standard, not a deadline. A capital conviction still requires proof to the strict standard in [[#Evidentiary Standard]], however old the case.

#### Key Elements
- No statute of limitations for murder, violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, or human trafficking.
- Time does not restore forfeited rights.
- Old convictions count toward the repeat-offender threshold indefinitely.
- No time limit on restitution or civil claims: restitution is owed until it is fully paid.
- Non-capital crimes use tiered limitation periods by severity and value: 3 years for minor (under \$500), 6 years for mid-level (\$500 up to \$10,000), 20 years for major (\$10,000 and up); thresholds may rise yearly with the national average wage.
- The evidentiary standard, not a deadline, protects against weak old cases.

#### Rationale
Forfeiture follows from the act, not from how quickly it is prosecuted ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#General Rules of Forfeiture|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]). Federal law already sets no limitation period for offenses punishable by death (18 U.S.C. § 3281). For non-capital crimes, the federal default is 5 years (18 U.S.C. § 3282). Norway scales its limitation periods by the maximum sentence, from 2 years for the least serious offenses to 25 years for the most serious, and France sets 20 years for serious crimes and 6 years for mid-level offenses. The dollar thresholds are set below what the typical full-time worker earns in the same time. The full-time median is \$65,052 a year (about \$1,251 a week), so \$500 is under half a week's pay and \$10,000 is about eight weeks. Because the framework ranks retribution and incapacitation above rehabilitation, more serious offenses carry longer periods.

## Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty

>[!tldr] In short
> Deliberate murder is punished by death, and so are violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking proven to the same strict standard. Attempted murder and knowingly putting others at extreme risk of death are treated the same way, even if no one dies; ordinary accidents are not. Killing or trying to kill a victim or witness is always capital, and most abuse within families, which rarely meets the capital proof standard, is punished on the non-capital tracks. The sentence is fixed for adults, not left to a jury's sympathy, and the offender's mental state does not change it; minors whose crimes are egregious enough are sentenced like adults. Offenders pay restitution from their own assets. Current Supreme Court rulings forbid much of this, so the position calls for those rulings to change.

#### Position
Voluntary murder is punished by death. Violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking established under the same evidentiary standard are punished by death as well; they are egregious rights violations. The same four cruelty acts are capital when done to a person if they cause lasting injury. The four acts are extreme violence, intentional neglect (a chosen pattern of keeping it up), beating without drawing blood, and psychological abuse (withholding food, water, or shelter, not out of neglect but as deliberate punishment). Lasting injury is harm, physical or psychological, that requires ongoing treatment, does not heal within a recovery period measured in months, or leaves permanent damage. This includes neglect and other acts that leave lasting psychological harm. At the capital tier, lasting injury, including psychological injury, must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt through evaluation by a licensed clinician for humans, or a veterinarian or veterinary behaviorist for animals, documenting harm that requires ongoing treatment, persists for months, or is permanent. Below the capital tier, lasting injury must be proven by clear and convincing evidence, with the same clinician or veterinary evaluation. Without lasting injury, they are egregious, the [[#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|egregious tier]]: three [[Glossary#Mark|marks]] at once, reaching the threshold: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses. Beating means force beyond ordinary discipline: a closed fist, an object, or repeated strikes; a swat on the rear as ordinary discipline is not beating. See [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Animal Cruelty Law|Animal Cruelty Law]]. The sentence is mandatory once guilt is established; it is not left to the discretion of a jury weighing sympathy for the offender. Juveniles fall under the same rules: a minor whose crime is egregious enough reaches the capital tier or three marks like an adult. See [[#Juvenile Offenders]]. The offender's mental competence, intellectual capacity, or understanding of the proceedings does not reduce the sentence, because none of these change what was done to the victim. Incompetence alone does not lead to execution. An offender who was completely unaware and incompetent is removed from society, imprisoned or confined, because they are too dangerous to be around, as set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Free Will and Responsibility|Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]. But some offenders cannot be changed from incompetence and remain a threat: they are violent and show no sign of change. Such a person is a threat to society and is not kept alive merely for the sake of keeping them alive; they deserve to die. The same holds for an animal in confinement that remains a threat. See [[#Insanity Defense]]. Attempted murder is on the capital track: a deliberate attempt to kill is treated as murder whether or not the victim dies. So is conduct that knowingly imposes an extreme, unjustified risk of death on others, where the person understands the danger and goes ahead anyway with blatant disregard for life and rights. This applies even if no one is harmed. Street racing through traffic at extreme speed is one example. The test is awareness and severity: the person knew the danger and the risk was grave, not merely careless. Ordinary accidents and ordinary negligence stay outside the capital track and are handled as [[#Lesser Homicide|lesser homicide]].

> [!question]- "Hasn't the Supreme Court already ruled that mandatory death sentences, executing the mentally incompetent, and executing for rape are unconstitutional?"
> Yes. Under current doctrine all three are barred. *Woodson v. North Carolina* (1976) struck down mandatory death sentences for murder. *Ford v. Wainwright* (1986) and *Atkins v. Virginia* (2002) bar executing the insane and the intellectually disabled. *Coker v. Georgia* (1977) and *Kennedy v. Louisiana* (2008) bar the death penalty for rape where the victim was not killed. The position calls for those rulings to change.

#### Key Elements
- Voluntary murder is capital.
- Violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, and human trafficking are capital under the same standard.
- The four cruelty acts are capital when they cause lasting injury.
- Attempted murder and knowingly imposing an extreme risk of death are capital.
- The sentence is mandatory once guilt is established.
- Mental competence does not reduce the sentence.
- Juveniles are sentenced like adults when the crime is egregious enough.
- Offenders pay restitution from their own assets.

#### Rationale
These acts are egregious rights violations. The victim is owed the protection of the law, and the offender has forfeited the protections they denied. The sentence is fixed so that sympathy for the offender does not override what was done to the victim. Current Supreme Court doctrine bars much of this; the position requires those rulings to change.

> - *Ford v. Wainwright*, 477 U.S. 399 (1986)
> - *Atkins v. Virginia*, 536 U.S. 304 (2002)

>[!tldr] In short

#### Position

#### Key Elements
- Ordinary negligence and accidents are not capital.
- Excessive self-defense close to justified is lesser homicide.
- Prosecution must prove intent.
- Lighter sentence than voluntary murder.

#### Rationale
The capital track is reserved for deliberate or knowingly extreme risk. Ordinary carelessness does not meet that bar. Excessive self-defense that is close to justified is still a rights violation but of lower severity.

## Police Power and Accountability

>[!tldr] In short
> Police are heavily funded and expanded. Officers who break the law are punished more than ordinary citizens. Body cameras are required. Ultra-high-crime areas get militarized enforcement.

#### Position
Police are heavily funded and expanded, and the responsibilities and dangers of the job are very well paid and well respected. Officers who break laws are held to higher criminal liability and punishment than regular citizens, and the same applies to elected officials. Body cameras are mandated nationwide. Ultra-high-crime areas receive militarized police enforcement, and high-crime areas receive increased enforcement with proper protections and punishments to destroy crime, under zero tolerance. Theft and burglary are treated severely, and any erosion of a high-trust society by criminals and bad actors is addressed harshly.

> [!question]- "Does militarized policing threaten civil liberties?"
> Militarized enforcement is limited to ultra-high-crime areas and comes with proper protections and punishments, including body cameras and higher criminal liability for officers who break the law. Criminals infringe on the sentient rights of others, and their victims are owed the utmost protection under the law; the civil liberties at stake are first of all the victims'.

#### Key Elements
- Heavy funding and expansion of police; high pay and respect.
- Higher criminal liability for officers and elected officials who break the law.
- Nationwide body-camera mandate.
- Militarized enforcement in ultra-high-crime areas; increased enforcement in high-crime areas.
- Zero tolerance; severe treatment of theft and burglary.
- Harsh response to any erosion of a high-trust society.

#### Rationale
Criminals infringe on the sentient rights of others, and their victims deserve the utmost protection under the law. A force with the funding, numbers, and standing to do that job is a precondition for the rest of the framework. Each additional police officer prevents an estimated 0.06 to 0.1 homicides, roughly one life saved for every 10 to 17 officers hired. Pairing that power with higher liability and recorded interactions keeps it accountable. Concentrating the heaviest enforcement where crime is worst directs state power at the places where rights violations actually happen.
