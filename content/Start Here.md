## Core Principles
A short tour of the framework. Each section below is introduced in its own words, taken from that section's opening principles, with a link to read more. Key terms are defined in the [[Glossary]], and every topic's one-line position is collected in [[Where I Stand]].

## [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework]]
- **Sentientist:** Moral status and negative rights attach to beings capable of first-order subjective experience, or those on a clear developmental trajectory toward it.
- **Negative-rights focused:** Rights function as side-constraints against interference and harm. Positive rights that compel others to provide goods or services are generally rejected. Minimal tax-funded basic services (education, public safety, infrastructure) are accepted as a narrow practical exception.
- **Threshold deontologist:** Core rules against violating sentient rights normally hold. They may be overridden only after a clear threshold of rights violation is crossed, at which point the violator forfeits corresponding protections. Fetal moral weight scales with proximity to sentience and is grounded primarily in the responsibility created by voluntary procreation. Duties to sustain another being arise only from voluntary responsibility; where no voluntary act created the dependency, as in rape, withdrawing support differs from killing.

## [[2. Political and Social Character]]
Priority is given to cultural cohesion, high-trust social conditions, national sovereignty, and strong borders. The state's primary duties are to its own citizens and to the long-term continuity of the political community. Immigration is selective and oriented toward compatibility with the shared Western value structure, judged at the country level and through values vetting of every immigrant, and cultural assimilation is paramount. The border is secured, with lethal force used when necessary. "Western" names a value structure, not an ethnicity, so compatibility is open to anyone who holds those values.

## [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties]]
Strong individual liberties and property rights with government limited to protecting sentient rights, public safety, and order. Opt-in mechanisms where possible; harsh accountability for criminals and officials; skepticism of overreach but support for effective enforcement.

- **Surveillance State** (Practical application / tension with privacy; better after privacy is defined)
- **Due Process & Rule of Law** (Procedural protections and accountability once the state acts)
- **Police & Law Enforcement** (The concrete enforcement arm of the above principles)
- **Voting & Elections** (How political authority is selected and constrained — placed last because it is more about the machinery of government than core civil liberties)

## [[4. Religion & Secularism]]
Strict separation of religion and government in the United States. Religions that do not violate sentient rights are tolerated; those that do are restricted or banned in their harmful aspects. Private conscience and association protected; public institutions remain secular.

---

## [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance]]
Limited government, individual responsibility, low taxation, and maximum cultural and economic freedom, with only minimal safety nets. The state prioritizes free markets, personal choice, and fiscal restraint. Opt-in social programs and basic public services are acceptable; mandatory redistribution is not, beyond the narrow exceptions named in Welfare and Entitlements. Minimal necessary taxes fund core services: K-12 education, police, fire, emergency services, infrastructure upkeep, and the bare-minimum safety floor defined in Welfare and Entitlements. K-12 is funded by each state from its general revenue at about \$10,000 per child, as set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Education]].

---

## [[6. Criminal Justice & Security]]
Retribution, incapacitation, and the protection of victims' sentient rights take priority over rehabilitation. Those who murder, commit violent rape, child molestation, child rape, kidnapping, or human trafficking, or who repeatedly violate the rights of others, forfeit the protections they denied their victims. Punishment is swift, certain, and public, and capital punishment applies only where guilt is established beyond any real doubt. Enforcement is heavy, with military-style action against cartels and high-crime threats.

---

## [[7. Cultural & Social Issues]]
Traditional nuclear family preference, free speech absolutism (with narrow exceptions), meritocracy, cultural compatibility for cohesion, and personal responsibility. Strong opposition to identity politics and progressive social engineering, and skepticism of mandates.

---

## [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations]]
America First realism with selective Western imperialism. Prioritize U.S. sovereignty, security, cultural cohesion, and manufacturing strength. Engage forcefully against direct threats (especially Iran and extremist Islam) while avoiding open-ended nation-building. Participate in alliances and institutions only when they clearly serve American interests.

---

## [[9. Energy & Environment]]
Practical, gradual transition guided by free-market innovation and sentient-rights considerations. Avoid disruptive mandates or taxpayer-funded subsidies. Prioritize human (and herbivore) welfare over apocalyptic framing while conserving nature where it does not conflict with rights or development.

---

## [[10. Bioethics, Progress, & Technology]]
Strong support for technological advancement and transhumanism, constrained only by sentient rights. Free-market and private innovation preferred; government role limited to regulation against existential threats, rights violations, and national security. Opt-in privacy and data rules; animal testing redirected to forfeited rights holders.
