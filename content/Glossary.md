## About this glossary
Key terms used across the site, each defined in the site's own words and linked to where it is set out.

---

## Agency
Responsibility rests on agency, which scales with cognitive and experiential sophistication; only an agent can generate duties through voluntary choice or forfeit rights through deliberate violation.

Set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Minors and Incompetent Adults|Ethical and Philosophical Framework: Minors and Incompetent Adults]].

---

## Capital evidentiary standard
Nobody is executed unless the proof is overwhelming and backed by objective evidence such as video and physical evidence, not just testimony.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Evidentiary Standard|Criminal Justice & Security: Evidentiary Standard]].

---

## Debt brake
An automatic rule that raises the temporary income surcharge when debt runs above its legislated path, is suspended in recessions, and forces spending cuts if it stays on.

Set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Fiscal Policy|Domestic Policy & Governance: Fiscal Policy]] and [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#^surcharge|Taxation]].

---

## Desert
Desert means punishment matched to what the offender earned by the crime, regardless of whether it deters.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Deterrence and the Justification for Capital Punishment|Criminal Justice & Security: Deterrence and the Justification for Capital Punishment]].

---

## Earnings-only top-up
Low earners receive a top-up built into the flat tax's zero bracket: a negative income tax paid only on earned income, never for not working.

Set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Domestic Policy & Governance: Welfare and Entitlements]].

---

## Grave tier
The grave tier covers grave abuse that does not cause lasting injury; it counts as three marks at once: execution, experimentation, or life with labor, as the victim chooses.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|Criminal Justice & Security: Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor]].

---

## Felony and misdemeanor
A felony is any offense worth two or more marks, or any capital-tier crime. A misdemeanor is any offense worth one mark or a fine without a mark.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Criminal Justice & Security: Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life]].

---

## Fixed-term labor track
The fixed-term labor track is the lesser tier, one or two marks, served for a term the judge sets in proportion to the harm, with a known end date.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|Criminal Justice & Security: Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor]].

---

## Income-scaled fine
Any criminal, civil, or regulatory fine set in proportion to the offender's income and wealth. Individuals pay day fines with no floor or cap, always alongside labor that scales with severity. Corporations pay a share of global revenue, or their gain from the offense if higher, and the CEO is personally responsible by default, serving the labor and paying a personal fine; the same applies to heads of nonprofits and religious groups.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Income-Scaled Fines|Criminal Justice & Security: Income-Scaled Fines]].

---

## Last-resort allowance
A strictly means-tested allowance for severe disability and the elderly poor, from general revenue under the backstop logic, with fraud enforcement and periodic medical reviews; a narrow exception.

Set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Domestic Policy & Governance: Welfare and Entitlements]].

---

## Mark
A mark is a recorded conviction counted toward the repeat-offender threshold, and every mark includes supervised hard labor. Every mark requires proof beyond reasonable doubt.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Criminal Justice & Security: Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life]].

## Moral anti-realism
Moral anti-realism is the view that there are no moral facts that hold independently of what anyone thinks or prescribes.

Set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Moral anti-realism|Ethical and Philosophical Framework: Moral anti-realism]].

## Negative rights
Negative rights are side-constraints against interference and harm.

## Rebuttable presumption (immigration)
A starting assumption against admission that an individual applicant can overcome with clear and convincing evidence of conduct and record, never by declaration alone. It applies to Muslim applicants and to applicants from countries where Islamist movements govern or where most people back state-enforced religious law.

Set out in [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Foreign Policy & International Relations: Immigration and Borders]].

## Repeat-offender threshold
The repeat-offender threshold is three marks. When an offender reaches three marks, the offender forfeits the right to life.

Set out in [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Criminal Justice & Security: Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life]].

## Rights forfeiture
Forfeiture is triggered only when an agent crosses the threshold set in Axiom 6: violating another's negative rights through force or fraud, or directly imposing a severe, non-consensual risk of serious harm.

Set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Axioms|Ethical and Philosophical Framework: Axioms]].

## Sanctuary Levy
A single flat, time-limited levy on income above its own line, never above 3%, legally dedicated to sanctuary care for the animals alive when animal agriculture, commercial fishing, breeding mills, and animal testing shut down, to repaying the sanctuary bonds, and to a closing endowment for the last animals. It may only be lowered, and it ends when the national sanctuary population falls below 1% of its starting animal units and the bonds are repaid.

Set out in [[9. Energy & Environment#Sanctuary Plan|Energy & Environment: Sanctuary Plan]] and [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Domestic Policy & Governance: Taxation]].

---

## Sanctuary license
The license a nonprofit must hold to run a sanctuary under the Sanctuary Plan after the emergency phase. It sets care standards, sterilization, adoption vetting and unannounced inspections, and bars the sale of animals or animal products and all breeding. Licensed operators are paid a fixed amount per animal per year from the Sanctuary Levy, are accredited by an independent body and inspected by the state, and hold use of the land, never ownership, while any animals remain on it. Each site transfers from the state once its operator passes accreditation.

Set out in [[9. Energy & Environment#Handover to Nonprofit Operators|Energy & Environment: Handover to Nonprofit Operators]].

## Sentience basis (sentientism)
Any being, biological or artificial, that possesses first-order subjective experience is to be treated as a rights-bearer under my framework. Higher-order self-awareness is not required.

Set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Axioms|Ethical and Philosophical Framework: Axioms]].

---

## Sentience class
The statutory class a species falls in for criminal purposes, set by an expert board on the best available scientific consensus and revised only for acts after a revision takes effect. The high class covers mammals, birds, cephalopods, and decapod crustaceans such as crabs and lobsters; fish and shrimp are placed species by species; bivalves and insects sit on the lowest, precautionary tier. Deliberately killing a high-class animal outside self-defense is murder on the capital track; killing a lower-class animal is a mark; deliberately killing a lowest-tier animal carries a small fine.

Set out in [[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#^sentience-classes|Ethical and Philosophical Framework: Sentience Determination]].

## Transition levy
A temporary tax of 16% of all wages, split between worker and employer, that replaces today's payroll taxes and pays only for the Social Security and Medicare benefits already promised, then the debt. It never rises above 16%, steps down as the debt is paid, and ends when the protected benefits end.

Set out in [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Domestic Policy & Governance: Taxation]].

## Net fiscal voting bar
Only net taxpayers vote. A person is a net taxpayer for the prior completed tax year when taxes paid exceed personally directed transfers received and income-tax liability is positive. Personally directed transfers on the received side are cash, food, housing, or non-emergency medical support funded by general taxation — including the last-resort allowance, the earnings-only top-up, and refundable credit cash-outs. A refundable credit cash-out is only the amount paid when income-tax liability would otherwise be ≤ \$0; the portion that offsets positive liability is not received. Non-refundable relief — exemptions, deductions, and non-refundable credits, including the mothers' income-tax exemption — is not received. Taxes on the paid side are federal income tax, the employee share of payroll / FICA, and state income tax; the employer share of FICA does not count. Income-tax liability is federal and state liability after ordinary non-refundable credits, measured before the mothers' income-tax exemption; that exemption does not by itself fail the positive-liability gate when pre-exemption liability is positive. Grandfathered Social Security and Medicare, unemployment insurance, veterans' benefits, private insurance, charity, mutual aid, emergency shelter, emergency medical care, K-12, roads, defense, and other universal or shared public goods do not count as transfers. Excluding grandfathered Social Security and Medicare from the received side does not waive the positive-liability gate: an SS-only or payroll-only adult with \$0 pre-exemption income-tax liability does not vote. The franchise tracks current net contribution to the tax system that funds transfers, not lifetime payroll history. The rule exists because the franchise decides other people's money, and letting non–net-taxpayers cast those votes is a massive conflict of interest. The bar lifts when a later completed tax year shows net taxpayer again. The unit is the tax-filing unit; joint filers share one status, so a stay-at-home spouse on a joint return that is a net taxpayer votes. Also called the net taxpayer voting bar.

Set out in [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Voting and Elections|Authority & Civil Liberties: Voting and Elections]] and [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#^net-fiscal-vote|net fiscal voting bar]].

## Welfare voting bar
Superseded name for the [[#Net fiscal voting bar|net fiscal voting bar]] (net taxpayer voting bar): the franchise is restricted to net taxpayers, not merely barred on binary means-tested receipt.
