## Core Principles
This page measures today's political parties against the framework. Each party is described from its own official platform, then compared section by section with the positions set out on this site. No party matches the framework. Each one aligns with some principles and conflicts with others, and the pattern of those overlaps says more than any party label. The United States gets the most detail. Selected foreign systems follow for contrast.

## Part A: United States

### The Party Landscape

>[!tldr] In short
> Most Americans are independents, but almost all votes go to the two major parties. The framework judges each party position by position.

#### Position
American politics is a two-party system with a large unaffiliated middle. Third parties shape the debate but win almost no federal offices. The framework treats party labels as bundles of positions, not identities, and judges each bundle item by item.

>[!example]- Data: party lean by sex, marriage, education and race
> Survey and exit-poll figures as reported by the cited sources.
>- Pew, registered voters (2023 data): women 51% Democratic, 44% Republican; men 52% Republican, 46% Democratic. Married men 59% Republican; married women 50% Republican; never-married women 72% Democratic. Source – [Pew Research Center: Partisanship by Gender, Sexual Orientation, Marital and Parental Status](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/04/09/partisanship-by-gender-sexual-orientation-marital-and-parental-status/)
>- Pew, by race and education: white adults without a college degree show no sex gap (men 64% Republican, women 62% Republican). Among white college graduates, men are 53% Republican while women are 57% Democratic and 42% Republican. Black women are 84% Democratic and Black men 81%; Hispanic men are 61% Democratic and Hispanic women 60%. Source – [Pew Research Center: Partisanship by Race, Ethnicity and Education](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2024/04/09/partisanship-by-race-ethnicity-and-education/)
>- The 2024 gender gap in the presidential vote was about 20 points, roughly the same as in 1980, 2012, 2016 and 2020. Source – [AEI: The Exit Polls from A to Z](https://www.aei.org/commentary/the-exit-polls-from-a-to-z/)

> [!question]- "If no party fits, why compare them at all?"
> Voters choose among the options that exist. Mapping where each party agrees and disagrees with the framework shows which trade-offs a vote for that party involves.

#### Key Elements
- In 2025 a record 45% of Americans identified as independents, with 27% Democrats and 27% Republicans. Counting independents who lean toward a party, Democrats led 47% to 42%.
- In the 2024 presidential election Trump won 49.80% of the popular vote and Harris 48.32%. The Green, Libertarian, and Constitution candidates together took about 1%.

#### Rationale
Party identification is weak while results are concentrated. Most voters do not identify with either major party, yet nearly all votes still go to one of them. That gap is why the framework is set out as positions rather than as an affiliation ([[2. Political and Social Character#Relation to Other Political Traditions|Relation to Other Political Traditions]]).

### Democratic Party

>[!tldr] In short
> Full alignment on same-sex marriage. Directional alignment on marijuana and drug reform. Conflict on NATO, Israel, policing and crime, wages, taxes, entitlements, abortion, capital punishment, immigration, group preferences, and gender policy.

#### Position
The 2024 Democratic platform pairs an expanded federal role in wages, health care, and climate with a path to citizenship for people who entered unlawfully. I share its acceptance of same-sex marriage. I conflict with it on immigration and borders, alliances, Israel, policing and crime, economics, guns, abortion, and capital punishment.

> [!question]- "Isn't the Democratic Party the party of civil liberties?"
> No. Denying negative rights is not protecting civil liberties. A party that takes people's earnings through compulsory redistribution, mandates what they must pay and buy, and restricts their right to defend themselves is not defending liberty. Civil liberties are best defended by a party that is strong on crime and protects civilians' security and rights, because a person who is not safe from violence, theft, and coercion is not free in any meaningful sense. The platform's minimum wage, progressive taxation, and mandatory entitlement system all rest on the compulsory redistribution I reject ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]), and its record on crime leaves civilians less secure ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]).

> [!summary]- Key Elements
> - **Platform:** a federal minimum wage of "at least \$15-an-hour"; no tax increase for anyone earning under \$400,000, with higher taxes above that; support for Medicaid expansion; a ban on assault weapons and universal background checks; restoring abortion access after Roe; a "pathway to citizenship for Dreamers"; a net-zero emissions goal; strengthening NATO; support for Israel; "fund the police, not defund the police."
> - **Full alignment:** marriage freedom ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Marriage Law|Marriage Law]]).
> - **Directional alignment:** drug reform, where the party moves toward legal marijuana and I make drugs legal for personal use away from minors ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drugs and Vice|Drugs and Vice]]).
> - **Mixed:** guns, where its background checks and red flag laws match mine and its assault-weapons ban does not ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gun Rights|Gun Rights]]).
> - **Conflicts:** unconditional support for NATO, where I join alliances only when they serve U.S. interests ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Alliances and NATO|Alliances and NATO]]); a two-state solution and a base that leans Palestinian, where I reject a two-state solution ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Israel and the Middle East|Israel and the Middle East]]); policing and crime, where the party is not pro-police or anti-crime in practice and I heavily fund police and punish harshly ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]); the minimum wage ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Labor and Unions|Labor and Unions]]); progressive rates instead of a flat tax ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]); a mandatory entitlement system ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]); abortion access past the sentience line ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Abortion and Bioethics|Abortion and Bioethics]]); no support for capital punishment ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]); a path to citizenship for people who entered unlawfully ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Immigration and Borders]]).
> - **Culture:** the platform champions "an ambitious equity and racial justice agenda," including expanded federal contracting for small disadvantaged businesses, which conflicts with the framework's ban on group preferences ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]). It supports "medically necessary gender-affirming care" and pledges to pass the Equality Act, which would add gender identity as a protected category. The framework protects race, biological sex, and sexual orientation but does not recognize gender identity as a legal category ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions|Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions]]).

#### Rationale
I agree with the Democratic Party on same-sex marriage and on several social freedoms. It disagrees on whether government should set wages, redistribute income, and run compulsory insurance. Because the platform rests on those tools, it scores low on the economic principles despite the agreement on marriage.

##### Directional Alignment
**Not directionally aligned.** In order of weight, the divergences are crime, group-based equity, gender policy, and then the size of the state. On crime the platform funds police ("fund the police, not defund the police") but moves away from punishment. It would "restrict state and local practices like solitary confinement," limit federal reincarceration "to only serious violations," and expand second-chance programs. It does not support capital punishment. The framework moves the other way, toward mandatory death for murder and violent rape and [[Glossary#Rights forfeiture|forfeiture]] at three marks ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]; [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]]). It then champions "an ambitious equity and racial justice agenda," against equal treatment without preferences ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]), and "medically necessary gender-affirming care" and the Equality Act, against the framework's biological-sex positions ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]). Last come a mandated wage floor, higher taxes on top earners, and expanded public coverage, against free labor markets, a flat tax, and opt-in programs ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]; [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]). I fully agree on same-sex marriage, but that is one point of agreement inside an opposite trajectory; its NATO, Israel, and police-funding language does not match my positions in practice.

> - Democratic National Committee – [2024 Democratic Party Platform](https://democrats.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2024-Democratic-Party-Platform.pdf)

### Republican Party and the MAGA Faction

>[!tldr] In short
> Full alignment on borders, DEI, sex-based policy, missile defense, and alliances only in the U.S. interest. Directional alignment on punishment, policing, guns, abortion, Israel, energy, and limited government (the party goes partway). Conflict on animal agriculture, broad baseline tariffs, entitlements, and private-school vouchers.

#### Position
The 2024 Republican platform, written under Trump's leadership, combines tax cuts and deregulation with large-scale deportation, broad tariffs, and a promise not to cut Social Security or Medicare. The framework shares its emphasis on border enforcement, gun rights, policing, energy production, and Israel. It conflicts with the populist economics: broad baseline tariffs that go beyond the framework's cautious, targeted use, and an untouchable entitlement system.

> [!question]- "Isn't the MAGA movement national conservatism, which the framework partly shares?"
> Partly. The framework shares its emphasis on borders, patriotism, and a strong military ([[2. Political and Social Character#Civilizational Nationalism|Civilizational Nationalism]]). It parts ways on broad baseline tariffs, which go beyond the framework's view that tariffs are good in theory and questionable in practice, used as leverage, to protect strategic industries, and against national-security threats such as China ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Trade and Economics|Trade and Economics]]), and on protecting a mandatory Social Security system "with no cuts." That pledge rules out the opt-in transition the framework requires.

> [!question]- "How does the platform's abortion position compare?"
> The platform leaves abortion to "a vote of the People, from within the States" and opposes late-term abortion. The framework sets a single rights-based line ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Abortion and Bioethics|Abortion and Bioethics]]). In the framework, a right does not change from state to state ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Federalism|Federalism]]).

> [!summary]- Key Elements
> - **Platform:** "carry out the largest deportation operation in American history"; make the Trump tax cuts permanent and add "no tax on tips"; baseline tariffs; protect Social Security and Medicare "with no cuts"; "DRILL, BABY, DRILL"; cancel the electric-vehicle mandate; close the Department of Education; the right to keep and bear arms; replenish police forces; an Iron Dome missile shield; stand with Israel.
> - **Full alignment:** border enforcement and deportation ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Border Security and Immigration Enforcement|Border Security and Immigration Enforcement]]); ending DEI preferences; sex-based policy; missile defense ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Military Spending and Projection|Military Spending and Projection]]); alliances judged by the U.S. interest ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Alliances and NATO|Alliances and NATO]]).
> - **Directional alignment:** punishment, where federal executions are restored by executive order but death is not mandatory ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]); policing, funded but with qualified immunity kept ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]); guns, where the party protects ownership but most of its House members voted against red-flag funding and its states spread permitless carry, while I keep background checks and red flag laws ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gun Rights|Gun Rights]]); abortion, through state bans rather than my single line ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Abortion and Bioethics|Abortion and Bioethics]]); Israel, stopping short on Iran ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Israel and the Middle East|Israel and the Middle East]]); energy, where EV mandates are cut but the ethanol mandate is set at record volumes ([[9. Energy & Environment#Subsidies and Mandates|Subsidies and Mandates]]); limited government, through deregulation alongside deficits.
> - **Culture:** the platform pledges to "keep men out of women's sports," "ban Taxpayer funding for sex transition," teach "Knowledge and Skills, Not CRT and Gender Indoctrination," and enforce civil rights laws "to stop schools from discriminating on the basis of Race." These match the framework's sex-based and equal-treatment positions ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions|Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]). It does not state protection for sexual orientation in employment, which the framework requires.
> - **Conflicts:** broad baseline tariffs, beyond my cautious, targeted use ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Trade and Economics|Trade and Economics]]); a mandatory entitlement system protected "with no cuts"; a tax code that adds carve-outs instead of a flat 20% (scored 0, since rate cuts still move toward lower, simpler taxes); leaving abortion to the states; federal tax-credit scholarships for private and religious schools, where my money follows the child only to public and charter schools ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Education|Education]]); the push to override California's Prop 12, where I ban animal agriculture ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]).

#### Rationale
On security and borders the Republican Party is the closest major party to the framework. On economics it is closer than the Democrats on regulation and energy but not on trade or entitlements. The populist turn has moved the party toward broad baseline tariffs, where the framework treats tariffs cautiously and uses them in a targeted way, and toward a protected status for current entitlements, which runs against the framework's opt-in principles.

##### Directional Alignment
**Directionally aligned.** On borders, policing, guns, energy, education, and sex-based policy, the platform moves the same way as the framework ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Border Security and Immigration Enforcement|Border Security]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions|Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions]]). The main exceptions run against that direction: broad baseline tariffs, beyond the framework's cautious, targeted use ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Trade and Economics|Trade and Economics]]), and a pledge to protect Social Security and Medicare "with no cuts", which rules out the opt-in transition ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]).

### Libertarian Party

>[!tldr] In short
> Full alignment on opt-in welfare, free labor markets, self-defense, and equal treatment in law. Directional alignment on drugs and vice (the party goes further). Conflict on capital punishment, borders, alliances, and private-employer discrimination.

#### Position
The Libertarian Party is the closest match to the framework on economics and personal freedom. It is the furthest on punishment and borders. Its platform repeals the income tax, phases Social Security out into a voluntary private system, keeps government out of labor contracts and marriage, and repeals laws against victimless crimes. It also opposes the death penalty and calls for open movement across borders.

> [!question]- "The framework agrees with libertarianism on rights. Why not adopt the Libertarian platform?"
> Because the framework accepts far more force against people who violate rights. The platform states, "We oppose the administration of the death penalty by the state," and it supports "unrestricted movement of human as well as financial capital across national borders." The framework holds the opposite on both ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]; [[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Immigration and Borders]]).

#### Key Elements
- **Platform:** repeal the income tax and abolish the IRS; phase out Social Security toward a private voluntary system; labor contracts outside government's scope; repeal laws on drugs, gambling, and sex work; restitution to victims; the Second Amendment and self-defense; no government role in marriage; a sufficient military, no entangling alliances, no compulsory service; opposition to the death penalty; open borders.
- **Full alignment:** opt-in retirement ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Social Security and Medicare Transition|Social Security and Medicare Transition]]); no minimum wage ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Labor and Unions|Labor and Unions]]); self-defense with no duty to retreat ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Self-Defense and Defense of Others|Self-Defense and Defense of Others]]); equal legal treatment.
- **Directional alignment:** [[Glossary#Negative rights|negative rights]] and limited government ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]); vice, where the party legalizes the commercial sale and public use of all drugs and I legalize personal use only ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drugs and Vice|Drugs and Vice]]).
- **Mixed:** guns, where the party protects ownership but opposes every law that restricts or monitors firearms, including the background checks I keep ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gun Rights|Gun Rights]]); taxes, where abolishing the income tax overshoots my flat 20% ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]).
- **Culture:** the platform says government should not treat anyone differently by "sex ... or sexual orientation," and that gender identity "should have no impact on the government's treatment of individuals." It also holds that private organizations "retain their rights to set whatever standards of association they deem appropriate." The framework agrees on equal legal treatment by sex, but bars private employers and businesses from treating people by race, sex, or sexual orientation, and records birth sex in law ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]).
- **Conflicts:** the death penalty; open borders; non-interventionism and avoiding alliances ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Alliances and NATO|Alliances and NATO]]); no position on animal rights.

#### Rationale
The framework sits closest to libertarianism on what government may do to peaceful people. It differs sharply on what government must do to people who violate rights and to threats from outside the country. The party's 2024 nominee won 0.42% of the vote.

##### Directional Alignment
**Directionally aligned on economics and personal liberty; not on security.** The platform shrinks the state toward voluntary exchange, as the framework does ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Political Philosophy|Political Philosophy]]). It moves the opposite way on the use of force against people who violate rights and on borders, where the framework accepts far more state power ([[2. Political and Social Character#Order and Security|Order and Security]]).

### Green Party

>[!tldr] In short
> Full alignment on ranked-choice voting. Directional alignment on animal welfare, drugs, sex work, and same-sex marriage. Conflict on economics, energy subsidies, nuclear power, defense, Israel, bail, voting rules, guns, and capital punishment.

#### Position
The Green Party combines an ecosocialist economic program with the strongest animal-welfare commitments of any US party. The framework shares the animal-welfare direction and the interest in voting reform. It conflicts with the Greens on economics, energy, defense, and punishment.

> [!question]- "The Greens want animal protections. Doesn't that make them the sentientist party?"
> Yes, they are the closest on animal rights. They would extend the Animal Welfare Act to rats, mice, and birds, replace factory farms (CAFOs), and use non-animal chemical testing where available ([[10. Bioethics, Progress, & Technology#Animal Rights and Sentience Technology|Animal Rights and Sentience Technology]]). However, nearly all other social and cultural issues are disaligned: they are the opposite on crime and immigration, and the opposite on redistribution. Unfortunately, though they are the best on animal rights, that is nearly all they are aligned on. I would rather put my lot into the vastly more aligned party and rely on private innovation to produce lab-grown meat.

#### Key Elements
- **Platform:** "abolish the death penalty"; single-payer health care; an Ecosocialist Green New Deal; a national minimum wage indexed to inflation; phasing out nuclear power and fossil, nuclear, and biomass subsidies; replacing CAFOs and banning corporate ownership of farms; extending the Animal Welfare Act; cutting the defense budget; nuclear disarmament; proportional representation and ranked-choice voting.
- **Directional alignment:** animal welfare, where the party replaces factory farms and I ban animal agriculture ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]); drug decriminalization and decriminalized sex work, which I legalize under licensing ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drugs and Vice|Drugs and Vice]]); same-sex marriage ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Marriage Law|Marriage Law]]).
- **Full alignment:** ranked-choice voting in every election ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Ranked-Choice Voting|Ranked-Choice Voting]]).
- **Culture:** the platform supports "affirmative action," names feminism among its key values, and would add "sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression" to civil rights law. The framework rejects group preferences, agrees on protecting sexual orientation, and does not recognize gender identity as a legal category ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Feminism and Sex Equality|Feminism and Sex Equality]]).
- **Conflicts:** single-payer health care ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Healthcare|Healthcare]]); the minimum wage; the phase-out of nuclear power; defense cuts ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Military Spending and Projection|Military Spending and Projection]]); abolishing the death penalty; a planned economy ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Economic System|Economic System]]); publicly funded renewables, where I subsidize nothing without opt-in ([[9. Energy & Environment#Subsidies and Mandates|Subsidies and Mandates]]); national proportional representation, alongside opposition to the voter ID and civics exam I require ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Voting and Elections|Voting and Elections]]); bail as a right ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Pretrial Detention and Bail|Pretrial Detention and Bail]]).

#### Rationale
The Greens show that animal welfare is not tied to free markets. The framework's [[Glossary#Sentience basis (sentientism)|sentientism]] is shared on the left, while its economic and security positions are shared on the right. The party's 2024 nominee won 0.56% of the vote.

##### Directional Alignment
**Not directionally aligned.** In order of weight: on crime, the platform says "Abolish the death penalty," calls for "freedom on bail as a right of all defendants charged with non-violent crimes," and seeks to reverse mandatory sentencing, the opposite of the framework's capital and compelled-labor system ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]; [[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|Compelled Labor]]). On race, it supports "affirmative action" and would "Teach all students about white supremacy and intersectionality" ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Curriculum Content|Curriculum Content]]). On gender, it would add "gender identity, and gender expression" to civil rights law ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]). On economics, it favors a planned economy, single-payer care, a national minimum wage, and defense cuts ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Economic System|Economic System]]). Two overlaps are genuine: animal welfare ([[1. Ethical and Philosophical Framework#Animal Rights and Sentientism|Animal Rights and Sentientism]]) and decriminalizing consensual adult sex for pay, which the framework already allows under regulation ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drugs and Vice|Drugs and Vice]]).

### Democratic Socialists of America and the Progressive Wing

>[!tldr] In short
> Conflict at the foundation: on property, taxation, policing, prisons, bail, borders, the Senate, Israel, and gender policy. Directional only on drug decriminalization and marriage.

#### Position
The Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) is not a ballot party. It is an organization whose members, including Representatives Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Rashida Tlaib and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, mostly run as Democrats. Its program calls for collective ownership of major industries, wealth taxes, abolition of police and prisons, open migration, and abolition of the Senate. It conflicts with the framework at the foundation.

> [!question]- "Is democratic socialism just Nordic-style social democracy?"
> No. DSA states, "We want to collectively own the key economic drivers ... such as energy production and transportation," and its program calls for "public ownership of the largest corporations and essential industries." Nordic social democracies keep private ownership and market economies (see Part B). DSA's goal is a different economic system ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Economic System|Economic System]]).

> [!question]- "Does the progressive wing share the full DSA program?"
> Not necessarily. Many progressive Democrats back Medicare for All and a Green New Deal without endorsing police abolition or public ownership of industry. This page scores DSA's written program, not every progressive politician.

> [!question]- "Why do progressive politicians as a group run against the framework?"
> Because their shared commitments start from the opposite premises, and the gaps can be ranked. First is crime, where the two are opposite: the framework imposes death for murder and violent rape ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]), forfeiture at three marks ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]]), compelled labor for every custodial sentence ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|Compelled Labor]]), no duty to retreat ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Self-Defense and Defense of Others|Self-Defense]]), and larger, better-funded police ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]); progressive platforms would abolish the death penalty, widen bail and shorten incarceration, and, in DSA's case, move "towards fully abolishing the police and prison system." Second is group identity: DEI preferences and critical race theory's account of racism as embedded in law and institutions, against one rule for every individual ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]). Third is how prevalent gender identity has become in progressive politics, against the framework's biological-sex positions ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]). After those come property, taxes, and the size of the state. The framework treats property as a negative right ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Property Rights|Property Rights]]); progressives favor public or collective ownership of key industries. It sets a flat tax and a limited state ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]); they favor progressive and wealth taxes to fund a larger state. Some overlaps exist, such as decriminalizing drug use ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Drug Policy|Drug Policy]]), detention not set by ability to pay ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Pretrial Detention and Bail|Pretrial Detention and Bail]]), and, among Greens, animal welfare. Ending qualified immunity is a surface agreement with the opposite purpose: the framework pairs it with stronger policing, progressives with shrinking it.

#### Key Elements
- **Program:** "Demilitarize police departments, disempower police unions, and redirect funding to public services as steps towards fully abolishing the police and prison system"; end qualified immunity; "Abolish ICE ... Legalize migration, grant amnesty for all immigrants ... end visa caps and quotas"; "abolish the Senate"; proportional representation; public ownership of the largest corporations; "aggressive wealth taxes"; Medicare for All; a Green New Deal; "End all military and economic aid to Israel." DSA reports more than 95,000 members.
- **Directional alignment:** drug decriminalization ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Drugs and Vice|Drugs and Vice]]); same-sex marriage ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Marriage Law|Marriage Law]]).
- **Surface only:** ending qualified immunity, which I pair with heavily funded police while DSA pairs it with cutting police budgets "annually towards zero" ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]).
- **Culture:** the program calls for ending restrictions on "gender expression and transition" and describes current law as "patriarchal restrictions" on family life, while asking to "ban sex discrimination." The framework agrees on banning sex discrimination but holds that sex is biological and that minors receive no transgender treatment ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]).
- **Conflicts:** collective ownership ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Property Rights|Property Rights]]); wealth taxes ([[5. Political and Social Character#Order and Security|Order and Security]]); open migration and amnesty; abolishing the Senate ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Pretrial Detention and Bail|Pretrial Detention and Bail]]).

#### Rationale
The framework begins with negative rights and private property. DSA begins with collective ownership. The two disagree at the foundation, and almost every later position follows from that.

The disagreement can be ranked. Crime comes first, because the two are opposite. The framework makes murder and violent rape capital ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]), forfeits the right to life at three marks ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Repeat Offenders and Forfeiture of the Right to Life|Repeat Offenders]]), serves every custodial sentence as compelled labor ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Abolition of Permanent Incarceration and Compelled Labor|Compelled Labor]]), recognizes no duty to retreat ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Self-Defense and Defense of Others|Self-Defense]]), and funds larger, better-paid police ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]). DSA's program would "Demilitarize police departments, disempower police unions, and redirect funding to public services as steps towards fully abolishing the police and prison system," and the Green platform says "Abolish the death penalty."

Second is group identity. DEI preferences and the premise of critical race theory, that racism is "embedded in legal systems and policies" rather than only in individual bias, organize politics around groups. The framework applies one rule to every individual and bars treatment by race, biological sex, or sexual orientation in either direction in employment, admissions, and business ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]). Third is the prevalence of gender identity in progressive politics: DSA would end restrictions on "gender expression and transition," against the framework's position that sex is biological and that minors receive no transgender treatment ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]).

The economic divergences follow. On property, DSA's goal of public ownership of "the largest corporations and essential industries" overrides the owner's rights that the framework treats as basic ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Property Rights|Property Rights]]). On the size of the state, "aggressive wealth taxes" and Medicare for All require a larger and more redistributive government, against the framework's flat tax, opt-in programs, and limited state ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]; [[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]).

Some agreements are surface only. Both would end qualified immunity, but for opposite purposes: the framework holds officers to a higher standard while expanding and funding police, and DSA treats it as a step toward shrinking and abolishing them ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Police Power and Accountability|Police Power and Accountability]]). Both would stop setting detention by ability to pay, but the framework detains anyone found dangerous ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Pretrial Detention and Bail|Pretrial Detention and Bail]]). These agreements are narrow and do not change the overall direction.

##### Directional Alignment
**Not directionally aligned.** In order of weight, DSA runs opposite to the framework on crime, group identity and race, gender policy, and then property, taxes, and the size of the state. Its points of agreement are narrow and do not change that direction.

### European Union

#### Position
The European Parliament elected in 2024 is led by the center-right European People's Party (188 seats), followed by the Socialists and Democrats (136). The national-conservative groups, Patriots for Europe (84), European Conservatives and Reformists (78), and Europe of Sovereign Nations (25), together outnumber the Socialists. The Union pools trade and regulation across member states, which the framework views cautiously as a supranational layer above national self-government ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Global Institutions|Global Institutions]]).

#### Key Elements
- 2024 seats: EPP 188, S&D 136, Patriots 84, ECR 78, Renew 77, Greens/EFA 53, Left 46, ESN 25, non-attached 33.
- The rise of the national-conservative groups mirrors the populist turn among American Republicans.

##### Directional Alignment
Using the same order as the US profiles (crime, then DEI and critical race theory, then gender policy, then property, taxes, and the size of the state), no EU group is fully aligned, because of crime. The EU Charter of Fundamental Rights states, "No one shall be condemned to the death penalty, or executed," so every party that accepts the Union's legal order rejects the framework's capital punishment ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]). On gender, the Commission's LGBTIQ equality strategies, now renewed for 2026 to 2030, move toward legal recognition of gender identity, against the framework's biological-sex positions ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]). The national-conservative groups (Patriots, ECR, ESN) are the closest on borders and national sovereignty, the area where the framework is most skeptical of the Union ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Global Institutions|Global Institutions]]).

**Closest alignment:** the national-conservative groups (Patriots for Europe, ECR, ESN), for their emphasis on borders and national sovereignty. Conflict: like every group, they operate within a Charter that bars the death penalty.

### Nordic Social Democracy

#### Position
The Nordic countries combine market economies and private ownership with large, tax-funded welfare states. Denmark has the highest tax-to-GDP ratio in the EU. Wages are set mainly through collective agreements rather than a statutory minimum wage. This model is the strongest argument that a generous welfare state can coexist with free markets. The framework still rejects it because the welfare state is compulsory ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]). It also shows how DSA's program differs from social democracy: the Nordic countries kept private ownership.

#### Key Elements
- **Aligns:** market economies and the absence of a statutory minimum wage.
- **Conflicts:** compulsory welfare financed by high taxes.

##### Directional Alignment
**Not directionally aligned on the size of the state; partly aligned on crime in Sweden.** The large compulsory welfare state runs against the framework ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Welfare and Entitlements|Welfare and Entitlements]]). On crime, Sweden's 2022 Tidö Agreement between the Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals, and Sweden Democrats called for "dubbla straff för gängkriminella" (double penalties for gang criminals), an end to sentencing discounts for offenders over 18, and tougher penalties for violent and sexual crimes. Doubled gang sentences took effect in August 2026. That moves in the framework's direction on punishment, but no Nordic party proposes capital punishment ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]).

**Closest alignment:** the Sweden Democrats and their Tidö partners (Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals), whose agreement produced the crime package. Conflict: the welfare state they govern within stays compulsory and tax-funded.

### United Kingdom

#### Position
Labour won 411 seats in 2024 with 33.7% of the vote. The Conservatives won 121 seats with 23.7%, and the Liberal Democrats 72 with 12.2%. A large majority on a third of the vote shows how first-past-the-post voting turns pluralities into majorities, the same mechanism behind the American two-party system ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Voting and Elections|Voting and Elections]]). Like the US, Britain has a right-of-center and a left-of-center party, with a third party far underrepresented relative to its vote share.

##### Directional Alignment
On gender, the most important British development came from the courts rather than the parties. In For Women Scotland v The Scottish Ministers (2025), the Supreme Court held unanimously that "sex," "man," and "woman" in the Equality Act 2010 "refer to biological sex," which matches the framework's definition ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]).

**Closest alignment:** Restore Britain, led by Rupert Lowe, is the closest on crime. It pledges to "Hold a Binding Referendum on Restoring the Death Penalty," saying "It is just to execute those guilty of the most heinous crimes," and to "Deport All Illegal Migrants" ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]). It also aligns on residency and citizenship. It would deport a legally resident foreign national who "lives in social housing, claims benefits," "fails to integrate," or "commits crime," and would strip citizenship from "dual-national Islamic extremists." The framework adopts both in adapted form: listed benefits, a two-year English and civics test, removal for any crime above a basic infraction, and a court finding by clear and convincing evidence. On extremists it goes further than deportation: terrorism and treason are punished by death or secure confinement, with citizenship forfeiture added for dual nationals, under religion-neutral offenses and without statelessness ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Removal of Non-Citizen Residents and Loss of Citizenship|Removal of Non-Citizen Residents]]). Conflict: it describes Britain as "a Christian nation," where the framework keeps religion out of government ([[4. Religion & Secularism#Role of Religion in Government|Role of Religion in Government]]). Reform UK is next. Its 2024 contract promised "Prison for violent crimes and possessing a knife," to "Scrap all Diversity, Equality and Inclusion (DE&I) roles and regulations," and "No gender questioning, social transitioning or pronoun swapping" in schools ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Equal Treatment in Employment and Business|Equal Treatment]]; [[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions|Gender Identity in Schools and Institutions]]). Conflicts: it makes no death-penalty pledge, and replacing the Equality Act outright would leave open whether sexual orientation stays protected in employment, which the framework requires.

In the 2025 federal election the CDU/CSU won 28.6% (CDU 22.6%, CSU 6.0%) and the Alternative for Germany (AfD) came second with 20.8%. Proportional representation lets a populist-right party grow inside parliament instead of inside an existing party, as MAGA did inside the Republican Party. Cultural & Social Issues#LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues|LGBTQ+ and Identity Issues]]).

### Switzerland

#### Position
Switzerland limits the power of parties through direct democracy. A popular initiative needs 100,000 valid signatures within 18 months to force a national vote on a constitutional amendment. An optional referendum needs 50,000 signatures within 100 days to put a new federal law to a vote. Citizens can overrule what parties agree on in parliament. Of the systems on this page, it is the closest to the framework's preference for decentralized power under citizen control ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Federalism|Federalism]]), though the framework protects rights from majority votes as well.

##### Directional Alignment
**The Swiss People's Party (SVP) is the closest on crime and borders.** Its 2010 initiative for the automatic deportation of foreigners convicted of serious crimes passed with 52.91% of the vote and a majority of cantons. The Social Democrats, Greens, FDP, and CVP opposed it ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Border Security and Immigration Enforcement|Border Security]]).

**Closest alignment:** the SVP, for its record on deporting foreign offenders and on borders.

### Singapore and Japan

#### Position
Both countries have long been governed by a dominant party and both keep capital punishment. Singapore's People's Action Party won 87 of 97 seats with 65.57% of the vote in 2025. Singapore carried out at least 9 executions in 2024, mostly for drug offences, against 25 in the US. Japan's Liberal Democratic Party has governed almost continuously since 1955, though it lost its lower-house majority in 2024. Japan retains the death penalty but carried out no executions in 2024. The framework shares their emphasis on order. It conflicts with Singapore on executing people for drug offences, since the framework reserves death for violent violations of rights ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Drug Policy|Drug Policy]]).

##### Directional Alignment
**Crime:** both countries keep capital punishment under their long-governing parties, the closest match to the framework on this page ([[6. Criminal Justice & Security#Murder, Rape, and the Mandatory Death Penalty|Mandatory Death Penalty]]); Singapore's use of it for drug offences is the exception noted above. **Gender and marriage:** in 2022 Singapore's Parliament repealed Section 377A, which criminalized sex between men, 93 to 3, a move toward the framework. It also added Article 156 to the Constitution so that no fundamental-liberties challenge can invalidate a law defining "marriage as a union between a man and a woman," which conflicts with the framework's marriage freedom ([[7. Cultural & Social Issues#Marriage Law|Marriage Law]]).

**Closest alignment:** Singapore's People's Action Party and Japan's Liberal Democratic Party, the long-governing parties under which capital punishment is kept. Conflicts: Singapore executes for drug offences and protects a man-woman definition of marriage from constitutional challenge.

## Summary Scorecard

>[!tldr] In short
> No American party matches my positions. Republicans score highest (+9): they lead on borders, DEI, sex-based policy, and defense, and move my way on punishment, policing, guns, and abortion, though they keep qualified immunity, defend animal agriculture, and fund private schools through tax credits. The Libertarian and Constitution parties tie (+3). The Libertarians match on economics and personal freedom but oppose the death penalty and leave private employment unprotected; the Constitution Party matches on borders, punishment, and sex-based policy but grounds law in religion and rejects protection for sexual orientation. Democrats follow (−10), agreeing on marriage and drug reform but opposing me on crime, alliances, Israel, economics, group preferences, and gender policy. The Greens and DSA conflict most (−13); DSA runs further than the Democrats on punishment, wages, and feminism, and the Greens are the only party that moves my way on animals.

#### Method
Each US party is scored on sixteen core principles of the framework, using its official platform or program together with its officeholders' record and its base's stance. Each party is graded against the realistic options, not against a perfect match: a party that moves my way earns alignment, and conflict is reserved for parties that move the opposite way. On the eleven general rows, +1 means the party moves my way, 0 means it is mixed or silent, and −1 means it moves the opposite way. The crime row (mandatory death penalty, firm punishment) and the four culture rows (DEI and group preferences, gender and transgender policy, feminism, and protected traits) are double-weighted from −2 to +2: ±2 for full alignment or full conflict, ±1 for directional. An unweighted total, counting only the sign of each row, is shown beneath the weighted total. The culture rows are measured against the framework's equal-treatment rule: race, biological sex, and sexual orientation are protected in either direction, gender identity is not a legal category, and there are no DEI preferences. Scores are summed. Against progressive politics, the framework ranks its divergences in this order: crime first, then DEI and critical race theory, then gender policy, then property, taxes, and the size of the state. Apart from the double weighting, the total shows how many principles a party shares, not how important each one is, so it should be read alongside the profiles above. Under the earlier equal-weight scorecard of twelve rows the totals were Democrats −7, Republicans +2, Libertarians +3, Greens −5, DSA −6, Constitution +3.

> [!question]- "Isn't the scorecard self-serving? Of course the framework ranks parties by how close they are to itself."
> Yes, by design. The scorecard measures alignment with this framework, not which party is better in general, and a reader who holds different principles would score the parties differently. What keeps it honest is the method. Each score rests on the party's own platform and its record in practice, the rows and weights are published, mixed or silent positions score zero, and each party is graded against the realistic options rather than against a perfect match, and conflicts are listed even for the parties that score highest, such as the Republican platform's protection of officers from lawsuits. The double-weighted rows score −2 to +2, ±2 for full alignment or full conflict and ±1 for directional. The unweighted totals count only the sign of each row, so readers can see what the weights change. Without them Republicans still lead (+7), the Libertarian and Constitution parties tie (+3), Democrats follow (−8), and the Greens and DSA tie at the bottom (−9).
