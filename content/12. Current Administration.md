## Core Principles
I grade the current presidential administration's major actions against my positions here. Donald J. Trump took office for his second term on January 20, 2025. Each action is graded on direction as well as detail, because no administration will match my positions exactly: **Aligned** (direction and details match), **Directionally aligned (details differ)** (moves the way I do, by a different route or to a different degree), **Mixed** (parts point both ways, or I have no settled position), **Directionally opposed** (moves against my positions, with some overlap), and **Opposed**. Grades reflect my positions, set out in the sections linked from each entry; descriptions are drawn from the official text of each action.

## Immigration and Borders

>[!tldr] In short
> 8 actions graded: 1 Aligned, 5 Directionally aligned (details differ), 1 Mixed, 1 Directionally opposed.

#### Third-country removals allowed to resume
**September 29, 2026** · **Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)**

In *Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D.*, the Supreme Court stayed a district court judgment that had vacated the administration's guidance on removing people with final orders to countries not named in those orders, and agreed to hear the case, with argument set for December 9, 2026. Under the guidance, people can be sent to countries that have promised not to persecute or torture them without further steps; for other countries, the government gives notice and generally waits at least 24 hours for a fear claim. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson would have denied the stay.

Removal to a safe third country matches my position ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Removal of Non-Citizen Residents and Loss of Citizenship|Removal of Non-Citizen Residents]]). It differs in detail: I would never return anyone to persecution or torture, and I require notice and a real chance to be heard ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Due Process and Rule of Law|Due Process and Rule of Law]]), and a 24-hour window, or none where the country has given assurances, may fall short of that. The grade may change after the Court rules.

> - Supreme Court of the United States – [Docket No. 26-426: Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D.](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-426.html)

#### Refugee ceiling for fiscal year 2027
**September 25, 2026** · **Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)**

Presidential Determination No. 2026-24 caps refugee admissions for fiscal year 2027 at 17,500, allocated primarily to Afrikaners from South Africa under Executive Order 14204, and keeps the suspension and vetting rules of Executive Orders 14161 and 14163 in force.

A low ceiling matches my view of admission as a privilege granted in the national interest ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Immigration and Borders|Immigration and Borders]]). It differs in detail: I would select immigrants by individual fit with Western values and assimilation, not by national or ethnic group ([[2. Political and Social Character#Western Values and Compatibility|Western Values and Compatibility]]).

#### Limits on universal injunctions
**June 27, 2025** · **Grade: Mixed**

In *Trump v. CASA*, arising from challenges to Executive Order 14160, the Supreme Court held that federal courts generally may not issue universal injunctions.

The ruling is procedural. I keep court review of executive action ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Due Process and Rule of Law|Due Process and Rule of Law]]), and the ruling narrows the remedy without removing that review.

#### Diesel fuel tax deferral
**October 5, 2026** · **Grade: Mixed**

Executive Order 14435 directs the Treasury to defer federal excise taxes on diesel fuel incurred from October 5 through December 31, 2026, without penalties or interest, using its disaster-relief authority; tells the IRS not to penalize dyed diesel used on highways in that period; and asks the Treasury to explore legislation forgiving the deferred amounts. The order cites restricted global diesel supply and high prices for farmers and truckers.

Taxing less, and an end date written into the order, fit my low-tax and time-limit rules ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]; [[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Emergency Powers|Emergency Powers]]). It conflicts in that relief goes to chosen industries rather than across the board, forgiveness would add to deficits I require to be closed ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Fiscal Policy|Fiscal Policy]]), and part of the relief supports animal agriculture, which I would end ([[9. Energy & Environment#Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat|Agriculture, Grazing, and Habitat]]).

Lower taxes and lower spending match my direction ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Taxation|Taxation]]), but I target a balanced budget and reducing the debt ([[5. Domestic Policy & Governance#Fiscal Policy|Fiscal Policy]]), and the law adds to deficits.

#### Reciprocal tariffs
**April 2, 2025** · **Grade: Directionally opposed**

Executive Order 14257 imposed a baseline tariff on most imports, with higher country rates, under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA).

I hold tariffs to be good in theory but questionable in practice, used cautiously and targeted at leverage, strategic industries, and national-security threats ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Trade and Economics|Trade and Economics]]). Broad baseline tariffs go beyond that. On February 20, 2026, in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, the Supreme Court held 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs, and Executive Order 14389 ended the IEEPA duties the same day. That outcome fits my position, which places taxing power with the legislature.

#### Russia sanctions law waived
**October 16, 2026** · **Grade: Directionally opposed**

An executive order of October 16, 2026 implements the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, signed September 18. It sets a 500% added duty on Russian goods and 100% added duties on the largest buyers of Russian oil and gas and the top facilitators of sanctions evasion, then waives all of those duties and most of the Act's sanctions under the Act's national-interest waiver, until the President or Secretary of State decides otherwise. Sanctions under earlier Russia executive orders stay in place.

I treat Russia as an aggressor and use sanctions where they weaken an enemy's capacity to threaten the United States ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Sanctions and Economic Statecraft|Sanctions and Economic Statecraft]]). Waiving the new measures as soon as they take effect runs against that. Directional rather than full opposition because existing sanctions remain and the waivers can be lifted.

>[!quote]- Sources
> - The White House – [Implementing the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 (2026)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/implementing-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026/)

#### Control of Venezuelan oil exports and revenue
**January 9, 2026** · **Grade: Directionally aligned (details differ)**

After the capture, the United States took control of Venezuela's oil exports. Executive Order 14373 holds the sale proceeds in U.S. Treasury custody as Venezuelan sovereign property, with disbursements authorized by the Secretary of State, and shields them from attachment by creditors under a declared national emergency. The administration reported more than \$13 billion in sales by July 2026, with about \$3 billion disbursed by April for Venezuelan government salaries, oil-industry supplies, and other approved uses.

Securing the region and cutting off hostile actors matches my direction ([[8. Foreign Policy & International Relations#Interventionism vs Non-Intervention|Interventionism vs Non-Intervention]]). The details need to change to fit my position: control should defeat and contain the threat without governing the territory, and end on a fixed date unless Congress renews it ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Emergency Powers|Emergency Powers]]); creditors' claims should be settled through the courts rather than blocked by emergency ([[3. Authority & Civil Liberties#Due Process and Rule of Law|Due Process and Rule of Law]]); the revenue belongs to Venezuelans, whose property rights hold whatever their government did, so it needs full public accounting, published audits, and spending only for their benefit; and costs to American taxpayers should be disclosed.

#### Capture of Nicolás Maduro
**January 3, 2026** · **Grade: Aligned**
